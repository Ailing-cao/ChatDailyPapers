## Towards Scalable Diagnostic-Driven Whole-Slide Image Retrieval for Pathology Archives
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07935v1
- **Authors**: ['Jingsong Liu', 'Youssef Wally Han Li', 'Weiwei Zhou', 'Jing Dai', 'Elisabeth Wetzer', 'Peter J. Schüffler']
- **Abstrat**: The growing adoption of digital pathology has rapidly expanded collections of hematoxylin-and-eosin whole-slide images, yet efficient navigation through these archives remains challenging, as metadata is often inconsistent or unavailable. Content-based image retrieval addresses this challenge by identifying similar cases directly from visual representations instead of manual annotations or metadata. Existing retrieval systems have demonstrated promising performance but largely rely on handcrafted indexing pipelines and predefined sampling strategies, constraining scalability and generalization. Here, we present CLEAR-WSI (Constant-Length Embedding for Automatic Retrieval of Whole-Slide Images) as a scalable, diagnostic-driven slide-retrieval framework, validated on both public and clinical data. Using a fixed-length slide embedding, CLEAR-WSI refines retrieved candidates with either a predicted diagnostic label or the ground-truth label, depending on the retrieval setting. Newly processed cases are subsequently added to the expanding memory bank, allowing the retrieval archive to grow over time. On a comprehensive benchmark consisting of two public and one private clinical datasets representing four retrieval tasks, CLEAR-WSI improves Precision@5 over the strongest evaluated baseline, with gains on CAMELYON16 (3.10), BRACS-1 (2.75), BRACS-2 (1.95), and X-HER2 (3.75). Additional ablation and latency studies further examine the contribution of each component and highlight its potential for scalable deployment.





## Seeing Beyond the Next Step: World-Model-Guided Human-Like Navigation in Multi-Agent Scenes
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07730v1
- **Authors**: ['Seong-Eun Hong', 'JuYeong Hwang', 'Jinhyun Kim', 'Hanyoung Jang', 'HyeongYeop Kang']
- **Abstrat**: Selecting a next motion in a populated scene is not just a matter of avoiding what is currently in the way: an action can be locally feasible yet drift onto the road instead of the crosswalk, cut into a stream of crossing pedestrians, or bottleneck a narrow passage. These failure modes are invisible from the current state alone, and acting on them requires reasoning about what each candidate action will cause. World models offer exactly this, namely action-conditioned predictions of the future, but rolling them out at every decision step is a cost deployable navigation systems cannot pay, and the field has largely traded foresight for speed. We argue this is a false dichotomy. The natural place for a world model in human-aware navigation is at training time, as a preference oracle that distills foresight into a fast policy, rather than at inference time, as an online planner. We pair a discrete visual action prior with two task-aligned world models, one for nearby social dynamics and one for future scene semantics, that score candidate actions by their predicted consequences and refine the policy through group-relative preference distillation. At deployment, both world models are removed and the policy acts in a single forward pass. Across five held-out evaluation scenarios, this yields navigation that is simultaneously safer, more socially and group-coherent, and more goal-directed than strong reactive and learned baselines, with naive observers rating the resulting motion as more human-like. Taken together, our findings point to a different approach for bringing world-model foresight into deployable agents: not accelerating rollouts, but amortizing them into the policy so that the cost of looking ahead is confined to training. ## Fast and Reliable Open-Vocabulary Object Search with Absence Detection from Robot Memory
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07493v1
- **Authors**: ['Nicholas Paul', 'Devson Butani', 'Eric Martinson']
- **Abstrat**: Mobile manipulators deployed in everyday human environments, such as homes for assistive care or flexible automation, often need to locate specific objects on demand from accumulated observations. Equally important, they must reliably determine when a target is absent before committing to navigation or interaction. Existing approaches demand either slow exhaustive re-processing or expensive 3D representations, and are evaluated on recall and accuracy rather than specificity, leaving their ability to abstain on missing targets unmeasured. We find existing systems that search using text or visual embedding almost never abstain on absent objects achieving at most 9\% specificity. To support accurate search at speed, we present a two-stage architecture that decouples fast candidate retrieval from verified selection, enabling both high recall and reliable absence detection from sequential RGB observations with pose. We evaluate eight retrieval-selection configurations plus an exhaustive search baseline across ScanNet apartment scenes, including significant numbers of absent objects. Adding a visual verification step to caption-based retrieval matches or exceeds exhaustive search reliability while requiring an order of magnitude fewer detector calls. We reproduce ScanNet scores on a physical robot operating entirely on-board with a single consumer GPU running in real time.

- **Authors**: ['Iaroslav Kolomiets', 'Iaroslav Sheipak', 'Mikhail Reppo', 'Vsevolod Egorov', 'Viktor Chertopolokhov', 'Isaac Chairez']

- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07687v1

- **Abstrat**: Visual-inertial odometry (VIO) can degrade during aerial inspection in repetitive or poorly textured environments. Inspired by the fairy tale of Tom Thumb, the UAV creates visual features on demand by releasing ground markers or marking walls through physical contact. ## FREE-LIVO: Physically Decoupled Yet Tightly Coupled LiDAR-Inertial-Visual Odometry
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07463v1
- **Authors**: ['Reon Tabata', 'Kenji Koide', 'Shuji Oishi', 'Taku Okawara', 'Mizuho Aoki', 'Aoki Takanose', 'Masashi Yokozuka', 'Jun Miura']
- **Abstrat**: LiDAR-Inertial-Visual Odometry (LIVO) integrates complementary LiDAR, camera, and IMU measurements to achieve robust state estimation. However, most existing methods assume a fixed relative pose and accurate time synchronization between sensors. Toward modular and heterogeneous robotic systems, this paper presents FREE-LIVO, a tightly coupled odometry method that requires neither a rigid LiDAR-camera rig nor LiDAR-camera time synchronization. The proposed method uses a pretrained image feature matcher to establish correspondences between a camera image and a dense LiDAR intensity image generated from a single LiDAR scan using a generative model. From the resulting 3D point-to-pixel correspondences, we construct reprojection factors that directly constrain LiDAR and camera poses at different acquisition times. These factors are jointly optimized in a single factor graph together with constraints from point cloud registration, visual feature tracking, and the IMU measurements associated with each sensor. On six sequences from the public M3DGR dataset, FREE-LIVO achieves an average absolute trajectory error (ATE) RMSE of 0.45 m without provided LiDAR-camera extrinsic parameters and exhibits minimal accuracy degradation under time synchronization errors of up to 0.5 s. Furthermore, experiments with the LiDAR and camera mounted on separate robots demonstrate that the method can jointly estimate the trajectories of independently moving sensors.

## High resolution 3D localization of ultrasound sparse sources using a SAGE algorithm
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07966v1
- **Authors**: ['Sophie V. Heymans', 'François Rottenberg', 'Tatiana Gelvez-Barrera', 'Adrian Basarab', 'Barbara Nicolas']
- **Abstrat**: Reliable localization and characterization of cavitation activity are central to many ultrasound imaging and therapy monitoring applications. Passive acoustic mapping (PAM) can estimate cavitation distributions without prior assumptions but remains limited by the system point spread function. While advanced PAM methods improve spatial resolution, their performance may degrade for transient emissions with uncertain emission times. In this work, we propose a parametric source localization framework based on the Space-Alternating Generalized Expectation-Maximization (SAGE) algorithm to estimate source positions and emitted waveforms directly from array channel data. A greedy source extraction and localization procedure is first used to initialize the model order and source parameters, after which SAGE iteratively refines each source estimate. The proposed method was validated on 49 simulated acquisitions containing up to seven simultaneously emitting sources with emission time uncertainties of 12 microseconds, achieving a source localization precision of 0.24 mm (standard deviation of the total localization error). Experimental evaluation on proton-induced nanodroplet vaporization data acquired with an ultrasound matrix array demonstrated improved spatial resolution compared to a beamforming-based approach and sub-millimeter agreement between vaporization maps and the proton distribution in all three spatial dimensions. These results highlight the potential of parametric source localization for high-resolution proton therapy monitoring and provide a general framework for the localization of simultaneously emitting sparse ultrasound sources.

## ProCompNav: Proactive Instance Navigation with Comparative Judgment for Ambiguous User Queries
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06223v5
- **Authors**: ['Junhyuk Kwon', 'Seungjoon Lee', 'Hyejin Park', 'Kyle Min', 'Jungseul Ok']
- **Abstrat**: In human-robot interaction, agentic robots should resolve ambiguous user requests through proactive clarification while keeping user burden low. We study this challenge in interactive instance navigation, where an agent must identify and reach the user-intended target among similar objects. Existing approaches ask open-ended questions and select a candidate that strongly matches the accumulated user-provided information. However, this process can require lengthy user responses and lead the agent to make premature decisions without distinguishing the target from distractors. In two human studies, participants preferred ProCompNav's binary questions to open-ended questions and its batched delivery to interleaved interaction. Our results highlight the need to jointly consider task performance and user-friendly interaction strategies when designing agentic robots.





## MOMASPLAT: Dynamic Novel-View Demonstration Augmentation for Mobile Manipulation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08372v1
- **Authors**: ['Sateesh Kumar', 'Arnav Balaji', 'Arpit Bahety', 'Skand Peri', 'Georgios Pavlakos', 'Roberto Martín-Martín']
- **Abstrat**: Visuomotor policies trained through imitation learning are highly sensitive to changes in camera viewpoint. This is particularly problematic for mobile manipulation (MoMa), where robots rarely execute manipulation from the exact same base pose used during demonstration collection due to navigation uncertainty, localization drift, and environmental constraints. Recent approaches use novel-view synthesis (NVS) to augment demonstrations with unseen viewpoints, but they assume static scenes and therefore fail to re-render the manipulated object during the post-contact interaction phase, leaving the most critical parts of the demonstration unaugmented. We present MOMASPLAT, a novel-view demonstration augmentation framework for mobile manipulation that synthesizes full manipulation trajectories from unseen robot base poses, including dynamic object interactions. We evaluate MOMASPLAT on five real-world mobile manipulation tasks involving both rigid and articulated objects across three scenes, and validate the fidelity of its synthesized demonstrations against ground truth in simulation. MOMASPLAT substantially improves robustness to unseen base poses, more than doubling the average success rate of the strongest baseline and maintaining non-trivial success at large base displacements where all baselines fail, while reducing the need for largescale real-world data collection.

- **Abstrat**: Reward machines (RMs) have been proposed as a reward formulation in reinforcement learning (RL) to capture non-Markovian tasks where credit assignment depends on history. By representing reward functions as automata, RMs expose the task's temporal structure and allow such rewards to be handled within a Markovian framework. However, they assume a labelling function, a proposition-grounding oracle for observations, which must be manually engineered for each environment, limiting their wider applicability. In this work, we address this limitation by proposing RM-VLM, a novel approach in which a large language model (LLM) generates an RM from a natural-language task description, and a vision-language model (VLM) acts as the labelling function, grounding the RM's propositions over image-based observations during RM-guided policy learning. We evaluate RM-VLM on three domains covering non-Markovian grid-world navigation, robotic manipulation, and 3D object collection. Our experimental results show that RM-VLM consistently achieves higher task success rates than existing VLM-based reward methods across all tested domains.

## Geometry-Evolving Barrier Functions for Safety Evaluation in Dynamic Environments
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07788v1
- **Authors**: ['Zhi Zhang', 'Francesco Belardinelli', 'Alessandro Abate']
- **Abstrat**: Autonomous systems operating in dynamic environments often involve multiple interacting agents, giving rise to high-dimensional safety evaluation problems. Hamilton--Jacobi (HJ) reachability provides rigorous finite-horizon safety guarantees, but solving a global HJ partial differential equation (PDE) over the entire state space leads to prohibitive computational complexity. In this paper, we introduce Geometry-Evolving Barrier Functions (GEBFs), a novel representation for dynamic safety evaluation that propagates local safety geometry of the solution to a terminal-value HJ PDE. By evolving the value, gradient, and curvature information along a Hamiltonian-induced reference trajectory, GEBFs provide a compact representation of the local HJ safety landscape. Under suitable local regularity assumptions, we establish consistency between the GEBF and the value, gradient, and Hessian of the HJ solution along an HJ-optimal characteristic. Building on these theoretical results, we develop an iterative algorithm that couples local geometric propagation with trajectory refinement. We further establish a trajectory-wise one-sided relationship between the GEBF and HJ value function, together with a fixed-point consistency result for the iterative scheme. Experiments on widely adopted agent--obstacle collision-avoidance benchmarks, across low- and high-dimensional systems, demonstrate that GEBFs accurately capture the evolution of dynamic safety boundaries, while achieving substantially lower computational cost than conventional HJ reachability.

## H-BRICK: Hierarchical Boundary Reachability Index with Compressed Kleene Closure for Directed Grid Graphs
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08119v1
- **Authors**: ['Tolga Özaslan', 'Elif A. Özaslan']
- **Abstrat**: Repeated directed reachability queries arise in online path planning, task-agent allocation, and swarm control on static grid maps, where reachability is asymmetric and full transitive closure is storage-prohibitive. H-BRICK partitions the graph into tiles, computes a local Boolean Kleene closure within each tile, and projects it onto the tile's boundary ports. The resulting directed port-to-port summaries are then composed recursively across shared interfaces up to a single root covering the graph. A batched reachability evaluation further demonstrates that sharing endpoint lifts across many-source/many-target queries amortizes hierarchical traversal and, at k=64, yields a 5.0x-22.9x throughput improvement over the scalar query loop. The results show that hierarchical boundary compression is most advantageous in query-intensive spatial graphs where online search traverses large reachable regions and the hierarchy maintains compact boundary interfaces.

## Peer-Grounded Counterfactual Path Planning for Chronic Health Management
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30838v2
- **Authors**: ['Saman Khamesian', 'Hassan Ghasemzadeh']
- **Abstrat**: Effective behavioral intervention in chronic disease management requires a sequence of incremental steps, each grounded in what similar individuals have achieved. Multimodal records of daily behavior make such evidence available outside the clinic. Counterfactual explanation, an explainable AI approach, can turn these records into guidance, yet endpoint-only methods return a target state without a route to it. We propose POROS (Peer-Grounded Optimal Routes Over States), a domain-agnostic framework rooted in Bandura's self-efficacy theory and Festinger's social comparison theory. POROS constructs a Behavioral Progression Graph, a directed acyclic graph over observed patient states in which every edge requires both peer-grounded behavioral proximity and strict improvement in the observed health outcome. Every edge is bounded by a feasibility threshold derived from the cohort's day-to-day behavioral changes. Minimum-cost paths through this graph decompose behavioral gaps into incremental, peer-grounded steps. Compared with an endpoint-only baseline, POROS returns multi-hop paths to the same target for 14.4% and 19.8% of days below the 70% TIR threshold, reducing the per-step change in carbohydrate intake, insulin dose, and bolus timing by 38-42% on the larger cohort. POROS also supports improvement within the same outcome class, connecting 78.1% and 83.7% of outcome-ordered pairs of well-controlled days. Together, these findings demonstrate how POROS translates multimodal health records into incremental, peer-grounded guidance for chronic disease management through observational path planning.
