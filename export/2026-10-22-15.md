## NavBind: Diagnosing Instruction-Action Binding at Route Forks in Navigation VLAs
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29203v1
- **Authors**: ['Josh Qixuan Sun', 'Xiaoying Xing', 'Yurun Chen', 'Huaiyuan Weng', 'Wentao Zhu', 'Mark Crowley']
- **Abstrat**: Vision-language-action (VLA) navigation models translate visual observations and instructions into actions. Navigation success assesses whether an agent reaches the goal. However, reaching the goal successfully does not establish whether the model genuinely follows the instructions or relies on visual cues or navigation history. We introduce NavBind to diagnose instruction-action binding: whether changing the instruction influences the probabilistic distribution of the actions, with the navigation context fixed. We construct 3,252 route-fork contexts by recombining route-aligned instruction segments from R2R-CE and RxR-CE. Each context pairs full instructions that share the description of the route already traveled but specify different branches. Both are evaluated under the same agent state and navigation history. We compare generated local action sequences, the likelihoods of fixed reference action sequences under both instructions, and changes in visual attention and attribution. Across the evaluated models, higher reported navigation success does not imply that the model more faithfully follows the instructions.





## Navigation Explainability for Object Avoidance with LLMs for Assistive Robots
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28920v1
- **Authors**: ['Courtney Young', 'Yi Lian', 'Taery Kim', 'Sehoon Ha']
- **Abstrat**: Blind and visually impaired individuals face uncertainty when navigating unfamiliar areas. Whether a cane or a guide dog is used for navigation, this may prove to be challenging. To mitigate this challenge, we present the Context-Aware Navigation Explanation framework that explains guide dog movements for obstacle avoidance and navigation using a large language model. This system integrates perceptual context with planned future trajectories to generate natural-language explanations of robot movement. We deploy the system on a Unitree Go2 platform in indoor navigation scenarios and evaluate how different explanation context configurations affect user trust and explanation satisfaction. Results show a strong positive relationship between explanation satisfaction and trust, and suggest that combining obstacle information with future trajectory leads to the most effective explanations. These findings highlight the potential of context-aware language explanations to improve transparency and trust in assistive robot navigation.

## Hilti-Trimble-Oxford Dataset: 360 Visual-Inertial Benchmark with Floor Plan Priors for SLAM and Localization
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06464v2
- **Authors**: ['Samuele Centanni', 'Yuhao Zhang', 'Yifu Tao', 'Julien Kindle', 'Frank Neuhaus', 'Tilman Koß', 'Aryaman Patel', 'Michael Helmberger', 'Emilia Szymańska', 'Torben Gräber', 'Maurice Fallon']
- **Abstrat**: Automated progress monitoring on construction sites is an active area of research and development. Robot and human-carried mapping systems have been developed to build 3D maps of building and infrastructure projects. While LiDAR-based mapping systems achieve high accuracy, the cost of LiDAR can be prohibitive. Consumer-grade cameras with wide field of view ("360 cameras") combined with embedded inertial measurement units (IMUs) provide a cost-effective alternative. To support change detection and progress monitoring, highly accurate visual simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) and floor plan localization systems are required. In this paper, we present a high-quality dataset collected at an active construction site, which captures realistic challenges such as variable lighting conditions, moving workers, fast motions, and repetitive structures. The dataset offers thirty visual-inertial sequences recorded across ten floors over an eight-month period of the construction project. Ground truth trajectories are collected using a high quality LiDAR-inertial SLAM system rigidly attached to the 360 camera. Additionally, we report the results of an open research challenge evaluating the best visual SLAM and localization systems from around the world. The Challenge attracted substantially higher participation in SLAM, with 62 teams compared to 22 in floor plan localization, which suggests that this task receives less attention from researchers and motivates further exploration through proposed dataset and challenge, available at: https://hilti-trimble-challenge.com/dataset-2026.

## Reading the Room: Scale-ambiguous Semantic Registration of Sparse VSLAM Maps to Floor Plans in Partially Built Environments
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29647v1
- **Authors**: ['Bjorn Stragier', 'Emilia Szymańska', 'J. Micah Prendergast', 'Lukas Rosenberger Schmid']
- **Abstrat**: Many robotic construction applications, such as progress tracking, require registering a sensor trajectory to a prior 2D floor plan. Visual capture is attractive for its low cost and availability, but aligning a visual simultaneous localization and mapping (VSLAM) map to a floor plan is difficult; VSLAM recovers geometry only up to an unknown similarity transform, the sparse point cloud is cross-modal to the symbolic floor plan, sites are only partially built during capture, and construction sites are visually repetitive, degrading map quality while removing distinctive features. Existing methods address at most two of these challenges. We thus introduce Reading the Room(RR), a global-local approach to register scale-ambiguous, sparse, semantic VSLAM maps to partially built floor plans. Our key idea is to define a single robust, differentiable, class-balanced semantic-geometric energy function that governs all stages of the pipeline. We develop two global initialization schemes, leveraging a single inaccurate user-provided correspondence if provided, or estimating the initialization globally using a cross-modal matching network. Both schemes are designed to find initial candidates with respect to the energy function, which is then directly optimized for local refinement. We evaluate RR on 33 sequences across three active construction sites against classical and learning-based baselines, achieving a 4.5x improvement in mean absolute trajectory error over the strongest baseline in the general unconstrained setting, which increases to 7.6x when an anchor is provided.

## Onboard UAV State Estimation for Fast Flights Without External Localization Systems: A2RL Drone Racing Finalist Approach
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01860v2
- **Authors**: ['Filip Novák', 'Matěj Petrlík', 'Matej Novosad', 'Parakh M. Gupta', 'Robert Pěnička', 'Martin Saska']
- **Abstrat**: Fast flights with aggressive maneuvers in cluttered GNSS-denied environments require fast, reliable, and accurate UAV state estimation. In this paper, we present an approach for onboard state estimation of a high-speed UAV using a monocular RGB camera and an IMU. Our approach fuses data from Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO), an onboard landmark-based camera measurement system, and an IMU to produce an accurate state estimate. Using onboard measurement data, we estimate and compensate for VIO drift through a novel mathematical drift model. State-of-the-art approaches often rely on more complex hardware (e.g., stereo cameras or rangefinders) and use uncorrected drifting VIO velocities, orientation, and angular rates, leading to errors during fast maneuvers. In contrast, our method corrects all VIO states (position, orientation, linear and angular velocity), resulting in accurate state estimation even during rapid and dynamic motion. Our approach was thoroughly validated through 1600 simulations and numerous real-world experiments. Furthermore, we applied the proposed method in the A2RL Drone Racing Challenge 2025, where our team advanced to the final four out of 210 teams and earned a medal.

## Rethinking Video Autoencoders for Interactive World Models
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30129v1
- **Authors**: ['Adrien Ramanana Rahary', 'Yutong Bai', 'Nicolas Dufour', 'Patrick Pérez', 'David Picard']
- **Abstrat**: Video world models predict future frames from past frames and actions. They serve as simulators in which agents train, plan or are evaluated, and that humans can control interactively. They generate in the latent space of a video autoencoder, whose compression sets their cost per frame and how long each action waits to take effect. Yet world models often reuse the autoencoders of text-to-video generators, built for offline generation with no agent waiting and, without their training data and code, not retrainable at another compression. We therefore ask how, where and how much a world model should compress. We build CADRE, an open family of causal diffusion representation autoencoders spanning multiple spatio-temporal compressions. Its members differ only in a small bottleneck on frozen features and share a diffusion decoder self-distilled to one or a few steps. We train the same world model on each autoencoder in two domains, manipulation and navigation, with two environments each. CADRE forms most of the latency-quality frontier, especially with a moving camera. Our study also leads to four findings. At strong compression, a diffusion decoder is sharper than perceptual regression. In robot manipulation, temporal compression is more effective in the autoencoder than in the world model. Manipulation and navigation call for opposite compressions: temporal compression costs little when the camera is still, and spatial compression when it moves. Surprisingly, with a still camera, coarser spatial tokens hurt motion far more than image quality. Code, models and a live demo are at https://kyutai.org/blog/2026-10-21-cadre





## Asymmetric On-Policy Distillation Learns In-Context Exploration
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30105v1
- **Authors**: ['Sriyash Poddar', 'Yanda Bao', 'Jacob Krantz', 'Matthew Chang', 'Xavier Puig', 'Roozbeh Mottaghi', 'Natasha Jaques', 'Abhishek Gupta']
- **Abstrat**: Sequential decision-making under partial observability is difficult because agents must explore to infer hidden, task-relevant information before acting effectively. Learning such information-gathering behaviors tabula rasa via online reinforcement learning is sample-inefficient, whereas offline imitation averages expert actions across hidden states and fails to learn exploration. In this work, we propose ASTEROID, a simple and effective student--teacher supervised learning framework that can learn test-time exploration. Although a privileged expert never needs to explore, we demonstrate that a history-conditioned student can learn to do so when trained with on-policy contexts: the student first collects on-policy context under partial observability, and the privileged expert provides action labels conditioned on that context. By distilling expert actions directly conditioned on the student's on-policy history, the student learns to systematically resolve partial observability, i.e., search while uncertain and exploit once the hidden state is revealed. This enables efficient test-time exploration (similar to Bayesian posterior sampling) without incurring the impractical training-data costs of reinforcement learning. Across a diverse range of eleven different environments, including discrete exploration benchmarks, robotic manipulation, navigation, and Procgen environments, ASTEROID achieves ~2x higher returns under fixed exploration budgets, uses ~100x fewer interactions than RL baselines, and outperforms frontier models at exploration. Finally, our method transfers policies trained in simulation to vision-denied real-world robot manipulation, exhibiting efficient exploration and increasing success by +40% over standard distillation. Overall, it provides a simple yet surprisingly effective framework to learn efficient test-time exploration.





## OSCAR: Obstacle Survival Curves for Adaptive Robot Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00990v3
- **Authors**: ['Hshmat Sahak', 'Aoran Jiao', 'Nicholas Rhinehart', 'Tim Barfoot']
- **Abstrat**: A mobile robot following a graph of known routes can make costly navigation errors when a temporary obstacle blocks a critical edge: waiting too long behind a parked cart wastes time, but immediately rerouting around a person who would move in a few seconds is also inefficient. Standard reactive obstacle avoidance addresses local motion around obstacles, while fixed wait-or-reroute rules ignore how long different obstacle types tend to persist. We propose OSCAR: an adaptive survival-modeling framework for graph-based navigation with temporary blockages. Assuming obstacle class labels are available at encounter time, the robot learns class-conditioned residual clearance-time distributions from online experience, including right-censored observations when it reroutes before observing clearance. These survival models are integrated into a time-dependent graph planner that maintains obstacle memory and computes a patience threshold at each blocked edge: how long to wait before taking an alternate route. The method continuously updates its clearance estimates across episodes and uses them to balance waiting against rerouting. We evaluate the approach in simulation and on a real mobile robot in a university atrium with obstacles including people, chairs, bins, and tubes. In simulation, the learned policy's time-to-goal converges to within 1% of an oracle with access to ground-truth clearance distributions after fewer than 20 observations per obstacle class, outperforming all heuristic baselines. Real-world deployment confirms that the policy improves online, adapting its patience thresholds from experience across 50 navigation episodes.

## Action-Conditioned World Model for Goal Plane Guidance in Robotic Neck Ultrasound

## PerMIT: Securing Persistent Memory for LLM Agents via Machine-Checked Integrity Theorems and Scalable Community Trust
