## InFeR: Informed Failure Resilience in Learned Visual Navigation Control
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24680v3
- **Authors**: ['Zishuo Wang', 'Joel Loo', 'David Hsu']
- **Abstrat**: While imitation learning (IL) has enabled successful visual navigation in many common environments, IL policies are prone to unpredictable failures under out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. This necessitates failure-resilient policies, which not only detect failures, but also recognise their sources and recover from them autonomously. We propose InFeR, a general framework for building IL policies with informed failure resilience without failure or recovery demonstrations. Given an IL policy's architecture and training dataset, InFeR retrains a new OOD-aware policy with a latent space structured for OOD failure detection, with a modified objective based on the Variational Information Bottleneck. It applies a visual explainability technique, Grad-CAM, to attribute failures to specific image regions and inform a heuristic policy for recovery. All these are achieved without requiring additional training data. Real-world experiments show that InFeR enables informed failure recovery across two different policy architectures, supporting long-range navigation in complex environments.





## CRA: Continuous Risk Assessment for Safety-critical Visual Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22080v1
- **Authors**: ['Lihao Zheng', 'Ying Wu', 'Chen Jiang', 'Randy Freeman']
- **Abstrat**: Traditional safety-critical control methods often rely on predefined constraints that separate states into safe and unsafe regions. While recent studies have extended geometric safety toward semantic safety, real-world safety is rarely purely binary. The risk associated with an object or human can vary continuously with its physical properties, like motion, and interaction context; for example, a person carrying a heavy object or a hot object moving at high speed may require a different safety response from an ordinary static obstacle. In this paper, we propose a vision-based continuous risk assessment framework for safety-critical visual navigation. Our method uses RGB and LiDAR observations to estimate four risk factors (kinetic, potential, contact-severity, and thermal) and combines them into a continuous risk score for each assessed object or person. These estimates are integrated into a Poisson safety function with a Laplace guidance field as a safety filter, enabling online monitoring and intervention in open-world robot navigation. We demonstrate the proposed method in a simulated restaurant scene.





## PathoGaze1.0: An AI-Ready Navigation, Eye-Tracking, and Decision Dataset in Pathology

- **Authors**: ['Veronica Thai', 'Rui Li', 'Meng Ling', 'Shuning Jiang', 'Jeremy Wolfe', 'Raghu Machiraju', 'Yan Hu', 'Zaibo Li', 'Anil Parwani', 'Jian Chen']

The testbed supports interaction techniques found in clinical tools and collects viewport, mouse actions, and diagnostic decisions, enabling cross-links between WSI image stimulus, gaze, navigation behaviors, and diagnostic decisions. ## Every Camera Grows Up: Camera-Height Regression in Interactive Video World Models
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21782v1
- **Authors**: ['Sheng Lu', 'Guangpin Tao', 'Tong Lu', 'Ying Tai']
- **Abstrat**: Interactive video world models serve as simulators for embodied agents: from a starting image and an action trajectory, they generate the views the agent would see. The starting image fixes how high the camera is, for example 0.15 m for a floor robot or 2.2 m for an overhead camera, and a faithful simulator must keep this height as the camera moves. We show that six recent open-weight models fail to do so: as motion replaces the starting view with generated content, they move the camera toward human eye level, a failure we call camera-height regression. Our benchmark, EyeHeight, pairs six rendered elevations (0.15 m to 2.2 m) and four calibrated real-world rig heights with scale-free multi-view height estimation. All six models lose their initial height during a $180^\circ$ turn-and-walk rollout: initialized at 0.15 m, only 0-20% of clips end within $1.3\times$ of the starting height as inferred viewpoints converge to human heights of 1.1 m to 1.5 m, whereas ground-truth renders remain within a few percent. In Matrix-Game models, homography-tracked floor motion stays consistent with constant elevation during rotation even as newly outpainted room geometry yields a higher reconstructed viewpoint. Regression grows with rotation angle, begins while the start frame remains in context, and largely reverses in SANA-WM when a $360^\circ$ turn restores the initial view. We trace this pull to human-centric video priors in which floor-level views are nearly absent: in SANA-WM, textual height cues and dense floor-disparity maps do not prevent it, whereas classifier-free text guidance during the first half of denoising strengthens it. Combining unguided sampling with height-balanced fine-tuning recovers more retention but costs 5 MUSIQ points, a loss fixed during those same early steps. Viewpoint drift alone shifts the paths predicted by visual navigation policies.

## DGV-DLIVO: Degeneracy-Guided Visual Fusion for Direct LiDAR-Inertial-Visual Odometry in Degenerate Tunnel Environments
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22385v1
- **Authors**: ['Junrui Zhang']
- **Abstrat**: Reliable localization in long underground infrastructures remains a major challenge for autonomous inspection robots. In hydropower spillway tunnels and drainage galleries, repetitive geometric structures provide insufficient constraints along the tunnel axis, while weak texture and illumination variations further degrade visual estimation. Under this coupled degradation, existing LiDAR-inertial and visual-inertial odometry systems may lose the ability to recover continuous longitudinal motion. This paper presents DGV-DLIVO, a degeneracy-guided visual fusion for direct LiDAR-inertial-visual odometry for extreme tunnel environments. Instead of treating vision as a general complementary sensor, the proposed method exploits the LiDAR-degenerate direction as an active guidance signal to determine informative visual regions, establish LiDAR-anchored visual associations, and perform subspace-selective fusion. The visual constraints are introduced only into the deficient motion subspace, while unreliable LiDAR information in the same direction is adaptively suppressed and reliable geometric constraints are preserved. Real-world experiments in hydropower tunnels demonstrate that representative state-of-the-art LIO, LIVO, and VIO methods suffer from stagnation, oscillation, severe under-travel, curved drift, or divergence, whereas DGV-DLIVO maintains continuous and directionally consistent tunnel-axis motion estimation under both cart-mounted and handheld acquisition. It achieves a 91.4% progress ratio over the 40m tunnel sequence and significantly improves longitudinal recovery and trajectory stability. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of degeneracy-guided adaptive fusion for robotic inspection in severely degraded underground environments. The source code and a supplementary video are publicly available at https://github.com/0RayZhang0/DGV-DLIVO.

## Pressure Drop: Gravity Vector Estimation from a Distributed Barometric Pressure Sensor Array
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23120v1
- **Authors**: ['William Dubois', 'Nicolas Lauzon', 'Johann Laconte', 'François Pomerleau']
- **Abstrat**: While gravity is often used in mobile robotics as a global constraint for Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) algorithms, most estimation methods rely on indirect observation of the gravity vector. In this paper, we propose the use of altimeter measurements to directly observe the direction of the gravity vector. We provide a theoretical evaluation of the estimation noise and validate it through extensive experimental data, along with an evaluation of the impact of intersensor distance on our gravity vector estimates. Experimental results demonstrate that a median error of $0.87^\circ$ on the tilt estimation can be achieved with our system in semi-static scenarios. Furthermore, we demonstrate the potential of this approach by comparing it with state-of-the-art methods based on Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs). - **Authors**: ['Junseong Pyo', 'Hyeongjin Kim', 'Jongwook Han', 'Seungwon Kook', 'Sungjib Lim', 'Yohan Jo']
- **Abstrat**: Household robots often need to operate appliances, and smart appliances offer both control panels and device APIs. When choosing between pressing a button and calling an API, several planners go by whether they can read the panel, not by API latency.\footnote{We will release our code and data upon publication.}





## Network-Exposed Configuration Attacks on LLM-Controlled Social Robots
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22761v1
- **Authors**: ['Stanley Akingbola', 'Ugbede Ibrahim', 'Yan Lin Aung', 'Quang Dan Le', 'Hong Qing Yu']
- **Abstrat**: Large language models (LLMs) in embodied robots create security dependencies beyond inference. We investigate a network-exposed configuration attack on the Pollen Robotics Reachy Mini Lite, allowing an unauthenticated attacker to replace personality settings and the cloud LLM's system prompt through a management interface, without adversarial conversational input. Eleven prompt-injection attempts across five categories were reported as resisted, whereas both configuration-layer attack paths produced observable behavioural changes on physical hardware. We demonstrate local and network-adjacent access, mapping the weakness to CWE-306 (Missing Authentication for a Critical Function) and CWE-1327 (Binding to an Unrestricted IP Address), with a reported CVSS v3.1 score of 6.5 (Medium). A configuration endpoint also accepts cross-origin requests without a CORS preflight (CWE-352; 6.5 Medium), while the persistent-memory sink stores content without enforcing its stated restrictions. A source review of 59 public repositories identifies missing authentication in 10 of 11 source-verifiable genuine applications, or 11 of 11 including a framework-default case. These findings indicate recurrence within the sampled repositories, not prevalence in commercial products. We characterise a deployment gap between developer-oriented network assumptions and consumer-facing embodied AI security: inference-layer refusal cannot prevent unauthorised changes to model instructions. Potential consequences include surveillance through onboard cameras and microphones and social engineering of nearby users. Proof-of-concept code, evidence and the survey are available at https://bit.ly/reachypi.

## DGF-Asym: Adaptive Peer Weighting for Collective Estimation under Noisy and Faulty Sensing
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22532v1
- **Authors**: ['Mohammad Nomaan Husain', 'Tanja Katharina Kaiser']
- **Abstrat**: Collective estimation under unreliable sensing requires robots to adaptively balance personal observations and social information from peers. We propose DGF-Asym, a decentralized collective estimation method extending the DeGroot-Friedkin model with asymmetric peer weighting based on retrospective predictive performance. The method supports consensus formation without prior sensor-noise statistics or explicit fault models while keeping inter-robot communication asynchronous. ## ROSMonitoring in Action: Runtime Monitoring and Enforcement for ROS 2 Actions
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22407v1
- **Authors**: ['Giacomo Paltrinieri', 'Angelo Ferrando']
- **Abstrat**: Robotic applications often rely on long-running operations, such as navigation, manipulation, and inspection, which in ROS~2 are commonly implemented as actions. Unlike topics and services, actions involve a structured lifecycle comprising goal submission and acceptance, feedback, status updates, result retrieval, and cancellation. Existing versions of ROSMonitoring support topics and services, but do not reconstruct this lifecycle or expose action-level events to a runtime-verification oracle. In this paper, we extend ROSMonitoring with runtime monitoring and enforcement for ROS~2 actions. Actions are declared directly in the monitoring configuration and automatically instrumented through the underlying ROS~2 communication endpoints. The generated monitor reconstructs action-level events, correlates them through goal identifiers, maintains the state of active goals, and translates violations into protocol-aware enforcement operations. We evaluate the implementation through controlled stress tests that vary the number of action invocations, workload size, and client–server topology. The results show approximately linear growth in monitoring cost with repeated action invocations under a fixed workload, while larger action workloads and a greater number of configured client–server pairs introduce additional overhead. These results characterise the scaling behaviour of the current implementation under the tested sequential workloads while highlighting the main performance limitations of the current synchronous implementation.





## Beyond Episodic Recall: Experience-to-Knowledge Evolution for Lifelong Embodied Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22321v1
- **Authors**: ['Weitao An', 'Chenghao Xu', 'Xu Yang', 'Cheng Deng']
- **Abstrat**: Lifelong embodied navigation requires agents to accumulate experience through continuous interaction and reuse it for subsequent goal searches. Existing memory-enhanced methods typically retain historical observations or construct structured scene representations. However, storing observations or high-dimensional semantic embeddings incurs growing storage costs. Meanwhile, structured representations support retrieval and semantic reasoning but do not explicitly evolve accumulated experience into environment-specific knowledge. To address these limitations, we propose Experience-Evolving Memory (EvoNav), a novel two-timescale memory framework. It organizes semantic observations, spatial relations, and task-feedback cues into sparse experience atoms. During online operation, experience atoms continually consolidate repeated observations and form dynamic links based on transitions. At the slower timescale, EvoNav distills accumulated category co-occurrences and feedback signals into environment-specific structural knowledge without relying on predefined object--room relations. For each new goal, EvoNav combines direct goal evidence from experience atoms with learned structural knowledge to guide goal-directed search on the current traversability map. Experiments on single-goal, sequential multi-floor, and multimodal navigation demonstrate effective experience reuse and improved path efficiency with compact persistent memory, together with successful transfer to a real robot.





## Inertial Video World Models
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22314v1
- **Authors**: ['Yuan Gao', 'Fan Xu', 'Yu Guo', 'Yuxu Lu', 'Zhengru Fang', 'Hao Wu', 'Xiaomeng Huang']
- **Abstrat**: The physical world has inertia: it evolves from what already exists rather than being recreated at every instant. However, many existing video world models lack an explicit mechanism for inertial state evolution, leaving the continuity of motion and structure implicit in video prediction. During autoregressive rollouts, repeated reconstruction can accumulate errors and destabilize the predicted dynamics. To address this limitation, we introduce InertialWorld, an Inertial Video World Model that makes inertia a structural prior for autoregressive prediction: existing scene evidence is carried across time rather than reconstructed at every step. Central to this formulation is Inertial Memory, which turns memory from passive predictive context into an evolving video state with two complementary views: a dense Eulerian grid preserves scene-aligned evidence, while persistent Lagrangian carriers bind local content to learned canonical coordinates. At each step, the model transports the carrier content according to action-conditioned motion, predicts the complete next video state from the transported carriers and dense grid, and uses a learned gate to combine transported and newly predicted content into the memory for the next step. Extensive experiments across multiple domains, including robot manipulation, 3D game simulation, and open-world navigation, demonstrate that InertialWorld achieves competitive autoregressive prediction performance against strong video world model baselines. Codes link: https://github.com/YuanGao-YG/InertialWorld
