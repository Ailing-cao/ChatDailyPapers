## Velox: Spatial Memory Makes World Models Efficient
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22311v1
- **Authors**: ['Qiuhong Shen', 'Shizun Wang', 'Xinchao Wang']
- **Abstrat**: Video world models turn actions and camera motion into long visual rollouts, making video a natural interface for interactive simulation. Such interaction requires responsive generation, yet coherent rollouts must retain visual memory of what has already been generated. Chunked video diffusion models usually keep this context in growing KV caches, so later chunks become progressively slower. To address this bottleneck, we introduce Velox, a training-free attention router for region-level context selection using frustum slab correspondences from streaming geometry. Velox represents historical blocks as camera frustum slabs with depth intervals and obtains current-block depth through recency-priority multiwarp. For well-covered regions, frustum slab overlap softly modulates each layer's model-derived block importance. Uncovered regions retain the model-derived route without geometric bias. The fixed token-block granularity matches FlashAttention-style tiled execution and reduces the attention term that grows with rollout length. Frustum slab construction runs asynchronously with denoising on the same H200. At chunk 48, Velox accelerates attention by $4.36\times$ and denoising by $2.43\times$. On the full 158-case WBench navigation split, it remains within 0.15 API-free average points of dense LingBot-Fast-Cam. Please see our project page at https://velox-world.com/velox/ for more details.





## Onboard Lightweight LLM Reasoning for Human-Aware Safe UAV Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22092v1
- **Authors**: ['Siwei Cai', 'Yuwei Wu', 'Vijay Kumar', 'Lifeng Zhou']
- **Abstrat**: Aerial robots operating near people must treat a pedestrian, a parked car, and a wall differently, yet conventional perception--planning stacks reduce all of them to occupied geometry---a \emph{human-aware safety gap} in which category-specific clearance is unavailable to the planner and temporary occlusions remove pedestrian-specific constraints from its map. We present a modular, fully onboard pipeline that closes this gap by turning visual observations into context-dependent safety constraints for real-time UAV navigation. A shared BLIP encoder drives scene captioning and text-conditioned segmentation, which is fused with depth to localize obstacles in 3D. Obstacle-specific risk is inferred by a lightweight LLM (Qwen-2.5-0.5B) stabilized with retrieval-augmented generation and grammar-constrained decoding that restricts its output to a planner-compatible JSON schema. The LLM reasons once per object category rather than per frame: its verdicts are cached per class, so previously seen categories are resolved without inference and new ones trigger a fresh retrieval-grounded query; detections are additionally given persistent identities and held in the map across occlusions. The perception pipeline runs at 10\,Hz on a Jetson Orin NX, while fallback LLM queries take approximately 0.8\,s and remain off the control-critical path. In Gazebo, low-altitude urban navigation among pedestrians and vehicles shows a roughly $3\times$ reduction in collision events over a geometric planner, and semantics-aware landing among moving actors raises success from 34\% to 96\%. The 224-pixel encoder input limits reliable small-object perception at range; conservative pedestrian margins and short-term persistence mitigate this limitation during navigation.





## VesperMUSE: Multi-Modal Fusion Vision Transformer for Image Semantic Segmentation Under Diverse Light Conditions
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21990v1
- **Authors**: ['Raul Balmez']
- **Abstrat**: Semantic segmentation is critical for the reliable navigation and scene understanding of autonomous systems, yet its performance degrades severely in low-light environments due to noise, artifacts, colour distortion, and the loss of structural details. To address this challenge, this work introduces VesperMUSE, a unified multi-modal framework designed to achieve robust image semantic segmentation across diverse illumination conditions. The architecture addresses the light deficiency problem on multiple levels: proposing a novel physics-informed synthetic low-light adaptation algorithm, approaching Low-Light Image Enhancement (LLIE) through a thermal-guided paradigm, and finally aiming to recover the intrinsic scene details for semantic segmentation through a multi-modal fusion approach. Following enhancement, the framework dynamically integrates the primary RGB features with auxiliary thermal representations and surface normal maps highlighting geometric discontinuities and material boundaries. At the core of this integration is a highly scalable, modality-agnostic self-attention mechanism that extracts both global context and fine local features from an arbitrary number of auxiliary inputs. By combining and projecting these hierarchical multi-modal representations into a unified component, the attention module allows the network to selectively attend to relevant cross-modal cues without requiring any architectural modifications when different sensor configurations are used. Extensive evaluations demonstrate that VesperMUSE successfully recovers missing visual cues, providing a flexible, state-of-the-art (SOTA) solution for machine perception in extreme and unstructured real-world environments.





## Towards bio-inspired visual navigation: a neuromorphic navigation paradigm for MAVs
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21978v1
- **Authors**: ['Junjie Jiang', 'Ankang Zhou', 'Zheng Fang']
- **Abstrat**: Biological visual navigation achieves remarkable robustness at extremely low power, whereas frame-based robotic systems relying on artificial neural networks remain limited in both respects, particularly for energy-constrained micro aerial vehicles (MAVs) operating in complex environments. Inspired by the event-driven, sparse, and asynchronous principles of biological visual systems, neuromorphic sensing and computation provide a promising alternative for robust and energy-efficient navigation. In this paper, we propose the first end-to-end event-driven neuromorphic paradigm for MAV visual navigation in complex environments by integrating neuromorphic vision, spiking neural networks, and neuromorphic processors. The proposed method first extracts the environmental representation from event streams using a spiking perceptual representation module. This representation is fed into a spiking navigation module, where guidance learning and population coding enable efficient policy learning with few time steps. The navigation module then outputs a waypoint converted into executable trajectory points. Experiments show that the proposed neuromorphic method achieves navigation performance comparable to ANN-based methods while markedly reducing power consumption. The spiking perceptual module consumes only 19.57% of the power of its ANN counterpart, while the spiking navigation module deployed on Intel Loihi consumes only 12.37% of that required by its ANN counterpart running on NVIDIA Orin NX. Beyond prior neuromorphic systems focused mainly on relatively simple navigation subtasks, we demonstrate a complete neuromorphic visual navigation system spanning simulation and real-world deployment. Our results establish the feasibility of the neuromorphic paradigm for autonomous navigation and provide a bio-inspired pathway toward robust and low-power MAV navigation.

## AVALON: Zero-Shot Indoor Navigation with Human-Seeded Topological Memory
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21586v1
- **Authors**: ['Jing Xia', 'Changyan Li', 'Yuduo Wang', 'Guiyin Tian', 'Bo Wang', 'Yifan Liao', 'Mingjun Li', 'Shiyan Hu']
- **Abstrat**: Indoor service robots often enter unmapped buildings whose layouts and task constraints are already familiar to users. We address this human-familiar, robot-unseen setting with AVALON, a topological navigation framework organized around human-seeded layered spatial memory, node-verified topological execution, and task-triggered, evidence-gated human--robot memory co-updating. The robot has no prior on-site traversal or site-specific training. Deployment requires only a floor plan, place clues, and an initial topology-odometry alignment. Visual depth point-goal navigation leverages verified node arrivals to perform translational re-anchoring, without requiring a robot-constructed global SLAM map. Human--robot memory co-updating fuses complementary sources of information. Human operators specify task constraints that are unobservable via vision and report offline environmental modifications, whereas the robot provides physical-state evidence through active in-situ validation. Task relevance governs whether human-reported environmental changes undergo robot validation during the active mission. Field-collected perceptual evidence gates memory updates, maintaining an explicit separation between human-specified permissions and robot-observed traversability. Our experiments on a Unitree Go2 show that AVALON with X-NavDP completes a 12.81 m Intersection-to-Dining mission in 46.4 s (5/5) with no mapping, 40.9% less robot time than DimOS-Go2 (78.5 s, 4/5), while policy-only X-NavDP times out in all of the 5 runs. These demonstrate the superiority of the proposed topological navigation framework.





## From Optical Flow to Safety: Visual Safety Certification via Scale-Free Relative Motion
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21374v1
- **Authors**: ['Bazeela Banday', 'Jishnu Keshavan']
- **Abstrat**: Safe autonomous navigation using monocular vision remains challenging because explicit obstacle states are not directly observable. This letter introduces a visual safety-certification paradigm that uses scale-free, depth-normalized relative motion encoded by monocular optical flow as the representation for collision-risk certification, without reconstructing obstacle position, velocity, geometry, or a metric world model. Unlike conventional optical-flow-based navigation approaches that rely on image motion to shape avoidance behavior, the proposed framework directly encodes collision-cone geometry and safety constraints in this relative-motion representation. An online Concurrent Learning observer estimates the normalized relative velocity and provides a computable estimation-error bound. This uncertainty is propagated into the collision cone through angular inflation and a monocular robot-footprint margin, yielding a Robust Vision-Based Collision Cone Control Barrier Function (RV-C$^3$BF). An optimal-decay CBF quadratic program minimally modifies the nominal control input to enforce the resulting robust safety constraint under the stated assumptions. Extensive simulation and real-world experiments on a TurtleBot3 in cluttered environments with static and dynamic obstacles demonstrate collision avoidance using only monocular vision and onboard proprioception, with the estimation-error bound containing the measured error in $98.3\%$ of simulation samples and the robot achieving collision-free navigation in $93$ of $100$ real-world trials.





## DocPilot: Interactive Agent Orchestration and Training for Long Visual Document Understanding
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21327v1
- **Authors**: ['Zhuowen Liang', 'Xiaotian Lin', 'Yuyu Luo', 'Haixun Wang', 'Nan Tang']
- **Abstrat**: Multimodal long-document question answering requires models to seek sparse evidence across pages and modalities, understand heterogeneous document content, and reason over the collected evidence. Even advanced vision-language models (VLMs) can overlook sparse evidence and struggle with cross-modal reasoning in a single pass. Existing document agents further introduce multi-step interaction, but some still rely on predefined workflows or coarse-grained actions, limiting the flexibility and granularity of document reasoning. We introduce DocPilot, an agentic document framework that supports expressive orchestration and makes complex document solving learnable by small language models (SLMs). DocPilot orchestrates evidence seeking through page-level navigation and fine-grained evidence localization, and integrates local visual inspection, code-based numerical reasoning, and cross-modal reasoning for multimodal understanding. To transfer these capabilities to small models, we propose curriculum-based agentic training that progresses from single-ability learning to adaptive orchestration through supervision from data-grounded reasoning traces and executable interaction trajectories. Across MMLongBench-Doc, DocBench, FinRAGBench-V, and MADQA, DocPilot achieves strong gains with frontier VLM backbones, while DocPilot-4B delivers competitive performance against substantially larger models, demonstrating strong effectiveness and generalization across diverse long-document reasoning settings. The code is available at https://github.com/HKUSTDial/DocPilot.





## Vision-Guided Closed-Loop Autonomous Navigation of Light-Driven Janus Microbots
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21222v1
- **Authors**: ['Kanhu Charan Andia', 'Harsh Prashant Katole', 'Parth Nitinkumar Bhosale', 'Hemant Kumar', 'Dhruv Pratap Singh']
- **Abstrat**: Artificial microbots capable of precise and autonomous navigation could provide versatile platforms for microscale transport, manipulation, and task execution in complex environments. However, reliable navigation becomes increasingly challenging at the micrometre scale, particularly when the surrounding environment changes dynamically. Here, we develop a vision-guided closed-loop navigation framework for light-driven cube-shaped hematite/TiO2 Janus microbots with a characteristic size of approximately 3 μm. Real-time visual localization using a custom-trained YOLOv11 object-detection model is integrated with discretized waypoint-based magnetic steering to enable precise navigation along complex prescribed trajectories, with more than 90% of the measured trajectory deviations remaining below 6 μm across the tested path geometries. The framework further enables arbitrary user-defined free-hand trajectories and autonomous navigation through a physically confined maze. In dynamically changing environments, A* path planning enables real-time path replanning while avoiding multiple independently swimming 5 μm spherical Janus micromotors. Finally, the microbot selectively captures multiple cargo particles and autonomously transports and delivers them to a predefined location while simultaneously avoiding dynamic obstacles. These results establish an integrated platform combining programmable trajectory control, autonomous path planning, real-time environmental adaptation, and task execution within a single light-driven Janus microbot system.





## RoPE-Flow: Monocular Vision-based Robot Pose Estimation via 2D Graph-Conditioned 3D Flow Matching
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21086v1
- **Authors**: ['Liangjing Shao', 'Wanhao Liu', 'Zhiwei Fang', 'Rulin Zhou', 'Quanlu Zhang', 'Changjing Liu', 'Beilei Cui', 'Yiming Huang', 'Hongliang Ren']
- **Abstrat**: Camera-to-robot pose estimation from a monocular image is significant for vision-based robot navigation and control. However, recovering 3D structural features from 2D observations remains a challenging task. Existing camera-to-robot pose estimation methods either rely on time-consuming iterative rendering or involve sophisticated 2D-3D registration which suffers from projection ambiguity, accumulated errors for distal joints and imbalanced learning for different joints. In this work, we propose RoPE-Flow, an end-to-end flow matching-based framework for camera-to-robot pose estimation. From the predicted 2D heatmaps, we propose a Keypoint-centered Feature Extractor to obtain positional features along with contextual representations from keypoints-centered multi-scale patches. Moreover, we introduce Graph-guided Condition Generator, which embeds a learnable adjacency map to generate condition for flow matching based on correlation among keypoints. Besides, we introduce Bone Length Loss and Ray-Depth Loss for topological consistency and scale stability, with joint-wise weighting to mitigate varied prediction difficulty across joints. The proposed method outperforms state-of-the-art methods for pose estimation across different types of robots with mutiple cameras. Besides, we establish a novel cross-scene benchmark to demonstrate the outstanding performance of RoPE-Flow on zero-shot cross-scene robot pose estimation. Meanwhile, animated visualizations are shown on the website: ropeflow.netlify.app

## Praxist: From Experimental Artifacts to Solution Lineages
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25955v2
- **Authors**: ['Jin Li', 'Ahmed Murtadha', 'Zhiyu Wang', 'Qiwen Chen', 'William Chen', 'Yifei Wu', 'Guan Wang', 'Cheng Gong', 'Andy L. Siy', 'Jiayi Yang', 'Mengsha Huang', 'Wenhao Li', 'Yixuan Liu', 'Shuailin Pan', 'Mingli Yuan', 'Sen Song', 'Yuhao Sun']
- **Abstrat**: Autonomous R&D agents now write, run, and improve executable artifacts under automated evaluation-but largely as laboratory instruments: shown on curated benchmarks, with gains that are hard to trace to a cause and costs well above what sustained engineering practice absorbs. The limitation is structural. Most systems treat each attempt as nearly self-contained, so logs, memories, and search trees record what happened without establishing which design element produced an improvement, whether its evidence survived validation, or how it recombines with others. Long campaigns therefore keep re-learning the same lessons. We introduce Praxist, a lineage-centered generational system that converts reproducible artifacts and evaluator outcomes into a typed evidence graph of findings, lane-structured frontiers, and agendas. Separating local artifact construction from cohort-level evidence synthesis lets later attempts inherit validated mechanisms, unresolved claims, and useful constraints, and leaves results attached to an inspectable lineage. On the standardized 75-task MLE-bench suite, the finalized official-grader results give Praxist 60 medals (80.0%), 49 of them gold, against 55 medals (73.3%) and 34 gold for a Claude Code baseline on Claude Opus 4.8-at a recorded model spend of $3054 versus $38370, roughly a twelfth of the cost. Five case studies-quantitative trading, LiDAR-inertial-visual SLAM, tokamak magnetic control, rocket landing, and job-shop rescheduling-carry the same process into open-ended engineering problems, improving on each task-native baseline in headline accuracy, survival, or resource cost, with the discovery path on record. Stronger artifacts at an order of magnitude less spend, each backed by an auditable lineage, are, to our knowledge, first brought together here: the operating profile production research requires, not the one a benchmark demonstration establishes.





## Line-Augmented cuVSLAM: Robust Visual Odometry in Low-Texture Environments
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20958v1
- **Authors**: ['Dara Molloy', 'Terry Wu', 'Wonju Lee', 'Abhilash Nand Kumar', 'David Adley', 'Conor Ferguson', 'Hesam Rabeti']
- **Abstrat**: Autonomous mobile robots in warehouses and production facilities operate in precisely the environments that defeat point-feature visual odometry: featureless aisles, uniform walls, repetitive racking and specular floors. Such environments are, however, rich in straight-edge structure: rack uprights, door frames, wall junctions and cabinet outlines present high-contrast linear edges stable across wide viewpoint changes. A line-feature extension to cuVSLAM, a CUDA-accelerated visual-SLAM system, is presented that recovers this signal. Lines are parameterised as Plücker coordinates for geometric transport and as the minimal 4-DOF orthonormal representation of Bartoli and Sturm for optimisation; a Cauchy-robust point-to-line residual with analytic Jacobians is folded directly into cuVSLAM's per-frame Gauss-Newton pose solve, and the same line state is jointly refined in the Schur-complement sliding-window bundle adjustment. An adaptive information weight causes lines to assume more of the estimation burden exactly as point features become scarce, and the extension adds residual blocks to existing solvers rather than introducing a second estimator, reducing exactly to the unmodified point pipeline when disabled. Evaluated in RGB-D mode under SE(3) Umeyama alignment with no scale correction, line features reduce mean absolute trajectory error by 38.5% over the 22 sequences of the TUM RGB-D Freiburg3 collection and by 30.1% over the 8 Replica room and office sequences, where median translation error falls by 38.9% and median rotation error by 66.4%, with zero tracking failures in any configuration tested.

## LIN-SLAM: Lightweight LiDAR--Inertial Neural SLAM for Real-Time Onboard Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21199v1
- **Authors**: ['Shiqi Che', 'Yijie Liu', 'Shuo Wang', 'Wenxin Liu']
- **Abstrat**: Geometric light detection and ranging (LiDAR)--inertial simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) provides efficient state estimation but lacks the continuous map representation and global refinement capabilities of neural implicit SLAM, while neural implicit methods remain computationally expensive. We propose Lightweight LiDAR--Inertial Neural SLAM (LIN-SLAM), a hybrid method that couples high-rate geometric LiDAR--inertial estimation with neural implicit tracking, continual mapping, and global refinement for accurate real-time onboard SLAM. Experiments on twelve representative sequences from three datasets spanning 32-, 64-, and 128-channel LiDAR configurations show that LIN-SLAM achieves the lowest overall mean absolute trajectory error (ATE) among the evaluated state-of-the-art geometric and neural implicit methods, while substantially reducing runtime compared with existing neural implicit SLAM methods. On a Unitree Go2 equipped with an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX and a 16-channel LiDAR, LIN-SLAM sustains real-time localization and neural mapping over a 1.47-km indoor trajectory through challenging repetitive corridor environments, with an endpoint closure error of only 0.141~m. To the best of our knowledge, LIN-SLAM is the first LiDAR--inertial SLAM system with a neural implicit backend to achieve real-time online operation on a mobile robotic platform while maintaining state-of-the-art localization accuracy. The code will be released upon acceptance.

## Context Matters! Social, Spatial, and Semantic Coordination with an Embodied Agent in Immersive Analytics
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21484v1
- **Authors**: ['Andrew Cunningham', 'Nicolas Barbotin', 'Adam Drogemuller', 'Jack Faser']
- **Abstrat**: Embodied AI agents in Augmented Reality (AR) could enable more natural collaboration in immersive analytics by engaging users through spatial, conversational, and visual interaction.
