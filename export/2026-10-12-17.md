## Make It Flow: Animating Water in 3D Gaussian Splatting Scenes
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13860v1
- **Authors**: ['Kjell Keune', 'Florian Hahlbohm', 'Jannis Möller', 'Susana Castillo', 'Martin Eisemann', 'Marcus Magnor']
- **Abstrat**: Explicit 3D scene representations such as 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) have recently enabled high-quality, real-time novel view synthesis. However, these methods assume static scenes, limiting realism in the presence of dynamic phenomena ubiquitous in real-world environments. While coherent motion (e.g., humans or vehicles) can be captured and reconstructed, high-frequency effects such as flowing water remain intractable under casual capture conditions, where dense multi-view, high-speed acquisition is not feasible. We present a method to animate water in static 3DGS scenes, enabling visually plausible motion without requiring dynamic capture or physical simulation. Our approach estimates 2D optical flow from reference images or model renderings and lifts it into a consistent 3D Eulerian motion field. We propose a cinemagraph-inspired algorithm that applies this motion field to deform Gaussian primitives, producing temporally seamless looping animations. The resulting representation retains real-time rendering performance, significantly enhances perceptual realism, and preserves free-viewpoint navigation within the scene. We evaluate our method on a newly captured dataset of in-the-wild waterfall scenes and demonstrate clear improvements over prior work in dynamic Gaussian splatting and 3D cinemagraph generation in visual quality and computational efficiency.





## HorizonNav: Geometry-Guided Video Diffusion for Anticipatory 3D Robot Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13849v1
- **Authors**: ['Hajira Amjad', 'Valerii Serpiva', 'Viacheslav Liubimov', 'Didar Seyidov', 'Dzmitry Tsetserukou']
- **Abstrat**: We present HorizonNav, a video diffusion-based framework for anticipatory robot navigation that transforms a single RGB image and a text prompt into a geometrically aligned representation of plausible surrounding 3D space. Given the initial observation and a textual description of the reconstruction objective, a vision-language model, guided by designed instructions, interprets the scene and constructs structured prompts for a video diffusion model. The resulting video sequence provides plausible future observations of the environment, which are subsequently processed using monocular ORB-SLAM3 for camera pose estimation and Video Depth Anything for dense depth prediction. Our central contribution is a geometry-guided depth alignment stage that combines the estimated camera poses with triangulated visual landmarks to constrain dense depth predictions across multiple views. This alignment improves the geometric consistency of the reconstructed 3D environment, addressing inconsistencies that temporal coherence of the generated video alone cannot resolve. The experimental evaluation of navigation in imagined 3D environment shows that across a 19-scene horizon study, planning-feasible scenes increase from 13/19 at P20 to 15/19 at P81, while successful RRT trials increase from 62/90 to 75/90. In a separate corridor study, 6 of 25 evaluable generated-camera trajectories violate the reconstructed collision constraints; RRT planner recovers feasible alternatives in 5 of those 6 cases. A larger archive contains 58 usable reconstructions from 67 attempted cases, of which 36 support a downstream plan and 22 do not, exposing reconstruction and free-space failure modes rather than hiding them without lidar. Across 15 corridor reconstructions, the mean reconstruction error is approximately 0.30 m at matched structural landmarks.





## GuidedNav: Shaping Vision-Language Navigation Representations through Pre-action Attention and Spatial Guidance
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13122v1
- **Authors**: ['Jingyu Guo', 'Jiaxin Huang', 'Chengxing Lin', 'Xiaoyu Luo', 'Cheng Wen', 'Shanshan Ye', 'Tingjin Chu', 'Shaoli Huang', 'Mingming Gong']
- **Abstrat**: Vision-and-language navigation (VLN) requires an agent to track progress through an extended instruction, identify landmarks relevant to its current objective, and understand the surrounding spatial structure to act. End-to-end navigation policies typically rely on action supervision to implicitly learn these capabilities. Without explicit guidance on the evidence underlying each decision, however, policies can overfit to correlations predictive of training actions rather than learn the task-relevant grounding and geometry needed for generalization. Our insight is to guide the representations that connect instructions to actions: identifying what remains to be done determines which visual evidence matters, while understanding the geometry around that evidence informs how to move. We introduce GuidedNav, a unified framework that explicitly shapes these representations through training-time guidance. Subtask and landmark guidance steer native pre-action attention toward the active instruction segment and relevant visual regions, grounding action prediction in the current objective. Spatial guidance aligns the policy's internal representations with geometric features that capture long-horizon context and local spatial structure. Together, these objectives strengthen navigation capabilities while avoiding the inference overhead of intermediate textual reasoning and online geometric encoding. We also introduce Guided-R2R and Guided-RxR, two annotation-enriched navigation datasets that support more interpretable VLN research. GuidedNav establishes state-of-the-art performance among the compared monocular RGB policies, improving success rates by 8.1% and 13.1% relative to the strongest prior results on R2R-CE and RxR-CE val-unseen, respectively, while achieving faster inference. Extensive experiments further demonstrate the effectiveness and real-world transferability of our approach.





## Multimodal Image-based Robotic Assistance and Guidance Environment (MIRAGE): A Novel Unified Framework for Surgical Navigation and Visualization
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12780v1
- **Authors**: ['Ehsan Nasiri', 'Yi Zheng', 'Michael A. Kokko', 'Xiaoyao Fan', 'William R. Warner', 'Keith D. Paulsen', 'Ryan J. Halter']
- **Abstrat**: This paper presents the computational architecture and quantitative preclinical evaluation of MIRAGE, a multimodal image-guided robotic surgery framework for real-time visualization and navigation. It integrates stereovision scene reconstruction, augmented-reality (AR) video overlay, quantified heads-up display (HUD) navigation, and intraoperative ultrasound within the da Vinci console's TilePro display (Intuitive Surgical). Results support MIRAGE as a preclinical technical-validation platform for accurate, responsive, and extensible multimodal surgical guidance.





## Visual-Wi-Fi Navigation to Signal-Emitting Devices
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12733v1
- **Authors**: ['Lihao Wang', 'Tianjian Xu', 'Tianmin Shu', 'Renjie Zhao']
- **Abstrat**: Indoor navigation to task-relevant targets remains difficult when the target is small, occluded, or visually indistinct. Meanwhile, smart appliances, mobile devices, and IoT tags emit Wi-Fi signals during everyday communication, providing a pervasive but underused spatial cue. We study visual-Wi-Fi navigation, where a robot reaches a signal-emitting device in an unmapped environment using onboard visual observations and Wi-Fi packets overheard from the device, without connecting to the device or modifying infrastructure. Realizing this requires pose-dependent Wi-Fi observations during closed-loop policy learning and reliable spatial cues from bearing measurements corrupted by indoor multipath propagation on a moving robot. We present WiFiSpaces, which extends the Habitat embodied simulator with online ray-traced Wi-Fi sensing, rendering Wi-Fi observations for a robot-mounted antenna array at continuous poses during policy rollouts. On top of it, we build ViFiNav, a localization-aware navigation framework: an online source-localization algorithm exploits robot motion to disambiguate and robustly aggregate bearings from multiple viewpoints into a confidence-aware source estimate, and a learned policy fuses this evolving estimate with depth observations and learns to act on it as it is refined. Experiments show that exploiting robot motion is key to accurate source estimates, and that fine-tuning on ray-traced Wi-Fi observations in WiFiSpaces improves the success rate of ViFiNav. The policy transfers zero-shot to unseen scene datasets, and we deploy it on a real robot with a Wi-Fi sensor built from a commodity router, without any real-world training.

## Reference-Free Food Volume Estimation from Egocentric Stereo Video
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12735v1
- **Authors**: ['Vivan Kumar', 'Gautham Vinod', 'Fengqing Zhu']
- **Abstrat**: Current stereo vision-based approaches for food portion estimation are hindered by the user burden of using a mobile phone to capture multiple images or placing a reference object in the scene for scale calibration. As a step towards passive dietary intake monitoring, we introduce a method that uses stereo video from a wearable egocentric device to reconstruct food items in true physical scale to accurately estimate volume. By utilizing FoundationStereo for zero-shot stereo matching, SAM 2 for food segmentation, and TSDF fusion over a set of keyframes, our method yields a visually and metrically accurate 3D mesh of any plated food item from a single stereo recording. We leverage the unique offerings of wearables, such as a factory-calibrated stereo baseline and camera poses estimated on-device via visual-inertial odometry (VIO), to perform fully automatic reconstruction without a scale reference object in the scene. We show strong initial volume estimation results across five recorded food items, achieving a mean absolute percentage error of 9.1%.

## When Faster Decisions Do Not Make Better Agents: An Empirical Study of Jev in Agent Workflows
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13092v1
- **Authors**: ['Yuchen Ling', 'Shengcheng Yu', 'Zhenyu Chen', 'Chunrong Fang']
- **Abstrat**: AI agents depend on repeated decisions to accomplish tasks, with large language models (LLMs) commonly serving as their core decision makers. The recent emergence of Jev offers an opportunity to make these decisions faster and at lower cost. However, it remains unclear whether such local savings can be translated into efficient and reliable task completion. In this paper, we investigate this efficiency transfer across five agents in two representative application settings, embodied tasks in ALFWorld and mobile app use in AndroidWorld. Across 4,500 task executions, we jointly evaluate task effectiveness, decision-level and task-level resource use, and execution behavior. Experimental results show that Jev integration substantially reduces decision cost and latency across all five agents, with task-level gains varying by workflow. Task success remains close to baseline levels on ALFWorld and declines more substantially in two mobile agents. Auxiliary reasoning and additional interactions absorb decision savings, while failed recovery and premature completion limit successful delivery. Mean cost per successful outcome increases in one agent even as spending per attempt falls. These findings highlight the importance of preserving progress, supporting recovery, and verifying completion when integrating efficient decision models into agent workflows. Their practical value should be assessed through successful task delivery, recovery behavior, and resources per delivered outcome.

## Mind the Spatial Gap: A Benchmark for Geospatial Reasoning and Constrained Spatial Editing
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12881v1
- **Authors**: ['Zheng Ren', 'Yanning Wang', 'Ziheng Huang', 'Fei Huang', 'Shiqi Wang', 'Ziqian Ye', 'Yang Yue']
- **Abstrat**: Understanding and manipulating spatial relations is fundamental to AI systems that reason about physical environments and construct grounded world models. However, existing evaluations of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) for spatial reasoning rely predominantly on static question-answering (QA) over small-scale or synthetic scenes, rarely probing whether models can modify spatial configurations while satisfying explicit geometric constraints. We introduce GeoSpatialBench, a benchmark designed to evaluate an escalating progression of spatial cognition, ranging from spatial relation understanding and geometric reasoning to constrained spatial editing. Grounded in real-world urban data across six geographically diverse cities, GeoSpatialBench evaluates a comprehensive taxonomy of 28 topological, directional, metric, and ordering relations across structured GeoJSON and map image modalities, with ground-truth references and constraints verified via GIS operations. Our empirical evaluation across frontier MLLMs uncovers a stark spatial gap: structured GeoJSON consistently outperforms map images, with multimodal combinations offering no reliable gain over GeoJSON alone, and while models show reasonable competence on qualitative topological and ordering, performance drops on quantitative metric relations. Crucially, editing performance on ordering relations declines relative to QA, and most failed edits violate at least one specified spatial constraint. These results highlight a gap between passively recognizing spatial relations and actively manipulating geometric configurations. GeoSpatialBench provides an open, rigorous diagnostic testbed to benchmark these spatial limitations, laying essential empirical groundwork for physically grounded world models and embodied spatial AI.

## BT-Agent: Deploying Onboard Robot Agents with Behavior Trees and Multi-Fidelity Evaluation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13862v1

- **Abstrat**: Deploying language-model agents onboard mobile robots requires task reasoning and responsive execution within limited compute and memory. Ablations identify structured tool calls and informative failure feedback as the most useful components.

## CLIDD: Cross-Layer Independent Deformable Description for Efficient and Discriminative Local Feature Representation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09230v2
- **Authors**: ['Haodi Yao', 'Fenghua He', 'Ning Hao', 'Yao Su']
- **Abstrat**: Robust local feature representations are essential for spatial intelligence tasks such as robot navigation and augmented reality. Establishing reliable correspondences requires descriptors that provide both high discriminative power and computational efficiency. To address this, we introduce Cross-Layer Independent Deformable Description (CLIDD), a method that achieves superior discriminability by sampling directly from independent feature hierarchies. This approach utilizes learnable offsets to capture fine-grained structural details across scales, without ever constructing a unified dense feature map, eliminating the memory and computation overhead of building a full-scale representation for a sparse sampling process. To ensure real-time performance, we implement a hardware-aware kernel fusion strategy that maximizes inference throughput. Furthermore, we develop a scalable framework that integrates lightweight architectures with a training protocol leveraging both metric learning and knowledge distillation. This scheme generates a wide spectrum of model variants optimized for diverse deployment constraints. Extensive evaluations demonstrate that our approach achieves superior matching accuracy and exceptional computational efficiency simultaneously. Furthermore, our high-performance configuration outperforms a broad range of current state-of-the-art methods while exceeding 200 FPS on edge devices. These results demonstrate that CLIDD delivers high-precision local feature matching with minimal computational overhead, providing a robust and scalable solution for real-time spatial intelligence tasks.

## CeDR-Nav: Cross-Embodiment Mapless Navigation via Offline Demonstration Recasting
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13007v1
- **Authors**: ['Xianghui Wang', 'Xinming Zhang', 'Yiwei Qian', 'Siwei Cheng', 'Shanze Wang', 'Dan Zhang', 'Wei Zhang']
- **Abstrat**: Learning-based methods have demonstrated strong performance in mapless robot navigation. However, a learned policy remains restricted to the embodiment on which it was trained. Many cross-embodiment navigation methods require time-consuming online training and can still underperform target-specific policies. In fact, demonstrations collected on a source embodiment already contain navigation experience that is potentially reusable for deriving a policy for each target embodiment, but the embodiment mismatch prevents these demonstrations from directly serving as valid supervision for the target. In this article, we present CeDR-Nav, a cross-embodiment demonstration recasting framework that trains a dedicated navigation policy for each target embodiment from existing source demonstrations without online data collection. Geometry-consistent scaling with per-demonstration validity assessment converts the source demonstrations into target training data, and a target-specific Gaussian mixture policy generates feasible avoidance maneuvers for each target embodiment. Extensive quantitative simulations and real-world deployments across multiple robot platforms demonstrate the effectiveness of CeDR-Nav, with an average success-rate gain of 13.8 percentage points over DRL-DCLP in cross-embodiment simulations and target-specific policy training completed within seconds.
