## Illumination-Robust Localization around Unknown Space Objects using Multispectral Image Fusion
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12203v3
- **Authors**: ['Eric J. Elias', 'Michael Esswein', 'Jonathan P. How', 'David W. Miller']
- **Abstrat**: As on-orbit operations grow, so does the need for precise navigation around unknown resident space objects (RSOs), such as spacecraft, debris, and asteroids. Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) algorithms are often studied for mapping an RSO and estimating the inspector's relative pose, but conventional cameras struggle during eclipse, while lidar, though illumination-robust, has higher SWaP. Thermal-infrared cameras track targets through difficult lighting but lack the resolution and feature-richness of visible cameras. Images of a target satellite in low Earth orbit are simulated in both visible and thermal bands, and fused at the pixel level to combine each sensor's strengths for front-end tracking. Thermal imagery is further exploited by a novel silhouette-based visual place recognition (VPR) method that detects loop closures without training, runs entirely on CPU, and outperforms all baselines. Integrated SLAM performance is compared using visible, thermal-infrared, and fused imagery across lighting conditions, trajectories, and place recognition methods; using fused imagery for front-end tracking and our thermal-silhouette place recognition method performs best. The results show that visible and thermal modalities each provide unique information that is exploited differently at various stages of navigation. These low-SWaP sensors could ultimately enable reliable proximity operations on smaller, lower-cost spacecraft.





## LoC-IN: Real-Time Localization of Cameras in Neural Rendered Prior LiDAR Maps
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16349v2
- **Authors**: ['Joni Vanherck', 'Steven Moonen', 'Brent Zoomers', 'Kobe Werner', 'Jeroen Put', 'Lode Jorissen', 'Nick Michiels']
- **Abstrat**: Accurate camera localization is crucial for robotics and Extended Reality (XR), enabling reliable navigation and alignment of virtual and real content. Existing visual methods often suffer from drift, scale ambiguity, and depend on fiducials or loop closure. This work introduces a real-time method for localizing a camera within a pre-captured, highly accurate colored LiDAR point cloud. By rendering synthetic views from this cloud, 2D-3D correspondences are established between live frames and the point cloud. A neural rendering technique narrows the domain gap between synthetic and real images, reducing occlusion and background artifacts to improve feature matching. The result is drift-free camera tracking with correct metric scale in the global LiDAR coordinate system. Two real-time variants are presented: Online Render and Match (R&M), and Prebuild and Localize (P&L). We demonstrate improved results on the ScanNet++ dataset and outperform existing SLAM pipelines.





## PRIME: Perception Feedback with Situational Memory Embeddings in VLA Models
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22040v2
- **Authors**: ['Erik Deinzer', 'Naya Baslan', 'Luca Paparusso', 'Narunas Vaskevicius', 'Peter Knott', 'Luigi Palmieri']
- **Abstrat**: Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving operate primarily through feedforward inference across the perception--reasoning--planning hierarchy. While modern architectures maintain temporal recurrence within the perceptual module, early perception remains blind to downstream reasoning and navigation goals, processing visual inputs agnostically without prioritizing cues informed by prior decisions. To bridge this gap, this paper introduces PRIME, a learned feedback mechanism that conditions the VLA perceptual queries on a novel Situational Memory. By aggregating latent representations of past perception, reasoning, navigation goals, and predicted behaviors across an L-step window via cross-attention, PRIME enables intent-driven perceptual attention at minimal computational cost, adding only a maximum of 29.7M parameters (0.41% of the 7.3B-parameter base model). Evaluated on the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, PRIME achieves a state-of-the-art Driving Score of 82.47 (+4.73 over ORION) and a Success Rate of 60.00% (+5.38 percentage points), the highest reported Driving Score among published VLAs trained on Think2Drive demonstrations.





## UNITE: Consolidating Embodied VLM Specialists via Shared-Anchor One-Bit Factorization
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20767v1
- **Authors**: ['Tao Tao', 'Anke Tang', 'Enneng Yang', 'Zhi Hou', 'Xiaogang Wang', 'Dacheng Tao']
- **Abstrat**: Embodied capabilities such as visual grounding, spatial reasoning, navigation, and driving understanding are often developed in separate vision-language specialists fine-tuned from a common pretrained model.Deploying the specialists separately multiplies storage, whereas merging them into one dense model forces conflicting task updates to share parameters and loses part of each specialist's expertise.We introduce UNITE, a framework that consolidates such specialists into a compact shared-backbone mixture of experts. UNITE keeps the feed-forward block of each specialist as a routable expert and represents the expert bank through a shared-anchor one-bit factorization: a shared floating-point anchor plus expert-specific, row-scaled binary deviations.The anchor is optimized jointly with the scales and signs through closed-form alternating updates, using only source weights and no calibration data.Router adaptation then adjusts token-level expert selection with the backbone and experts frozen, updating fewer than $0.6$M parameters ($<0.01\%$ of the $8.8$B active parameters). Built from four Qwen3-VL-8B specialists, the complete UNITE model occupies $20.3$ GB, $1.16\times$ the storage of one dense specialist and $59.6\%$ below the uncompressed MoE. Across $22$ benchmarks and R2R Val-Unseen, UNITE matches six static merging methods in mean retention of best-specialist performance, leads all of them on Scan2Cap, ScanQA, and all four navigation metrics, and surpasses every source specialist on all five 3D scene understanding benchmarks (up to $+19.1$ on Scan2Cap) and on R2R navigation. Relative to the uncompressed MoE, factorization is nearly lossless on $21$ of $22$ benchmarks; the exception is VSI-Bench, where frame-count experiments suggest impaired use of cross-frame evidence.UNITE thus consolidates complementary embodied capabilities near single-model storage.





## Vision-Language Grounded Semantic Navigation for Autonomous Mobile Robots
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20558v1
- **Authors**: ['Ravindran Vasoor Saravanan', 'Chittin Varunav Ravi', 'Madhumita Singh', 'Thirulok Sundar Mohan Rasu']
- **Abstrat**: Language-guided navigation enables mobile robots to interpret natural language commands and navigate toward user-specified targets in unstructured environments. However, integrating open-vocabulary visual perception with classical robotics pipelines for mapping and navigation remains a challenging problem. ## EVIDENT: Evaluating Evidence Grounding in GUI Agents
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20488v1
- **Authors**: ['Yerin Nam', 'Minkyung Cho', 'MinKyu Kim', 'SeungWoo Song', 'Hangyeol Yoo', 'Jihyo Kim', 'KyungTae Lim']
- **Abstrat**: GUI agents are increasingly evaluated by whether they complete web tasks, but surface success can hide a critical failure: the final answer may not be supported by evidence observed during navigation. We introduce EVIDENT, an evidence-driven benchmark for evaluating whether GUI agents ground their decisions in observable web evidence. EVIDENT contains 220 real-world English and Korean tasks spanning semantic grounding, contextual inference, and visual grounding, each paired with human-curated evidence metadata specifying what must be verified. We further separate surface task completion from evidence-grounded success using three metrics: TSR, ESR, and GSR, which respectively measure outcome success, required evidence observation, and claim-level evidence support. Experiments on commercial and open-source GUI agents reveal substantial gaps between surface success and evidence-grounded success, especially in contextual and visual tasks. These results show that current evaluations can overestimate agent reliability and highlight the need to assess not only whether agents answer, but whether they verify.





## Navi-Agent: Unlocalized Monocular Navigation Agent
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20388v3
- **Authors**: ['Wenyuan Xie', 'Mengyang Hong', 'Yongzhong Wang', 'Yanbiao Ji', 'Yijin Zhou', 'Shaokai Wu', 'Shalayiding Sirejiding', 'Huayi Zhou', 'Yi-Chao Chen', 'Ma Ling', 'Yue Ding', 'Hongtao Lu']
- **Abstrat**: Vision-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE) requires an embodied agent to execute long-horizon instructions in unknown environments. Existing zero-shot VLN-CE systems typically maintain spatial states through geometric localization or coordinate-based representations. Recent geometry-constrained navigation removes depth and globally consistent coordinates, but maintaining persistent spatial awareness for place confirmation, progress verification, and recovery remains challenging. We present Navi-Agent, a zero-shot VLN-CE agent that constructs a coordinate-free spatial state from visual observations and executed motion histories. Navi-Agent organizes this state as a navigation topology, where nodes represent visual places and edges represent motion transitions. This representation enables observation-based approximate self-localization, task progress verification, and visual revisitation-based recovery. Navi-Agent performs closed-loop navigation by decomposing instructions into sub-goals, executing local visual navigation, and verifying visited places through the constructed spatial state. Experiments on zero-shot VLN-CE benchmark and real-world robot platforms show that Navi-Agent achieves state-of-the-art performance among geometry-constrained methods while remaining competitive with approaches relying on geometric localization.





## Toward Planning Representations That Separate Task Structure from Visual Content
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19621v1
- **Authors**: ['Aarav Srivastava', 'Vishwas Sathish', 'Rajesh P. N. Rao']
- **Abstrat**: Effective planning requires representations that preserve task-relevant geometry and object identity while ignoring visual variation that does not alter the task. Existing evaluations of visual generalization often change several factors simultaneously, making the source of a failure difficult to identify. We introduce a controlled evaluation of object-goal navigation (ObjectNav), the task of reaching an instance of a named object category, in procedurally generated ProcTHOR houses. Training and evaluation houses share floor plans, object locations, goals, and start states, while visual factors are changed individually or in combination. Across model-free reinforcement learning (RL), model-based RL, and agents using frozen pretrained visual representations, our results show that appearance changes impair navigation, but their effects differ sharply by factor. Surface material changes account for most of the average change in success, whereas clutter and lighting have little effect. The magnitude of a global image statistics shift does not reliably predict performance: changing only the goal object's appearance can substantially impair an end-to-end agent despite a small image-level change. We found that a model based agent that plans over frozen features from the pretrained DINOv2 image encoder achieves the highest success (0.83) under the full shift, but remains sensitive to surface appearance. Our experiments reveal an important requirement for planning representations: they must separate invariant spatial task structure from appearance.





## Breaking Déjà Vu: Independent Auditing of Visual Place Recognition through Vision-Language Reasoning
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12818v3
- **Authors**: ['Sania Waheed', 'Michael Milford', 'Sarvapali D. Ramchurn', 'Shoaib Ehsan']
- **Abstrat**: Visual place recognition (VPR) is a key enabler of accurate localization and long-term autonomous navigation in robotics applications, such as loop closure detection for simultaneous localisation and mapping (SLAM). However, real-world VPR deployment relies on selecting an image matching threshold that balances precision and recall. These thresholds are typically tuned using labeled validation data and fixed during deployment, making them unreliable under environmental changes where ground truth is unavailable. This is particularly problematic in safety-critical robotics, where accepting a false loop closure can corrupt the estimated trajectory and map. In this work, we introduce Visual Place Recognition Auditing, an independent post-retrieval verification framework that leverages Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to assess retrieved matches by reasoning jointly over query and candidate images. Unlike conventional verification methods, our approach performs instance-level verification without requiring architecture-specific confidence measures, dataset-dependent thresholds, or prior knowledge of the deployment environment. We evaluate our method on six benchmark datasets using five state-of-the-art VPR methods and four VLMs. Results show that VLM-based auditing improves recall@1 by 13.6% on average as compared to state-of-the-art methods while reducing false acceptance rates to 12%, maintaining precision above 95% and coverage above 75%.





## VANDERER: Map-Free Exploration using Future-Aware and Visual-Curiosity-Guided Diffusion Policy
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14879v2
- **Authors**: ['Venkata Naren Devarakonda', 'Raktim Gautam Goswami', 'Prashanth Krishnamurthy', 'Farshad Khorrami']
- **Abstrat**: Mobile agents require efficient exploration strategies to map unseen environments and autonomously plan tasks. Traditional methods rely on generating occupancy maps and optimizing the sequence in which unexplored regions are visited. However, beyond requiring additional sensor suites for reliable pose and depth estimation, occupancy map-based exploration policies often neglect environmental traversability constraints. To address these limitations, we propose VANDERER, an exploration framework that leverages a Visual Curiosity Module (VCM) to guide pre-trained diffusion policies using only monocular image data. This curiosity module predicts the outcomes of proposed actions via a navigation world model and evaluates them through a curiosity cost. The cost then guides the diffusion process toward generating actions that maximize exploration. Evaluated across diverse simulated environments, VANDERER outperforms established baselines, achieving $2.3\times$ the exploration efficiency of Frontier Search. Our results reveal a correlation between visual and geometric curiosity in outdoor environments, demonstrating that VANDERER can effectively leverage this relationship for efficient exploration using simple robotic setups.

## Unreasonable Confidence from Simulator Misspecification: Directional Robustness on the SPEED+ Sim-to-Real Benchmark
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20321v1
- **Authors**: ['Nikolay Dolgov']
- **Abstrat**: Vision-based navigation systems for orbit must be trained almost entirely on synthetic imagery, because real labelled images of a given spacecraft in flight rarely exist. Benchmarks like SPEED+ are built for that situation: the training set comes from a simulator, and the test sets are real images of a satellite mockup under two kinds of lighting. That simulator approximates how light reflects off mylar and aluminium, and like every scientific simulator it is misspecified. Its error is radiometric, since it misses the specular highlights, hard shadows and sensor saturation common in real images. We probe the resulting synthetic-to-real gap, the central risk for such systems, with a deliberately simple monocular pose estimator. In a 2x2 study of backbone adaptation and photometric augmentation, trained on synthetic images and tested on both real hardware-in-the-loop domains, we find three things. (1) The model does not know it is failing: regions conformally calibrated to 90% on synthetic data cover only 9-15% of rotation errors on sunlamp and 21-38% on lightbox, so a wrong pose carries the confidence of a correct one. (2) Transfer is asymmetric: scaling data and features improved synthetic rotation error from 1.65 to 1.03 rad but moved the harshly lit sunlamp domain by only 0.05 rad, under twice its seed s.d., whereas adapting the backbone's last stage carried 63-83% of its extra synthetic gain to both real domains. (3) Once the backbone adapts, augmentation helps only in the direction it models: our photometric stack improved the diffusely lit lightbox domain from 1.17 to 0.99 rad on every seed but moved sunlamp no more than seed-to-seed variation, while with the backbone frozen it helped sunlamp more. Every post-review experiment was pre-registered, and the code, per-image predictions and pre-registration record are at https://github.com/lokindoo/spacecraft-pose-estimation.





## Flying on Imagination: DreamerV3-Style World Models under Environment Distribution Shift for Quadrotor Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05015v2
- **Authors**: ['Luca Zanatta', 'Grzegorz Malczyk', 'Kostas Alexis']
- **Abstrat**: DreamerV3-style reconstructive world models offer a route to vision-based navigation for aerial robots through learned latent dynamics. Yet their behavior under shifts in the training environment and their ability to support navigation using imagined observations, predictions generated by rolling the latent dynamics forward without new sensory input, remain insufficiently characterized. We study four training-environment regimes for depth-based quadrotor navigation, spanning a fixed layout, a finite set of layouts, low-discrepancy continuous obstacle sampling, and i.i.d. uniform sampling. We cross-evaluate reconstruction before and after Reinforcement Learning fine-tuning, separately evaluate the resulting policies in simulation, and transfer them to a real quadrotor without real-world fine-tuning. We demonstrate onboard flight driven by imagined state and depth: a 24.7m mission in which 66% of flight time and 76% of traveled distance occur during repeated 5s sensor-free imagination windows, with prediction quality degrading over each open-loop interval and recovering after sensory re-grounding. Additionally, in closed-loop hardware transfer, all policies succeed in a setting close to the simulated geometry, whereas the policy trained from a single fixed layout fails under a corridor-topology and obstacle-type shift, while the other three succeed.





## Depth-Aware Path Planning and Collision Avoidance for Robotic Nursing Assistants Using Real-Time 3D Vision

- **Authors**: ['Peeyush Thakur', 'Dharmendra Sharma', 'Archit Sharma', 'Sandeep Gupta', 'Anuj Nandanwar', 'Laxmidhar Behera']

Furthermore, real-world experimental results validate the proposed approach as a computationally efficient and safety-aware navigation solution for autonomous healthcare robots.

## Polygraph: Large-Scale Multi-Session Trajectory Optimization using Pose Graphs
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21163v1
- **Authors**: ['Petar Lukovic', 'Tian Yi Lim', 'Marc Pollefeys', 'Pablo Speciale', 'Hermann Blum', 'Zuria Bauer']
- **Abstrat**: Robots, vehicles, and wearable devices often traverse the same environment many times. Each pass produces its own trajectory from onboard visual or visual-inertial odometry. These trajectories are locally accurate but drift over time and live in unrelated coordinate frames. Turning many such independent trajectories into one globally consistent set is the large-scale multi-session trajectory optimization problem. Existing solutions largely work offline. All sessions must be available up front, one session is fixed as the reference frame, and a single fused map is then optimized. This paper presents Polygraph, a multi-session trajectory optimization pipeline that processes sessions online, one keyframe and one spatial cluster at a time, and never builds a 3D map. Keyframes are grouped into local clusters. When a cluster closes, it is matched against every other session through learned image retrieval. Candidate pairs are verified with sparse feature matching, monocular metric depth, and robust pose estimation. A two-tier consensus gate admits a cross-session edge into the shared pose graph only once independent evidence corroborates it. A cheap incremental solve keeps the graph current, and a periodic robust batch solve prunes outliers. We evaluate Polygraph on stereo outdoor driving, HoloLens and iPad indoor traversals, legged indoor locomotion with a Boston Dynamics Spot, and large-scale outdoor vehicle and handheld routes. Against the closest prior work on the same GPU model, using its reported timings, Polygraph runs faster, commits fewer loop-closure edges, and matches or improves on accuracy.

- **Authors**: ['Peeyush Thakur', 'Dharmendra Sharma', 'Archit Sharma', 'Sandeep Gupta', 'Anuj Nandanwar', 'Laxmidhar Behera']
- **Abstrat**: This paper presents a depth-aware path planning and collision avoidance framework for the safe navigation of robotic nursing assistants in cluttered environments with low-height obstacles. Conventional LiDAR-based navigation systems, while accurate for mapping and localization, fail to detect transparent or low-height obstacles, whereas vision-based systems are prone to noise and occlusion. The proposed framework integrates RGB-D depth sensing, rule-based obstacle avoidance, and the NavfnROS global path planner to drive the robot from an initial point to a goal point. The proposed approach is implemented on the Husky A200 robot, equipped with a Kinova Gen3 arm, to form a complete setup for a robotic nursing assistant. This system features a multi-layer architecture for global planning, perception fusion, and real-time control in the ROS-Gazebo environment. Experimental evaluations in a simulated cluttered environment demonstrate up to 40\% higher success rate and 35\% improvement in minimum clearance compared with single-sensor baselines. Furthermore, real-world experimental results validate the proposed approach as a computationally efficient and safety-aware navigation solution for autonomous healthcare robots.

## CoopDistill: Equilibrium-Guided Multi-Agent Policy Distillation for Cooperative Driving
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21069v1
- **Authors**: ['Shiyu Fang', 'Sheng Zhao', 'Jiarui Zhang', 'Chengkai Xu', 'Xiaocong Zhao', 'Peng Hang', 'Jian Sun']
- **Abstrat**: Cooperative driving extends autonomous driving from individual vehicle decisions to joint maneuvers that improve traffic safety and efficiency. Optimization-based methods impose safety constraints but often fix paths to simplify multi-vehicle planning, limiting spatial flexibility. Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) explores alternatives through interaction, but coordinated learning and safe execution remain challenging. Their integration nevertheless faces challenges in exploration efficiency, experience allocation, and execution reliability. To address these challenges, this paper proposes CoopDistill, an equilibrium-guided multi-agent policy distillation framework. A potential-game (PG) formulation provides a fixed-path longitudinal reference, around which a residual MARL policy learns longitudinal acceleration, conflict-approach timing, and lateral path corrections. A hard-case curriculum concentrates experience on interactions that PG leaves unresolved or substantially delayed. A comparative rollout selector evaluates the joint consequences of learned corrections against PG before choosing the executed action. Experiments in merging, roundabout, and intersection scenarios compare CoopDistill with six baselines at four connected automated vehicle penetration levels. Relative to PG, success improves by 2.1--27.1 percentage points and delay decreases by 7.3--27.3\%, with no observed collisions. Code is available at https://github.com/FanGShiYuu/CoopDistill





## Grounding Driving VLA via Inverse Kinematics
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21061v3
- **Authors**: ['Junsung Park', 'Hyunjung Shim']
- **Abstrat**: Counterfactual obstacle insertion exposes blind planning in representative Driving VLAs: their trajectories remain largely unchanged despite a vehicle appearing directly ahead. An empirically calibrated bias-variance analysis identifies how ego-predictable training examples admit a shortcut through ego status and commands. We address this failure with an inverse-kinematics-inspired architecture that anchors trajectory decoding on current and predicted future visual states. First, a next visual state prediction objective that requires the LLM to predict the future visual scene provides dense visual supervision and suppresses shortcut paths. Second, a separate Inverse Kinematics Network (a cross-attention-based conditional diffusion model) that takes only the current and future visual states as input is designed to suppress reliance on ego status and textual shortcuts during trajectory decoding. Our 0.5B model achieves state-of-the-art trajectory planning performance on NAVSIM-v1, NAVSIM-v2, and nuScenes using cameras alone, without LiDAR or a BEV encoder. Against the matched OpenDriveVLA* baseline, it improves NAVSIM PDMS and EPDMS by 19.0 and 20.4 points, respectively. Controlled ablations and intervention analyses support improved use of visual features, with future-state prediction sharpening hazard-specific responses.





## EPI-SST: Renewal-Structured State-Space Transformers for Epidemic Forecasting
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20352v1
- **Authors**: ['Yiyun Chen', 'Zewen Liu', 'Shengpu Tang', 'Max S. Y. Lau', 'Wei Jin']
- **Abstrat**: Epidemic forecasting supports public health decisions ranging from capacity planning to intervention timing, for which both point accuracy and calibrated uncertainty are essential. Although neural models have advanced point forecasting, producing reliable probabilistic predictions over multiple future horizons remains challenging. Renewal models encode epidemic propagation, while neural forecasters learn flexible multi-horizon representations; integrating these strengths within a probabilistic future rollout remains underexplored. We propose EPI-SST (Epidemic State Space Transformer), a probabilistic forecaster centered on an evidence-driven renewal transition. At each horizon, neural evidence produces a bounded update to an internal multiplier and advances a finite-memory renewal state that generates the future mean trajectory. A separate heteroscedastic Gaussian head controls predictive scale using a validation-selected log-variance penalty. Across four epidemic surveillance benchmarks, EPI-SST achieves the lowest pooled RMSE on every dataset, reducing RMSE by up to 14.2 percent relative to the strongest baseline. It also achieves the lowest continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) at horizons h = 4, 5, 6 on every dataset, with reductions of up to 43 percent relative to the strongest probabilistic baseline. During the abnormal 2019-2020 influenza season, EPI-SST reduces CRPS by 16.9 percent and calibration error by 78.9 percent; a three-seed replication preserves its distributional lead. Ablation studies and frozen-path interventions show that the renewal transition, attention conditioning, horizon coupling, and uncertainty-scale control make complementary contributions to predictive performance.





## Automatic Intermodal Loading Unit Identification using Computer Vision: A Scoping Review
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17707v3
- **Authors**: ['Emre Gülsoylu', 'Alhassan Abdelhalim', 'Derya Kara Boztas', 'Ole Grasse', 'Carlos Jahn', 'Simone Frintrop', 'Janick Edinger']
- **Abstrat**: Background: The standardisation of Intermodal Loading Units (ILUs), including containers, semi-trailers, and swap bodies, has transformed global trade, yet efficient and robust identification remains an operational bottleneck in ports and terminals. Objective: To map Computer Vision (CV) methods for ILU identification, clarify terminology, summarise the evolution of proposed approaches, and highlight research gaps, future directions and their potential effects on terminal operations. Methods: Following PRISMA-ScR, we searched Google Scholar, dblp and OpenAlex for English-language studies with quantitative results and charted the included studies across methods, datasets, failure modes, and evaluation metrics. Results: We reviewed 95 studies published between 1990 and 2026. A shift from static (e.g., OCR-gates) to vehicle-mounted camera setups is observed. Only 26 studies report an end-to-end accuracy (median 94.5%, range 19-100%). Eight datasets are publicly available, yet none is commonly used as a benchmark, and six studies describe a deployed system and seven a field test. Conclusions: We propose the term ILU identification for this broader task, which includes finding the unit and its code and covers semi-trailers and swap bodies. The literature still reflects its origin at the gate: challenges typical of moving cameras receive the least attention, and no study reports separate results for semi-trailers or swap bodies. Evaluation requires public benchmarks that cover these conditions and all ILU types, with end-to-end accuracy results. Promising directions are synthetic data, unified end-to-end models, recognisers built for codes without linguistic context, and mission planning with automatic coverage path planning for vehicle-mounted cameras. Addressing these challenges could reduce human errors, turnaround times and operational costs and increase resilience against disruptions.





## When AI Agents Should Repair, Replan, or Abandon: A Threshold Theory of Plan Commitment
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19663v1
- **Authors**: ['Gaurav Tewari']
- **Abstrat**: AI agents often discover mid-execution that their plan is broken, for example when an action fails or a tool is withdrawn. Deployed systems use conventions such as retry loops or "always replan," and a recent benchmark finds no fixed strategy dominates. This paper derives the optimal policy. In the model, a disruption destroys the causal support of part of a plan; repair is a partial reset that preserves the plan's structure, replanning is a full reset, and changing a plan is costly because others have conditioned on it. The optimal policy is a threshold in effective disruption depth: repair shallow damage, replan after deep damage, and abandon the goal when even a fresh plan is not worth its cost. Depth depends on plan structure. In expectation, a single broken action forces 1+R(G)/n actions to be revalidated, where R(G) counts the pairs of actions joined by a dependency path; to first order, a fully serial plan switches to replanning at raw damage about w times smaller than a plan of w parallel chains. A closed-form stability-price threshold determines when a worse repaired plan should be kept over a better fresh one. As language-model planners push replanning costs toward zero, the stability price becomes the only force sustaining plan commitment. Under repeated disruptions, revision frictions make commitment hysteresis optimal: the agent keeps executing a plan it would never choose fresh, and each repair adds patch debt until the debt outweighs repair's cost advantage and the agent rebuilds. A value-of-diagnosis result states when measuring damage is worth its compute and shows that "always replan" is optimal when damage is predictably deep and replanning is the better fallback. A separately implemented numerical checker tests each proposition (zero violations; 1.6 million static-model evaluations), and the model's regime map reproduces that benchmark's regime flips qualitatively.





## Bayesian spatiotemporal modeling of passenger trip assignment in metro networks
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22403v2
- **Authors**: ['Xiaoxu Chen', 'Alexandra M. Schmidt', 'Zhenliang Ma', 'Lijun Sun']
- **Abstrat**: Assigning passenger trips to specific network paths using automatic fare collection (AFC) data is a fundamental application in urban transit analysis. The task is a difficult inverse problem: the only available information consists of each passenger's total travel time and their origin and destination, while individual passenger path choices and dynamic network costs are unobservable, and behavior varies significantly across space and time. We propose a novel Bayesian hierarchical model to address this problem by jointly estimating dynamic network costs and passenger path choices while quantifying their uncertainty. Our model decomposes trip travel time into four components (access, in-vehicle, transfer, and egress), each modeled as a random walk over the day. To capture heterogeneous passenger behavior, we introduce a multinomial logit model with spatiotemporally varying coefficients. We manage the high dimensionality of these coefficients using kernelized tensor factorization with Gaussian process priors to effectively model complex spatiotemporal correlations. We develop a tailored and efficient Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm for model inference. A simulation study demonstrates the method's effectiveness in recovering the underlying model parameters. On a large-scale dataset from the Hong Kong Mass Transit Railway, our framework demonstrates better estimation accuracy over the benchmarks. The results reveal significant spatiotemporal variations in passenger preferences and provide robust uncertainty quantification, offering transit operators a powerful tool for enhancing service planning and operational management.

## KaleidoKV: Rearranging Evidence for Efficient KV Cache Reuse in Offline RAG Inference
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19253v1
- **Authors**: ['Peiting Xie', 'Xingyu Tan', 'Xiangjun Zai', 'Chen Chen', 'Xiaoyang Wang', 'Wenjie Zhang']
- **Abstrat**: Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) augments large language models (LLMs) on question-answering (QA) tasks with retrieved evidence at inference time. Related questions in an offline batch often retrieve overlapping evidence, but prefix caching reuses key-value (KV) states only for identical token prefixes, making reuse dependent on evidence order. Although all questions and evidence are known before execution, existing reordering methods commit to early grouping decisions and can miss sharing opportunities that require several requests to move together. Evaluating alternative layouts can incur planning costs that offset inference savings, while concurrent requests may still recompute shared prefixes before their KV states become available. To address these challenges, we propose KaleidoKV, a framework that plans evidence orders across the batch and controls when requests enter the engine while preserving each request's evidence set and content. Joint layout planning revises formed groups through coordinated request moves and keeps a move only if it lowers planned prefill, the computation required when each shared prefix is computed once. A global prefix index selects evidence orders by locating reusable prefixes and evaluates each move incrementally from only the affected request paths. Prefix-ready admission tracks nested prefix dependencies, delaying requests whose shared prefixes are still being computed while allowing independent requests to proceed. Experiments on four QA workloads and four LLMs show that KaleidoKV achieves the highest throughput and lowest mean time to first token in all 16 settings, with approximately 1.24-1.75x the throughput of the strongest baseline, including planning time. With Llama-3.1-8B under the default configuration, actual prefill stays within 0.5% of planned prefill across all four workloads.





## EdgeNet City: Context-Aware AI-RAN Planning toward Autonomous Urban Connectivity
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19231v1
- **Authors**: ['Ziming Liu', 'Boxuan Shan', 'Qixing Deng', 'Wenchao Xue', 'Xiaoli Chu']
- **Abstrat**: The evolution from 5G toward 6G calls for more anticipatory and context-aware Cell-on-Wheels (COW) planning in wireless networks. We present EdgeNet City, an online framework that grounds free-form natural-language inputs in geographic activity representations and path-gain data. A catalog-referenced representation links activity roles, geographic features and predefined demand profiles, separating semantic selection from deterministic spatial computation. The algorithm combines prescribed demand with relative propagation support to screen service areas for subsequent COW assessment. We publicly release a London dataset with 22,678 transmitters, approximately 449.4 billion path-gain records and retained receiver geometry. Within prepared propagation domains, the online prototype reuses these records without new ray tracing or live operator measurements. In a matched 20-event study across six London environments, the expanded representation increases mean spatial overlap with the reused full technical-review reference from 0.4929 for the single-feature Venue-only baseline to 0.7203. Rule-based and language-model planners select identical activities in this panel. With need-aware ranking and a three-cell diagnostic budget fixed, the expanded representation increases mean Reference-need Capture by 25.44 percentage points over Venue-only.





## WA-JEPA: Goal-Aware Predictive Feedback Action Refinement for a World-Action Model
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19183v1
- **Authors**: ['Tao An', 'Weihang Liang', 'Tianqi Wang', 'Chuqing Cao', 'Wenming Tan', 'Kai Zhang', 'Haibo Lei', 'Tao Xie', 'Lijun Zhao', 'Ruifeng Li']
- **Abstrat**: JEPA-based world models predict action consequences in latent space, but representative planning approaches rely on candidate-action search, requiring repeated evaluation of action sequences and incurring substantial online computational cost. Direct action generation avoids this search, yet how to leverage latent predictions to guide action generation given task goals remains a key challenge in translating learned physical dynamics into effective decisions. We introduce WA-JEPA, a world-action model that couples action generation, action-conditioned latent prediction, and goal-aware feedback. The model first generates an action proposal and predicts the resulting future scenes and proprioceptive states, then refines the proposal through two complementary feedback paths, using the task goal as a reference. Goal-discrepancy residual feedback (GDRF) uses differences between the latent representations of predicted scenes and the task goal to guide chunk-wise action refinement. Future-scene context feedback (FSCF) preserves fine-grained predicted scene representations to guide subsequent action refinement. Jointly learned from offline demonstrations, this process iteratively updates the same plan before execution, making future predictions directly participate in action generation without candidate-action search. We evaluate short-horizon goal reaching, long-horizon tasks, and full-task execution. The results show that goal-aware predictive feedback supports competitive task performance while maintaining a compact model and low online computational cost. Code is available at https://github.com/Dovisya/WA-JEPA.





## Passage-Focused Soft Coordination for Hierarchical Multi-Robot Navigation in Bottleneck Environments
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18877v1
- **Authors**: ['Haoze Dong', 'Stepan Dergachev', 'Meng Guo', 'Konstantin Yakovlev', 'Zhongkui Li']
- **Abstrat**: In multi-robot navigation, rigid global schedules are sensitive to execution deviations, while local methods lack long-horizon coordination. We propose a passage-focused framework combining soft global coordination with flexible local execution. A passage-induced graph of convex sets supports priority-ordered planning with two levels of soft coordination. Passage-aware route selection accounts for passage usage and traversal directions, while route-restricted continuous space-time planning penalizes temporal conflicts. The resulting references guide a model predictive path integral controller that avoids collisions online. Adaptive temporal and geometric tracking accommodates execution deviations, while selective replanning maintains coordination without rigid trajectory reservations. Across 1,200 instances in 4 different environments with 5-30 robots, the framework achieves a near-perfect success rate and outperforms evaluated baselines in makespan and flowtime. Hardware experiments with 8 robots demonstrate successful completion of all trials without collisions. These results highlight the effectiveness of passage-focused coordination in balancing long-horizon cooperation with flexible execution.
