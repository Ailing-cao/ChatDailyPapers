## NereidVLA: Learning to Understand and Navigate the Underwater World
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24864v1
- **Authors**: ['Chenjie Zhao', 'Zhiliang Zhu', 'Quan Chen', 'Yuquan Xue', 'Xingxue Shi', 'Puyue Wang', 'Zhiqiang Zhao', 'Kangye Zheng', 'Boyang Xu', 'Mingwei Lin', 'Dejun Li']
- **Abstrat**: Underwater navigation requires translating visual spatial cues into motion, yet marine vision-language datasets rarely include paired robot trajectories. We present NereidVLA, a vision-language-action model that transfers marine spatial knowledge to language-conditioned 3D navigation. We first train Sea2Know on approximately two million marine examples to learn target grounding, spatial relations, and scene structure without paired robot-motion supervision. We then adapt it using expert trajectories executed under simulated underwater vehicle dynamics. Given RGB histories and a language instruction, NereidVLA predicts discrete motion tokens that decode into local 3D trajectories for closed-loop tracking. Evaluation combines an independent six-dimensional underwater navigation understanding benchmark with offline prediction and closed-loop navigation. Under matched navigation training and execution settings, marine spatial pretraining increases simulation success from 72.4% to 84.4%. Without fine-tuning on the real test environment, NereidVLA achieves 78.75% success in laboratory-pool robot trials, compared with 47.50% for InternVLA-N1. These results show that marine spatial supervision can support downstream motion learning and physical underwater navigation.





## Risk-Triggered Search: Behavior-Preserving Safe Control
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24409v1
- **Authors**: ['Xinyu Deng', 'Bohao Lan', 'Kunpeng Li', 'Mingkai Yuan', 'Weihang Pan', 'Binbin Lin']
- **Abstrat**: Pre-trained visual world models generate multi-step action plans from current and goal observations, yet terminal-proximity objectives fail to prevent forbidden-zone traversal or rigid-body collision sliding. Purely visual interfaces may lack analytical dynamics, structured states, or explicit collision geometry, requiring internal prediction of action-conditioned process risk. We propose Risk-Triggered Search (RTS) for frozen Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) world models. Rather than relying on static state features that cannot distinguish candidate plans sharing an observation, RTS unrolls action-conditioned future latent prefixes in the frozen world model and trains a lightweight risk head with process-violation supervision. To intervene selectively while preserving safe nominal behavior, RTS uses the same predicted prefix-risk criterion as both the search trigger gate and hard feasibility gate: safe nominal plans execute directly, whereas alarms trigger local Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) search to minimize nominal deviation within the feasible set. To prevent isotropic Euclidean minimal modification from shrinking obstacle-directed actions into zero-amplitude stalling, we introduce an amplitude-preserving metric $d_{\mathrm{AP}}$ that retains tangential progression while maintaining a unique zero at the nominal plan. Under unified gating and this unique-zero condition, bypassing safe-state search yields closed-loop trajectories strictly identical to every-step same-kernel search. Evaluated under a process-safety benchmark and Safe Arrival Rate (SAR) metric across five tasks, RTS improves SAR over Direct by 12.67--41.67 percentage points on navigation and articulated control and +2.08/+4.17 pp on Cube/PushT boundary cohorts, maintaining 100% action identity with every-step search while cutting controller time by 17.71%--70.41% across 1,968 paired trajectories.

## Allocentric Memory, Egocentric Acting: A Dual-Frame VLA for Urban Aerial Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24247v1
- **Authors**: ['Haotian Xu', 'Yue Hu', 'Zhengqiu Zhu', 'Tuo Feng', 'Wenguan Wang', 'Quanjun Yin']
- **Abstrat**: Language-goal aerial navigation requires a UAV to reach a destination specified in natural language using observations collected during flight. A key challenge is linking changing egocentric perception with spatial evidence anchored to stable locations for long-range navigation. We propose CityNav-VLA, a dual-frame vision-language-action model for urban navigation integrating dynamic egocentric context with persistent allocentric spatial memory. The UAV incrementally accumulates visual observations on a fixed north-up map initialized with geographic and landmark priors, preserving evidence across viewpoint changes. Cumulative ablations show that navigation-map features yield the largest gains, while metric coordinates further improve endpoint accuracy. With a 4B-scale backbone, inference averages 0.68 s per decision with 8.6 GB peak memory on a single A100, presenting remarkable model efficiency.

## Measuring the Conservatism of Latent Safety Filters for World Models
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23685v1
- **Authors**: ['Johannes Mootz', 'Mani Amani', 'Reza Akhavian']
- **Abstrat**: A latent safety filter decides from the latent of a world model whether a planned action is safe. Conformal calibration sets the threshold of the filter so that it accepts an unsafe action only with a small probability. However, the filter can still be conservative and refuse many safe actions, depending on the latent. To measure the conservatism that a latent forces on the filter, we group similar latents and actions into cells. A filter that gives one decision per cell can refuse the unsafe transitions of a cell only by refusing the safe ones in it as well. We call the share of safe transitions that lie in such cells the overlap. We prove that the overlap is the minimum share of safe transitions that such a filter has to refuse in order to accept no unsafe transition in the data. Both the overlap and the conformal calibration can be applied to any latent. We use this to compare three existing designs, in which the safety labels train a classifier on top of the world model, the world model itself, or a separate encoder beside it. In a visual navigation task, the separate encoder has the lowest overlap, and at the same conformal level its filter refuses the fewest safe actions and lets the planner reach the most goals of the three.

## Proprioceptive Simultaneous Base and Terrain Elevation Estimation for Legged Robot Locomotion
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24310v1
- **Authors**: ['Michael Maravgakis', 'Stylianos Piperakis', 'Panos Trahanias']
- **Abstrat**: Legged robots in visually degraded environments must rely exclusively on proprioceptive sensing, under which global position and yaw are unobservable. Vertical drift is particularly detrimental, as it corrupts the height reference required by swing-leg trajectories and can destabilize the robot. We treat the terrain itself as a persistent proprioceptive landmark: every footstep provides a height measurement that anchors the vertical axis. We propose Simultaneous Base and Elevation Estimation (SBEE), a real-time open-source framework in which a Left-Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (LI-EKF) for floating-base estimation and a grid-based elevation map are coupled in a bidirectional loop, mirroring SLAM with terrain elevation in place of landmarks. Stable foot contacts insert uncertainty-aware height samples into the map, and previously mapped cells are fed back as uncertainty-weighted vertical pseudo-measurements that act analogously to loop closures. Unlike contact-aided invariant filters, the LI-EKF consumes a kinematic velocity measurement instead of augmenting the state with a contact model per leg, so its state dimension is independent of the number of legs. We evaluate SBEE in MuJoCo on quadrupedal and bipedal platforms and on the ANYmal-D GrandTour dataset over 296 m of locomotion, reducing the absolute trajectory error by 22.1% relative to the best of three state-of-the-art proprioceptive estimators.

## Assessing Vision-Language Models for Early Prediction of Unsafe Situations in Robotics
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24146v1
- **Authors**: ['Muhammad Farooq', 'Aitor Arrieta', 'Paolo Arcaini', 'Shaukat Ali', 'Thomas Peyrucain']
- **Abstrat**: Vision-Language Models (VLMs) process visual information and textual instructions and, by using their ability to understand and reason about context, are well-suited for predicting robot failures before they occur. However, evidence supporting this remains limited. To address this, we present an empirical evaluation of VLMs for predicting robot failures. In particular, we experiment with Qwen, InternVL, and LLaVA-OneVision across three robotic case studies (HuRoN, Pal Robotics, and CrowdBot), analyzing both video and odometry data for early failure warning (i.e., before an unsafe situation occurs). Specifically, we evaluate their performance in classifying situations as safe, potentially unsafe, and unsafe, together with uncertainty quantified using various metrics, and study the relationship between uncertainty and performance. Our results showed that Qwen provides the strongest overall classification and prediction-reliability performance, while InternVL shows stronger early-warning capability in several configurations. The results further show that the effect of robot-state information and temporal history depends on the model and evaluation objective. Overall, our findings indicate that general-purpose VLMs can support early robot-safety monitoring, but the most suitable configuration depends on whether the priority is accurate assessment of safety-state, reliable confidence and uncertainty, or timely warning before unsafe events.
