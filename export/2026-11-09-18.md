## Accuracy potential of visual localization exploiting high-end street-level imagery
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24409v2
- **Authors**: ['Jonas Meyer', 'Stephan Nebiker', 'Pascal Theiler', 'Norbert Haala']
- **Abstrat**: Many applications, such as surveying, autonomous navigation, robotics and augmented reality, require accurate pose information with respect to a reference frame. Visual localization can complement GNSS positioning, whose availability and accuracy are often limited. However, the accuracy potential of visual localization has not yet been systematically investigated against survey-grade requirements, and no publicly available large-scale outdoor dataset provides ground-truth poses accurate enough for such an investigation. We address both gaps. First, we present the FHNW Muttenz dataset. It covers a contiguous 10 km street network, mapped in two mobile mapping campaigns about 1.5 years apart, and comprises high-resolution reference imagery as well as query sequences from four cameras in five scenes. The two campaigns are co-registered to each other with an accuracy of 5 mm. Co-registration of the query images to the reference imagery yields ground-truth poses accurate to 6-9 mm in position and well below 0.1° in orientation. All images are georeferenced with an accuracy of about 2.5 cm. Second, we use this dataset to systematically evaluate sparse feature-based visual localization with a modular pipeline built from established components, using the high-resolution reference imagery directly as the scene representation. For daytime imagery and pre-calibrated cameras, the median pose errors with respect to the reference imagery are 1-5 cm in translation and 0.03-0.1° in rotation. Given an accurately georeferenced scene representation, visual localization not only approaches the accuracy of survey-grade GNSS positioning but also provides full orientation information. The dataset is publicly available at https://fhnw-muttenz-vl-dataset.github.io/





## VLD: Visual Learned Distance as an Interface for Reinforcement Learning Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07976v3
- **Authors**: ['Lazar Milikic', 'Manthan Patel', 'Jonas Frey']
- **Abstrat**: Training end-to-end policies from image data to directly predict navigation actions for robotic systems has proven inherently difficult. Existing approaches often suffer from either the sim-to-real gap during policy transfer or limited action-labeled training data. To address this problem, we introduce Visual Learned Distance (VLD), a scalable framework for image-goal navigation that decouples perception learning from policy learning. We first train a self-supervised distance-to-goal predictor on about 3,000 hours of internet-scale and simulated video, providing a distance signal that a reinforcement learning (RL) policy can minimize. The RL policy is trained separately in simulation using privileged geometric distances perturbed by GeoNoise, a learned noise model that reproduces the viewpoint- and overlap-dependent errors of the trained distance predictor. At deployment, the policy consumes VLD predictions zero-shot, without retraining, inheriting ``where to go'' from large-scale visual training while retaining the robust navigation behaviors learned in simulation. This error modeling is key to the transfer: with VLD inputs, GeoNoise-trained policies reach $58.7\%$ success on HM3D and $76.2\%$ on Gibson, whereas policies trained with generic temporally correlated noise collapse to $4.9\%$ and $11.5\%$. On a physical robot, the VLD-driven policy succeeds in $88\%$ of trials, compared with $60\%$ for a strong end-to-end image-goal baseline that outperforms it in simulation. Our decoupled design thus provides an alternative and scalable path toward reliable navigation policies that transfer from simulation to the real world.





## Polarization calibration of fisheye cameras using skylight in the wild
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08911v1
- **Authors**: ['Shuo Wang', 'Shiqi Che', 'Dianze Dong', 'Wenxin Liu']
- **Abstrat**: Skylight polarization provides directional cues for robotic navigation, but fisheye lens effects and analyzer imperfections can degrade polarization measurements across the field of view. We present a joint lens-analyzer calibration method that explicitly models incidence-dependent lens transmission together with analyzer gains and angular offsets in a unified eight-parameter formulation. Given known geometric calibration, rotations between camera frames can be estimated using visual features and cross-frame directional polarized light consistency can be used for polarization calibration. Therefore, our calibration approach requires only a short handheld outdoor sequence under natural skylight, without dedicated polarization calibration equipment or a predefined sky polarization model. On real-world outdoor sequences, the proposed method achieves a weighted cross-frame angle-of-linear-polarization (AoLP) mean absolute error below 0.5° across all incidence angles, with a 73.4% reduction in the 80°-90° incidence angle region, and generalizes to an independent sequence with an overall mean absolute error of 0.466° without refitting. Preliminary solar-azimuth experiments using a camera mounted on a quadruped robot further show improved alignment with the visible solar center, supporting the potential of calibrated AoLP measurements for relative-heading estimation and future polarization--inertial navigation. The calibration software tool will be released upon acceptance of this paper.

## Computer-Use Agents Are Embodied Generalists
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09847v1
- **Authors**: ['Pranjal Aggarwal', 'Sean Welleck']
- **Abstrat**: In many areas of AI, general-purpose models have increasingly replaced handcrafted methods and specialized architectures. We argue that embodied AI is poised for a similar shift. Our key claim is that embodied tasks can be formulated as interaction with digital software. Specifically, we build software that renders an agent's view of a physical environment on a screen and provides controls for acting in it. A computer-use agent (CUA) can then perform embodied tasks through the same screenshots and keyboard and mouse actions that it uses to operate ordinary software. Using a single CUA design and changing only the software, our entries win first-, second-, third-, and sixth-place prizes across four ECCV workshop competitions, without any competition-specific agent tuning. These results suggest that the skills CUAs learn on software, such as visual grounding, precise clicking, and exploration, already carry over to embodied tasks, giving CUAs a far broader scope in embodied AI than previously realized. We therefore advocate that computer-use agents can be embodied generalists. Further, we advocate that, by following the training strategy that made computer-use agents general, embodied AI can converge on a single general model as well, ushering in a new era of general embodied AI. Website: https://pranjal2041.github.io/cua-embodied





## Vision to Geometry: 3D Spatial Memory for Sequential Embodied MLLM Reasoning and Exploration
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02458v3
- **Authors**: ['Zhongyi Cai', 'Yi Du', 'Chen Wang', 'Yu Kong']
- **Abstrat**: Embodied agents are expected to assist humans by actively exploring unknown environments and reasoning about spatial contexts. When deployed in real life, agents often face sequential tasks where each new task follows the completion of the previous one and may include infeasible objectives, such as searching for non-existent objects. However, most existing research focuses on isolated goals, overlooking the core challenge of sequential tasks: the ability to reuse spatial knowledge accumulated from previous explorations to guide subsequent reasoning and exploration. In this work, we investigate this underexplored yet practically significant embodied AI challenge. Specifically, we propose 3DSPMR, a 3D SPatial Memory Reasoning framework that utilizes Field-of-View (FoV) coverage as an explicit geometric prior. By integrating FoV-based constraints, 3DSPMR significantly enhances an agent's memory, reasoning, and exploration capabilities across sequential tasks. To facilitate research in this area, we further introduce SEER-Bench, a novel Sequential Embodied Exploration and Reasoning Benchmark that spans two foundational tasks: Embodied Question Answering (EQA) and Embodied Multi-modal Navigation (EMN). SEER-Bench uniquely incorporates both feasible and infeasible tasks to provide a rigorous and comprehensive evaluation of agent performance. Extensive experiments verify that 3DSPMR achieves substantial performance gains on both sequential EQA and EMN tasks.

## Mechanisms of Misgeneralization in Physical Sequence Modeling
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20299v2
- **Authors**: ['Kento Nishi', 'Raphael Tang', 'Karun Kumar', 'Core Francisco Park', 'Hidenori Tanaka']
- **Abstrat**: Generative sequence models are often trained to plan motion in physical domains, from robotics to mechanical simulations. When constructing a dataset to train such a model, engineers may curate demonstrations to specify how trajectories should be distributed over a physical quantity like travel distance or mechanical energy. For example, a roboticist building a maze navigation agent might choose demonstrations whose travel distances cover a fixed range uniformly, hoping to constrain the agent's expected power usage. We find that standard deep learning can violate this intent: each generated trajectory can seem plausible on its own, but the aggregate distribution over the physical quantity is wrong. We call this failure physical misgeneralization. In this work, we identify and formalize the problem, develop and validate an account of its mechanism, and use that account to compare and design mitigations. Using controlled synthetic tasks, we show that physical misgeneralization arises when local errors typical of the model class propagate through the physical measurement to shift the recovered distribution. We estimate these errors with a data deviation kernel, and we use it to predict which physical quantity values gain or lose mass under real diffusion sequence models trained on both toy and applied trajectory generation tasks. Finally, our mechanistic interpretation helps identify which mitigations are structurally promising. We first compare interventions that modify the dataset and generative interface, then show that the mechanistic understanding we develop throughout this paper suggests a novel way to construct data representations informed by the kernel. We show that our proposed mitigation, which transforms the coordinate system under which the generator learns and samples, effectively reduces quantity drift.

## DexDICE: Reconciling Safety and Performance via Dual-Expert Offline Imitation Learning
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09433v1
- **Authors**: ['Seokin Seo', 'Sung Kuk Shyn', 'Minji Seo', 'Yisoo Lee', 'Jongmin Lee', 'Hongseok Yang', 'Kee-Eung Kim']
- **Abstrat**: Ensuring high returns while providing strong safety guarantees in offline imitation learning (IL) is a fundamental challenge: existing approaches either rely on explicitly specified cost functions, which are difficult to design for complex real-world safety constraints, or require expert demonstrations that are both safe and high-performing, which are often too costly to collect. We introduce dual-expert imitation learning, which leverages two complementary data sources: (i) safety-compliant demonstrations that faithfully follow safety guidelines but may be suboptimal, and (ii) performance-oriented demonstrations that achieve high returns but may violate safety guidelines. To address this problem, we propose DexDICE (Dual-expert offline imitation learning via stationary DIstribution Correction Estimation). Instead of conventional distribution constraints, DexDICE combines DICE-style offline learning with safe support constraints, enabling agents to exploit high-return behaviors while remaining within safety-compliant regions. We prove that the ideal solution satisfying this constraint exactly has zero unsafe-region visitation with high probability. We evaluate DexDICE on a re-curated DSRL benchmark and validate it on a real-world mobile-robot navigation task trained directly from human teleoperation demonstrations, where it substantially outperforms baselines and remains robust to data scarcity and quality degradation.





## Modular magnetic soft robotic catheter enables visualized intervention in the pancreatobiliary tree
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14837v2
- **Authors**: ['B. Calmé', 'N. J. Greenidge', 'A. Metcalf', 'A. Bacchetti', 'G. Loza', 'D. Kpeglo', 'P. Lloyd', 'V. Pensabene', 'J. H. Chandler', 'P. Valdastri']
- **Abstrat**: Diagnosis and therapy in the pancreatobiliary tree are limited by access. Guidewires reach deep into the ducts but are blind, whereas cholangioscopes provide vision but are too large to readily enter the pancreatic body and tail, the cystic duct, or the gallbladder. At the millimeter scale of these ducts, every function added to a steerable catheter competes with steering for cross-section and adds bending stiffness. We present a 1.47-mm modular magnetic soft robotic catheter in which each function is built into the magnetic body, onto its surface, or onto the footprint of the camera, and we measured what each function costs in bending under bedside fields. Imaging and fiber Bragg grating (FBG) shape sensing accounted for most of this cost, whereas a compliant gripper, magnetic anchoring balloons, and a micrometer-thick ultrasound-triggered release shell together reduced the tip angle at 8 mT only from 70° to 64°. The anchoring balloon increased the gripping force about threefold without stiffening the catheter during navigation. In live pigs, the robotic catheter performed operator-initiated task-autonomous cannulation of the native papilla without sphincterotomy and was teleoperated under onboard vision 75 mm into the pancreatic duct and, without a guidewire, through the cystic duct into the gallbladder, whose lumen it imaged. Anchoring, grasping, and payload release were each demonstrated in vivo. Adding each function at a measured cost to steering allows a soft robot small enough for these ducts to intervene as well as navigate.

## FASTFUNNELS: Agile Multi Robot Navigation in Narrow Spaces via Adaptive Dynamic Funnels
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09005v1
- **Authors**: ['Vignesh Rajagopal', 'Rohan Chandra']
- **Abstrat**: We focus on the problem of high-speed multi-robot navigation in cluttered spaces with tight spatial constraints. A key challenge is balancing safety with agility, as existing methods often trade one for the other (slowing down near a bottleneck to avoid collisions versus failing to swerve in time at high speeds), especially for car-like systems. Our approach belongs to the family of formation control methods, in which Ackermann-steering agents coordinate while navigating tight spaces. However, current formation control techniques rely on rigid, pre-defined shapes and transitions that depend on initial conditions, the environment, and vehicle dynamics, and are impractical to maintain at high speeds. For example, agents passing through a small gap must first form a square, transform into a line using analytical equations, and then return to a square. In contrast, our approach is inspired by a droplet of water that continuously adapts its shape to the space it flows through without slowing down. We propose a hierarchical algorithm in which, at the high level, a reinforcement learning policy produces a dynamic, blob-like elliptical patch that morphs its shape in real time, squeezing, stretching, and orienting itself around obstacles and through tight spaces. At the low level, the robots are controlled via distributed model predictive control (DMPC) to stay inside the patch, which acts as a soft forcing function, while avoiding collisions with each other. We evaluate our approach, FastFunnels, on simulated F1TENTH racing cars across 14 scenarios with N=1 to N=6 robots, averaged over 20 trials each. FastFunnels achieves an 89% completion rate at 7.73 m/s, while all baselines either fail to complete the track or succeed only at very low speeds (0.07-1.79 m/s, about 40% completion rate).

## EvoPlan: Evolutionary Neuro-Symbolic Robot Planning with Spatio-Temporal Guarantees
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06724v2
- **Authors**: ['Bhavya Sai Nukapotula', 'Samin Moosavi', 'Haoze Wang', 'Luke Duncan', 'Diya Shakkottai', 'Varun Murali', 'Srinivas Shakkottai']
- **Abstrat**: LLM-based robot planners are fluent but cannot guarantee that their plans are executable or safe. Classical PDDL planners can guarantee symbolic validity, but only once the problem is fully specified, and they make poor use of an LLM's ability to read context and repair plans. We present EvoPlan, a neuro-symbolic framework whose guarantees are symbolic validity and satisfaction of a constraint learned from data. The planner works with locally-hosted open-weight models, so it does not require a cloud service. EvoPlan has three parts. First, an offline procedure that mines a single global Signal Temporal Logic (STL) mobility constraint from demonstrations. Perturbed demonstrations supply the missing negatives without encoding any rule, and evolutionary search fits the constraint, which captures driving rules or social-navigation preferences within a modelled taxonomy of norms. We use the mined constraint to shield driving and navigation policies on Bench2Drive and HA-VLN-CE. Second, an evolutionary PDDL planner: an LLM proposes and repairs plans, programmatic validators decide which ones survive, and the validated portion of the plan grows over iterations. On open-world ALFWorld and NL-PDDL Blocksworld, it approaches classical planning when the goal vocabulary agrees with the action model, and it stays robust when the two disagree and classical planning fails. Third, a constrained execution loop: Nav2 uses the costmap to ground the plan into waypoints, which are checked against the mined constraint. Violations and execution failures update the PDDL problem for re-planning. We demonstrate the full pipeline in Gazebo and on a Clearpath Jackal robot. More details and videos are on the project page: https://nbhavyasai.github.io/evoplan/

## Lightweight Path-Aware Frontier Re-ranking for LiDAR-Based UAV Exploration
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09561v1
- **Authors**: ['Duc Manh Do', 'Thanh Nguyen Canh', 'Ngoc Minh Do', 'Mai Thanh Thai', 'Xiem HoangVan']
- **Abstrat**: Autonomous 3D exploration with UAVs requires efficient target selection under limited flight time and onboard computation. In conventional Euclidean-distance-based frontier pipelines, geometric distance may poorly represent the actual collision-free travel cost in cluttered environments, leading to inefficient frontier choices. This paper presents a lightweight path-aware frontier re-ranking method for LiDAR-based UAV exploration. The method first retains a small top-N set of promising frontier candidates using a lightweight utility function and then applies time-bounded RRT* planning to estimate their feasible path costs for re-ranking. The path generated for the selected frontier is further reused for navigation to reduce redundant global planning. Experiments in three warehouse-like environments show that the proposed method consistently produces shorter exploration trajectories than conventional frontier baselines, with traveled-distance reductions of up to 14.1%, while reducing exploration time by up to 9.5% in two environments and remaining competitive in the third. The method maintains map detail comparable to the conventional OctoMap-based baseline, demonstrating an effective trade-off between exploration efficiency and mapping quality.

## eSharp: Diffusion-based Sharp Monocular Metric Event-to-Depth Estimation

- **Authors**: ['Qiushi Dai', 'Aniket Dashpute', 'Yuhao Liu', 'Rishubh Parihar', 'Guha Balakrishnan', 'Ashok Veeraraghavan']
- **Abstrat**: Depth perception is fundamental for autonomous navigation, industrial inspection, and mixed-reality systems. It often operates under strict constraints on power and form factor. In such cases, monocular depth estimation (MDE) is often used. Even as recent advances continue to improve monocular depth quality, conventional frame-based MDE systems remain challenged by fast motion and high-dynamic-range scenes. This work introduces eSharp, an MDE framework that addresses these problems using an event camera. eSharp repurposes pretrained diffusion priors for event-only MDE and produces accurate metric depth with unparalleled sharpness on real data. Experiments show that eSharp outperforms prior event-to-depth baselines in both metric-depth accuracy and spatial fidelity, establishing diffusion models as a promising foundation for event-driven 3D perception.

## LACART: Lookahead-Augmented Collision Avoidance and Reference Tracking Strategies for Safety-Critical Robots
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09640v1
- **Authors**: ['Pedro Lopes', 'Amit Shivam', 'Luis Tiago Paiva']
- **Abstrat**: Autonomous racing provides a demanding testbed for collision avoidance under tight geometric and computational constraints. Building on a recent conference contribution and a master's dissertation, LACART combines lookahead-based control barrier functions (CBFs), a relaxed control Lyapunov function (CLF), and quadratic programming (QP) to modify nominal vehicle commands for obstacle avoidance and reference tracking. The method handles circular obstacles and track boundaries in a common optimisation layer. Comparative simulation results against Huang \emph{et al.} \cite{r9} show similar full-track paths and lower median computation times: 0.31 versus 0.33 ms per step and 0.023 versus 0.025 ms per QP solve. The dissertation further demonstrates a 1:43-scale vehicle with camera-based estimation and wireless actuation at a reported 25 Hz control rate. We identify the conditions linking lookahead-point clearance to body clearance, illustrate hardware limitations, and propose a recorded demonstration of safety intervention and recovery. The contribution targets ground-robot collision avoidance and real-time safety monitoring.





## RADAR Perception for Dynamic Obstacle Avoidance onboard small-scale Quadrotor UAVs
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01855v2
- **Authors**: ['Dnyandeep Mandaokar', 'Bernhard Rinner']
- **Abstrat**: Fast dynamic obstacle avoidance (DOA) on uncrewed aerial vehicles (UAVs) demands not only low-latency control and actuation but also reliable, long-range perception to detect obstacles and estimate their motion in time to maneuver. We present, to the best of our knowledge, the first mmWave RADAR-based perception-and-control system for fast onboard DOA. We derive and analyze latency and spatial bounds that relate sensing range, relative speed, and control delay, yielding sufficient conditions for successful avoidance. We further extend prior latency analyses to include the decision latency of the tracker and trigger to evaluate them per trial. Our computationally efficient system combines a lightweight interacting multiple models (IMM) tracker with a control-barrier functions (CBF) controller that directly outputs evasive accelerations. In 300 throw experiments spanning three object sizes and varying visibility (light and dark), it achieves mean position errors of at most 0.15 m, 0.93m, and 0.87m in the x, y, and z directions, with a similar spread in 90 experiments in smoke. An onboard implementation on a Raspberry Pi 4B demonstrates real-time feasibility with an end-to-end processing latency of approximately 14ms per RADAR frame and a system latency of 75ms from first detection to the avoidance command. The UAV successfully avoided obstacles in all 20 tethered indoor flights and all 39 untethered outdoor avoidance maneuvers across three ball sizes, during both hover and forward flights at up to 5m/s with measured closing speeds up to 14.6m/s.





## Horizon-Coupled Obstacle Guidance for Relative-Action Diffusion Policies
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09340v1
- **Authors**: ['Guangyuan Wang', 'Haishan Xia', 'Zitao Zhang', 'Kejia Chen', 'Yuanchen Sun', 'Zhenguo Sun', 'Donghao Zhang', 'Yu Zhang', 'Alois Knoll']
- **Abstrat**: Real-world robot deployment requires adapting learned policies to unseen obstacles without collecting new demonstrations or retraining. Existing policy-level guidance often applies obstacle corrections independently at individual horizon steps, which may address local obstacle violations but fail to coordinate the action sequence into a complete, task-directed detour. How obstacle responses should be propagated across the action horizon to coordinate complete, task-directed avoidance motion remains underexplored. We introduce an inferencetime obstacle-guidance framework for pretrained relative-action diffusion policies. It rolls out cumulative relative actions into a workspace trajectory, evaluates obstacle interactions along the trajectory with an SDF-based obstacle model, and propagates each obstacle response to all contributing action increments during denoising. Real-robot experiments across seven obstacleconstrained scenarios spanning four manipulation tasks show that our method achieves 105/140 (75.0%) collision-free task successes, compared with 8/140 (5.7%) for absolute-action pointwise guidance. Ablations show that the relative-action horizoncoupled formulation provides the larger observed improvement across both obstacle models.

- **Abstrat**: Autonomous 3D exploration with UAVs requires efficient target selection under limited flight time and onboard computation. This paper presents a lightweight path-aware frontier re-ranking method for LiDAR-based UAV exploration. The method first retains a small top-N set of promising frontier candidates using a lightweight utility function and then applies time-bounded RRT* planning to estimate their feasible path costs for re-ranking. ## Closed-Loop Object-Informed Control for Non-Prehensile Robot Manipulation

- **Abstrat**: Non-prehensile robot manipulation is challenging due to discontinuous, long-horizon interactions between the robot and the objects it manipulates. Sampling-based model predictive control methods are effective with discontinuous contact but face challenges with finding promising trajectories in long-horizon planning. We propose a closed-loop object-informed (CLOI) method that splits the problem into object-level planning to find long-horizon object poses that lead the object to its goal, and robot-level planning to select robot actions that follow those poses. ## BEVCast: Path-Adaptive BEV for Efficient End-to-End Autonomous Driving
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08631v1
- **Authors**: ['Qiren Wang', 'Mingyu Guo', 'Zixuan Wang', 'Weisong Shi']
- **Abstrat**: Camera-based driving models build a dense bird's-eye-view (BEV) grid and spend the same computation on every cell of it, although the vehicle crosses only a few of those cells within the planning horizon. We present BEVCast, a two-stage BEV whose resolution adapts to the predicted driving path. A coarse pass encodes the whole scene on a grid of 4.096 m cells. A heat head trained on the recorded path assigns a heat to every coarse cell and the cells with the highest heat are refined into 1.024 m cells under a fixed budget. ## XIT: Exploration and Exploitation Informed Trees for Active Gas Distribution Mapping in Unknown Environments
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13739v3
- **Authors**: ['Mal Fazliu', 'Matthew Coombes', 'Sen Wang', 'Cunjia Liu']
- **Abstrat**: Mobile robotic gas distribution mapping (GDM) provides critical situational awareness during emergency responses to hazardous gas releases. However, most systems still rely on teleoperation, limiting scalability and response speed. Autonomous active GDM is challenging in unknown and cluttered environments, because the robot must simultaneously explore traversable space, map the environment, and infer the gas distribution belief from sparse chemical measurements. We address this by formulating active GDM as a next-best-trajectory informative path planning (IPP) problem and develop XIT (Exploration and Exploitation Informed Trees), a sampling-based planner that grows a single shared tree toward multiple frontier goals, coupling exploration of unresolved regions with informative gas sampling en route. High-fidelity simulations demonstrate the benefits of XIT in terms of GDM quality and efficiency, and a scaled real-world experiment demonstrates its practicality. ## CascadeRec: Deployed Adaptive LLM Compute Allocation for Multi-Tenant Content Recommendation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08566v1
- **Authors**: ['Kai Zhao', 'Guilin Zhang', 'Xiang Cheng', 'Xiquan Cui', 'Henry Heng', 'Xu Chu', 'Alet Blanken']
- **Abstrat**: Large language models (LLMs) are attractive rerankers for recommendation. Running an expensive model on every request is costly, and for users with sparse interaction histories it often lowers quality. We present CascadeRec, a two-tier LLM reranking cascade deployed in production at a large financial planning and analysis (FP&A) SaaS provider. It serves content recommendations to enterprise tenants on AWS EKS and GKE. A lightweight Tier-1 model reranks every request. A router escalates only the requests where extra inference-time compute is likely to help, and sends them to a more expensive Tier-2 procedure (self-consistency sampling, multi-path reasoning, or a larger model). Compared with uniform use of the expensive tier, CascadeRec has reduced inference cost by about 10%, with minimal latency impact and no quality regression observed to date. Segment-level analysis with paired-bootstrap confidence intervals shows that uniform LLM reranking lowers NDCG@10 for sparse-history users by up to 30%, and its only statistically significant gain appears on the segment with the longest histories. Escalating cold users can make the loss larger instead of repairing it. Which segments benefit from escalation depends on the domain, and this motivates the segment-gated rollout we use in production. We describe the deployment architecture, the measured benefits, and lessons learned, including inter-tenant load imbalance as a driver for cascading and the handling of LLM output-parsing failures at the serving layer.
