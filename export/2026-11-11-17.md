## DeRep: Learning Depth Representations for End-to-End Navigation in Cluttered Environments
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13499v1
- **Authors**: ['Hanyu Jin', 'Kenji Shimada']
- **Abstrat**: End-to-end navigation in cluttered environments relies on compact visual representations to support collision avoidance and goal-directed motion. Existing methods typically learn these representations jointly with the policy or through observation reconstruction, which does not explicitly specify what information the representation should preserve. Moreover, they are usually evaluated with a single downstream policy learner, although the benefit of a representation may depend on how the policy is learned. It therefore remains unclear how different representation-learning objectives affect downstream navigation performance and robustness. This work proposes DeRep, a navigation-oriented representation-learning method that combines local bird's-eye-view reachability supervision with depth reconstruction. To compare representation objectives under controlled conditions, we pretrain encoders with a common architecture and freeze them during downstream learning. We evaluate the representations using a linear policy probe, flow-matching imitation learning, and reinforcement learning with PPO. DeRep achieves the lowest linear policy probe prediction error and outperforms reconstruction alone across all six tested PPO conditions. In imitation learning, both reachability-based objectives achieve higher mean success rates than reconstruction alone, with similar mean success rates between the reachability-only and joint objectives. These results demonstrate consistent benefits of incorporating reachability supervision into depth representation pretraining across different downstream learning methods and evaluation conditions. We further deploy the PPO policy with the frozen DeRep encoder in Gazebo and on a physical quadrotor without fine-tuning, demonstrating its capability for zero-shot sim-to-sim and sim-to-real transfer.





## Multimodal Road Closure Mapping via Cross-Modal Graph Attention
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13192v1
- **Authors**: ['Izzeddin Teeti', 'Vaibhav Kango', 'Nidhi Sridhar', 'Mohamed Moustafa']
- **Abstrat**: Accurate and timely identification of road closures is critical for large-scale navigation and logistics systems, yet remains challenging due to the need to jointly reason over visual evidence, spatial context, and road network topology. Existing approaches either rely on manual map updates or focus on detecting closure-related objects without resolving which road segment is affected. In this work, we introduce a unified multimodal and multitask framework that jointly performs road closure classification (no, partial, complete) and road segment association within a single learning architecture. Our model integrates graph-structured map data, satellite imagery augmented with projected street-level detections, and street-view images using modality-specific encoders and cross-modal transformer attention, enabling explicit reasoning over visual cues and network topology. Evaluated on nearly 20K real-world scenarios collected from a large-scale commercial vehicle fleet, our approach achieves 72.7% closure classification accuracy and 60% road segment association accuracy, outperforming frontier vision-language models by over 28 percentage points on closure classification while being over 4,000x smaller and 100x faster at inference. Extensive ablations demonstrate the importance of cross-modal attention, edge-aware pooling, and graph message passing for accurate geospatial reasoning. This work establishes the first benchmark for joint road closure classification and segment-level attribution, and shows that task-specific architectural inductive biases substantially outperform general-purpose multimodal models for structured geospatial understanding.





## SNIF: a Self-Navigator Intelligent Filter correcting off-center and spin-history artifacts in quasi-random radial MRI
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13056v1
- **Authors**: ['Tanguy Boucneau', 'Brice Fernandez', 'Peder Larson', 'Luc Darrasse', 'Xavier Ma{î}tre']
- **Abstrat**: For self-navigation and self-gating in MRI, self-navigators are extracted from the complex values of the k-space center, called DC-signal, through a temporal frequency analysis. However, this signal often carries artifacts. This paper introduces a Self-Navigator Intelligent Filter (SNIF) that models the physical origin of the k-space center artifacts to properly estimate them and thus more accurately remove them. The artifact affecting the DC-signal $S_{DC}$ generally has two main origins: the jitter of the k-space center (''off-center artifact'') and the cumulative history of the spins (''spin-history artifact''). Both add noise-like artifacts to the signal. The off-center effect can be modeled as a perturbative signal $S_0$ and the spin-history effect as a convolution kernel $h$ so that the measured DC-signal is $S_{DC} = S_m + S_0 \star h$, where $S_m$ is the motion-related component which effectively contains the expected self-navigators. Methods to compute $h$ and $S_0$ were developed, which gives the possibility to extract $S_m$ from $S_{DC}$. They were implemented and evaluated on thoracic UTE datasets acquired on a healthy volunteer. Qualitative visual comparison and quantitative spectral analysis of the DC-signal before and after SNIF demonstrate that the method improves both the accuracy and the precision of the extracted respiratory and cardiac self-navigators. By considering the physical origin of the DC-signal artifacts, SNIF makes both respiratory and cardiac self-navigators more sensitive to motion, which supports finer MRI motion tracking.

## GEMOR-GS: Global Extrinsic-calibration-refined Mapping and Online Relocalization for 3D Gaussian-Splatting SLAM
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13528v1
- **Authors**: ['Yuchen Yang', 'Ming Ding', 'Xinyu Wu', 'Hesheng Wang']
- **Abstrat**: Photorealistic and metrically consistent world representations are a primary requirement for LiDAR--inertial--visual SLAM systems that serve robots over continuous operation. 3D Gaussian splatting makes such representations attainable by supplying an explicit, renderable, and queryable scene model. We present GEMOR-GS (Global Extrinsic-calibration-refined Mapping and Online Relocalization for 3D Gaussian-Splatting SLAM) to make the Gaussian map geometrically accurate, globally refined, and re-enterable, providing a better appearance for robot perception and decision-making. To improve the 3DGS map, the LiDAR--camera extrinsic is first estimated offline by direct-method alignment over dedicated calibration segments and reused by all following online and offline processes. Under the calibrated geometry, keyframe poses and Gaussian parameters are refined alternately under a gated differentiable-rendering and LiDAR--projection objective. The final global reconstruction is then rebuilt from the refined keyframe schema with all online constraints lifted. For the robot SLAM lifecycle, the method recovers the pose from the 3DGS map after tracking loss through an XFeat bypass map, RANSAC-PnP coarse localization, and Gaussian RGB-D warp refinement. Experiments on one self-collected and five public sequences quantify each element: the offline reconstruction wins all paired keyframes against the online map by 14.3--19.9 dB, improves PSNR by approximately 9 dB over Gaussian-LIC2, and achieves 86--100% held-out relocalization success tied to extrinsic quality. The results show that GEMOR-GS improves the geometric accuracy, appearance fidelity, and re-enterability of the 3DGS map, serving as a dependable robot world model for SLAM and downstream tasks.

## NIF-VIO: Visual--Inertial Odometry Across Inertial and Non-Inertial Reference Frames
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13662v1
- **Authors**: ['Zichao Shu', 'Lijun Li', 'Zhuochen Li', 'Zetao Chen', 'Jianyu Wang']
- **Abstrat**: Conventional visual--inertial odometry (VIO) assumes that observations from cameras and an inertial measurement unit (IMU) are jointly consistent in an inertial reference frame. However, this assumption breaks down under non-inertial conditions. Inside accelerating or rotating carriers, visual observations of the interior reflect carrier-relative motion, while IMU measurements contain additional carrier-induced components, which can compromise conventional fusion. We therefore present NIF-VIO for localization relative to the local environment across inertial and non-inertial reference frames. It adapts visual--inertial fusion based on measurement consistency. As inertial conditions change, early constraint isolation is combined with local reoptimization to limit error propagation before inconsistency confirmation and repair the affected local geometry. Under non-inertial conditions, a compact model with temporally shared parameters is introduced to account for carrier motion while preserving inertial information that complements visual constraints. Experiments on public benchmarks and a self-collected dataset demonstrate that NIF-VIO achieves competitive performance under inertial conditions and improved localization accuracy and availability under non-inertial conditions.

## CFAR Detection on Non-Coherently Integrated Angular Maps for mmWave Radio SLAM
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14250v1
- **Authors**: ['Paolo Tosi', 'Ossi Kaltiokallio', 'Marcus Henninger', 'Silvio Mandelli', 'Mikko Valkama']
- **Abstrat**: Reliable multipath detection is essential for millimeter-wave simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM), where propagation paths reflecting off the environment provide geometric constraints beyond the dominant line-of-sight component. This paper develops constant false alarm rate (CFAR)-based detectors for beam-swept bistatic integrated sensing and communications (ISAC). Starting from conventional CFAR on the I/Q datacube, we derive a modified detector for non-coherently integrated angular power maps, where the post-integration noise statistics differ from those assumed by standard cell-averaging CFAR. To improve practicality, we introduce a multi-cell cell-under-test formulation and a plus-shaped training window that reduces computational cost while accounting for sidelobe structure. The proposed methods are evaluated on experimental 60 GHz indoor measurements using a snapshot-SLAM estimator. Compared with a singular value decomposition (SVD)-based detector, the proposed CFAR approaches reduce the overall positioning RMSE from 0.30 m to 0.24-0.26 m, with the largest improvement in non-line-of-sight snapshots. The multi-cell non-coherent detector further lowers the computational complexity without degrading positioning accuracy, demonstrating its suitability for practical multipath radio-SLAM.

## OnlyAct: Extracting Action Representations from Foundation Models for World Models
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14420v1
- **Authors**: ['Runjia Li', 'Minghao Chen', 'Junyu Xie', 'Philip Torr', 'Andrea Vedaldi', 'Tomas Jakab']
- **Abstrat**: Video world models promise general-purpose interactive simulators, but their training is limited by action supervision: action labels are scarce, fragmented across incompatible specifications, and do not scale with the unlabeled video pretraining corpus. A common workaround jointly trains an action encoder to compress consecutive frames into a latent action and a decoder to reconstruct the next frame from the previous frame and that latent, forcing the latent to capture only new information. This has two limitations: learning the action space from scratch can limit generalization to unseen domains, and the bottleneck trades expressiveness for separation, with tighter settings losing nuance and looser ones risking scene leakage under domain shifts. We argue for a different starting point: rather than learning actions from scratch, we start from a pretrained foundation model (V-JEPA 2), whose embeddings of a frame pair already carry substantial action information after large-scale, diverse video pretraining. The remaining challenge is to extract the action while discarding the scene. We propose a label-free contrastive adaptation framework that pulls together frame pairs sharing the same motion under a different scene and pushes apart pairs from the same scene with different motion, reshaping the geometry of the pretrained space without imposing a reconstruction bottleneck. We introduce a unified evaluation protocol along three axes (action expressiveness, scene invariance, and action-conditioned video generation quality) across robot manipulation, camera navigation, game, and driving benchmarks, most of which are unseen during adaptation. Compared with annotation-free latent-action methods, our representations are substantially more expressive while matching their scene invariance, and they yield world models that transfer demonstrated actions to new scenes more faithfully.





## ReACT: Reflection-Consistent Modulation and Cross-Load Consistency for Humanoid Velocity Tracking Under Asymmetric Loads
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13929v1
- **Authors**: ['Xianghui Wang', 'Aoran Fu', 'Liyou Luo', 'Shanze Wang', 'Xiaoyu Shen', 'Dan Zhang', 'Wei Zhang']
- **Abstrat**: In recent years, humanoid loco-manipulation has progressed to whole-body grasping, lifting, and transport. A basic requirement underlying these tasks is carrying the object to its destination, which depends on the locomotion controller accurately executing velocity commands from the navigation policy. However, carrying an object in one hand introduces a persistent asymmetric upper-body load that can degrade velocity tracking. Persistent tracking biases can accumulate into position and heading deviations, increasing the risk of collisions and navigation failure. To address this problem, we present ReACT, a framework for accurate velocity tracking under asymmetric upper-body loads. Reflection-consistent disturbance modulation uses the estimated wrench to modulate actor features for load compensation while preserving action correspondence under reflected observations. Cross-load tracking consistency training compares loaded and unloaded conditions under the same velocity commands to promote consistent velocity tracking across loading conditions. Simulation experiments on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot show improved velocity tracking and reduced directional bias under varying asymmetric loads. Real-world experiments demonstrate collision-free goal reaching in all 40 tested navigation trials under unilateral hand loads with a fixed mapless navigation policy.





## A Faster and Efficient Indoor User Localization Using Bistatic MIMO Radar
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13828v1
- **Authors**: ['Shadab Anjum', 'Sourav Banerjee', 'Neel Kanth Kundu']
- **Abstrat**: Indoor user localization is essential for applications such as asset tracking, robotic navigation, and smart-building automation where satellite-based positioning is unavailable. Bistatic MIMO radar exploits bistatic range measurements and geometric diversity from distributed antennas, but low-SNR conditions make the resulting estimation ill-conditioned. We formulate indoor localization as a Tikhonov-regularized nonlinear least-squares problem and solve it with a damped Gauss-Newton (DGN) algorithm, in which each iteration reduces to a closed-form 3x3 linear solve followed by an Armijo backtracking line search. Simulations show accuracy on par with, and runtime far faster than, an SDP-based benchmark.





## From Depth to Safe Depth: Uncertainty-Aware Monocular Perception for Mobile Robot Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13769v1
- **Authors**: ['Saeid Firouzi Daghigh', 'Saeed Ayat']
- **Abstrat**: Monocular depth foundation models make it tempting to replace depth sensors on mobile robots with a single RGB camera. We show that, used off the shelf, this is unsafe: in a simulated indoor navigation benchmark, a standard Nav2 stack driven by Depth Anything V2 (metric) collides in 63\% of 120 episodes, because the model systematically over-estimates the distance to nearby obstacles. We propose a lightweight plug-in module that turns predicted depth into \emph{safe depth}. A 0.58\,M-parameter head on top of the \emph{frozen} depth model predicts, per pixel, a log-depth correction $μ$ and an uncertainty $σ$, trained with a $β$-weighted Gaussian negative log-likelihood. The navigation stack consumes the one-sided bound $\hat d_{\text{safe}}=d\,e^{μ-kσ}$, where $k$ is set by conformal calibration on a held-out world, and uncertainty also decides which pixels may be treated as floor. The correction halves the depth error (AbsRel $0.262\rightarrow0.134$) and reduces near-range over-estimation from 59.8\% to 12.0\%. In navigation, safe depth raises the success rate from 37\% to 70\% and lowers collisions from 63\% to 3\%, close to the 2\% of ground-truth depth. A constant safety margin calibrated to the same pixel coverage reaches only 30\% success because it freezes the robot; uncertainty-aware safety wins 52 of 56 discordant paired episodes against it. Gains hold on glass, texture-less walls and dark regions.





## A computational framework for understanding embodied plans
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13669v1
- **Authors**: ['Aalap D. Shah', 'Basil Saeed', 'Joshua B. Tenenbaum', 'Tobias Gerstenberg', 'Hyowon Gweon', 'Ilker Yildirim']
- **Abstrat**: Reasoning about people's plans and goals based on their actions is fundamental to human social interaction and essential for cooperative, embodied artificial intelligence. Formalizing this capacity in naturalistic, embodied settings has been a longstanding challenge. Here, we present GenEA, a novel agent representation capable of reasoning over multi-step, long-horizon embodied plans. At the heart of GenEA lies a multilevel state-space that couples the coarse biomechanics of an articulated body and its interactions with nearby objects by extending the task-and-motion planning framework from robotics with simulation-based intuitive physics. By embedding this state-space in a generative model, GenEA yields a joint probability distribution over goals and embodied plans. Across several experiments spanning the qualitatively distinct domains of object manipulation and navigation, featuring diverse tasks and multiple behavioral measures, we show that GenEA, through simple transformations of this joint distribution, recapitulates how humans reason about embodied plans. Crucially, GenEA consistently outperforms strong alternative models, including plausible heuristics, standard task-and-motion planning, and a state-of-the-art vision-language model. GenEA thereby offers a new perspective on the architecture of human reasoning and social cognition, suggesting that embodied artificial intelligence systems seeking to seamlessly interact with humans should adopt GenEA-like generative models of action planning.





## Latent Barrier Steering: Hierarchical Safety for Generative Planning
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13227v1
- **Authors**: ['Renhao Zhang', 'Mingzhe Li', 'Bruno Castro da Silva']
- **Abstrat**: We introduce Latent Barrier Steering (LBS), a hierarchical semantic-to-path framework for safe flow-matching-based generative planning. Existing control-barrier-function (CBF) safety filters for diffusion and flow-matching planners act locally on generated samples, either during sampling or after prediction. Such repair is effective for small violations, but can struggle when a new constraint blocks the whole selected behavior mode rather than merely perturbing it locally. LBS decouples behavior selection from path certification through two coordinated interventions: it first steers the generator's behavior latent toward rollouts with larger safety margin, then decodes the steered latent and applies a path-space CBF quadratic program (QP) only for residual violations. We show that latent steering increases the margin available to the path-space corrector, yielding finite-time recovery guarantees and reduced correction burden. Experiments across navigation and robot end-effector planning tasks show that LBS preserves safety while improving goal reaching and reducing correction burden compared with path-level safety filters.

## SIGMO: Sigmoid Gated Interaction-Aware Motion Planning
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14414v1
- **Authors**: ['Avinash Singh', 'Manish Saroya', 'Fatemeh Cheraghi Pouria', 'Faizan Tariq', "Jovin D'sa", 'David Isele', 'Yosuke Sakamoto', 'Sangjae Bae']
- **Abstrat**: Despite the widespread adoption of autonomous vehicle technologies, reliable navigation within cluttered environments remains arduous due to the necessity of interaction-aware strategies; example scenarios include: narrow passages with multiple agents, lane changing in dense traffic, and crossing unsignalized intersections. These scenarios often require highly proactive strategies, simultaneously demanding interpretability and quick adaptability, which challenge end-to-end and data-driven methods. An effective strategy requires: scalability to multiple agents, adaptability to changes in other agents' behavior, and harmony with surrounding traffic without freezing or becoming overly conservative. To meet these requirements, we present SIGMO, a sigmoid-gated interaction-aware motion planner, with two main contributions. First, we formulate polynomial sigmoid functions that analytically estimate yield scores of surrounding agents using physics-grounded interaction features that capture driving intuitions. Second, we integrate the polynomial sigmoid functions into a receding-horizon planner, which facilitates interaction-aware planning with arbitrary trajectory predictions. We validate SIGMO in interaction-intensive simulations and hardware experiments. The results show that SIGMO balances proactive and defensive behavior while remaining computationally lightweight, serving as a strong baseline in a mobility stack. The simulation and hardware experiment videos are available here: https://sites.google.com/view/sigmoid-polygate.

## Safe Approximate Nonlinear Model Predictive Control Without Expert Demonstrations
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24503v2
- **Authors**: ['Mihaela-Larisa Clement', 'Mónika Farsang', 'Johannes Edelmann', 'Manfred Plöchl', 'Radu Grosu', 'Ezio Bartocci', 'Agnes Poks', 'Luca Marzari']
- **Abstrat**: Repeated online optimization makes nonlinear model predictive control (NMPC) computationally demanding for real-time applications. Neural approximations reduce this cost but often require large datasets of expert NMPC solutions. We introduce Sequential Approximate MPC (Seq-AMPC), a label-free recurrent framework that generates complete control sequences along predicted trajectories using parameters shared across the prediction horizon. The policy is trained directly through known dynamics with losses for control cost, constraint satisfaction, and a cost-decrease condition aligned with a feasible-backup safety filter. Building on this filter, we introduce a stage-cost-dependent acceptance tolerance that preserves recursive feasibility, constraint satisfaction, and asymptotic convergence under nominal MPC assumptions, independently of neural approximation error. We further derive Hoeffding-based finite-sample bounds for the expected fraction of applied proposals and closed-loop cost discrepancy relative to NMPC. We evaluate Seq-AMPC on a quadcopter, chain of masses, obstacle-avoidance vehicle, and robotic manipulator, comparing recurrent and feedforward policies under label-free and imitation training. Across four benchmarks, training takes 0.2-1.7 GPU hours per policy without expert control labels. The filtered controller applies 97.2-99.1% of neural proposals and achieves mean closed-loop cost ratios within 0.3% of NMPC on jointly completed episodes. Tests with suboptimal feasible initial plans further demonstrate the benefit of neural replanning.

## Fastener-centric acquisition and AND/OR graph representation of state-dependent disassembly knowledge from CAD-derived virtual assemblies
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13379v1
- **Authors**: ['Shiki Iwase']
- **Abstrat**: Design-stage disassemblability assessment requires knowledge of product structure and the operations available in each assembly state, but CAD assemblies do not directly specify state-dependent disassembly operations. This paper proposes a fastener-centric virtual-disassembly framework that acquires such knowledge from CAD-derived virtual assemblies. Fasteners jointly releasing the same constraint are grouped into operation-level sets, avoiding enumeration of arbitrary individual-fastener orders. Their removability is evaluated through state-dependent visibility and tool-access checks and torque-driven virtual-removal trials. After fastener removal, graph-based reasoning identifies candidate moving units, whose separation is evaluated by initial-motion screening, path generation, and physics-based validation. The acquired operations, states, dependencies, validated paths, and simulation-derived attributes are organized into a Transformed AND/OR Graph for subsequent planning and design evaluation. Exhaustive verification with a controlled toy assembly found no branching violations for introduced tool-accessibility and precedence conditions. Evaluation with a 54-component OpenManipulator Chain gripper demonstrated state-dependent fastener-removal and part- or subassembly-separation operations. Visibility and tool-accessibility evaluations pruned 51.4% of fastener-set candidate evaluations, while removal of obstructing components enabled previously inaccessible fastener operations. Operation-level grouping and screening thereby limited detailed physical validation to mechanically plausible candidates. The results demonstrate the feasibility of acquiring product-specific disassembly knowledge from CAD-derived virtual assemblies before manufacture, supporting subsequent disassembly reasoning and design-for-disassembly evaluation.
