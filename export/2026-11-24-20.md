## ABI: Actor-Free Behavior-Induced Representations for Zero-Shot Test-Time Guidance
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30636v1
- **Authors**: ['Yifei Yao', 'Yude Zou', 'Guanbo Jiao', 'Jianming Ma', 'Xing Gao', 'Chunhua Shen']
- **Abstrat**: Behavior-cloning policies show strong ability in sequential control by generating coherent multi-step action chunks, yet their purely imitative objective limits zero-shot generalization to unseen tasks. While offline reinforcement learning offers principled policy improvement, existing methods either require task-specific reward supervision or remain tied to a specific actor during critic training or policy deployment, inflating training cost and restricting cross-policy generalization. We observe that evaluating actions under the data-collecting behavior largely preserves their relative value ordering across target policies, offering a way around actor coupling. Combining this property with reward-free successor prediction, we propose Actor-Free Behavior-Induced Representations (ABI) to learn multi-horizon action-chunk representations from unlabeled trajectories without reward supervision or actor training. At test time, pairing the frozen representation with lightweight task readouts forms a chunk critic that guides target policies without parameter updates and transfers directly across distinct unseen policies. Our analysis establishes when the value ordering is preserved across policies and when selecting among proposed chunks improves closed-loop return. Across 65 locomotion, navigation, and manipulation tasks, ABI's chunk critic guides weak imitation policies to match or surpass zero-shot RL baselines with high sample efficiency, outperforms existing zero-shot critics on identical proposals, and improves externally trained RL actors without retraining. From raw pixels, ABI delivers substantial guidance gains and surpasses prior zero-shot RL baselines on visual manipulation, highlighting its potential for visuomotor control.





## Self-Supervised Online Robot-Agnostic Traversability Estimation for Open-World Environments
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28442v3
- **Authors**: ['Julia Hindel', 'Simon Bultmann', 'Houman Masnavi', 'Daniele Cattaneo', 'Abhinav Valada']
- **Abstrat**: Self-supervised online traversability estimation enables robots to continuously learn from unlabeled open-world experiences and adapt their navigation behavior toward safe and efficient trajectories. Existing approaches either rely on handcrafted proprioceptive traversability scores, limiting robot-agnosticism, or cluster prior data, preventing online learning. Moreover, many continual learning methods incur substantial memory and computational costs, hindering onboard deployment. We introduce COTRATE, an online learning framework for continuous traversability estimation from multimodal, unlabeled robot experience. Our method first infers robust traversability scores using a robot-agnostic, learning-based online terrain assessment module operating on proprioceptiveand inertial signals. These scores then supervise a visual traversability network through a novel alignment loss that associates visual embeddings with online terrain assessments. To mitigate forgetting during continual learning with minimal overhead, we propose a diversity-aware feature selection strategythat preserves performance using a compact replay memory. We further show that the learned traversability representation supports knowledge transfer across different robot platforms with different locomotion kinematics. We evaluate COTRATE on a dataset of $\approx$ 50,000 images collected with two robotic platforms across 11 outdoor terrains, and benchmark it on navigation tasks in three representative outdoor environments. We make the dataset, code, and trained models publicly available.





## VeriWorld: A Verifiable Visual SWE-Bench for Spatial Reasoning in 3D Environments
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30226v1
- **Authors**: ['Yan Zheng', 'ShengYun Peng', 'Xuyao Liang', 'Xiaoyan Cong', 'Nuo Chen', 'Bangya Liu', 'Zihan Wang', 'Wenyan Cong', 'Zhiwen Fan', 'Zhangyang Wang']
- **Abstrat**: Vision-language models (VLMs) are increasingly used for spatial tasks such as navigation and manipulation, where they must infer geometry and object relationships from visual input in order to act correctly. Existing benchmarks usually test perception and reasoning in isolation, making it hard to tell whether failures come from visual understanding, downstream reasoning, or the bridge between them. We introduce VeriWorld, a benchmark for spatial reasoning in interactive 3D environments with executable actions and deterministic verification. For task families where all three channels are well-defined, we evaluate matched visual, structured, and combined input conditions on the same instance, environment, and verifier; families without a clean visual/structured/combined split are reported separately. This design supports controlled comparisons of perception, reasoning, and their interaction. Using this setup, we uncover two diagnostic findings. First, models often perform much better when task-relevant information, such as object identity, current state, or target location, is given explicitly than when it must be read from the rendered scene, revealing a recurring visual-to-structure gap. Second, action-space and harness design can change outcomes even under the same task and information condition, showing that interaction protocol is itself a confounding variable. VeriWorld is open-sourced to support reproducible and fine-grained evaluation of spatial reasoning in VLMs.





## PathStream: Towards Streaming Diagnostic Copilots for Whole-Slide Pathology

Without additional training, its diagnostic capabilities generalize to new educational pathology videos, three external centers, and an unseen organ.





## DUViN: Diffusion-Based Underwater Visual Navigation via Knowledge-Transferred Depth Features
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02983v2
- **Authors**: ['Jinghe Yang', 'Minh-Quan Le', 'Mingming Gong', 'Ye Pu']
- **Abstrat**: Autonomous underwater navigation remains a challenging problem due to limited sensing capabilities and the difficulty of constructing accurate maps in underwater environments. In this paper, we propose a Diffusion-based Underwater Visual Navigation policy via knowledge-transferred depth features, named DUViN, which enables vision-based end-to-end 4-DoF motion control for underwater vehicles in unknown environments. DUViN guides the vehicle to avoid obstacles and maintain a safe and perception-aware altitude relative to the terrain without relying on pre-built maps. To address the difficulty of collecting large-scale underwater navigation datasets, we propose a method that enables transfer under domain shifts from simulation with clear images to underwater environments by leveraging depth features and introducing a novel model transfer strategy. Specifically, our training framework consists of two phases: we first train the diffusion-based visual navigation policy on a clear image dataset collected in simulation using a pre-trained depth feature extractor. Secondly, we retrain the extractor on an underwater depth estimation task and integrate the adapted extractor into the trained navigation policy from the first phase. Experiments conducted in simulation and in an indoor water tank provide proof-of-concept validation of the effectiveness and sim-to-real transferability of our approach.





## MobileNeTTT: a Lightweight DNN-based Time-To-Transit Estimator for Visual Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29454v1
- **Authors**: ['Chiara Boretti', 'Philippe Bich', 'Manuel Morteo', 'Roberto Tron', 'John Baillieul']
- **Abstrat**: Time-to-Transit (TTT) is a perceptual cue, proxy for depth, that can be computed in the visual cortex, allowing animals to estimate the time remaining to move past features in the environment. We show how TTT can be estimated and used for bio-inspired navigation of a mobile robot equipped with a monocular camera. The paper describes a lightweight neural network estimation method, named MobileNeTTT, that (1) uses the full field of view and provides a rich array of visual feature TTT estimates; (2) is relatively insensitive to camera rotation; and (3) automatically adjusts to the geometry of the path and the presence of obstacles. Because TTT is a coarse visual cue, it is far simpler to use for robot navigation than current large visual depth foundation models. Our navigation model uses a 1.95M-parameter network that is two to three orders of magnitude smaller than well-known large vision foundation models, making it able to run in real time on edge hardware. Simulation and actual laboratory experiments validate the proposed approach in dynamic and spatially complex environments. Code, data, and video are available.





## NavDecision: Parallel Multi-Step Action Prediction with Persistent Self-Referential Learning for Vision-and-Language Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28796v1
- **Authors**: ['Kai Wu', 'Yufan Hu', 'Quan Fang', 'Boning Zhang', 'Junyu Gao']
- **Abstrat**: Vision-and-language navigation (VLN) requires timely decisions based on instructions and visual observations. However, autoregressive VLN models generate action descriptions token by token, introducing substantial inference overhead. Decision models offer an efficient alternative but face challenges in maintaining short-horizon action coherence and long-horizon goal alignment. To address these challenges, we propose NavDecision, an efficient multi-step VLN decision model. ## JevNavAgent: A Single-Forward Visual Navigation Agent with Trajectory-Level RL for Gigapixel Pathology
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28766v1
- **Authors**: ['Yonghuang Wu', 'Zhenyang Liang', 'Pengfei Wang', 'Yize Ma', 'Guoqing Wu', 'Jinhua Yu']
- **Abstrat**: Gigapixel whole-slide images (WSIs) exceed single-pass vision-language model (VLM) capacity at cellular resolution, while multiple instance learning relies on query-agnostic offline patching without interactive reasoning. Multi-step VLM navigation agents inspect regions on demand, yet face two bottlenecks: (1) autoregressive action decoding incurs heavy latency, throttling exploration and online multi-trajectory RL; and (2) offline distillation suffers from an existential-versus-universal stopping asymmetry between positive detection and negative exclusion, inducing thumbnail and kinematic shortcuts while leaving policies prone to passing metastases without stopping. Under the Jev (System One) paradigm, a model predicts distributions over predefined decision spaces in one forward pass (0 generated tokens) while deterministic code executes navigation. We build JevNavAgent, a 2B-parameter agent combining factorized stopping, spatial-candidate, and contextual-bilinear answer heads with overview modality dropout. We pair Stage-1 offline counterfactual trajectory alignment (mitigating depth and panning shortcuts) with Stage-2 online trajectory-level GRPO (Traj-GRPO), using decoding-free rollouts to optimize branch-level lesion discovery, in-polygon stopping, and step-wise confidence gain. On Clean Visual WSI-VQA, JevNavAgent achieves 51.57% multi-scale (50.43% terminal-step) overall and 51.39% focal accuracy, outperforming frontier VLM agents and 4B-11B open-source cascades while running nearly 12x faster than frontier APIs (5.63s vs. 66.64-70.04s). On CAMELYON16 Official Test, Traj-GRPO reaches 22.45% localization (converting 11/12, or 91.7%, of visited tumors into grounded stops) with 93.75% specificity and 69.77% slide accuracy, surpassing Stage-1 SFT under matched stopping rules alongside zero-shot budget scaling.

## WorldWeave: Rethinking Embodied Navigation through Cross-Task World Modeling
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28553v1
- **Authors**: ['Xuan Yao', 'Jiahao Zhang', 'Rongjunchen Zhang', 'Kaixuan Huang', 'Junyu Gao', 'Changsheng Xu']
- **Abstrat**: Mobile embodied agents continuously alter their observations through their own motion, while external dynamics further shape future interactions. Different mobile embodied tasks therefore capture complementary patterns of state evolution, yet jointly learning world dynamics across such interactions remains less explored. We investigate this problem through Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) and Embodied Visual Tracking (EVT), which respectively involve navigation toward a stationary goal and interaction with a moving target. We propose WorldWeave, a cross-task world model that couples shared predictive dynamics with task-specific adaptation. Instead of conditioning state transitions on heterogeneous physical actions, WorldWeave learns latent actions from observed state changes via inverse-forward dynamics, establishing a shared transition representation across tasks. The resulting future-state predictions are adaptively integrated into task-specific representations for downstream decision making. Experiments across multiple VLN and EVT benchmarks demonstrate consistent improvements over specialized baselines.

## Confidently Lost: Silent Failure and Edge Deployment of Dense Visual SLAM under Photon Starvation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30173v1
- **Authors**: ['Shrikrishna Bhagirath Rajule', 'Srinath Neelamagam', 'Aniket Bera']
- **Abstrat**: Dense visual SLAM built on learned two-view geometry is evaluated in good light; in low light it fails silently. Two frames of 9-level quantized noise, no scene at all, give MASt3R-SLAM's network a cross-view geometric self-consistency of 0.936; a real dark frame pair gives 0.935. Starved of structure, the network invents the same geometry for both views and the system trusts the agreement: on a dark sequence it consumes 1,605 frames, emits 7 keyframes, rejects no factor-graph edges and reports full frame rate, while over 95% of the correspondences it accepts violate the ground-truth epipolar geometry. One inflated statistic is read by two subsystems against thresholds in opposite directions, so keyframing starves while relocalization accepts almost every candidate. The same statistic, read at keyframe-pair baselines, separates the confident collapse from working runs on the ETH3D dark sequences without ground truth or extra computation (in-sample). Low-light-adapted weights take tracked trajectories from 1/6 to 5/6, as does retuning two thresholds; neither yields accurate ones, because the adaptation withdraws confidence from wrong geometry rather than finding right geometry. Replayed on a Jetson AGX Orin carried by a legged robot, the mechanism's signature reproduces on the two darkest of four routes, and which of its faces appears varies with route geometry and sensor encoding. The network does not lose confidence in the dark; it manufactures it.

## WorldLeague: Benchmarking Physical Agency
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30989v1
- **Authors**: ['Xinyang Zhou', 'Qi Li', 'Xinchao Wang']
- **Abstrat**: AI systems can now operate Blender, follow instructions in complex video games, and manipulate objects with robot arms. What range of physical goals can today's AI systems achieve autonomously? Answering this question requires testing systems on tasks that demand different combinations of perception, memory, planning, and action. We introduce \textbf{WorldLeague}, a benchmark that compares AI systems on a common set of \textbf{60 simulated robot tasks} by measuring progress toward each task's physical goal from the robot's trajectory. Robots explore scenes to gather evidence in \textbf{WorldSeek}, rearrange objects in \textbf{WorldShape}, and adapt to changes in \textbf{WorldShift}. Fable leads in four dimensions, while GPT-6.1-Sol and GPT-6-Astra tie for first on rule-following tasks. Even when VLMs answer every final question correctly, they receive an Interactive Score of zero in 71.8\% of those episodes. WorldLeague gives Embodied AI, Physical AI, and Agentic AI a shared basis for comparing what AI systems achieve autonomously, from partial progress toward a physical goal to full goal completion. ## EnvTrap: Revealing the Environment-Only Attack Surface in Embodied AI via Consequence-Blind Action Execution
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30004v1
