## VITAL: From Visual Motion to Precise Action
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20604v1
- **Authors**: ['Ao Liang', 'Youquan Liu', 'Mingxiao Li', 'Wei Chow', 'Lingdong Kong', 'Yue Liao', 'Dongyue Lu', 'Yu Yang', 'Wei Yin', 'Runnan Chen']
- **Abstrat**: World-action models aim to turn visual anticipation into driving decisions, yet typically train action experts on trajectory representations defined independently of visual dynamics. This motivates deriving action targets directly from visual motion. We first examine a simpler setting in which trajectories are reconstructed from recorded future images. Even without the need for future prediction, reconstruction captures the dominant maneuver but leaves centimeter-scale errors. We therefore introduce VITAL (Vision-Induced Tokens for Action Learning), an action tokenizer that couples a visually derived motion scaffold with a compact residual encoding the trajectory error left by that scaffold. This representation makes visual motion the principal prediction target and gives small metric corrections a separate, normalized learning scale. Using flow matching, the action expert jointly predicts both components from the current image, ego state, and navigation command, without generating future images at inference. The same residual coordinates support bounded reinforcement post-training that refines execution while keeping the predicted visual scaffold fixed. With only one action denoising step, VITAL achieves 90.8 PDMS on NAVSIM v1 navtest, within 0.13 points of its ten-step score at 3.29x the end-to-end inference speed. Without retraining, it achieves 91.1 EPDMS on NAVSIM v2 navtest and 38.6 on navhard. Project page: https://worldbench.github.io/vital





## HyperLoop: Hyperspectral-Assisted Loop Closure Detection in 3D-SLAM for Planetary Exploration
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19742v1
- **Authors**: ['Annie Bhalla', 'Dennis Rotondi', 'Riccardo Giubilato', 'Kai O. Arras', 'Wolfgang Stuerzl']
- **Abstrat**: Autonomous navigation of planetary rovers is challenged by visually repetitive, featureless terrain. SLAM systems based on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) offer dense mapping but rely on RGB appearance descriptors that degrade under perceptual aliasing - a common failure mode in extraterrestrial environments where physically distinct locations appear visually identical. This paper presents HyperLoop, the first framework integrating hyperspectral imaging (HSI) into the loop closure module of a 3DGS-based SLAM system. Hyperspectral descriptors encode material-level spectral signatures complementing RGB features, providing robustness to illumination changes and visual repetitiveness. Two HSI descriptor families are introduced: Bag-of-Spectral-Words (BoSW), encoding spectral material distributions via TF-IDF weighted histograms, and PCA-K VLAD (PCAK-VLAD), capturing first-order residual statistics of PCA-compressed spectral signatures. Both are evaluated with SIFT-VLAD and NetVLAD RGB descriptors under Strict-AND and Weighted fusion strategies on a custom dataset from the Moon-Mars Outdoor Test Site (MMOTS) at DLR, comprising synchronized RGB-D and hyperspectral sequences across four planetary analog traverses (65.5 m total). A key finding is that the sensor's Red-NIR range (600-860 nm) provides limited discriminability for rock terrain: the best HSI fusion (SIFT-VLAD + BoSW, Strict-AND) achieves 5.04 cm ATE RMSE, within 1.8% of SIFT-VLAD alone (4.95 cm). The gain over NetVLAD (8.81 cm) is attributable to SIFT-VLAD, not HSI fusion. This motivates future adoption of SWIR sensors capturing diagnostic mineral absorption features absent from the 600-860 nm range for planetary SLAM missions.





## Cracking Mobile Manipulation: From Failure Diagnosis to Agentic Robot System
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19600v1
- **Authors**: ['Zixuan Wang', 'Jia-Ning Li', 'Yinzhou Tang', 'Yanzhao Yu', 'Biao Wu', 'Meng Fang', 'Xin Li', 'Yiwu Zhong']
- **Abstrat**: Recent vision-language-action (VLA) models have shown strong performance on local robot manipulation, yet their success does not readily translate to long-horizon mobile manipulation. In our experiments, a pretrained subtask VLA achieves 80% success rate on atomic manipulation tasks but only 19% when these skills are composed into mobile manipulation. We argue that this gap stems from failures across the execution process rather than from local manipulation capability alone. To uncover these bottlenecks, we systematically diagnose failures in long-horizon mobile manipulation and identify three dominant regimes: task planning, navigation and base positioning, and local manipulation. Based on this diagnosis, we develop failure-guided agentic intervention that augments a fixed subtask VLA with demonstration-based planning guidance, adaptive visual memory, accessibility-aware base adjustment, and local end-effector adjustment. On RoboCasa365, these interventions progressively improve task success from 19% to 47%, with the finalized system reaching 49% on held-out episode seeds, compared with 18% for the baseline. The same design improves performance from 14% to 37% on BEHAVIOR-1K and from 5/20 to 13/20 on real-robot tasks. These results suggest that reliable mobile manipulation can be advanced by diagnosing and intervening on the current bottleneck, rather than uniformly scaling the underlying policy. Our code will be released at https://github.com/PKU-VaLuE-Lab/Cracking-Mobile-Manipulation.git.





## GS-PRM: Gaussian Visual Roadmap for Image-Goal Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19541v1
- **Authors**: ['Ashraf Siddique', 'Kyung Min Han', 'Young J. Kim']
- **Abstrat**: Image-goal navigation requires a global planner that connects visually specified endpoints while providing observations that a local policy can follow. Three-dimensional Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) provides map-registered RGB, opacity, and depth renderings, but does not explicitly encode navigable topology. We introduce \method, a Gaussian visual roadmap whose milestones, local connections, and visual references are constructed directly through 3DGS rendering without deriving an occupancy grid or signed distance field. We define render-based collision-free (RC-free) configurations and paths. Panoramic depth supplies conservative clearance for milestone validation and adaptive coverage, while edge-aligned orthographic rendering tests the robot's swept volume. We establish conditional soundness and sufficient conditions for deterministic query completeness and probabilistic construction completeness of GS-PRM. To validate the performance of GS-PRM, we conduct experiments in both simulated and real-world environments, demonstrating long-horizon navigation using goal-conditioned policies guided by GS-PRM. Compared with a state-of-the-art 3DGS-based navigation system, our GS-PRM-based navigation system achieves up to a twice higher average success rate and reduces travel time by up to 57.2% when both systems complete the navigation task.





## More Than Seeing: Vision as Scalable Anchors in Native Multimodal Pre-Training
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19443v1
- **Authors**: ['Haoyuan Wu', 'Aoqi Wu', 'Jiani Hu', 'Bei Yu']
- **Abstrat**: Native multimodal pre-training trains a single model from scratch on text and multimodal data, enabling it to understand both language and visual inputs. However, how visual signals shape scaling behavior and support capabilities beyond visual understanding remains unclear. We investigate this question through controlled visual-content ablations across model sizes and training budgets. Specifically, we replace image embeddings with content-free padding token embeddings. Retaining visual content improves text prediction on image-text examples without materially changing the lowest text-only loss at a given compute budget, the optimal balance of model size and training data, or aggregate language scores. The relative multimodal loss benefit grows approximately as a power law of compute. To test benefits beyond understanding images, we evaluate spatial reasoning from text descriptions alone. Both conditions improve with model and data scaling. During training of the largest model, visual gains are most sustained in localization, navigation, and symbolic planning, which involve maintaining object configurations or composing spatial updates; gains in spatial relations, geometry, and physical commonsense are less consistent. These findings identify visual content as a scalable anchor for multimodal learning, with benefits that extend beyond visual inputs while preserving aggregate language performance.





## A VR Training System for Robot-Assisted Vascular Intervention with Dual-Mode Control and Model-Based Interaction Response

## MMA-SafetyBench: A Benchmark for Multimodal Agent Safety Evaluation

- **Abstrat**: Multimodal agents increasingly rely on visual interfaces to perceive state and select actions, creating an attack surface not captured by text-only safety evaluations. We study Visual Semantic Hijacking, in which deceptive but semantically meaningful interface elements, such as forged system alerts, cause an agent to depart from its authorized task. We propose a trajectory-guided procedure that identifies candidate injection points and inserts reproducible visual payloads into the observation stream. Controls covering all nine models and five domains show that attacks persist under neutral prompts. A safety-priority prompt lowers ASR without eliminating the failure mode.





## Learning Keypoint Constraints from Visual Demonstrations for Safe Motion Planning
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19295v1
- **Authors**: ['Zheng Qiu', 'Zhouyu Zhang', 'Ninghan Zhong', 'Glen Chou']
- **Abstrat**: We present Keypoint Constraint Learning (KCL), a framework for learning task-relevant motion constraints from visual demonstrations and for planning safely with the extracted constraints. To make constraint learning tractable from RGB-D observations, we represent demonstrations using sparse 3D object-centric keypoints and semantic mask centroids extracted through segmentation, visual feature clustering, and point tracking. In this reduced geometric space, we learn keypoint dynamics and infer distance- and orientation-based constraints that make the expert trajectories feasible and approximately locally optimal by enforcing KKT optimality conditions in the reduced state space. We then plan with the learned constraints under uncertainty, using robustness margins derived from accumulated prediction error, and provide a theoretical analysis of how keypoint perception error and learned dynamics error affect the recovered constraints. We evaluate KCL on visual demonstrations from navigation to rigid/deformable manipulation, showing that it recovers multi-stage geometric constraints and outperforms baselines in downstream safe planning.





## End-to-End Vision-Language-Action Policies for Autonomous Lunar Rover Docking
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19224v1
- **Authors**: ['Sitan Huang', 'Grace Gao']
- **Abstrat**: Surface navigation on the Moon is subject to extreme illumination contrast and deployment-time perceptual disturbances. While modular navigation pipelines have long supported planetary rover navigation, robustness is compromised by measurement errors that propagate through hand-engineered components. End-to-end learned policies are an alternative approach to navigation, which maps observations directly to control actions without intermediate components. Vision-language-action models, in particular, have driven significant breakthroughs in robotics and autonomous vehicle research. By leveraging internet-scale vision-language pretraining, these models can be exploited for their commonsense reasoning and visual understanding to provide robust generalization with limited task-specific data. However, their applicability and performance have yet to be characterized for lunar surface operations. In this study, we design a vision-language-action policy for autonomous lunar rover docking and characterize its performance relative to a modular navigation pipeline. We develop a scalable training framework that generates safety-aware expert demonstrations in a high-fidelity lunar simulation environment. In closed-loop evaluations, our policy performs competitively relative to the modular baseline under perceptual disturbances unseen during training. To address the tradeoff between compute consumption and closed-loop safety, the policy adapts its execution horizon by determining how far the rover may safely travel in open loop. Adaptive-horizon execution reduces inference frequency while limiting hazard-contact rates relative to fixed horizons in the evaluated scenarios.





## RingNav: Task-Shaped Circular Memory for Visual Heading
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19044v1
- **Authors**: ['Naitri Rajyaguru', 'Aryan Tajne', 'Stephen Jardim', 'Cornelia Fermuller', 'Yiannis Aloimonos']
- **Abstrat**: Tiny robots have limited computation and memory, yet visual navigation systems often maintain more state than a task requires. We ask whether persistent memory can instead be shaped by the task. We present \textbf{RingNav}, a \(10.6\)k--parameter visual heading estimator that constrains its memory to the circular manifold \(S^1\). RingNav learns only the turn between consecutive images, while heading is composed directly on the circle. On the TartanAir Visual SLAM Challenge, RingNav reduces mean absolute turn error by \(27\%\) and accumulated heading error over $4$ sec by \(15\%\) relative to a similarly sized learned-memory model. The structured update also gives exactly zero turn for identical frames and exact cancellation under sequence reversal. Without fine-tuning, RingNav transfers to real-world drone experiments and continues estimating heading under severe visual degradation. RingNav runs at $27.3$Hz on Raspberry Pi Zero 2W and can be deployed on an ultra-low power GAP8 processor at $33.4$Hz.

## VIGOR-MPC: Vision-Guided Objective Regulation MPC
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20333v1
- **Authors**: ['Jelena Trisovic', 'Rahel Rickenbach', 'Marek Landert', 'Panagiotis Minos', 'Melanie N. Zeilinger']
- **Abstrat**: Controlling an agent in an environment solely perceived through onboard vision is challenging: perception yields noisy, variable-length, and partially occluded context, while onboard compute is shared between perception and control, limiting the planning horizon executable in real time. We propose VIGOR-MPC, a modular student-teacher framework, agnostic to the perception pipeline, which addresses reference tracking across navigation tasks by distilling a privileged, field-of-view-aware long-horizon MPC into a computationally lightweight short-horizon MPC whose cost parameters are determined online from vision. The teacher operates in simulation with full state knowledge and a multi-trajectory formulation that guarantees the agent can stay within its field of view. The student observes only the noisy, occluded context produced by a perception module and learns, via a multi-level recurrent mapping, to infer cost parameters that aim to recover the teacher's behavior despite missing information. We evaluate VIGOR-MPC on vision-based autonomous racing in simulation and on hardware. Using only onboard perception of the track, VIGOR-MPC generalizes to tracks unseen during training and approaches the lap times of the privileged long-horizon teacher while reducing per-step computation by over 90%.

## Remember, Revisit, Revise: Scaffold Memory for Long-Horizon 3D Geometry
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19674v1
- **Authors**: ['Soumick Sarker', 'Mengmi Zhang']
- **Abstrat**: Geometry foundation models estimate camera poses and 3D scene structure from video sequences, but maintaining consistent reconstructions over long horizons remains challenging. Feed-forward models process long sequences as independent, fixed-length frame windows and align their predictions, causing pose and reconstruction errors to accumulate across windows. Streaming models retain information from past frames to inform future predictions, but cannot revise earlier estimates when later observations reveal errors. To address this, we introduce Scaffold MemOry for Revision (SMORE), a framework equipping frozen geometry foundation models with a revisable external memory for long-horizon inference. Inspired by hippocampal associative memory in biological brains, which links spatial context with sensory experience, SMORE associates where an observer is with what it observes, enabling past observations to be stored, retrieved, verified against new evidence, and revised when inconsistencies are detected. We evaluate SMORE with eight competitive geometry models on five datasets spanning camera pose estimation, multi-view depth estimation, 3D point-cloud reconstruction, simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM), and novel view synthesis. Across all evaluated models, SMORE consistently improves long-sequence pose estimation, reduces multi-view depth error by up to 58%, reduces Chamfer distance for 3D point-cloud reconstruction by up to 43%, and achieves average trajectory error as low as 2.9 cm in long-horizon SLAM. By retaining corrected poses and observations, SMORE further improves the fidelity and spatial consistency of large-scale 3D scene reconstruction. These results establish revisable external memory as a flexible interface for extending pretrained geometry models beyond fixed-context prediction toward iterative, error-correcting inference over long visual sequences.

## Limits of Statistical Assurance for Embodied and Agentic AI under Adaptive Adversaries
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18948v1
- **Authors**: ['Inderjeet Singh']
- **Abstrat**: Finite safety assessments cannot exclude an adversary that preserves unattacked behavior until certification and activates afterward. We characterize this limitation for embodied and tool-use AI. An adversary that matches the complete observation law until observable, irrevocable certification and retains intervention access afterward attains $v\mathbb{E}[(n-S_0)_+]$ expected post-certification events over $n$ trials, where $v$ is the active event probability and $S_0$ the unattacked certification time. The result covers adaptive and privately randomized evaluators. Switching at certification is exactly optimal for a fixed likelihood-ratio test under memoryless, event-increasing interventions without a binding intervention budget. A positive clean-certification requirement forces linear worst-case expected excess when intervention raises event rates over an authorized deployment horizon, even if certification is deferred to the deadline. In simulated RoCo collaboration, switching yields commanded-separation violations in 34 of 36 post-certification episodes. Preregistered randomized AgentDojo trials estimate 1.98 additional security violations per four-task continuation (95% interval [1.67, 2.29]). An adversarial language-model controller infers hidden certification from noisy evaluation receipts and times intervention accordingly. With permanent revocation, a mixture likelihood-ratio monitor bounds cumulative excess above a prespecified reference rate at square-root order, up to logarithmic factors, on every sample path. A single fixed alternative can permit linear expected excess under stationary attacks. Against a controller observing the monitor's rule and statistic, live mixture monitoring reduces violations from 12.00 to 3.81 per 24 assigned task opportunities. Revocation also reduces tasks executed.

## Tiered-Resolution Volumetric Mapping for Real-Time Simultaneous Planning and Mapping on Natural Outdoor Environments
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20476v1
- **Authors**: ['Afonso E. Carvalho', 'David Portugal', 'Paulo Peixoto', 'George Kantor']
- **Abstrat**: Real-time volumetric mapping for a planning robot is dominated by the cost of integrating and storing information at the finest resolution, yet only a small fraction of any map ever lies on or near a path the planner actually considers. We exploit this with a tiered-resolution mapping mechanism that integrates LiDAR into a single map structure at two resolutions simultaneously: fine inside a corridor around the robot's current plan, and coarse everywhere else. As opposed to traditional navigation pipelines where path planning uses the mapping result and provides no feedback, we propose a feedback loop where planning also informs mapping to dynamically integrate information at different leaf resolutions. Across two real-world teleoperated datasets, replayed and scored offline against an independent full-resolution map, the tiered maps are 13-15x smaller and require 6-8x less integration CPU time than a uniform fine map, while producing paths at a similar cost per meter as the ones generated from fine resolution maps on both scenarios. A uniform coarse map reaches the same size savings but its path quality collapses (+210% cost per meter) on the harder scenario, because it over-discretizes terrain the robot must traverse. Tiering approximates the coarse map's cheapness without its failure mode.

## Learning Navigation from Multimodal Demonstrations with Geometric Candidate Modes
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20241v1
- **Authors**: ['Chengyang Peng', 'Zhihao Zhang', 'Upama Pant', 'Yiming Wang', 'Keith A. Redmill', 'Ayonga Hereid']
- **Abstrat**: Offline navigation demonstrations are multimodal, because one observation often allows several valid actions, such as passing an obstacle on either side. Behavior cloning averages these actions, potentially leading to unsafe routes. Multimodal policies must discover the alternatives from the data, but similar states can lead to different routes whose current actions overlap. In navigation, the alternatives have a geometric cause and can be computed from one observation. We propose a hierarchical neural-network policy that computes candidate modes from local geometry and learns from demonstrations which mode to follow and how. A high-level policy selects a mode using the goal and its previous choice among a set of candidate modes, and a low-level policy turns it into an action. In simulation, the policy succeeds in over 95\% of seen and unseen test cases, compared with at most 43\% and 20\% for four offline learning baselines and 79\% and 67\% for a heuristic that uses the same modes without learning. ## Schrödinger's Navigator: Imagining an Ensemble of Futures for Zero-Shot Object Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21201v4
- **Authors**: ['Yu He', 'Da Huang', 'Zhenyang Liu', 'Zixiao Gu', 'Qiang Sun', 'Guangnan Ye', 'Yanwei Fu', 'Yu-Gang Jiang']
- **Abstrat**: Zero-shot object navigation (ZSON) requires robots to find target objects in unseen environments without task-specific fine-tuning or pre-built maps, a key capability for general-purpose service robots. Yet methods that perform well in simulation often degrade in cluttered real-world scenes with severe occlusion and latent hazards, where large unseen regions make single-scene inference brittle and unsafe. We propose Schrödinger's Navigator, a belief-aware framework that reasons at inference time over multiple trajectory-conditioned imagined 3D futures.
