## VLAN: Vision-Language Accessible Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18346v1
- **Authors**: ['Jie Hu', 'Yu Zheng', 'Wenzhi Wu', 'Weixing Feng', 'Cunlei Cheng', 'Jiaming Zhang']
- **Abstrat**: Accessible mobility is user-conditioned rather than scene-intrinsic, as walkable paths for one embodiment are inaccessible to another. Yet, existing benchmarks evaluate traversability under a fixed and limited embodiment. We present Vision-Language Accessible Navigation (VLAN), containing 1,000 scenes, 20K navigation trajectories, and 160K keyframes across diverse lighting, environmental conditions, scene semantics, and path geometries. For each trajectory, VLAN provides embodiment-specific actions and accessibility features for legged and wheeled agents, including humanoid and quadruped robots and people with visual or mobility impairments, while everyday obstacles and accessibility labels are categorized as Passable, Bypass, HighRisk, and Blocked. Unlike goal-only evaluation, our benchmark measures physical safety and alignment with user-specific constraints via goal-reaching, collision-aware, and accessibility-aligned metrics. We benchmark modern vision-language models and find they often fail to consistently adapt obstacle relevance, path selection, and safety criteria across user profiles, suggesting accessibility-aware navigation requires dedicated datasets, tasks, and metrics beyond conventional embodied navigation evaluation. All data and code open-source at https://github.com/InSAI-Lab/VLAN.

## From AI-Enabled Remote Patient Monitoring Toward Agentic Robotics: Embodiment, Governance, and Care Integration
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18484v1
- **Authors**: ['Ivana Ragusa', 'Lorenzo Guiducci', 'Filippo Cavallo']
- **Abstrat**: AI-enabled remote patient monitoring is evolving beyond passive data collection toward agentic AI systems that interpret patient states, coordinate care, and initiate action across domestic and clinical settings. This structured scoping review finds that mature agentic care has not yet emerged: Most systems remain human led, while stronger decision-action coupling and embodied AI remain largely confined to prototypes and pilots. Healthcare robotics makes the transition from monitoring to action especially visible by translating AI-generated recommendations and decisions into speech, movement, prompting, physical assistance, escalation, and care coordination, making embodiment, clinical integration, human oversight, and governance fundamental design requirements. Further progress requires safe and interoperable care architectures in which intelligent components operate through clinically validated and accountable workflows. We propose agentic robotics as a future direction for supervised systems embedded in the care ecosystem, connecting AI agents and robots with clinicians, caregivers, and patients through auditable workflows that can be interrupted and remain subject to human oversight and explicit AI governance.





## Vision-language models lag human performance on physical dynamics and intent reasoning
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01547v3
- **Authors**: ['Tianjun Gu', 'Jingyu Gong', 'Zhizhong Zhang', 'Yuan Xie', 'Lizhuang Ma', 'Xin Tan', 'Athanasios V. Vasilakos']
- **Abstrat**: Spatial intelligence is central to embodied cognition, yet contemporary AI systems still struggle to reason about physical interactions in open-world human environments. Despite strong performance on controlled benchmarks, vision-language models often fail to jointly model physical dynamics, reference frames, and the latent human intentions that drive spatial change. We introduce Teleo-Spatial Intelligence (TSI), which captures an important aspect of spatial reasoning: integrating physical dynamics and action goals to reason about actions and their spatial consequences. To evaluate TSI, we present EscherVerse, a large-scale open-world resource built from 11,328 real-world videos, including an 8,000-example benchmark and a 35,963-example instruction-tuning set. Across 27 state-of-the-art vision-language models and an independent analysis of first-pass human responses from 11 annotators, we identify a persistent teleo-spatial reasoning gap: the strongest proprietary model achieves 57.26% overall accuracy, far below first-pass human performance, which ranges from 84.81% to 95.14% with a mean of 90.62%. Fine-tuning on real-world, intent-aware data narrows this gap for open-weight models, but does not close it. EscherVerse provides a diagnostic testbed for purpose-aware spatial reasoning and highlights a critical gap between pattern recognition and human-level understanding in embodied AI.





## D-VLA: A High-Concurrency Distributed Asynchronous Reinforcement Learning Framework for Vision-Language-Action Models
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13276v3
- **Authors**: ['Yucheng Guo', 'Yongjian Guo', 'Zhong Guan', 'Wen Huang', 'Haodong Yue', 'Shuai Di', 'Junwu Xiong', 'Yicheng Gong', 'Xi Xiao']
- **Abstrat**: The rapid evolution of Embodied AI has enabled Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to excel in multimodal perception and task execution. However, applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) to these massive models in large-scale distributed environments faces severe systemic bottlenecks, primarily due to the resource conflict between high-fidelity physical simulation and the intensive VRAM/bandwidth demands of deep learning. This conflict often leaves overall throughput constrained by execution-phase inefficiencies. To address these challenges, we propose D-VLA, a high-concurrency, low-latency distributed RL framework for large-scale embodied foundation models. D-VLA introduces "Plane Decoupling," physically isolating high-frequency training data from low-frequency weight control to eliminate interference between simulation and optimization. We further design a four-thread asynchronous "Swimlane" pipeline, enabling full parallel overlap of sampling, inference, gradient computation, and parameter distribution. Additionally, a dual-pool VRAM management model and topology-aware replication resolve memory fragmentation and optimize communication efficiency. Experiments on benchmarks like LIBERO show that D-VLA significantly outperforms mainstream RL frameworks in throughput and sampling efficiency for billion-parameter VLA models. In trillion-parameter scalability tests, our framework maintains exceptional stability and linear speedup, providing a robust system for high-performance general-purpose embodied agents.

## Vision-Language-Action Models: A Life-Cycle Survey of Design, Training and Deployment
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18372v1
- **Authors**: ['Suvarna Kadam', 'Soran Parsa']
- **Abstrat**: Vision-language-action (VLA) models adapt web-pretrained vision-language models to output robot actions, so that web knowledge carries over to physical control. In under 4 years the field has moved from RT-2, the first VLA, to open models that fine-tune on a single consumer GPU and to proprietary generalists that clean unseen homes. This survey reviews 88 works from December 2022 to October 2026, mainly on manipulation and navigation, plus humanoid control, world-action models and evaluation. We follow a VLA through its life cycle, from model design to training and deployment, along seven axes. Model design covers (a) inputs and memory, what the model observes; (b) architecture, how its pipeline is built; (c) action output, how it produces motor commands; and (d) intermediate predictions, what else it predicts, from reasoning steps to future observations. Training covers (e) pretraining data, the mix of robot, web, human and simulated data, and (f) post-training, adaptation to the target robot. Deployment covers (g) real-time execution, keeping pace with the robot. We analyse 22 models on these axes and report their training data, model size, inference speed and hardware. Three trends emerge: (i) training data has broadened, from about 10,000 hours of teleoperated robot data ($π_0$) to 23,000 community-collected episodes (SmolVLA) and 20,000 hours of egocentric human video (GR00T N1.7); (ii) model sizes have split: most recent models have 3-7 billion parameters, while a new group under one billion runs on consumer GPUs; and (iii) generalist VLAs are emerging that handle both manipulation and navigation, such as UniVLA and Qwen-VLA. We also compare VLAs with world-action models, which predict future observations as well as actions. We review evidence that some VLAs with near-perfect benchmark scores fail when objects or the camera move slightly, and open up further research questions.

## Long-Distance Real-World Navigation of the Legged-Wheeled Robot Go2-W Using Deep Reinforcement Learning
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21387v2
- **Authors**: ['Takaaki Matsuzawa', 'Kiyoshi Irie', 'Tomoaki Yoshida', 'Taro Suzuki', 'Yoshitaka Hara', 'Masahiro Tomono']
- **Abstrat**: Legged-wheeled robots have been extensively studied for their potential to combine the efficient flat-ground mobility of wheels with the rough-terrain adaptability of legs. However, their application to long-distance autonomous navigation in real environments remains limited. This paper reports our efforts to build a deep reinforcement learning-based locomotion controller and an autonomous navigation system for the commercially available Go2-W legged-wheeled robot. The study focuses on the integrated system and the practical challenges associated with its sustained operation over long distances in real-world environments. For locomotion control, we extended our proprioception-only policy, previously developed for quadruped robots, to Go2-W, a 16-degree-of-freedom legged-wheeled robot. We additionally found that wheeled locomotion concentrates the load on the hip joints, resulting in heat concentration that hinders sustained travel. To address this issue, we developed a policy with periodic foot-lifting motion that distributes the joint loads and mitigates heat concentration. We evaluated the integrated system at the Tsukuba Challenge 2025. The robot autonomously traversed an approximately \SI{2.8}{\kilo\meter} route that included sidewalks, a park, and stairs without stopping owing to overheating.





## Per-Agent-Safe Navigation of Globally Driven Robot Collectives via Affine Field Design
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17859v1
- **Authors**: ['Seunghwan Jang', 'Steven Ceron', 'Wei Xiao']
- **Abstrat**: Safe navigation of robot collectives requires coordinating motion and deformation so that the collective can traverse constrained environments while ensuring each robot's safety. Existing controllers for swarmalator collectives largely rely on local control, where each robot receives an individual command, which causes the control and communication requirements to grow with the number of agents. We introduce a broadcast control formulation in which a single spatially affine field governs both the motion and deformation of the collective. The scalar component of the field regulates collective size, and its spatial gradient generates collective translation through non-reciprocal interactions. Both components enter the collective dynamics directly, which allows per-robot wall, actuation, and cohesion constraints to be enforced using control barrier functions within a single quadratic program for the whole collective. The resulting controller continuously adapts the collective size to the local environment, and enables safe passage through corridors narrower than the unconstrained collective without pre-planned resizing. We further derive how the available actuation budget determines the reachable speed and minimum passable corridor width, and we demonstrate that two independently driven collectives can simultaneously traverse a shared junction using distinct fixed phase labels without inter-collective coordination.





## ERA: Error-Resistant Autonomous Object Navigation with Predicted Depth
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17730v1
- **Authors**: ['Shunyang Huang', 'Shuo Wang', 'Wenyuan Xie', 'Mingyu Lin', 'Yue Ding', 'Hongtao Lu']
- **Abstrat**: Depth and 3D foundation models can predict geometry from RGB images, allowing zero-shot object navigation without a depth sensor. However, when predicted depth is mapped as if it came from a sensor, view-dependent errors accumulate in the map, remove exploration goals, and end the search before the agent can observe and correct them. We propose ERA, a training-free object navigation system that replaces the depth sensor with depth predicted from RGB images and poses. ERA follows a paradigm we call Move with Error: movement provides new views that correct map errors, and the corrected map enables further movement. It realizes this paradigm with revisable occupancy and with exploration from reachable viewpoints, which continues the search when no frontier remains, and verifies the target across views before stopping. On three benchmarks, ERA achieves state-of-the-art performance among training-free methods without depth sensors. Its map and exploration components also raise VLFM's success from 10.8% to 61.7% under the same predicted depth. We further deploy ERA on a real robot.





## X-Nav Technical Report: Real-World Long-Horizon Indoor-to-Outdoor Navigation on a Humanoid Robot
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17598v1
- **Authors**: ['Jiaxin Huang', 'Chengxing Lin', 'Xiaoyu Luo', 'Sirui Chen', 'Jingyu Guo', 'Aolin Li', 'Kairui Chen', 'Rui Zhen', 'Cheng Wen', 'Kerui Gu', 'Shunyao Wang', 'Zhihui Peng', 'Shaoli Huang']
- **Abstrat**: Long-horizon navigation from indoors to outdoors remains an open problem for humanoid robots: one instruction covers rooms, doors and roads, and the camera moves with the gait. Worken, AGIBOT's measure of productive robot work, depends on robots that reach their workplaces autonomously. We present X-Nav, to our knowledge the first system to take a bipedal humanoid through a language-instructed long-horizon mission from indoors to outdoors. X-Nav covers a pipeline from data to deployment. Its data engine collects navigation data in open-source scenes, in digital twins of deployment sites and on real robots. Its navigation model, MELIOR, takes only RGB images from the robot's built-in camera and comes in two versions: MELIOR-A predicts actions, and MELIOR-N predicts a navigation goal. Its agentic workflow, X-Navarch, turns a brief human instruction into sub-tasks and deploys MELIOR on the robot with explicit target navigation and obstacle avoidance. On the Lingxi X2, X-Nav completes a mission of about a hundred metres from an office lobby to a store in a neighbouring building; LiDAR and odometry are used for target approach and low-level control. The mission is completed with both versions of MELIOR: the robot follows a change of destination and walks through a door from the lobby to the road, and with X-Navarch it also passes static and dynamic obstacles on the route. During deployment, MELIOR is adapted with data collected in a digital twin of the site; the adaptation raises its closed-loop success rate in the twin, and the adapted model is then deployed on the robot at the real site. In addition, MELIOR achieves competitive results on the R2R-CE and RxR-CE benchmarks.





## Racing the Clock: Teaching VLNs Budget-Conditioned Object Search
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17442v1
- **Authors**: ['Maitree Meher', 'Siming He', 'S. Shankar Sastry', 'Jitendra Malik', 'Claire Tomlin', 'Jonas Frey']
- **Abstrat**: A rescue robot entering a building with thirty seconds on the clock should sprint toward the single most promising room; given ten minutes, it should instead explore a broader tour. Yet vision-language-navigation (VLN) models are typically evaluated under a fixed episode limit that the policy never observes, even though time-critical missions require behavior to adapt to the available budget. We study budget-conditioned object search: given a target category and an explicit action budget, an agent must find as many distinct target instances as possible before the budget expires. We investigate how to supervise VLNs for this task by comparing a privileged TSP expert, which plans near-optimal routes using the full map and true target locations, with a room-level Bellman expert that maximizes expected return under partial observability using high-level cross-scene semantic priors. Despite the TSP expert achieving substantially higher expert performance, its demonstrations are less effective for learning exploration: on held-out HM3D scenes, the Bellman-supervised VLN consistently outperforms the TSP-supervised VLN, with the advantage becoming more pronounced at longer budgets. At 2000 steps (500m), the Bellman-supervised VLN finds 71.4% of reachable target instances, compared with 65.5% for the TSP-supervised VLN and 44.8% for the pretrained VLN. These results highlight a distinction between optimal routing and optimal exploration: effective exploration supervision should optimize decisions under partial observability rather than imitate routes that are optimal only once the environment is fully known.





## Real-Time Diffusion Relighting as a Neural Sensor: Closing the Low-Light Domain Gap for Robotic Perception
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17438v1
- **Authors**: ['Shengjie Xu', 'Alexander Gao', 'Anshul Rai', 'Dae Yeol Lee', 'Guan-Ming Su', 'Ming C. Lin']
- **Abstrat**: Low-light conditions severely degrade robotic perception: object detectors trained on daylight imagery are prone to failure under dark and adverse weather conditions that fall outside of their training distribution. Furthermore, from an efficiency standpoint, existing Low-Light Image Enhancement (LLIE) methods are designed for offline processing and are therefore impractical to integrate into live control loops. To address these issues, we propose to embed a Generative Neural Sensor within the control loop that treats illumination as a generative prior rather than a signal processing problem, enabling task-relevant semantic recovery. We further propose an Real-Time Perceptual Architecture that decouples high-latency semantic recovery from low-latency robotic control. Rather than blocking the control loop, our system runs a large-scale image-to-image diffusion model in a background thread, and is activated only when a drop in detection confidence signals a perceptual uncertainty event, significantly reducing computational overhead. Our method sets a new state-of-the-art performance on the ExDark low-light benchmark with 87.1% mAP, and under adverse weather conditions, significantly boosts detection rates over competing methods. On a navigation task in a closed-loop autonomous driving simulator (CARLA), we achieve a 100% lap completion rate, establishing a simulation-based proof-of-concept for integrating high-latency generative models with standard robotic control autonomy stacks.

## STITCHER: Constrained Trajectory Planning in Complex Environments with Real-Time Motion Primitive Search
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14893v5
- **Authors**: ['Helene J. Levy', 'Brett T. Lopez']
- **Abstrat**: Autonomous high-speed navigation through large, complex environments requires real-time generation of agile trajectories that are dynamically feasible, collision-free, and satisfy state or actuator constraints. Modern trajectory planning techniques primarily use numerical optimization, as they enable the systematic computation of high-quality, expressive trajectories that satisfy various constraints. However, stringent requirements on computation time and the risk of numerical instability can limit the use of optimization-based planners in safety-critical scenarios. This work presents an optimization-free planning framework called STITCHER that stitches short trajectory segments together with graph search to compute long-range, expressive, and near-optimal trajectories in real-time. STITCHER outperforms modern optimization-based planners through our innovative planning architecture and several algorithmic developments that make real-time planning possible. Extensive simulation testing is performed to analyze the algorithmic components that make up STITCHER, along with a thorough comparison with three state-of-the-art optimization planners. Simulation tests show that safe trajectories can be created within a few milliseconds for paths that span the entirety of two 50 m x 50 m environments. Hardware tests with a custom quadrotor verify that STITCHER can produce trackable paths in real-time while respecting nonconvex constraints, such as limits on tilt angle and motor forces, with flight speeds up to 63 km/h.

## RCOA Extension and Applications
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02797v2
- **Authors**: ['Ricardo Tapia', 'Iman Soltani']
- **Abstrat**: The Relaxed Convex Obstacle Avoidance (RCOA) formulation is the first approach to enable a fully convex optimal control problem (OCP) for obstacle avoidance. Convergence analysis of RCOA yields an analytical framework that defines a unique characteristic: the ability to maintain obstacle avoidance (OA) efficacy even when obstacles reside beyond the controller's prediction horizon. In this paper, RCOA is extended to three-dimensional environments and apply it to Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) navigation. Furthermore, the formulation is enhanced to incorporate vehicle geometries, moving beyond point-mass representations to enable collision avoidance between 3D objects. Numerical simulations demonstrate that RCOA provides computational performance on par or exceeding state-of-the-art methods. Notably, RCOA is demonstrated to enable a Nonlinear Model Predictive Controller (NMPC) to execute aggressive maneuvers through narrow passages with reduced prediction horizons, ensuring real-time feasibility at frequencies exceeding 30~Hz.

## Grounding Object-to-Object Hazard Reasoning into 3D Risk Fields for Task-Conditioned Safety
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17566v1
- **Authors**: ['Mariana Sosa Guzmán', 'Yide Song', 'Amin Abyaneh', 'Gregory Dudek', 'Hsiu-Chin Lin']
- **Abstrat**: Safety in robotics does not solely depend on geometric constraints, but also on semantics: the given task, environment properties, and interactions between objects in the scene. We present a framework based on vision-language models (VLMs) to infer task- and scene-dependent safety constraints for robot manipulation. We provide the VLM a structured safety tool based on an RGB-D observation and a natural-language task instruction that first decides whether the task should be rejected since its execution may result in a non-recoverable hazardous outcome. Otherwise, it identifies preventable object-pair hazards and specifies the spatial relations that must be avoided. Open-vocabulary segmentation and depth information are then used to ground the detected objects in 3D and construct continuous risk fields that represent the corresponding hazardous regions. These fields are integrated with an obstacle avoidance module that corrects nominal robot motions during execution while accounting for the risk field. We evaluate the framework on preventable hazards, tasks requiring rejection, and safe variants in simulation and the real-world. Project website: https://vlm-hazard-riskfield.github.io

## Modeling the Frictional Contact of Continuum Robots with Environments through Linear Complementarity Problem
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18546v1
- **Authors**: ['Jia Shen', 'Junhyoung Ha', 'Yue Chen']
- **Abstrat**: Accurate friction modeling in continuum robots is essential for precise motion control and path planning. However, current approaches make predictions only for the mechanism's internal friction that is limited by the boundary forces of the Coulomb friction cone, which fail to characterize the static friction and motion hysteresis. In this paper, we propose a novel friction model based on complementarity constraints under the Coulomb friction assumption. This model predicts various friction effects caused by environmental interactions, including both static and sliding frictions and hysteresis behavior, as well as the corresponding robot deformation induced by these interactions, which are crucial for the practical motion planning and control of continuum robots. Additionally, real-time implementation of this model can be achieved, meeting the demand for computational efficiency in robotic applications. ## Disturbance-Aware Informative Planning in Spatiotemporal Environments
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18505v1
- **Authors**: ['Kaleb Ben Naveed', 'Haejoon Lee', 'Anouck Girard', 'Dimitra Panagou']
- **Abstrat**: Autonomous robots are widely used for persistent monitoring of spatiotemporal environments, where quantities of interest (QoI) change across space and time and require repeated measurements. In practice, robot motion can be affected by external disturbances that vary across the environment. When such external disturbances are not considered in trajectory optimization, it can lead to inaccurate predictions of robot motion and, consequently, the information acquired about the QoI during execution. However, estimating disturbances throughout the environment can be inefficient, as disturbances in regions with little overlap with monitoring trajectories have limited impact on monitoring performance. We therefore propose a two-stage informative planning framework for unknown, spatially varying but time-invariant disturbances, combining informed disturbance sampling with disturbance-aware trajectory optimization. Across four simulation scenarios, the proposed method reduces disturbance-learning time by 25.0--52.3 percent compared with environment-wide estimation and identifies paths that appear costly under nominal dynamics but become preferable when disturbances are considered.
