## ProtoVis-Nav: Prototypical Visual Imagination for Visual-Spatial Aligned UAV Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17330v1
- **Authors**: ['Guangyuan Sui', 'Yunzhong Hou', 'Chi Harold Liu']
- **Abstrat**: Vision language navigation (VLN) is very challenging for Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) since small errors in 3D motion prediction can lead to navigation errors and collisions. Unable to fathom execution outcomes in 3D environments, existing methods often struggle to predict accurate navigation waypoints from language instructions. To solve this, we introduce the Prototypical Visual Imagination framework, shortened as ProtoVis-Nav, which forces the model to imagine an outcome-associated local visual representation for the intended next UAV movement. Specifically, we predict feature prototypes and use their linear combination to estimate the visual feature of the target region. In this manner, the VLN model first imagines the local visual representation of the next intended location, and then uses predicted prototypes as context for generating the final action, promoting alignment between visual context and waypoint outputs. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the ProtoVis-Nav framework and show substantial improvements over existing baselines.





## UAV-Flow++: A Unified Suite for Benchmarking and Learning Language-Guided UAV Flight
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17163v1
- **Authors**: ['Donglin Yang', 'Xiangyu Wang', 'Yantong Zhong', 'Wenhao Zheng', 'Zihan Ding', 'Yue Liao', 'Hongsheng Li', 'Ke Xu', 'Si Liu']
- **Abstrat**: Language-guided UAV navigation has largely focused on long-horizon route planning, leaving short-range, object-relative flight maneuvers underexplored. To address this gap, we formulate Flying-on-a-Word (Flow), a task requiring a UAV to convert concise natural-language instruction, egocentric visual observation, and current state dynamics into continuous flight actions. Flow encompasses both primitive motion commands and complex object-interactive behaviors, assessing motion intent comprehension and spatial context grounding. To enable policy learning and closed-loop evaluation, we introduce UAV-Flow, a comprehensive dataset comprising 30,692 expert-piloted real-world trajectories across eight distinct motion categories, alongside 10,109 simulation trajectories in the UAV-Flow-Sim environment equipped with task-specific completion criteria. Furthermore, we develop FlowVLA, which undergoes imitation pretraining on mixed real-sim demonstrations and online post-training via Tail-Aware GRPO (TGRPO). By leveraging group reward distributions, TGRPO effectively reinforces high-reward action sequences. Evaluated on 273 held-out UAV-Flow-Sim episodes, pretrained FlowVLA achieves a mean success rate of 75.58%, outperforming the strongest baseline by 6.72 percentage points, which TGRPO post-training further elevates to 93.16% with superior sample efficiency. Physical UAV experiments demonstrate robust execution of object-relative commands in both indoor and outdoor real-world scenes. Together, Flow establishes a novel benchmark and learning framework for language-conditioned flight maneuvers.





## When Connectivity Is Not Enough: Cross-Layer Attacks on UAV C2 over 5G
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04662v5
- **Authors**: ['Wagner Comin Sonaglio', 'Ágney Lopes Roth Ferraz', 'André Elias Melo', 'Murray Evangelista de Souza', 'Sidnei Barbieri', 'Henrique Curi de Miranda', 'Guevara Noubir', 'Lourenço Alves Pereira Júnior']
- **Abstrat**: Beyond Visual Line of Sight (BVLOS) unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) operations increasingly use 5G standalone (SA) networks for command and control (C2) between the UAV and the ground control station (GCS). The 3rd Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) has specified mechanisms for authentication and authorization of unmanned aircraft systems (UAS) in this architectural setting. As a result, operators may treat registration state, Protocol Data Unit (PDU) session status, and IP reachability as evidence that the C2 path is available. In practice, however, these connectivity indicators alone do not guarantee that closed-loop control remains operationally safe. Attacks can degrade UAS C2 when timeliness degrades under shared User Plane contention, mobility continuity fails during Control Plane instability, or command integrity is violated at a trusted next-generation Node B (gNodeB). Such failures undermine connectivity as the central security indicator for UAV operations. In this paper, we demonstrate these issues using three distinct threat models on a reproducible Open5GS and UERANSIM testbed that carries Micro Air Vehicle Link (MAVLink) over the 5G User Plane, and we use a commercial Nokia core to ground deployment assumptions. We address timeliness, availability, and integrity through experiments in which attack success is defined as forcing an unsafe closed-loop state without a clean disconnect. We observe stale telemetry and heavy-tailed delay under co-tenant User Plane contention, failsafe after handover under Control Plane instability, and navigation hijacking after command rewriting at a compromised gNodeB. We further discuss why each threat model arises and evaluate mitigations for these cross-layer failures. Across the study, we disclosed five robustness issues, all with assigned CVEs.





## HAVANA: A Hierarchical Video-Action Model for Vision-Language Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24122v2
- **Authors**: ['Dongzhihan Wang', 'Yi Du', 'Chen Wang', 'Xiuxian Li', 'Liang Xu']
- **Abstrat**: Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) requires long-horizon instruction-consistent planning and short-horizon path planning grounded in the currently observed geometry. Anticipating how future observations evolve along the instructed route provides predictive guidance beyond the current field of view, motivating video-action models (VAMs) that learn representations of future visual states. However, many existing VAMs couple future visual prediction with action generation during inference, despite their distinct temporal requirements: instruction-conditioned route guidance can be reused across multiple control steps, whereas local trajectories must continuously adapt to fresh observations. To bridge this gap, we introduce HAVANA, a dual-system VAM that decouples predictive reasoning from reactive local planning. A slow system produces a compact language-conditioned latent plan from observation history, while a fast system combines the latest plan with the current observation to generate local trajectories. Their asynchronous execution enables rapid adaptation without repeated future prediction, and no future video is generated during inference. Experiments on OmniNavBench demonstrate state-of-the-art navigation performance over prior baselines, while zero-shot real-world deployments further show robust generalization to unseen environments.





## APG-Planner: Learning Vision-Based High-Speed Trajectory Planning via Analytic Policy Gradient
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15296v1
- **Authors**: ['Jinwoo Lee', 'Geunsik Bae', 'Changseung Kim', 'Jiwoo Kim', 'Woojae Shin', 'Hyondong Oh']
- **Abstrat**: Vision-based high-speed navigation requires a trajectory planner that can reactively generate smooth, collision-free trajectories from onboard visual observations. Deep reinforcement learning offers a promising approach to vision-based trajectory planning, as it enables the optimization of sequential decision-making policies in complex environments. However, model-free policy gradient methods rely on sample-based policy gradient estimation, which yields noisy gradient estimates and inefficient training. To this end, we propose a framework for learning trajectory planning policies using the analytic policy gradient (APG) method and use it to train APG-Planner, a vision-based high-speed trajectory planner. The proposed framework leverages differential flatness to analytically reconstruct vehicle states from planned trajectories, thereby enabling differentiable trajectory-level state transitions without requiring an explicit low-level dynamics model. By iteratively performing trajectory planning and differentiable state transitions, we construct a differentiable episode rollout and analytically compute policy gradients from episode-level losses, improving both training efficiency and policy performance. After approximately 30 minutes of training on a single GPU, APG-Planner demonstrates comparable or superior performance to existing baseline methods in simulation and achieves speeds of up to 11\,m/s in real-world flight experiments. We further validate the proposed framework on a skid-steer ground robot, demonstrating its applicability to differentially flat robotic systems beyond quadrotors.

## IncR-3DSGG: Incremental Posed RGB Image Based 3D Scene Graph Generation via Privileged Depth Supervision
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15429v1
- **Authors**: ['Mrunmai Phatak', 'Daniel Kienzle', 'Roman Küble', 'Jörg Hähner', 'Rainer Lienhart']
- **Abstrat**: Scene graphs provide a compact, structured representation of environments by capturing objects and their spatial or semantic relationships in a form suitable for embodied agents. Existing 3D scene graph generation methods often rely on depth sensing at inference, either to associate object instances across views via point cloud overlap or to compute inter-object relations from geometric cues, limiting deployment to platforms with depth sensors. We propose IncR-3DSGG, which builds 3D scene graphs incrementally from posed\footnote{RGB images with camera extrinsics estimated by an off-the-shelf visual SLAM system.} RGB images, eliminating the use of depth sensors at inference. Our method leverages a Parametric Relation Predictor (PRP) that learns to estimate pairwise distances and relative angles between objects from 2D visual features and 2D geometric cues, using 3D geometric supervision available only during training. These learned pairwise measurements are fused across views through Pose Graph Optimization (PGO)~\cite{grisetti2010tutorial} to recover metrically grounded 3D object positions and build a globally consistent 3D scene graph incrementally. On the real-world 3RScan~\cite{wald2019rio} dataset, our method attains $52.2$ mean object recall and $30.8$ mean predicate recall under the 3DSSG~\cite{wald2020learning} evaluation protocol, against $27.0$ and $17.6$ for MonoSSG~\cite{wu2023incremental}, while using no depth or reconstructed geometry at inference. This shows that relations and metrically grounded 3D object representations can be predicted from posed RGB images alone.

## QVS-LIVO RiskMap: Health- and Information-Aware View Selection for Multi-Camera LIVO and Off-Road Risk Mapping
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16205v1
- **Authors**: ['Zihan Pan', 'Jinghan Wang', 'Hang Li', 'Feng Cheng', 'Tianchen Liu', 'Gaoliang Peng']
- **Abstrat**: Multi-camera observations in off-road environments vary significantly in reliability, posing challenges when complementing degraded geometric constraints. Motivated by this, we propose Quality-aware View Selection Lidar-Vision Odometry Risk Mapping (QVS-LIVO RiskMap), which decouples visual processing for state estimation from that for semantic mapping. The front end evaluates camera observations through read-only pretracking on a shared lidar-inertial posterior, combining observation health with geometric complementarity. Channel health memory and switching hysteresis govern selection of at most one eligible view for direct photometric correction. The posterior is retained when no view is eligible or the correction fails acceptance. The mapping back end integrates semantic observations from all available views in a shared world grid. Vehicle-scale geometry constrains semantic risk corrections, while spatial and temporal evidence supports risk confirmation. The system also produces a local bird's-eye-view (BEV) risk map and a global 2.5D semantic-terrain risk map with obstacle instance voxels. On two field sequences, QVS-LIVO achieved horizontal root mean square errors of 0.910 m and 1.664 m. It reduced error by 43.3% relative to FAST-LIO2 on the degraded long-route sequence. Across 12 controlled camera-fault configurations involving dropout, black frames and 60 ms timestamp shifts, it maintained complete trajectories with errors of 1.54-2.23 m. When deployed onboard an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin, the system generates local and global maps at 9.94 Hz and 2.00 Hz, respectively, with a median local mapping latency of 32.4 ms. These runtime results demonstrate the real-time feasibility of selective visual state updates alongside multi-view semantic mapping under challenging off-road conditions. The code and related resources are available at: https://github.com/zihanPan-HIT/QVS-LIVO.

## riMESA: Consensus ADMM for Real-World Collaborative SLAM

- **Abstrat**: Collaborative Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (C-SLAM) is a fundamental capability for multi-robot teams as it enables downstream tasks like planning and navigation. ## A Tutorial on Infrastructure Self-Calibration for Radio-Based Indoor Positioning
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15503v1
- **Authors**: ['Paul Schwarzbach']
- **Abstrat**: Radio positioning needs the positions of its anchors, access points, base stations, or reconfigurable intelligent surfaces, and manual surveying is costly and error-prone. Calibrating this infrastructure from its own measurements is one estimation problem, studied in at least four communities as self-calibration, auto-positioning, anchor self-localization, network localization, or radio simultaneous localization and mapping. This tutorial teaches the problem through one factor graph. The measurement topology sets its factors, the reference information enters as priors, and other measurement models add nuisance states. The tutorial shows what ranges can identify, from rigidity to the motion a mobile device must contribute, and presents multidimensional scaling, nonlinear least squares, recursive filters, and convex relaxations as inference engines over this graph. One session of a ten-anchor ultra-wideband deployment is carried through every step, and recordings from a second, harsher deployment and a public partial mesh serve as contrasts. The examples illustrate three lessons. On a full mesh, choose a robust loss and set its scale first, and calibrate with the engine of the positioning system, since the engines then reach similar errors. On thinned or partial meshes, test the result at surveyed check points, because the start decides which minimum a solver reaches. On a line-of-sight deployment, estimate range offsets, which lower the error floor that a systematic ranging bias sets and a robust loss cannot remove. A decision tree and a table of their effects turn them into deployment guidance. Code and result tables are openly released, and the data are published separately.

## AFTER: A World Model with Adaptive Fast--Slow Timescales for Error-Driven Retention
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15215v1
- **Authors**: ['Haoyi Zhang', 'Qi Zhang', 'Yunfan Ren', 'Yi Bin', 'Lei Zhu', 'Heng Tao Shen']
- **Abstrat**: In robotics and embodied AI, a core challenge for world models is adapting to sudden, persistent, yet initially unknown changes in robot dynamics during interaction. For instance, when a robotic arm grasps an object with an unknown payload, the robot dynamics change suddenly. If the world model fails to adapt, its prediction errors accumulate over the open-loop rollout and ultimately mislead the robot's actions. Existing world models, however, struggle to simultaneously satisfy three critical requirements for dynamics adaptation: responsiveness, reacting promptly to sudden changes to curb prediction error accumulation; stability, distinguishing persistent changes from transient disturbances to prevent incorrect updates; and persistence, retaining the adapted dynamics without new observations so that later predictions do not revert to the old dynamics. Inspired by predictive coding in the human brain with multi-timescale adaptation, we propose AFTER-WM, a world model with Adaptive Fast-slow Timescales for Error-driven Retention, whose prediction errors drive a fast correction that fades and a slow correction that persists, while dynamically adjusting the combination weights of pre-trained experts without altering them, to overcome the trade-offs among responsiveness, stability and persistence. On five simulated robot datasets and real UAV data, AFTER-WM has a lower multi-step mean error than all ten compared models and the highest mean return in a joint-failure control task. In simulation, its multistep mean error is 16%-34% below the strongest compared model's and is also the lowest 1-4 steps after a change and under transient disturbances alone, and its step-40 error is the lowest as well, reflecting responsiveness, stability and persistence. All code (method, baselines, experiments) and datasets will be released online.





## CFGComposer: Compositional Audio-Text-to-Motion Generation via Copula Score Correction

Numerical simulations provide vector-field-tracking baselines where the nominal vector fields are reshaped with DIVAL. ## Robust Task-Aware State Estimation from Pre-trained Perception
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14781v1
- **Authors**: ['Guy Azran', 'Sarah Keren']
- **Abstrat**: Agents that perform state estimation using pretrained perception models, e.g., Vision-Language Models (VLMs), face the fundamental challenge that predictions are uncertain. At the other extreme, maintaining a full probability distribution over symbolic states is often intractable. Our algorithm, Probability-Ranked Valid State Search (PRaVaSS), takes advantage of the unique structure of this belief system to construct an MBS$_θ$ with correctness guarantees and polynomial complexity in the unconstrained case. Using a VLM as the backbone of our state estimator, we validate our method's theoretical properties, scalability with problem size, and improvement of downstream decision-making when used in a task-planning pipeline.
