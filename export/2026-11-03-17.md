## MoSoLoco: Monocular 3D Human Localization through Visual Latent Features and Social Context
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03189v1
- **Authors**: ['Yasamin Borhani', 'Javad Khoramdel', 'Yang Gao', 'Po-Chien Luan', 'Alexandre Alahi']
- **Abstrat**: Estimating the metric 3D position of people from a monocular camera is important for robot navigation and motion prediction. Lightweight pose-based localizers are attractive for robotic hardware with limited computational resources because they operate on compact 2D keypoints. However, they typically discard the richer visual representations computed by the pose estimator. We introduce MoSoLoco, a per-frame monocular 3D human localizer that combines the pose detector's internal visual features with the detected 2D skeleton, without requiring an additional RGB or depth encoder. MoSoLoco can further incorporate sparse same-frame multi-person context to refine individual position estimates. Experiments on JRDB and NuScenes show that reusing pose-detector representations improves localization, reducing the best JRDB error by approximately 23 percent relative to the strongest evaluated keypoint-based baseline. A sparse social-interaction module incorporates nearby detections from the same image for further refinement. Controlled trajectory-forecasting experiments show downstream benefits, while the complete pipeline remains suitable for efficient Jetson AGX Xavier inference.





## A Tightly Coupled Magnetic-Inertial SLAM System for Field Robots
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03106v1
- **Authors**: ['Chuan Huang', 'Tongxing Peng']
- **Abstrat**: Field robots operating in mines, tunnels, and disaster areas require reliable localization despite GNSS denial and degraded camera and LiDAR measurements caused by dust, smoke, or poor illumination. Missions involving both ground and aerial robots further motivate complementary sensing modalities that do not depend on visibility, preinstalled infrastructure, or wheel contact. This letter presents MISLAM, a tightly coupled magnetic-inertial simultaneous localization and mapping system using an IMU and a nine-magnetometer array, without relying on external odometry or platform-specific motion assumptions. The system combines the inertial navigation model with local polynomial magnetic-field models for short-term motion estimation and a global Gaussian process model for drift correction using an error-state Kalman filter. Offline evaluation on three indoor datasets collected with a handheld rig shows reductions in total endpoint closure error of 45.7% - 78.0% relative to magnetic-inertial odometry. Under poor illumination, MISLAM maintains tracking after visual-inertial odometry fails, achieving a horizontal closure error of 0.332 m, although vertical drift remains a limitation. These results support magnetic-inertial SLAM as a complementary localization modality for robotic sensor suites operating under degraded visual conditions.





## HaltNav: Reactive Visual Halting over Lightweight Topological Priors for Robust Vision-Language Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12696v3
- **Authors**: ['Zihui Yu', 'Pingcong Li', 'Bichi Zhang', 'Sören Schwertfeger']
- **Abstrat**: Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) is shifting from rigid, step-by-step instruction following toward open-vocabulary, goal-oriented autonomy. Achieving this transition without exhaustive routing prompts requires agents to leverage structural priors. While prior work often assumes computationally heavy 2D/3D metric maps, we instead exploit a lightweight, text-based osmAG (OpenStreetMap Area Graph), a floorplan-level topological representation that is easy to obtain and maintain. However, global planning over a prior map alone is brittle in real-world deployments, where local connectivity can change (e.g., closed doors or crowded passages), leading to execution-time failures. To address this gap, we propose a hierarchical navigation framework HaltNav that couples the robust global planning of osmAG with the local exploration and instruction-grounding capability of VLN. Our approach features an MLLM-based brain module, which is capable of high-level task grounding and obstruction awareness. Conditioned on osmAG, the brain converts the global route into a sequence of localized execution snippets, providing the VLN executor with prior-grounded, goal-centric sub-instructions. Meanwhile, it detects local anomalies via a mechanism we term Reactive Visual Halting (RVH), which interrupts the local control loop, updates osmAG by invalidating the corresponding topology, and triggers replanning to orchestrate a viable detour. To train this halting capability efficiently, we introduce a data synthesis pipeline that leverages generative models to inject realistic obstacles into otherwise navigable scenes, substantially enriching hard negative samples. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our hierarchical framework outperforms several baseline methods without tedious language instructions, and significantly improves robustness for long-horizon vision-language navigation under environmental changes.





## GeoGuesser Bench: Benchmarking Vision-Language Agents on Interactive Geolocation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02698v1
- **Authors**: ['Huanzhi Mao', 'Yichuan Wang', 'Michael Koo', 'Wenyuan Huang', 'Huy Doan', 'Jinghan Xia', 'Ziyao Wang', 'Joseph E. Gonzalez']
- **Abstrat**: Vision-language model (VLM) agents that act in the open world need two abilities: world knowledge to interpret what they see, and exploration to find what they cannot yet see. We study how agents combine the two through interactive visual geolocation. GeoGuesser Bench contains 282 tasks in an offline Street View environment where agents rotate, zoom, and move between real panoramas. Its three task types shift the balance from knowledge to exploration: agents (1) report the coordinates of their starting location, (2) name the enclosing street, city, state, or country, or (3) find, read, and count nearby objects. The coordinate tasks in (1) also include 42 real GeoGuessr World Championship rounds, whose expert guesses provide a human reference. We evaluate 23 VLMs: the best accuracy is 62.5% on place naming and 35.0% on object finding, and the best agent averages 4352.4 GeoGuessr points on the championship rounds, against 4770.3 for the better expert in each round. Beyond these headline numbers, world knowledge and exploration are only weakly correlated across models: agents that localize well do not necessarily explore well. To understand where agents fail, we categorize their errors into vision, navigation, and tool-use failures. Further ablations show that the strongest agents, such as Claude Opus 4.8, still perform well with limited views, while weaker agents degrade; customizing the environment thus adjusts task difficulty. The environment's verifiable rewards and restorable state also support training, and our Planner-Worker-Compressor harness cuts planner tokens by 57-61% by compacting the interaction history. We hope GeoGuesser Bench and its environment serve as a high-quality foundation for simulating real-world exploration at scale.





## Interactive 3D Cutaway Visualization for Subsurface Target Localization in Robot-Assisted Surgery
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02537v1
- **Authors**: ['Yuxin Chen', 'Wenxuan Wang', 'Wanwen Chen', 'Qi Zeng', 'Septimiu E. Salcudean']
- **Abstrat**: Purpose: Blended augmented reality (AR) overlays can provide ambiguous depth cues in which subsurface anatomy floats above the tissue surface. We investigate whether an interactive 3D cutaway visualization can improve robot-assisted target localization. Methods: We propose Virtual Box, an interactive 3D cutaway interface that allows users to independently manipulate the viewing perspective to inspect subsurface targets. In a controlled, counterbalanced within-subject tissue phantom study, 15 engineering students used Virtual Box and a depth-enhanced blended AR overlay to position a da Vinci instrument at virtual targets in a water tank. We used registered 3D transrectal ultrasound to measure 3D localization error and depth error. We also collected completion time and subjective ratings. Results: Virtual Box reduced mean 3D error from 12.92 to 4.96 mm (61.6% reduction) and depth error from 9.48 to 2.07 mm (78.2% reduction; both p < 0.001). Spatial understanding, depth perception, and confidence ratings were higher (all unadjusted p < 0.001), but completion time increased from 21.64 to 32.58 s (p = 0.008). Overall workload did not differ significantly. Conclusion: Virtual Box improved localization accuracy and perceived spatial understanding in this controlled task, with additional completion time, supporting further evaluation for surgical navigation.





## Reconstruction as Spatial Evaluation: Diagnosing Cross-View Spatial Intelligence in Large Vision-Language Models
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02026v1
- **Authors**: ['Junfei Wu', 'Qiuyue Wang', 'Xuhong Huang', 'Qihang Peng', 'Mingsheng Li', 'Jian Guan', 'Qiang Liu', 'Shu Wu', 'Liang Wang', 'Shuai Bai', 'Tieniu Tan']
- **Abstrat**: The spatial intelligence of large vision-language models (LVLMs) requires the ability to combine local visual observations into a geometrically consistent representation of the physical world. Many static benchmarks assess isolated spatial judgments, such as relative direction or distance, while interactive embodied evaluations combine perception with exploration, planning, control, and simulator-specific execution. To bridge this gap, we introduce RECONSPATIAL, a benchmark centered on multi-view 3D scene reconstruction across indoor, tabletop, and outdoor environments. Given real multi-view images and target-object queries, LVLMs are required to jointly predict oriented 3D bounding boxes in a shared, camera-anchored metric coordinate frame. Reconstructions are evaluated along two complementary axes: Structure for relative layout and Metric for physical scale and camera-anchored placement. Nine atomic spatial QA tasks, grouped into Geometric Perception and Spatial Relations, probe spatial capabilities involved in joint reconstruction. Offline navigation extends evaluation to path planning. Evaluation of 18 frontier LVLMs reveals a consistent gap between relative scene structure and metric reconstruction; even GPT-6-Astra scores 81.4% on Structure but only 58.7% on Metric. Tracing this gap, diagnostic analyses link atomic spatial capabilities to reconstruction quality and show that relational consistency alone does not ensure metric accuracy. Experiments with 3D map injection further separate scene construction from path planning, revealing how these geometric errors limit navigation success. Finally, spatial fine-tuning yields broad transfer across both spatial and general vision-language benchmarks.





## OpenNWM: A Generalist Navigation World Model with Latent Action Pretraining
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01844v1
- **Authors**: ['Dujun Nie', 'Ruiqi Song', 'Zixuan Wang', 'Hanqing Wang', 'Yifan Han', 'Baiyong Ding', 'Wenzhao Zheng', 'Hangbin Wu', 'Long Chen']
- **Abstrat**: Navigation world models predict future observations conditioned on navigation actions to support planning, but their generalization across real-world environments remains constrained by the limited coverage of action-labeled data. Action-free navigation videos provide diverse visual experience, yet lack the recorded actions required for action-conditioned training. We introduce OpenNWM, a generalist navigation world model with latent action pretraining. It learns universal latent actions from large-scale action-free videos through visual prediction pretraining, and further aligns them with physical motions via action-conditioned post-training. To support video pretraining, we curate NavAnywhere, a large-scale action-free navigation video dataset comprising 17.5 million visual observations from 15 heterogeneous sources. It captures navigation by ground robots, humans, and drones, with diverse viewpoints and motion patterns across five broad scene types: residential areas, public and commercial spaces, urban settings, parks, and natural landscapes. Experiments demonstrate improved visual prediction on in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks, together with improved navigation performance. These results show that latent-action pretraining enables diverse, action-free videos to improve dynamics learning and generalization for open-world navigation.

We contribute an empirically informed account of learning challenges, a structured mapping of learning demands to immersive affordances, and concrete design opportunities for immersive semiconductor education. These contributions guide the development and evaluation of immersive environments that support reasoning across scales, representations, and design processes.





## WorldSketch: Sketching World Models for Zero-Shot Mobile Manipulation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00513v1
- **Authors**: ['Jiaming Wang']
- **Abstrat**: Most agentic robotic systems use vision-language models (VLMs) either to orchestrate predefined skills or to directly generate actions, constraints, and control programs. The former is limited by the available skill library, while the latter requires VLMs to reason about precise geometry and contact. In this paper, we propose WorldSketch, a new paradigm for agentic robotics in which a VLM constructs a world model rather than generating robot actions. The VLM acts as an agent that actively queries perception tools to build and visually validate a minimal kinematic world model, capturing only task-relevant objects, their interaction geometry, and possible motions. A geometric planner then jointly optimizes contact and whole-body motion on the resulting model. Moreover, the world model is self-improving: execution feedback verifies outcomes, updates uncertain hypotheses, and enables the agent to revise the model when its predictions are contradicted. We demonstrate the generality of this paradigm across RoboCasa, LIBERO-PRO, and R2R-CE navigation, achieving zero-shot success rates of 91%, 97%, and 76%, respectively. On RoboCasa, WorldSketch outperforms the next-best zero-shot agent baseline by 58 percentage points while using 10 times fewer VLM input tokens; on LIBERO-PRO, it exceeds CaP-X by 82 percentage points. In navigation, it matches the success rate of step-by-step VLM agents while using 6 times fewer input tokens.





## Seeing Less in a Sparse World: Cross-View Geolocalization from Limited Field-of-View Observations
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00288v1
- **Authors**: ['Fahimul Aleem', 'Shruti Vyas']
- **Abstrat**: Cross-view geolocalization aims to determine the geographic location of a ground-level image by retrieving its corresponding satellite image, offering a promising alternative for localization in Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS)-denied or degraded environments such as urban canyons, dense forests, and disaster scenarios. Despite recent progress, existing approaches remain limited by the lack of globally diverse training datasets and benchmarks that are often geographically restricted. Furthermore, most benchmarks rely on panoramic ground views, which do not reflect the limited field-of-view imagery captured by real world cameras.   To address these challenges, we introduce Cross-View World 500k (CVW500k), a large-scale globally distributed dataset containing over 500,000 ground-satellite image pairs collected across diverse geographic regions and environments. Unlike previous datasets, CVW500k uses limited field-of-view ground images, providing a more realistic and challenging setting for cross-view geolocalization. In addition, we propose GeoQueryNet, a query-based cross-view fusion transformer framework designed to bridge the viewpoint gap between ground and satellite images. The model leverages a CLIP-based visual encoder with parameter-efficient LoRA adaptation and introduces a Cross-View Alignment Module that uses learnable query tokens to extract geographically consistent features across viewpoints. Our code can be found here: https://github.com/Fahim17/Cross_View_World_Dataset.git .

## Spade-SLAM: Sparse Correspondence Anchors for Dense Self-Supervised Visual SLAM
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01937v1
- **Authors**: ['Julian Philipp Schmitt', 'Weirong Chen', 'Ganlin Zhang', 'Felix Wimbauer', 'Daniel Cremers']
- **Abstrat**: We introduce Spade-SLAM, a self-supervised dense visual SLAM framework that substantially narrows the long-standing performance gap to supervised methods in a zero-shot setting. By demonstrating that high-fidelity tracking can be achieved without explicit ground-truth pose and depth labels, our approach offers a highly effective and label-efficient alternative to fully supervised pretraining. Our core contribution is a complementary sparse-to-dense training regime: While a warping-based photometric loss provides a dense training signal, we stabilize the optimization and suppress drift in ambiguous regions using a sparse correspondence loss derived from image feature matches. The code will be made publicly available at https://github.com/JulianPSchmitt/Spade-SLAM.





## Action-Consistent Decentralized Belief Space Planning with Inconsistent Beliefs and Limited Data Sharing: Framework and Simplification Algorithms with Formal Guarantees
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05962v3
- **Authors**: ['Tanmoy Kundu', 'Moshe Rafaeli Shimron', 'Anton Gulyaev', 'Vadim Indelman']
- **Abstrat**: In multi-robot systems, ensuring safe and reliable decision making under uncertain conditions demands robust multi-robot belief space planning (MR-BSP) algorithms. While planning with multiple robots, each robot maintains a belief over the state of the environment and reasons how the belief would evolve in the future for different possible actions. However, existing MR-BSP works have a common assumption that the beliefs of different robots are same at planning time. Such an assumption is often unrealistic as it requires prohibitively extensive and frequent data sharing capabilities. In practice, robots may have limited communication capabilities, and consequently beliefs of the robots can be different. Crucially, when the robots have inconsistent beliefs, the existing approaches could result in lack of coordination between the robots and may lead to unsafe decisions. In this paper, we present decentralized MR-BSP algorithms, with performance guarantees, for tackling this crucial gap. Our algorithms leverage the notion of action preferences. The base algorithm VerifyAC guarantees a consistent joint action selection by the cooperative robots via a three-step verification. When the verification succeeds, VerifyAC finds a consistent joint action without triggering a communication; otherwise it triggers a communication. We design an extended algorithm R-VerifyAC for further reducing the number of communications, by relaxing the criteria of action consistency. Another extension R-VerifyAC-simp builds on verifying a partial set of observations and improves the computation time significantly. The theoretical performance guarantees are corroborated with simulation results in discrete setting. Furthermore, we formulate our approaches for continuous and high-dimensional state and observation spaces, and provide experimental results for active multi-robot visual SLAM with real robots.

## Reduced Order Observers for Monocular Visual Inertial Odometry
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26612v2
- **Authors**: ['Amel Abi', 'Mouaad Boughellaba', 'Miaomiao Wang', 'Abdelhamid Tayebi']
- **Abstrat**: This work develops a reduced order observer framework of monocular visual-inertial odometry (VIO). The key idea consists in considering the gravity vector in the body-frame as an additional state and constructing an appropriate output that relates the monocular bearing measurements and their time derivatives to the body-frame velocity. This eliminates the need for the estimation of the landmark positions, leading to a six-dimensional linear time-varying (LTV) observer for the body-frame velocity and gravity, endowed with uniform global exponential stability guarantees. These estimates are subsequently used to recover the orientation almost globally, up to an unknown constant yaw offset, and the position up to an unknown constant translation. The mixed-bearing framework is further extended to account for the inertial measurement unit (IMU) biases, yielding a twelve-dimensional LTV observer endowed with local exponential stability guarantees. Numerical simulations are provided to illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed observers.

## Spectral-Convolutional Factorized Transformer for Joint Multipath Parameter Estimation: Applications to mmWave Positioning and Radio SLAM
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01491v1
- **Authors**: ['Yaya Etiabi', 'Wessam Ajib', 'Halima Elbiaze']
- **Abstrat**: Future 6G wireless networks are expected to evolve toward AI-native systems that support intelligent, environment-aware services, including positioning and mapping. Current research has already integrated AI into several aspects of communication, particularly wireless channel estimation. However, most learning-based channel estimators focus on communication-oriented objectives rather than explicitly estimating multipath parameters such as delay, angle of arrival (AoA), and angle of departure (AoD). These parameters provide essential geometric information for downstream tasks including positioning and radio simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM). In this work, we propose a Spectral-Convolutional Factorized Transformer (SCFT) for joint multipath component (MPC) estimation from noisy complex channel estimates and physical beam-pair descriptors. SCFT combines local spectral convolution, permutation-equivariant beam-set attention, and a query-based Transformer decoder to detect and classify line-of-sight (LoS) and non-line-of-sight (NLoS) paths and estimate their observed delays, AoAs, and AoDs. Experiments on realistic ray-tracing data show that SCFT reduces the generalized optimal sub-pattern assignment (GOSPA) error by 13.4% compared with ResNet, the strongest competing baseline. We further demonstrate this advantage in independent snapshot positioning and radio SLAM, where SCFT reduces position RMSE by 18.5% and 65.0%, respectively, relative to ResNet. These findings highlight the importance of explicitly recovering multipath geometry, as improvements in MPC estimation can translate into substantial gains in downstream tasks.
