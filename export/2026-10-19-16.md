## Anytime-Valid Certification of Robustness Margins for Safe Control via E-Values
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23746v1
- **Authors**: ['Panagiotis S. Trakas', 'Omid Mirzaeedodangeh', 'Lars Lindemann']
- **Abstrat**: Robust control uses the error between a known model and the true system as a robustness margin. However, this error is usually unknown: conservative estimates restrict feasibility and performance, while underestimates can result in violations of the control property. We estimate this robustness margin from online data using e-values, a statistical tool for hypothesis testing, in the setting of discrete-time control barrier functions (CBFs). Here, we measure the robustness margin as the difference between the observed CBF and the model-based CBF prediction. We then design a robust CBF controller in three steps by: (1) selecting a set of candidate margins, (2) obtaining validity certificates for these candidates via sequential tests based on e-values, and (3) updating the CBF controller with the smallest certified candidate. The deployed tests accumulate evidence against the hypothesis that an exceedance risk, the probability of the robustness margin exceeding the candidate margin, remains at or above a pre-specified failure probability $α$. Our tests are anytime valid and thus enable the use of certified candidates in the CBF controller, guaranteeing that the probability that the exceedance risk of a certified margin exceeds $α$ is at most a user-chosen confidence $δ$. We validate our approach on navigation problems across different environments with visualizations in NVIDIA Isaac Sim.





## Towards Unified World Models for Visual Navigation via Memory-Augmented Planning and Foresight
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08713v4
- **Authors**: ['Yifei Dong', 'Fengyi Wu', 'Guangyu Chen', 'Lingdong Kong', 'Qiyu Hu', 'Yuxuan Zhou', 'Xu Zhu', 'Jingdong Sun', 'Jun-Yan He', 'Qi Dai', 'Alexander G. Hauptmann', 'Zhi-Qi Cheng']
- **Abstrat**: Enabling embodied agents to imagine future states is essential for robust and generalizable visual navigation. Yet, state-of-the-art systems typically rely on modular designs that decouple navigation planning from visual world modeling, which often induces state-action misalignment and weak adaptability in novel or dynamic scenarios. We propose UniWM, a unified, memory-augmented world model that integrates egocentric visual foresight and planning within a single multimodal autoregressive backbone. UniWM explicitly grounds action selection in visually imagined outcomes, tightly aligning prediction with control. Meanwhile, a hierarchical memory mechanism fuses short-term perceptual cues with longer-term trajectory context, supporting stable and coherent reasoning over extended horizons. Extensive experiments on four challenging benchmarks (Go Stanford, ReCon, SCAND, HuRoN) and the 1X Humanoid Dataset show that UniWM improves navigation success rates by up to 30%, substantially reduces trajectory errors against strong baselines, generalizes zero-shot to the unseen TartanDrive dataset, and scales naturally to high-dimensional humanoid navigation. These results position UniWM as a principled step toward unified, imagination-driven embodied navigation. The code and models are available at https://github.com/UWMILab/UniWM.





## SimpleVLN: Towards Simple and Frontier Vision-Language Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23203v1
- **Authors**: ['Lingyi Hong', 'Weiren Cai', 'Jiayu Chen', 'Xinyu Zhou', 'Bailin He', 'Yunfei Guo', 'Qiao Sun', 'Boyu Ma', 'Jianfei Yang', 'Wenqiang Zhang']
- **Abstrat**: Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) requires embodied agents to ground language in visual observations and make long-horizon decisions. Existing methods often address these demands through task-specific architectures. As multimodal large language models (MLLMs) become increasingly capable, an important question remains: Have we truly unlocked the potential of native MLLMs for VLN before introducing task-specific architecture? We present SimpleVLN, an architecture-preserving framework developed through studies of navigation formulation, data construction, and optimization. At the formulation level, SimpleVLN combines single-turn decision inputs with fixed-budget global-local memory to efficiently use navigation history without unbounded context growth, while action chunks enable efficient output of multiple primitive actions per model invocation. For data construction, we combine expert demonstrations with policy-dependent DAgger experience and develop a scalable synthesis pipeline that generates large-scale tasks for reinforcement learning. At the optimization level, a progressive IL-DAgger-RL pipeline advances from action imitation to task-level outcome optimization. Comprehensive experiments validate the effectiveness of these three aspects. We instantiate SimpleVLN across multiple MLLM backbones and model sizes, demonstrating its generality. SimpleVLN achieves 76.7% and 80.1% success rates on the R2R-CE and RxR-CE splits, respectively, substantially outperforming existing methods. These results show that the navigation capabilities of native MLLMs can be effectively unlocked without task-specific architectural designs.





## PASS: A Trustworthy Precision Approach Simulation System for CAT III Integrity Algorithm Development
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23019v1
- **Authors**: ['Lang Zhu', 'Xin Zhang', 'Yulong Sun', 'Nan Liu', 'Jiahe Shen', 'Xiaodong Zhang', 'Xingqun Zhan']
- **Abstrat**: For fixed-wing aircraft, reliable localization during approach and landing is critical to flight safety. Visual--inertial navigation is promising, but aircraft navigation also requires integrity: trustworthy outputs and timely warnings when they should not be used. Real approach data are costly to collect, while many visual errors and failures required for integrity algorithm development are difficult to reproduce safely. We present PASS, a trustworthy Precision Approach Simulation System for CAT III integrity algorithm development. In a unified temporal and geometric framework, PASS integrates configurable airport environments, prescribed trajectories, inertial simulation, calibrated stereo images, and continuous runway-relative ground truth. Experiments with representative stereo--inertial estimators demonstrate the usability of the generated sequences and quantify localization errors across algorithms and environments. PASS provides a controllable foundation for developing and evaluating visual--inertial navigation and integrity algorithms for fixed-wing precision approaches up to CAT III requirements.





## PASS-VLN: Passability-Aware Local-Command Vision-and-Language Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22812v1
- **Authors**: ['Cong Zou', 'Xiangsheng Shi', 'Xuanhui Wang', 'Fubin Wang', 'Guangjun Ge', 'Chao Wang', 'Yu Wang']
- **Abstrat**: Reliable vision-and-language navigation (VLN) requires not only understanding instructions but also determining whether and how a robot can physically execute them. However, existing VLN benchmarks typically assume executable instructions and focus on long-horizon tasks. This leaves body-dependent local execution, including passability and collision-free execution, largely unexamined. Evaluating these factors within long-horizon tasks would entangle local physical execution with task planning and long-term spatial memory, making navigation failures difficult to attribute. We therefore focus on grounding local navigation commands in executable motion under environmental and robot body constraints. We present PASS-VLN, a passability-aware local-command VLN benchmark and model. The benchmark holds the scene, instruction, start pose, and reference route fixed while varying robot body width, requiring target completion when the instructed passage is feasible and stopping before blockage otherwise. Our model encodes body width through obstacle inflation in a depth-derived local bird's-eye view, fuses geometry with visual and linguistic context, and predicts discrete latent action codes for continuous closed-loop execution. Experiments show that PASS-VLN improves collision-free success by approximately 12 percentage points over a strong VLN model fine-tuned on our benchmark, while adapting reach-versus-stop behavior to body constraints and generalizing to unseen body widths. When integrated with a high-level planner, PASS-VLN achieves higher success with fewer local commands, while real-world deployment further demonstrates its effectiveness as a low-level executor. The benchmark and model will be released.

## HyVGGT-VO: Hybrid Dense Visual Odometry With Classical VO and Feed-Forward Model
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02107v2
- **Authors**: ['Junxiang Pang', 'Lipu Zhou', 'Baojie Chen', 'Zhenzhong Wei']
- **Abstrat**: Dense visual odometry (VO), which provides pose estimation and dense 3D reconstruction, serves as the cornerstone for applications ranging from robotics to augmented reality. Recently, feed-forward models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in dense mapping. However, when these models are used in dense visual SLAM systems, their keyframe-based processing often leads to sparse pose outputs, limiting continuous per-frame pose estimation. In contrast, traditional sparse methods provide high-frequency pose outputs but lack the capability for dense reconstruction. To address these limitations, we propose HyVGGT-VO, a novel framework that combines the computational efficiency of sparse VO with the dense reconstruction capabilities of feed-forward model. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to tightly couple a traditional VO frontend with VGGT. Specifically, we design an adaptive hybrid tracking frontend that dynamically switches between traditional optical flow and the VGGT model to ensure robustness. Furthermore, we introduce a hierarchical optimization framework that jointly refines VO poses and the scale of VGGT predictions to ensure global scale consistency. Under the odometry setting, compared with VGGT-SLAM 2.0, HyVGGT-VO reduces the average ATE by approximately 91% on EuRoC MAV and 20% on TUM RGB-D, and decreases the average relative translation and rotation errors by 46% and 92% on KITTI, respectively, while achieving 16.1 Hz pose output and 3.2 Hz dense-mapping updates with 6.5 GB of memory on an 8 GB laptop GPU.Our code is available at https://geneta2580.github.io/HyVGGT-VO.io.

## KFS$^4$LAM: Adaptive KeyFrame Selection with Structure-Aware Mapping for Endoscopic Gaussian Splatting SLAM
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22505v1
- **Authors**: ['Xinwei Ju', 'Zhehua Mao', 'Tianyi Song', 'Danail Stoyanov', 'Sophia Bano', 'Francisco Vasconcelos']
- **Abstrat**: Gaussian-splatting Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) augments conventional SLAM with a dense, renderable 3D scene representation, enabling high-quality reconstruction and novel-view synthesis. However, its localization, reconstruction, and rendering performance depends strongly on which observations are retained for mapping. Existing systems commonly rely on fixed temporal intervals or simple pose- and overlap-based criteria for keyframe selection. In colonoscopy, non-uniform camera motion, repetitive anatomy, specularities, and visual artifacts can make these criteria unreliable, leading to either redundant keyframes or missed informative viewpoints. We present KFS$^4$LAM (KeyFrame Selection with Structure-aware mapping for endoscopic Gaussian Splatting SLAM), which couples motion-guided adaptive keyframe selection with structurally supervised mapping and restricted temporal deformation. Accumulated motion guides adaptive keyframe selection, with complementary cues suppressing redundant or unreliable candidates. Density-aware mapping with anatomical contour supervision (colon folds) emphasizes structurally informative regions, while restricted temporal deformation accommodates local tissue dynamics without unconstrained geometric drift. We evaluate KFS$^4$LAM against Gaussian-SLAM baselines on C3VD and C3VD-v2, together with cross-backbone and component ablations. KFS$^4$LAM achieves strong overall performance across trajectory, rendering, and depth metrics, with pronounced gains on more challenging C3VD-v2 dataset. In the matched comparison with SplaTAM on C3VD-v2, it reduces rotational relative pose error by 20.3% and depth RMSE by 38.4%, while improving PSNR by 0.78 dB. Cross-backbone experiments further show that the framework is not tied to a single underlying SLAM architecture. Code and visualization results are available at https://xinweiju.github.io/KFSLAM/.

## F-LIVO: A Feature-Level Geometric Measurement Framework for LiDAR-Inertial-Visual Odometry
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23595v1
- **Authors**: ['Xinye Dai', 'Yapeng Wang', 'Shujie Zhou', 'Zihao Wang', 'Shiqi Zheng', 'Jiayu Hu', 'Yusheng Wang', 'Weiwei Song', 'Weiming Tang']
- **Abstrat**: Measurement-level LiDAR-Inertial-Visual Odometry (LIVO) can achieve high accuracy, and its practical resource requirements depend on the adopted association and map-maintenance strategy. Persistently attaching dense photometric patches or appearance attributes to mapped LiDAR points can introduce additional storage, association, and update costs. This paper presents F-LIVO, a filter-based LIVO framework that couples a visual-inertial subsystem and a LiDAR-inertial subsystem through feature-level point-to-plane constraints between visual points and LiDAR planes. To preserve cross-modal coupling when visual triangulation is weak, we further recover visual feature depth from the LiDAR map through local plane fitting and ray-plane intersection. In the LiDAR update, the covariance of each point-to-plane residual is adaptively scaled according to local planar reliability, thereby reducing overconfident updates on poorly conditioned surfaces. Because the proposed method relies on sparse geometric features rather than persistent dense photometric map attributes, it retains a compact map representation. Experiments on multiple public datasets show competitive, sequence-dependent accuracy. Across the evaluated degradation cases, F-LIVO maintains continuous estimation with comparatively low accumulated end-to-end drift. Under the evaluated resource configuration, it achieves the lowest mean and peak central processing unit (CPU) usage, the lowest peak resident set size (RSS), and the lowest RSS growth rate among the evaluated implementations, while R2LIVE retains a lower mean RSS. The source code will be made publicly available upon acceptance to facilitate reproducible research.

## FLoTS-SLAM: Optical Flow-Derived Uncertainty for Monocular Dynamic SLAM with Two-Stage Optimization
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23496v1
- **Authors**: ['Haorui Du', 'Lipu Zhou']
- **Abstrat**: Monocular simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) remains challenging in dynamic environments. Real-time dynamic object identification in such environments is a critical problem. Recent approaches estimate per-pixel uncertainty from rendering residuals or multiview feature inconsistency to identify dynamic regions. These methods lack direct motion evidence, and their accuracy may degrade when reconstruction is inaccurate or features are ambiguous. In addition, such uncertainty merely downweights pixels in bundle adjustment (BA), so inaccurate uncertainty estimates can still impair camera pose estimation and 3D reconstruction. Accordingly, we present a monocular RGB SLAM system for dynamic scenes. Specifically, a motion uncertainty estimation method is proposed to derive uncertainty from the residual between optical flow and camera motion induced flow, directly reflecting object motion. The estimation varies with frame alignment accuracy, reducing false detections and yielding accurate pixel-level masks of dynamic regions. To make BA more robust, we estimate camera poses from high confidence pixels. These poses are then used to reproject dynamic pixels during joint refinement of poses and depth, reducing the influence of object motion on both pose and depth updates. Moreover, we provide a quantitative evaluation of dynamic masks and analyze how these masks affect the downstream tracking and reconstruction of dynamic objects. Our approach improves the mean dynamic mask IoU by 35\% over a state-of-the-art baseline on DAVIS, and reduces the average ATE by 59\% on Bonn and 19\% on TUM relative to the SLAM backbone used in our system. The source code will be made publicly available.

- **Abstrat**: Delays in AI responses pose a significant challenge to immersive Virtual Reality experiences. ## Learning Robot Metareasoning: Adaptive Computation Budgets for Real-Time Control
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23662v1
- **Authors**: ['Lorenzo Magnino', 'Antonio Marino', 'Michael Amir', 'Daigo Shishika', 'Amanda Prorok']
- **Abstrat**: Planning while moving faces an historical trade-off between deliberation and reactivity. A planner running concurrently with execution needs time to compute, so the robot must commit to an open-loop action chunk for that duration: a longer commitment buys a better plan at the cost of feedback frequency. Existing controllers fix this action commitment, and consequently the computation budget, before deployment. However, the optimal computation time is highly state-dependent (due to factors such as unmodeled dynamics, safety constraints, and marginal optimisation gains), i.e. dynamic, therefore offline static tuning risks leaving systems blind during critical maneuvers or unable to find global effective solutions. We cast the identification of the optimal computation budget as a semi-Markov decision process (SMDP) in which a single decision defines both the robot action commitments and the time allocated to the solver. To solve this, we train a reinforcement-learning policy conditioned on the current state, the executing plan, and inexpensive planner statistics. Across ground-robot navigation, agile quadrotor flight, and contact-rich manipulation, the learned policy changes its budget with the demands of the scenario, discovering efficient reasoning patterns that outperform fixed-budget baselines. https://lorenzomagnino.github.io/learning-robot-metareasoning-review/

Our experiments on 8 robot configurations reveal substantial gaps: 1) robot demonstrations required for ICL are expensive to collect, and 2) it is implausible to trade longer execution for higher success rates with physical constraints. Fourth, we envision the next generation of embodied benchmarks. We develop a prototype that 1) derives tasks from real-world needs, 2) provides no in-domain demonstrations, and 3) judges agents on a world clock that keeps running while they deliberate. Taken together, our results suggest that GPT-6-Astra has largely crossed the threshold of embodied competence, but not yet that of practical embodied intelligence.





## TRAIN: Terrain-Aware Interactive Navigation for Quadruped Robots via Reinforcement Learning
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12724v4
- **Authors**: ['Wei Zhu', 'Irfan Tito Kurniawan', 'Ye Zhao', 'Mitsuhiro Hayashibe']
- **Abstrat**: This study introduces TRAIN: TeRrain-Aware Interactive Navigation, a deep reinforcement learning (DRL)-based framework for quadrupedal navigation across unstructured terrain and human-populated environments. Conventional quadrupedal navigation typically separates motion planning from locomotion control, neglecting whole-body constraints and terrain awareness. On the other hand, end-to-end methods are more integrated but require high-frequency sensing, which is often noisy and computationally costly. In addition, most existing approaches assume static environments, limiting their use in human-populated settings. To address these limitations, we propose a two-stage training framework with three DRL pipelines. (1) TRAIN-Loco employs an asymmetric actor-critic (AC) model for quadrupedal locomotion, enabling agile traversal of uneven terrains without explicit terrain or contact observations. (2) TRAIN-Nav applies a symmetric AC framework for navigation with interactive pedestrians, directly mapping transformed LiDAR data to ego-agent actions under differential-drive kinematics. (3) A unified pipeline, TRAIN, integrates TRAIN-Loc and TRAIN-Nav, supporting terrain-aware quadrupedal navigation in uneven and interactive environments. Comprehensive benchmarks against quadrupedal locomotion and robot navigation baselines demonstrate the effectiveness of TRAIN. Hardware experiments further confirm its potential for sim-to-real transfer.

## TransNav: Transformer-Based End-to-End Navigation Method for Static and Dynamic Quadrotor Obstacle Avoidance
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23635v1
- **Authors**: ['Jianghan Zhu', 'Zhuo Li', 'Yanjun Zhang', 'Jian Sun']
- **Abstrat**: Obstacle avoidance for autonomous quadrotors in cluttered environments remains challenging, especially when both dense static obstacles and moving obstacles are present. Existing methods are often tailored to either static or dynamic settings, which always limit their adaptability across diverse environments. This paper presents TransNav, a transformer-based end-to-end navigation method for quadrotor obstacle avoidance in both static and dynamic environments. TransNav employs a patch-based point-cloud encoding scheme that combines local feature extraction with global contextual modeling, enabling compact yet informative environment representation. The method integrates perception and navigation in a single architecture and directly predicts a finite-horizon action sequence, from which the first action is executed. Moreover, we develop an unsupervised learning strategy by incorporating a lightweight point-mass dynamics model, which directly optimizes trajectory-level costs over the predicted sequence through numerical gradient propagation. Thus, the policy can be efficiently trained without expert demonstrations. Simulation and real-world experiments show that the proposed method achieves strong performance in dense static environments and competitive obstacle-avoidance capability in dynamic scenarios, while maintaining low inference cost for onboard deployment.

## Metro-WM: Long-Horizon Latent Planning with Realisable Sub-Goals
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31868v2
- **Authors**: ['Royson Lee', 'Fady Rezk', 'Titouan Parcollet', 'Timothy Hospedales', 'Cristina Cornelio']
- **Abstrat**: Model-predictive control with Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) provides a strong zero-shot goal-reaching planner, but it is only effective over short planning horizons. Hierarchical extensions attempt to bridge this gap by learning a macro planner to predict intermediate latent sub-goals to guide the micro planner. In this work, we demonstrate that unconstrained latent sub-goal prediction is fundamentally flawed. A rigorous evaluation reveals that a leading state-of-the-art macro planner routinely emits physically unrealisable sub-goals. To resolve this, we introduce Metro-WM, a hierarchical framework that issues sub-goals by retrieving genuine states from prior experience rather than generating ungrounded latent vectors. Specifically, Metro-WM constructs a graph whose vertices are observed frames from offline expert demonstrations or random-action trajectories, allowing frames from different episodes to be connected and stitched into routes to the goal. Planning over the full graph also makes the system highly robust to execution errors: if the micro planner drifts off course, Metro-WM instantly finds a new optimal path from the current state. Our experiments show that Metro-WM achieves superior long-horizon success rates of up to 37.33 percentage points over the next best hierarchical approach while being up to 10.9 times faster, requiring both 13-56 times less offline compute and fewer tuned hyperparameters. Additional analysis reveals that Metro-WM finds shorter paths than the offline demonstrations, outperforms an oracle relying on the query's own demonstration, and maintains robust performance under extremely sparse dataset conditions.





## Online Constraint Optimization-Based Indirect-Adaptive Robust Control for Autonomous Vehicles with Unknown Friction
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22675v1
- **Authors**: ['Yupeng Zhou', 'I-Chia Chang', 'Ziran Wang', 'Bin Yao']
- **Abstrat**: Friction uncertainty is a significant challenge for autonomous vehicle control, undermining feasibility and performance. This paper proposes a novel two-layer control framework to achieve vehicle motion control under large friction uncertainty by combining optimization-based planning with indirect adaptive robust control (IARC). The upper layer solves a constrained optimal control problem to generate a feasible trajectory that satisfies all the constraints in a time-optimal manner along the desired path. The lower layer, IARC, adapts the unknown parameters related to friction uncertainty and combines model compensation with robust feedback to ensure transient and steady-state tracking performance. An estimation monitor is designed to prevent poor learning when data indicate a model mismatch, while a smooth parameter reset reduces the estimated tire capacity and limits abrupt tightening of constraints. The updated parameters are fed back into the model and constraints in the planner, enabling trajectory replanning as friction conditions change. Simulation results indicate that the proposed method achieves accurate tracking of the desired trajectory and online friction adaptation under large friction uncertainty while ensuring time-optimal vehicle motion. The simulation results demos are available at https://github.com/TedZhou0603/CO-IARC.
