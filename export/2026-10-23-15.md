## DuoCook: An Overcooked-Inspired Benchmark for Visual Planning and Coordination
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31511v1
- **Authors**: ['Ziyi Jiao', 'Heng Zhang', 'Drishti Regmi', 'Xiaokang Ye', 'Lianhui Qin', 'Peter Yichen Chen']
- **Abstrat**: Coordinating two players in a cooking task requires a vision-language agent to track changing scene states, sequence recipe steps, and allocate shared resources under a time budget. We introduce an Overcooked-inspired benchmark for evaluating these capabilities in Unreal Engine environments constructed using coding agents. A single vision-language model controls both players from top-down visual observations, selecting high-level actions while the engine executes navigation. The benchmark contains 20 maps in five categories. These categories support evaluation of centralized coordination under equipment contention, changing terrain, and handoff dependencies. A prop legend and pot ingredient-count indicators provide explicit visual cues, while grid-based destinations support spatial grounding. The evaluation compares four VLMs on all 20 maps, measuring task score, order completion, delivery precision, action acceptance rate, and model API expenditure. A memory ablation on the same 20 maps examines the effect of a shared, self-authored notebook while retaining six-response action history in both conditions. The strongest evaluated model, \texttt{gpt-6-astra}, achieves 26.2\% mean order completion. Notebook memory has mixed score effects across models, with positive mean differences for Claude and Qwen and negative differences for GPT and Gemini.





## Multimodal Progress-Residual Fusion for Anticipating Navigation Retreat in Robotic Flexible Endoscopy
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31028v1
- **Authors**: ['Chi Kit Ng', 'Tianchun Wu', 'Jiatan Sun', 'Yuming Sun', 'Jianru Zhang', 'Hongliang Ren']
- **Abstrat**: Flexible robotic endoscopes provide access through tortuous anatomy, where friction, deformation, and wall interaction separate commanded insertion from useful distal progress. Operators interpret this mismatch and retreat to recover a navigable configuration. Recognizing the approach to that decision requires relating motor intent to distal response and the local visual scene. We introduce Multimodal Progress-Residual Fusion (MPRF), a causal model that anticipates operator retreat from commands, electromagnetic (EM) tracking, and endoscopic video. A nominal-progress model predicts the response expected in normal or successful navigation; a temporal predictor combines its EM residual with all three observed modalities. In a 150-episode colon-phantom collection, MPRF achieves 0.580 +/- 0.007 AUPRC across three seeds, compared with 0.511 for the strongest reduced-modality control. Complete multimodal histories provide the principal ranking advantage, while the residual adds an explicit intent-response representation with a small, uncertain increment over raw fusion. Analysis of frozen alarms relates warning timing to the behavioral label and explains the increase in event recall under a longer matching window. These results establish a basis for behavioral assistance during operator-controlled navigation.





## TRACE: Traversability-aware Reactive Navigation via VLM-driven Adaptive Constraints Estimation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30994v1
- **Authors**: ['Mario A. V. Saucedo', 'Midhun Edatharam Kunnath', 'Elias Small', 'Akash Patel', 'Shruti Kotpalliwar', 'Christoforos Kanellakis', 'George Nikolakopoulos']
- **Abstrat**: This paper presents TRACE, a traversability-aware reactive navigation framework designed for the autonomous operation of ground robots in unstructured forest environments. Unlike traditional approaches, TRACE leverages VLM-based confidence maps from positive and negative natural-language prompt sets and combines them through evidential fusion to produce a dense traversability map that prioritizes the terrain based on signs of previously traversed paths. Traversability-annotated points are then extracted and used to derive the left and right boundaries of the traversable path via percentile binning followed by spline or regularized polynomial fitting. These boundaries are then incorporated as soft barrier constraints within an NMPC formulation, enabling safe and adaptive path following in complex natural environments. Rigorous experimental trials carried out in real-life forest environments demonstrate TRACE ability for smooth navigation in cluttered, deformable, and visually ambiguous terrains.





## H-2DGS: Height-Field Gaussian Splatting for Sharp and Deformable Neurosurgical Surface Reconstruction
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30910v1
- **Authors**: ['Zhang Zichuan', 'Bart Brouwers', 'Rembrandt Bakker', 'Maud Landers', 'Geert-Jan Rutten', 'Maxime Chamberland']
- **Abstrat**: Neurosurgical navigation commonly presents magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) as 2D slices, making it difficult to relate the anatomy in those slices to the cortical surface observed through the surgical microscope. We seek a common 3D visualization that combines a photorealistic reconstruction of the exposed cortex during tumor surgery with whole-brain MRI context and diffusion tractography pathways. This requires the reconstructed cortex to form a clear boundary around the brain anatomy rather than a diffuse volume that obscures internal structures. Conventional 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) can reproduce observed views but, under limited surgical view coverage, may distribute opacity through a thick and geometrically ambiguous band. We introduce height-field 2D Gaussian Splatting (H-2DGS), a three-stage pipeline that concentrates a robust 3DGS reconstruction, fuses its depth into a shared truncated signed distance field (TSDF) surface, and represents that surface with curved, surface-oriented Gaussian primitives. Separate height, color, and opacity bases provide local geometry and appearance, while a neighbor-transition objective coordinates overlapping patches. The explicit surface further supports postoperative alignment: MRI-derived depth deforms the reconstruction, and tracked-color supervision with alternating optimization preserves its appearance. Across five real neurosurgical cases, H-2DGS gives low multi-view depth inconsistency, a compact 7.97MB representation, and interactive rendering at 29--42ms per frame. The resulting sharp, deformable surface integrates clearly with tractography and MRI anatomy, including a postoperative resection cavity.





## Evaluation of Front-End Feature Matching for Lunar Visual SLAM
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30774v1
- **Authors**: ['Guillem Casadesus Vila', 'Grace Gao']
- **Abstrat**: Lunar rovers will primarily rely on cameras for localization until navigation satellite services are deployed, and will continue to need them afterwards for relative navigation, hazard detection, and surface operations. Visual odometry (VO) and Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) mostly estimate motion by matching features between frames. However, feature matchers are developed on urban, indoor and driving scenes, planetary studies compare them on orbital registration or within complete SLAM systems, and real lunar imagery with reference poses is scarce, so matching between rover frames on low-texture, harshly lit terrain, and its effect on VO, has not been compared. We benchmark eleven classical and learned detector-matcher combinations on nine synthetic, lunar-testbed, and terrestrial-analogue datasets, and a feature tracker, scoring pairwise pose accuracy against baseline and the trajectory error of the resulting VO. Under a common rotation-only metric, the SuperPoint pairings outperform SIFT with ratio-tested FLANN matching on both lunar testbeds and one analogue site, with smaller or unresolved differences on synthetic data. When rotation and translation direction are scored jointly, this SIFT pairing exceeds both SuperPoint pairings on four synthetic datasets, so synthetic results alone do not establish relative performance on real rover imagery. In stereo VO, the SuperPoint pairings are not consistently more accurate than SIFT, but only they keep tracking on all eight sequential datasets. Pairings with classical detectors depend on lighting while SuperPoint changes little: a low spotlight makes them more accurate than diffuse light on the same testbed views, consistent with more contrast and detected keypoints. SuperPoint with LightGlue is the fastest learned combination (16 ms per frame), and DINOv2 retrieval proposals still need geometric verification.





## PATH: Preference-Aware Traversability Estimation for Robotic Teleoperation in Off-Road Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30605v1
- **Authors**: ['Xianghao Meng', 'Wenshuo Wang', 'Cheng Shao', 'Lewei Feng', 'Qingxiao Liu', 'Canyu Wang', 'Junqiang Xi']
- **Abstrat**: Traversability estimation is critical for ground vehicle teleoperation, yet existing methods often neglect the human operator's preferences for speed and risk tolerance. To address this gap, we propose PATH-Net, a Preference-Aware Traversability estimation Hierarchical Network that adaptively generates preference-conditioned traversability cost maps and speed profiles for off-road teleoperation. Our framework integrates visual and geometric bird's-eye-view features with short-term historical vehicle states through a multi-head shared-backbone architecture. Our PATH-Net infers traversability cost maps while optimizing speed recommendations by using maximum entropy inverse reinforcement learning and sparse speed supervision. Our method incorporates operator preference via an asymmetric feature aggregation mechanism, splitting inference into a cost branch that prioritizes high-risk traversability regions and a speed branch that outputs preference-adjusted recommended speeds. Experiments with 15 operators across 225 teleoperated trajectories show alignment with user-specific navigation strategies, achieving modified Hausdorff distance of 0.17 m (aggressive) and 0.15 m (conservative). In the questionnaire assessment, participants reported lower NASA-TLX workload scores with PATH-Net recommendations than without recommendations, decreasing from 34.7 to 22.2. These results support preference-conditioned trajectory and speed recommendation for off-road teleoperation.





## OASIS: Online Anchor-centric Scene Inference System for Open-Vocabulary Retrieval and Navigation in Dynamic Environments
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30589v1
- **Authors**: ['Kaiyao Liao', 'Qingfeng Li', 'Chen Chen', 'Xiaoze Wu', 'Qing Sun', 'Jianwei Niu']
- **Abstrat**: Reliable robotic mapping in dynamic real-world environments is essential for embodied intelligence and long-term autonomy. However, existing methods often involve a trade-off: lightweight online mapping is efficient but captures limited semantic context, while dense mapping provides richer semantics at higher computational cost and is more vulnerable to distractors. They also struggle to relocalize objects after scene changes. To address these challenges, we propose the Online Anchor-centric Scene Inference System (OASIS), a unified system for online mapping, complex open-vocabulary retrieval, and navigation in dynamic environments. Motivated by the observation that indoor scenes are organized around stable semantic anchors, OASIS represents the environment using a sparse set of anchor nodes, each maintaining a unified multimodal representation that integrates semantic descriptions, visual features, and geometric states for reasoning, verification, and navigation. Its compact anchor-centric memory supports on-demand evidence extraction and hierarchical retrieval, reducing redundant storage while improving retrieval reliability. To handle scene dynamics, OASIS further reuses transient observations collected during navigation to relocalize moved objects. Experiments in both simulation and real-world environments, including standard retrieval benchmarks and navigation tasks under both static and dynamic settings, demonstrate that our system can perform complex open-vocabulary retrieval and navigation, while also enabling relocation of displaced objects after scene changes.





## MeSS: City Mesh-Guided Outdoor Scene Generation with Cross-View Consistent Diffusion
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15169v5
- **Authors**: ['Xuyang Chen', 'Zhijun Zhai', 'Kaixuan Zhou', 'Zengmao Wang', 'Jianan He', 'Dong Wang', 'Yanfeng Zhang', 'Mingwei Sun', 'Xuqin Wang', 'Rüdiger Westermann', 'Tao Wu', 'Konrad Schindler', 'Liqiu Meng']
- **Abstrat**: Mesh models have become increasingly accessible for numerous cities; however, the lack of realistic textures restricts their application in virtual urban navigation and autonomous driving. To address this, this paper proposes MeSS (Meshbased Scene Synthesis) for generating high-quality, styleconsistent outdoor scenes with city mesh models serving as the geometric prior. While image and video diffusion models can leverage spatial layouts (such as depth maps or HD maps) as control conditions to generate street-level perspective views, they are not directly applicable to 3D scene generation. Video diffusion models excel at synthesizing consistent view sequences that depict scenes but often struggle to adhere to predefined camera paths or align accurately with rendered control videos. Building on this insight, our approach enhances image diffusion models by improving cross-view consistency. The pipeline comprises three key stages: first, we generate geometrically consistent sparse views using Cascaded Outpainting ControlNets; second, we propagate denser intermediate views via a component dubbed AGInpaint; and third, we globally eliminate visual inconsistencies (e.g., varying exposure) using the GCAlign module. Concurrently with generation, a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) scene is reconstructed by initializing Gaussian balls on the mesh surface. Our method outperforms existing approaches in both geometric alignment and generation quality. Once synthesized, the scene can be rendered in diverse styles through relighting and style transfer techniques. project page: https://albertchen98.github.io/mess/

## Event-based SLAM Benchmark for High-Speed Maneuvers
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24033v2
- **Authors**: ['Sheng Zhong', 'Junkai Niu', 'Guillermo Gallego', 'Kaizhen Sun', 'Yang Yi', 'Zhiqiang Miao', 'Dewen Hu', 'Yaonan Wang', 'Davide Scaramuzza', 'Yi Zhou']
- **Abstrat**: Event-based cameras are bio-inspired sensors with pixels that independently and asynchronously respond to brightness changes at microsecond resolution, offering the potential to handle visual tasks in high-speed maneuvering scenarios. Existing event-based approaches, although successful in mitigating motion blur caused by high-speed maneuvers, suffer from many limitations. Some of them highlight a success of pose tracking for a fronto-parallel fast shaking camera closed to the structure, while others assume pure (optionally aggressive) three-degree-of-freedom rotations. The former requires persistent local map visibility within the field of view (FOV), whereas the latter fails to generalize to six-degree-of-freedom (6-DoF) motions where both linear and angular velocities may be large. Consequently, current successes do not fully demonstrate that event-based state estimation under arbitrary aggressive maneuvers is a fully solved problem. To quantitatively assess the extent to which the potential of event cameras has been unlocked, we conduct a thorough analysis of state-of-the-art (SOTA) event-based visual odometry (VO)/visual-inertial odometry (VIO) methods and report shortcomings in current public datasets. Furthermore, we introduce a benchmarking framework for event-based state estimation, called EvSLAM, characterized by sufficient variation in data collection platforms, diverse extreme lighting scenarios, and a wide scope of challenging motion patterns under a clear and rigorous definition of high-speed maneuvers for mobile robots, along with a novel evaluation metric designed to fairly assess the operational limits of event-based solutions. This framework benchmarks state-of-the-art methods, yielding insights into optimal architectures and persistent challenges.

## Dispersive Tree Agreements for Multi-Robot Coordination
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31540v1
- **Authors**: ['Shashank A. Deshpande', 'Blake T. Edwards', 'Jonathan P. How']
- **Abstrat**: Multi-robot coordination of nonlinear systems is naturally formulated as joint-space optimal control subject to constraints over groups of robots. Existing methods that approach joint optimality remain largely centralized, offline, and confined to collision-free motion planning, while decentralized, real-time counterparts target safe navigation without pursuing the joint optimum. This paper develops a decentralized, real-time planner that approaches the joint optimum as an anytime process. We extend a recent single-robot framework that searches dispersive trees of forward-propagated commands for differentially flat systems to coordinate a multi-robot team near-optimally. We first show that the inter-robot coupling can be effectively replaced by some local constraints, under which locally grown dispersive trees contain a near-optimal agreement. It is this structure that the robots pursue by decentralized column generation, negotiating rents on the shared space that guide the search of each robot's tree. The negotiation settles on an agreement whose near-optimality is certified at quiescence. Our protocol, WarpWeaver, implements this negotiation as an asynchronous real-time loop over a receding horizon, and demonstrates effective closure to quiesced agreements even when real-time deadlines cut the negotiation short. In simulation, WarpWeaver reduces total travel time by up to 22% against centralized offline planners on the Dynobench suite and by up to 37% against a decentralized real-time baseline, and nearly doubles the success rate of a task-specific planner on graph connectivity. Hardware experiments on six robots planning onboard demonstrate real-world implementability.





## Point-Level Refinable Semantic Mapping for Spatial Understanding
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31476v1
- **Authors**: ['Taimeng Fu', 'Yi Du', 'Zitong Zhan', 'Qiwei Du', 'Shaoshu Su', 'Zhipeng Zhao', 'Chen Wang']
- **Abstrat**: Open-vocabulary 3D semantic maps have emerged as a key spatial memory representation for long-horizon robot autonomy, enabling robots to reason about objects, their spatial relationships, and past observations across time. However, existing object-centric semantic maps are primarily designed for static environments and rely on an append-only point integration strategy, where newly observed points are accumulated but previously integrated points are rarely reevaluated. Consequently, perception errors accumulate over time, and outdated points persist after objects move or disappear. Although recent dynamic mapping methods update object-level representations in changing scenes, they do not explicitly revise noisy or obsolete points within each object. We propose an online refinable semantic mapping framework that continuously refines object representations through probabilistic point-level confidence updating. Each object point maintains a confidence score that is updated online using multi-view temporal evidence: consistent observations reinforce reliable points, while inconsistent segmentation masks, depth measurements, and visibility cues suppress spurious and outdated points. Combined with object tracking and re-identification to preserve instance identities under motion and relocation, our framework enables object-centric semantic maps to be continuously refined rather than monotonically accumulated. Experiments demonstrate improved mapping accuracy over the previous method by 3.46% on Replica and 2.55% on ScanNet, show robustness in dynamic environments and under perception noise, and provide an effective spatial memory for object navigation and embodied question-answering with success rates of 82.4% and 76.7%.

## Proprioceptive Safe Active Navigation and Exploration for Planetary Environments
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08905v2
- **Authors**: ['Matthew Y. Jiang', 'Feifei Qian', 'Shipeng Liu']
- **Abstrat**: Deformable granular terrains introduce significant locomotion and immobilization risks in planetary exploration and are difficult to detect via remote sensing (e.g., vision). Legged robots can sense terrain properties through leg-terrain interactions during locomotion, offering a direct means to assess traversability in deformable environments. How to systematically exploit this interaction-derived information for navigation planning, however, remains underexplored. We address this gap by presenting PSANE, a Proprioceptive Safe Active Navigation and Exploration framework that leverages leg-terrain interaction measurements for safe navigation and exploration in unknown deformable environments. PSANE learns a traversability model via Gaussian Process regression to estimate and certify safe regions and identify exploration frontiers online, and integrates these estimates with a reactive controller for real-time navigation. Frontier selection is formulated as a multi-objective optimization that balances safe-set expansion probability and goal-directed cost, with subgoals selected via scalarization over the Pareto-optimal frontier set. PSANE safely explores unknown granular terrain and reaches specified goals using only proprioceptively estimated traversability, while achieving performance improvements over baseline methods.





## ProTrav: Probabilistic Traversability with Elevation Uncertainty Propagation for Risk-aware Off-road Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30839v1
- **Authors**: ['Chuanlin Zuo', 'Miloš Prágr', 'Arun Kumar Singh', 'Mohammad Loni', 'Reza Ghabcheloo']
- **Abstrat**: Reliable traversability estimation is essential for autonomous navigation on unstructured terrain. State-of-the-art approaches typically evaluate traversability from deterministic terrain elevation, thereby overlooking spatial correlations in the terrain and failing to account for how elevation uncertainty propagates through nonlinear traversability mappings. We propose ProTrav, a probabilistic terrain representation that uses function-space sampling to generate spatially correlated terrain realizations and propagate elevation uncertainty to traversability cost. First, a Sparse Variational Gaussian Process (SVGP) reconstructs a probabilistic elevation field from observations. Then, posterior function-space sampling generates spatially correlated terrain realizations, preserving spatial dependencies between neighboring locations while propagating elevation uncertainty through geometric features into traversability cost. Consequently, a distribution of traversability cost at arbitrary location is established, where the mean and standard deviation quantify the expected traversability cost and associated uncertainty, respectively. A risk term is defined by moment-based Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) and incorporated into a Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) controller for risk-aware navigation. Simulation experiments demonstrate navigation success rates exceeding 90\% on different terrains, compared with below 40\% for a baseline, while reducing attitude variations. Furthermore, the proposed approach is validated on a robot traversing real-world off-road terrain.





## Continuous-Time Motion Planning and Steering with Global Convergence
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30607v1
- **Authors**: ['Mohsen Amiri', 'Sima Didari', 'Davoud Ataee Tarzanagh', 'Hankyu Moon', 'Yeongdae Kwon']
