## PlanarCityLoc: Breaking Silhouette Ambiguity in LoD Aerial Localization via 3D Planes
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26729v1
- **Authors**: ['Oussema Dhaouadi', 'Shao Jie Hu-Chen', 'Jakob Gollreiter', 'Samir Jusufi', 'Nikolas Rieger', 'Caroline Wendlandt', 'Christian Wender', 'Jingfeng Mao', 'Guangming Wang', 'Zuria Bauer', 'Marc Pollefeys', 'Daniel Cremers', 'Olaf Wysocki']
- **Abstrat**: Aerial visual localization is crucial for autonomous drone navigation in urban environments where GNSS signals are often degraded. Lightweight textureless Level-of-Detail (LoD) city models provide a geometric map alternative to textured assets. Existing silhouette-based methods rely on image-space alignment, which can leave different camera poses indistinguishable and favor incorrect poses when segmentation errors distort the building outlines. We present PlanarCityLoc, which moves beyond silhouette overlap by leveraging the planar geometry of building facades and roofs. Following geometry-aware initialization, we match image-side planar patches to LoD model planes and use the resulting 3D-to-2D correspondences to refine pose with Perspective-n-Point (PnP). The framework uses off-the-shelf geometry and segmentation models without dataset-specific training or fine-tuning. Experiments on UAVD4L-LoDv2 and Swiss-EPFLv2 achieve state-of-the-art localization recall among the evaluated off-the-shelf LoD-based methods. By grounding image-space alignment in 3D planes, PlanarCityLoc enables more accurate aerial localization with lightweight, textureless city maps.





## CAOS-SLAM: Class-Agnostic Object-Aware Real-Time vSLAM for Dynamic Environments
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26633v1
- **Authors**: ['Gaia Borghi', 'João Carlos Virgolino Soares', 'Angelo Bratta', 'Shahab Heshmati-Alamdari', 'Claudio Semini']
- **Abstrat**: Robust visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (vSLAM) in dynamic environments faces challenges due to the presence of moving objects that may introduce erroneous observations, corrupt the map reconstruction and overall degrade localization accuracy. Existing vSLAM approaches often rely on predefined semantic classes or costly pixel-wise classification, limiting their robustness or applicability in real-world scenarios. This paper presents CAOS-SLAM, a dynamic vSLAM framework that extends ORB-SLAM3 with a hybrid perception pipeline to detect, track, and filter both semantically known and previously unseen moving objects in real-time. On-demand panoptic segmentation is performed for object discovery, with candidate unknown objects instantiated through depth-based clustering of unclassified regions. Between panoptic updates, persistent object tracks are updated using lightweight detection when available or temporal mask propagation based on world-frame Kalman prediction, depth consistency, and appearance similarity. The estimated object motion is then used to reject dynamic features from the SLAM pipeline. Experimental results on multiple dynamic RGB-D datasets and real-world experiments demonstrate improved localization accuracy over state-of-the-art dynamic vSLAM approaches, achieving up to 59% lower localization error in scenes characterized by unknown dynamic objects and up to 88% lower error in heavily occluded scenes, while achieving a median processing rate of 30 FPS on a laptop. Furthermore, the tracking module provides estimates of object trajectories and velocities, supporting potential future applications in perception, navigation, and motion-planning tasks in dynamic environments. Code and dataset will be released upon acceptance.





## ReInsVLN: Revisiting Historical Evidence for Training-Free Vision-and-Language Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26545v1
- **Authors**: ['Siwei Cheng', 'Hao Wu', 'Zitong Qi', 'Xinming Zhang', 'Haoyu Xi', 'Peiran Yin', 'Haozhe Hu', 'Xudong Wang', 'Xiaoyu Shen', 'Wei Zhang']
- **Abstrat**: Vision-and-language navigation (VLN) relies on retaining and using relevant observations. Yet growing histories introduce redundancy, and retained information may remain underused. We introduce ReInsBench, a diagnostic benchmark that examines history utilization through controlled continuation trials. Its results show that history-review reinstruction can recover navigation failures using the existing task context. Motivated by this finding, we propose ReInsVLN, a training-free framework supporting memory construction and utilization in frozen navigators. Coverage- and Instruction-Aware Compression constructs compact visual context through temporal redundancy reduction, coverage-aware protection, and instruction-guided token selection. Forgetting-Inspired History Reminders guide navigators to revisit retained observations according to a predefined schedule, without additional model calls. On R2R Val-Unseen, ReInsVLN improves success rates by 8.48 and 3.37 percentage points over the native StreamVLN and InternVLA-N1, reaching 64.27% and 64.44%, respectively. Visual compression largely preserves navigation performance while reducing prefill latency and KV cache memory by up to 21.77% and 32.25%, respectively, on matched observation workloads. Real-world trials on humanoid and quadruped robots further support the applicability of ReInsVLN to sequential instruction following and ordinal navigation.





## Learning Geometry-Aware Planning Representations for Monocular Flight with Cross-Modal Supervision
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26508v1
- **Authors**: ['Wencan Feng', 'Bailing Tian', 'Yulin Hui', 'Zhiyu Li', 'Junjie Lu']
- **Abstrat**: Existing high-speed navigation systems for micro aerial vehicles (MAVs) commonly rely on depth cameras, stereo vision, or LiDAR to obtain explicit geometric information, while monocular cameras offer a lighter and lower-cost alternative. However, monocular flight remains challenging because RGB images offer no direct metric cues and visual appearance varies considerably across environments. We propose a geometry-aware monocular visual planning framework that predicts trajectories from two image frames, their relative pose, and the MAV state. Without explicitly reconstructing depth, the proposed method complements the downstream planning objective with feature-level geometric supervision by transferring the planning-relevant representation learned by a privileged depth expert to the RGB planner through cross-modal distillation. The framework first trains a depth-based geometric expert, then distills its planning features into a monocular branch using paired RGB-D observations, and finally adapts the planner to the downstream trajectory planning objective. This design encourages the policy to learn planning-relevant geometric features, enabling collision avoidance. We further use a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) simulator to render visual observations for data collection, paired RGB-D supervision, and simulation evaluation. The planner trained in simulation is then deployed in real-world environments without real-world fine-tuning, achieving obstacle avoidance at speeds of up to 6.5 m/s.





## LaMER: Language-Conditioned Multi-Expert Routing for Weakly Supervised Referring Expression Comprehension
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26404v1
- **Authors**: ['Yu Yu Chang', 'Tzu-Hsuan Weng', 'Youssef Mohamed Amin', 'Mohaned Ahmed Elsayed', 'Hung-Hsuan Chen', 'Min-Te Sun', 'Sherine Shawky']
- **Abstrat**: Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) localizes the image region described by a natural language expression, underpinning applications such as autonomous navigation, human-robot interaction, and medical image analysis. Under weak supervision, REC is challenged by the visual-linguistic semantic gap and the difficulty of inferring region-text correspondences from coarse image-level signals alone. Moreover, no single visual encoder captures all complementary properties---semantic alignment, fine-grained texture, and boundary awareness---while naively fusing multiple pretrained encoders yields heterogeneous, hard-to-reconcile representations. We propose LaMER, a weakly supervised REC framework that dynamically aggregates four frozen expert encoders through a language-conditioned Cross-Modal Routing Fusion module, adapting expert selection to each referring expression without instance-level bounding-box supervision. A Cell-Text Matching Head with Negative Anchor Augmentation, trained with a multi-objective loss combining contrastive, reconstruction, alignment, and load-balancing terms, further stabilizes optimization. LaMER achieves 77.67\%, 62.81\%, and 69.49\% Acc@IoU\(\geq 0.5\) on RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg, surpassing DViN and WeakMCN by up to 16.49 and 14.49 points, with ablations validating each component.





## GemNav: Discrete-Token Visual Robot Navigation using a Multimodal Large Language Model
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06882v3
- **Authors**: ['Peter Bohm', 'Saimunur Rahman', 'Abdelwahed Khamis', 'Sagun Man Singh Shrestha', 'Chris McCool', 'Peyman Moghadam']
- **Abstrat**: Visual navigation policies built on large pretrained models increasingly rely on large cross-embodiment datasets and expressive action-generation mechanisms. In this paper, we ask whether a pretrained Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) can be adapted efficiently for robot navigation by rethinking the representation and construction of navigation actions. We introduce GemNav, a visual navigation policy that represents each robot-local waypoint coordinate as a categorical distribution over discrete metric value bins and soft-decodes it into a continuous coordinate for execution. This probabilistic representation retains likelihood information over alternative coordinate values while providing a compact action space suitable for robot navigation. We further construct training trajectories using metric-distance goal sampling rather than fixed future frame offsets, exposing the policy to varying physical navigation horizons independent of dataset frame rate and robot speed. GemNav is trained on a single 8.7-hour public SCAND dataset, roughly three orders of magnitude smaller than competing cross-embodiment datasets. Extensive offline and closed-loop experiments show improved out-of-distribution generalization over frame-based sampling and substantially more reliable obstacle navigation than a matched continuous regression head. The same policy transfers without per-platform retraining across unseen environments and three physically distinct robot platforms.





## EmbodiedWorld-200K: Toward Embodied World Agents via Embodied-Planning Dataset and Interactive World Models
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25959v1
- **Authors**: ['X. Feng', 'J. Tang', 'Z. Lin', 'L. Chen', 'S. Shao', 'Q. Lu', 'K. Huang']
- **Abstrat**: Building embodied agents that can perceive, reason, and act across diverse 3D worlds is a central research direction toward artificial general intelligence. Yet most existing work remains confined to specific tasks in a single scene and transfers poorly to new environments, leaving general embodied agents for open-world settings under-explored. We refer to such agents as Embodied World Agents (EWAs), whose defining property is the ability to understand diverse visual environments and execute general actions in open-world conditions. To advance EWA research, we study open-world embodied navigation-planning and release EmbodiedWorld-200K, a large-scale dataset of over 200K samples spanning diverse scenes. Each example is equipped with multi-level annotations, ranging from low-level camera-motion trajectories to high-level semantic instructions. The dataset also provides unified evaluation splits and task-specific metrics, supporting offline pretraining and fair evaluation of EWAs. Beyond offline supervision, a defining characteristic of an EWA is its interaction with the environment; training solely on offline data is therefore insufficient, especially for generalizing to scenes not covered in the training distribution. Building dedicated real interactive environments, however, is often prohibitively expensive. To address this, we propose a novel training scheme that employs a video world model as a low-cost environment proxy and performs interactive reinforcement-learning (RL) post-training of the EWA within it. Empirically, pretraining on EmbodiedWorld-200K substantially improves the open-world embodied planning ability of the EWA, and further RL post-training inside a world model yields clear gains on out-of-distribution scenes. The dataset, baseline checkpoints, and training and evaluation code are available at https://xiaokunfeng.github.io/EmbodiedWorld-200K/.





## ScaleNav: Scalable Internet Video Pretraining for Language-Conditioned Local Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25690v1
- **Authors**: ['Yihui Yang', 'Yuanhong Yu', 'Jiawei Wang', 'Zhongrui Yu', 'Jia Shi', 'Kun Zhan', 'Ruizhen Hu', 'Xiaowei Zhou', 'Yu Liu', 'Sida Peng']
- **Abstrat**: We study language-conditioned local navigation, where a robot must navigate to a text-specified target visible in its initial observation and stop at the described location. End-to-end navigation policies require diverse training data to generalize across scenes, yet real-robot data is costly to collect and simulated data leaves a sim-to-real gap. Internet egocentric videos offer a scalable source of real-world navigation experience. Our key insight is to learn goal-directed visual dynamics from Internet egocentric videos, and use these dynamics to support robot action generation. To this end, we propose ScaleNav, a unified world-action framework with three-stage training. We first perform instruction-conditioned video pretraining on these videos to learn how observations evolve as the camera approaches instructed targets across diverse real-world scenes. Action alignment then uses action-labeled simulated data to train the model to predict executable action chunks. Joint fine-tuning subsequently optimizes all trainable components together for navigation. ScaleNav achieves success rates of 86.5% on a single-view adaptation of Short-Horizon OVON and 94.9% on our HabitatGS-LN benchmark, outperforming the evaluated baselines on both benchmarks. We further deploy and evaluate ScaleNav on a real quadruped robot, with results supporting the model's ability to generalize to real-world navigation. Code and videos are available at https://zju3dv.github.io/ScaleNav/





## Revealing and Bridging the Physical Executability Gap in Vision-Language Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25687v1
- **Authors**: ['Gang He', 'Zhile Cheng', 'Kepeng Xu', 'Zhenyang Liu', 'Yu-Wing Tai']
- **Abstrat**: Continuous vision-and-language navigation (VLN) is typically evaluated by goal-reaching success, assuming it reflects an agent's ability to execute language-grounded behavior. We show that this assumption can fail: permissive contact models in current simulators may mask physically infeasible actions, creating a physical executability gap between semantic competence and embodied realization. To bridge this gap, we introduce Visual-Semantic Traversability Alignment (VeSTA), a framework that aligns VLN policies with physically executable futures. We define visual-semantic traversability as the ability to predict instruction-conditioned trajectories that are semantically valid, geometrically feasible, and physically robust. VeSTA addresses this misalignment through two complementary mechanisms: improving executable support in trajectory generation and risk-aware selection among multimodal futures. Specifically, Trajectory Relative Group Policy Optimization uses strict-contact feedback to reshape the candidate distribution toward executable regions, while the Traversability Risk Critic learns a risk-adjusted scalar utility for candidate-wise future selection. Experiments on R2R and RxR reveal substantial dependence of existing VLN agents on simulator contact semantics. Under strict-contact evaluation, VeSTA substantially reduces collision-induced failures while preserving or improving goal-reaching performance, and controlled real-world robot experiments provide evidence that the learned preference transfers beyond simulation.





## AnyNav: Visual Neuro-Symbolic Friction Learning for Off-road Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12654v3
- **Authors**: ['Taimeng Fu', 'Zitong Zhan', 'Zhipeng Zhao', 'Yi Du', 'Shaoshu Su', 'Xiao Lin', 'Ehsan Tarkesh Esfahani', 'Karthik Dantu', 'Souma Chowdhury', 'Chen Wang']
- **Abstrat**: Off-road navigation is critical for a wide range of field robotics applications from planetary exploration to disaster response. However, it remains a longstanding challenge due to unstructured environments and the inherently complex terrain-vehicle interactions. Traditional physics-based methods struggle to accurately capture the nonlinear dynamics underlying these interactions, while purely data-driven approaches often overfit to specific motion patterns, vehicle geometries, or platforms, limiting their generalization in diverse, real-world scenarios. To address these limitations, we introduce AnyNav, a vision-based friction estimation and navigation framework grounded in neuro-symbolic principles. Our approach integrates neural networks for visual perception with symbolic physical models for reasoning about terrain-vehicle dynamics. To enable self-supervised learning in real-world settings, we adopt the imperative learning paradigm, employing bilevel optimization to train the friction network through physics-based optimization. This explicit incorporation of physical reasoning substantially enhances generalization across terrains, vehicle types, and operational conditions. Leveraging the predicted friction coefficients, we further develop a physics-informed navigation system capable of generating physically feasible, time-efficient paths together with corresponding speed profiles. We demonstrate that AnyNav seamlessly transfers from simulation to real-world robotic platforms, exhibiting strong robustness across different four-wheeled vehicles and diverse off-road environments.





## GOFusion: Real-Time Gaussian-Occupancy Submap Fusion for Fisher-Informed Autonomous Robot Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25261v1
- **Authors**: ['Boxin Bu', 'Simon Boche', 'Haoming Zhang', 'Jaehyung Jung', 'Jiaxin Wei', 'Hannah Schieber', 'Daniel Roth', 'Angela P. Schoellig', 'Stefan Leutenegger']
- **Abstrat**: In this letter, we present GOFusion, a real-time visual mapping system for autonomous robot navigation that anchors Gaussian primitives to local occupancy maps, providing both spatial awareness for path planning and high-fidelity appearance that enables information-driven robot navigation. GOFusion builds 3D Gaussian maps from pose estimates and local occupancy maps obtained via visual-inertial SLAM and enables pose corrections to maintain both geometric and visual consistency. Furthermore, it restricts the 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization to active submaps to bound GPU memory usage. Building on the appearance-rich Gaussian map, we introduce a rasterizer-integrated Fisher approximation that quantifies per-primitive sensitivity online, serving as an information-driven heuristic for Gaussian pruning and next-best-view planning. We evaluate GOFusion on public datasets and in real-world drone flights. It not only outperforms several baselines in mapping quality, but also achieves real-time, high-fidelity and uncertainty-aware mapping at scale. Finally, we demonstrate fully autonomous drone exploration for Gaussian map refinement using the proposed Fisher-informed heuristic. Code is available at https://github.com/Bennnoo/GOFusion. We provide a supplementary video demonstration at https://youtu.be/pLQKbxQvb6I.





## Map2Vid: Embodied Navigation Video Generation with Human-Friendly Motion Control
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25223v1
- **Authors**: ['Yulin Dai', 'Yuqian Fu', 'Tianwen Qian', 'Zhen Xing', 'Bojia Zi', 'Xiaoling Wang']
- **Abstrat**: Controllable video generation has recently achieved impressive visual quality, yet existing control paradigms remain poorly suited to embodied navigation. Text prompts are spatially ambiguous, while camera-trajectory control relies on low-level motion specifications that are unintuitive for human users and difficult to manipulate. In this work, we introduce Map2Vid, a novel task that reformulates embodied navigation video generation as a BEV-map-to-video synthesis problem, together with a large-scale benchmark for systematic evaluation. By using BEV maps, which provide a visual representation of global scene layout and intended navigation routes, Map2Vid enables human-friendly navigation video generation. To support this task, we construct a dedicated dataset with over 100K navigation sequences across 61 indoor scenes, each paired with egocentric videos, BEV maps, camera trajectories and language instructions. We further develop a unified framework that integrates multiple control signals, together with a curriculum training strategy that progressively weakens non-map inputs, enabling flexible inference under both full conditioning and map-only input. Extensive experiments across multiple foundation generation models demonstrate that controllable navigation video generation via BEV map is a viable and promising direction.





## Can MLLMs Find Their Way in a City? Exploring Emergent Navigation from Web-Scale Knowledge
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15933v4
- **Authors**: ['Dwip Dalal', 'Utkarsh Mishra', 'Narendra Ahuja', 'Nebojsa Jojic']
- **Abstrat**: Leveraging multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to develop embodied agents offers significant promise for addressing complex real-world tasks. However, current evaluation benchmarks remain predominantly language-centric or heavily reliant on simulated environments, rarely probing the nuanced, knowledge-intensive reasoning essential for practical, real-world scenarios. To bridge this critical gap, we introduce the task of Sparsely Grounded Visual Navigation, explicitly designed to evaluate the sequential decision-making abilities of MLLMs in challenging, knowledge-intensive real-world environment. We operationalize this task with CityNav, a comprehensive benchmark encompassing four diverse global cities, specifically constructed to assess raw MLLM-driven agents in city navigation. Agents are required to rely solely on visual inputs and internal multimodal reasoning to sequentially navigate 50+ decision points without additional environmental annotations or specialized architectural modifications. Crucially, agents must autonomously achieve localization through interpreting city-specific cues and recognizing landmarks, perform spatial reasoning, and strategically plan and execute routes to their destinations. Through extensive evaluations, we demonstrate that current state-of-the-art MLLMs, reasoning techniques (e.g., GEPA, chain-of-thought, reflection) and competitive baseline PReP significantly underperform in this challenging setting. To address this, we propose Verbalization of Path(VoP), which explicitly grounds the agent's internal reasoning by probing city-scale cognitive maps (key landmarks and directions toward the destination) from the MLLM, substantially enhancing navigation success. We further validate Mono-Hydra++ in a real ITC building deployment using RealSense RGB plus IMU and demonstrate embedded feasibility by deploying the ONNX/TensorRT FP16 M2H-MX-L perception model at 25.53 FPS on a Jetson Orin NX 16GB. These results show that Mono-Hydra++ can provide real-time metric-semantic mapping and scene graph construction for resource-constrained robotic platforms without relying on active depth sensors.

## SGM-SLAM: Scene Graph Matching for Data-Efficient Distributed SLAM

## AnchorFlow-VIO: Fixed-Template Monocular Visual-Inertial Odometry on LaMAria
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26210v1
- **Authors**: ['Sudhir Pratap Yadav']
- **Abstrat**: We present AnchorFlow-VIO, an open-source Basalt-derived monocular visual-inertial odometry system, and evaluate a uniform configuration on LaMAria. The system uses one grayscale camera and one IMU, combining factory IMU correction, a fixed timing adjustment, windowed initialization and fixed-template patch tracking. Server evaluation of all 63 test recordings yields control-point scores of 47.2, 37.1, 24.8, 35.1 and 15.6 for Short, Medium, Long, Low light and Moving platform. Test pose recalls at 5 m are 84.6%, 69.1%, 53.9% and 62.9% for the four categories reporting dense recall. On ten training recordings, mean control-point score is 41.38 and all 265,694 image timestamps receive poses, including 204 filled poses. One training platform recording diverges and scores zero.
