## SelfThinker: Self-Evolving Reasoning via Reliability-Verified Intrinsic Visual Thoughts
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40115v1
- **Authors**: ['Zhangquan Chen', 'Ruqi Huang']
- **Abstrat**: Unified multimodal models can both generate and understand images within a single set of weights, offering an opportunity to use image synthesis as training supervision for visual comprehension. However, existing visual chain-of-thought methods rely on human-annotated helper images during training and require slow image generation at test time. We present SelfThinker, the first self-evolving framework that turns a unified model's self-generated visual thoughts into stronger understanding without human image annotations. Given a visual problem, SelfThinker samples candidate helper images from its generation pathway and uses its frozen understanding pathway to score each candidate via conditional pointwise mutual information. High-utility visual thoughts then refine the generator via flow matching and serve as privileged teacher context for on-policy self-distillation. At inference, SelfThinker reasons directly from the original image in text without generating intermediate images. Although trained only on small synthetic visual-search and navigation datasets, SelfThinker transfers broadly to natural-image and spatial benchmarks, raising the seven-benchmark average of ThinkMorph-7B from 52.7 to 64.0 (+11.3 points). Controlled image-perturbation probes further show that SelfThinker surpasses ThinkMorph-7B by up to 40.4 points and its pretrained BAGEL-7B backbone by up to 23.2 points across fine-grained and spatial perception tasks. Our code is available at https://github.com/zhangquanchen/SelfThinker.





## Robust Scene Transfer for PointGoal Navigation via Privileged Sensor Guided Contrastive Learning
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05506v3
- **Authors**: ['Amirhossein Zhalehmehrabi', 'Tiziano Tezze', 'Alberto Castellini', 'Alessandro Farinelli']

At deployment, the agent relies only on monocular RGB observations together with standard task-related inputs such as goal position and proprioceptive signals, without access to LiDAR or other privileged sensors. Our method outperforms large pretrained vision models and standard contrastive baselines under severe appearance and semantic shifts. ## WNM-3D: A World Navigation Model with 3D Scene Conditioning for Closed-Loop VLN
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07267v3
- **Authors**: ['Yuehao Huang', 'Yunzi Wu', 'Xiaotao Zhang', 'Xinhai Li', 'Jiankun Dong', 'Sunyao Zhou', 'Jiajun Lv', 'Chi Zhang', 'Chenjia Bai', 'Yong Liu', 'Xuelong Li']
- **Abstrat**: Recent vision-language navigation (VLN) systems increasingly adapt pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) into vision-language-action (VLA) policies that map egocentric observations and language instructions directly to navigation actions. Although semantically capable, such action-centric training does not explicitly model how the agent's visual observations evolve under its predicted motion. Generative world-action models (WAMs) jointly predict future observations and actions, yet existing WAMs for continuous VLN do not condition joint future-view and action generation on geometry-aware representations inferred from the observation history. We present WNM-3D, a generative World Navigation Model with 3D scene conditioning for continuous VLN. A frozen feed-forward geometry encoder extracts cross-view representations from monocular RGB history, and a trainable 3D Scene-to-Token Adapter converts them into a fixed-length scene prefix for the world-action Diffusion Transformer. Through block-causal attention, this prefix remains visible to every future video-action block throughout joint denoising, providing persistent geometric context. We train WNM-3D with supervised world-action learning, DAgger-style adaptation on policy-visited states, and Counterfactual DanceGRPO refinement. Across GN-Bench and VLN-CE, WNM-3D consistently outperforms the backbone-matched WNM-2D counterpart, demonstrating gains from 3D scene conditioning across distinct visual domains. WNM-3D further outperforms strong VLN baselines on GN-Bench and demonstrates closed-loop deployment on a physical robot. Component and stage-wise ablations support the Adapter design and the complementary roles of DAgger-SFT and subsequent reward-guided refinement.

## Online Dense Reinforcement Learning with Planning-Grounding Agent for UI Navigation on Live IOS Apps
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39264v1
- **Authors**: ['Peitong Duan', 'Khashayar Gatmiry', 'Jeffrey Nichols', 'Samy Bengio', 'Emmanuel Abbe']
- **Abstrat**: While frontier vision-language models demonstrate strong capabilities in user interface (UI) navigation, their high inference costs prohibit everyday use. Conversely, smaller models trained via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or offline reinforcement learning often memorize static trajectories, leading to brittle execution and an inability to recover from errors. To address these limitations, we propose a novel online reinforcement learning framework that trains UI navigation agents directly on live iOS applications. Our framework decouples the agent into two specialized components: a Planning agent and a UI Grounding model. We rely on an LLM-as-a-Judge to provide comprehensive reward signals, combining dense, step-level feedback to evaluate intermediate progress with a terminal reward for overall task completion. Evaluations on rigorous benchmarks, including UINavBench, iOSWorld, and AndroidWorld, demonstrate that our online RL trained agents substantially outperform both SFT and terminal-only RL baselines while accelerating training convergence. Notably, our 8B RL agents achieve nearly a sevenfold improvement over the SFT baseline in out-of-distribution generalization to Android environments, while outperforming Gemini-3.5-Flash and tying with Gemini-3.1-Pro on real iOS applications.

## What Happens to the Goal? Distinct Failures of Policy Alignment in Goal Misgeneralization
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19731v2
- **Authors**: ['Julia Barth', 'Armin Sommer', 'Kunvar Thaman', 'Jannik Schilling']
- **Abstrat**: Goal misgeneralization is a common failure mode in which an agent behaves competently out of distribution but pursues a proxy that coincided with the intended goal during training instead of the goal itself. However, we still have a weak understanding of what happens to the intended goal in misgeneralizing agents and what enables them to be recovered without retraining. We study this across eight visual navigation and manipulation tasks, including robot control. While tracing where goal information remains available internally, how strongly it influences the policy, and what role it plays in action selection, we uncover that the goal is lost at one of three different depths. This depth predicts which recovery works in each task. When the goal is followed, but outcompeted by the proxy, steering the existing policy redirects it to the goal's location, raising goal reach from 16.0% to 95.3%. When it is read, but not followed, steering fails, but a lightweight new downstream wiring recovers goal reach from 31.6% to 84.3%. When it is not read, none of the methods work, as the policy does not use the goal information. The same tracing also reveals what replaces the goal: functional target substitution, where behavior follows an alternative goal, a policy shortcut, where behavior follows a learned action strategy, or cue competition between intended and proxy goal cues. Knowing where the goal is lost tells us whether an agent can be repaired or has to be retrained. Goal misgeneralization should therefore be studied and evaluated inside the agent, not only from its behavior.

## Scale-free wave localization at topological multicriticality
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40125v1
- **Authors**: ['Zhijie Xue', 'Yuan Tian', 'Nanyu Chen', 'Xiujuan Zhang', 'Tao Li', 'Shuang Zhang', 'Fuxin Guan']
- **Abstrat**: Spatial wave localization at boundaries is conventionally restricted to exponential decay. Here, we observe a scale-free boundary mode exhibiting anomalous polynomial decay, highlighted by strictly linear spatial envelopes, driven by topological multicriticality in acoustic metamaterials. We establish a universal geometric bulk-boundary correspondence rooted in singularity theory by mapping n-th order topological multicriticality onto Arnold An catastrophe. We prove that defectiveness in the spatial transfer matrix enforces a (n-1)-th power spatial decay of boundary modes. At a topological tri-critical point, the singularity of winding function manifests as an A2 fold catastrophe. This dictates a strictly linear spatial decay, marking a sharp departure from the conventional exponential scaling paradigm. Using acoustic metamaterial platforms tuned to a tricritical point, we experimentally demonstrate this linear decay envelope in 1D, and further generalize it to two-dimensional systems to realize scale-free corner modes with simultaneous linear decay along orthogonal directions. While existing critical topology frameworks rely on entanglement spectra to diagnose mode existence in gapless regimes, our work bridges catastrophe theory with topological matter at multicriticalities, advancing the field from abstract mode identification to deterministic real-space wavefunction engineering.

## Gym-Anything for Robotics: Scaling physical simulations with digital agents
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40565v1
- **Authors**: ['Pranjal Aggarwal', 'Sean Welleck']
- **Abstrat**: Realistic, fast robotics simulations could enable large-scale training of embodied AI models that transfer to the real world. However, despite decades of progress, available simulations cover only a narrow range of real-world tasks and physical phenomena. The core bottleneck is that creating better simulations requires significant expert effort and therefore does not scale. To address this, we introduce Gym-Anything for Robotics, a framework in which coding agents create realistic robotics simulations. Our core insight is that simulation creation is a coding agent task, so improvements in coding agents translate directly into richer and more faithful simulations. We select simulations based on U.S. GDP data to cover economically valuable physical work, define what makes a good simulation and a good task, and develop an adversarial creation-audit loop that checks each simulation and task against these definitions. Using this pipeline, we build RoboWorld, a set of 25 simulations with 25 test and 175 training RL environments of varying difficulty. Even the best frontier models fail on every RoboWorld test task, achieving an average reward of only 0.06. Building on RoboWorld, we introduce several new ways for frontier models to improve, including in-context learning across repeated attempts, a privileged teacher that reads the simulator state and teaches a student in context, self-assisted learning from the model's own privileged experience, and code policies that generate demonstrations across varied scenes to train vision-language-action models (VLAs) such as $π_{0.5}$. Each of these substantially improves performance. We release our pipeline, RL environments, and benchmark to facilitate future research. Code and data are available at https://gym-anything.github.io/robotics





## UniffordInteractor: Reasoning the Unified Affordance for Human and Multi-Object Interaction

While existing HOI methods have effectively explored human interaction with individual or fixed objects, they still show limitations in complex multi-object interaction scenes. Extensive experiments demonstrate that UniffordInteractor surpasses SOTA counterparts in human and multi-object interaction generation.

## Measuring the Inaccessible: Can AI Evaluate Accessibility in Heritage Tourism?
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38642v1
- **Authors**: ['Sabrina Armenio', 'Manuele Leonelli']
- **Abstrat**: Accessibility assessment underpins the right to participate in heritage tourism, yet evaluation of archaeological sites remains artisanal: fieldwork does not scale, is often carried out without accessibility training, and is reported in forms that reach neither the sector nor disabled visitors. AI appears to offer a shortcut. We argue that assessment is measurement and inference under sparse, expert-mediated evidence: AI transforms how that evidence is captured and communicated, whereas evaluation, which turns evidence into defensible judgements, demands properties current generative systems lack. In a five-park field study in Spain and Italy, an AI desk audit scored only 66% of dimension-site combinations, could not observe embodied and organisational dimensions, and mirrored sites' online self-presentation. Simple Bayesian models instead quantify the confidence rankings deserve and calibrate desk evidence against fieldwork. We propose a separation of roles between AI and interpretable statistical evaluation as the basis for a reliable accessibility atlas.





## Recursive Self-Improvement in Physical AI: Mechanisms, Evidence, and Evaluation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38266v1
- **Authors**: ['Xiaofeng Han', 'Kaize Luo', 'Zenghuang Fu', 'Zichong Zhu', 'Zhe Feng', 'Changwei Wang', 'Rongtao Xu', 'Weiliang Meng', 'Xiaopeng Zhang']
- **Abstrat**: Embodied systems can update policies, world models, skills, and executable programs from deployment experience, allowing one interaction to benefit later tasks. When a system also revises and reuses its data-acquisition, feedback, or learning procedures, experience changes the process that produces capability, introducing recursive self-improvement. This paper surveys research in physical AI through the connection between persistent capability and its improvement process. We first explain the functional distinction between learner updates and improvers, then compare policy learning, imagined experience, memory and skill accumulation, and executable harnesses. These approaches connect to revisions of curricula, rewards, and research programs in different ways. An L0-L3 rubric distinguishes task-local adaptation, persistent capability gain, inherited process revision, and verified improver gain. Representative benchmarks and within-study contrasts relate these distinctions to existing results. We further propose common-data and interactive comparisons that evaluate reference and revised improvers from shared learner starts, separating the use of experience from its acquisition. This perspective connects the diverse methods, physical operating conditions, and evaluation evidence, underpinning embodied RSI. A comprehensive list of studies in this survey is available at https://github.com/nkd-lkz/physical-ai-rsi-survey.

- **Abstrat**: Robotic navigation typically assumes a collision-free route to the goal exists; in cluttered environments with movable obstacles, however, reaching the goal may require physical interaction. ## Don't Risk It!: Trajectory-Conditioned Distributional Risk for LiDAR-Only Crowd Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40050v1
- **Authors**: ['Mathai Mathew Pulicken', 'Laksh Nanwani', 'Basant Sharma', 'Phani Teja Singamaneni', 'Arun Kumar Singh', 'K. Madhava Krishna']
- **Abstrat**: Efficient crowd navigation requires anticipating dynamic collision-free space without relying on fragile object tracking. We propose the Don't Risk It Planner (DRIP), an end-to-end LiDAR-only planning framework that couples occupancy forecasting with trajectory-conditioned clearance and uncertainty estimation. Operating directly on LiDAR-derived occupancy grid histories, DRIP predicts a distribution over minimum robot-obstacle clearance for candidate control sequences to evaluate collision risk. ## Normalizing Flow-Enhanced Message Passing for Multirobot Collaborative Localization
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29868v2
- **Authors**: ['Han Shen', 'Guanghui Wen', 'Liangming Chen', 'Ming Cao']
- **Abstrat**: Accurate, robust, and adaptive localization is essential for various robotic operations. This paper proposes a parametric message passing (MP) algorithm for distributed collaborative localization, unifying Gaussian belief propagation (GBP) for robot state estimation and mean-field (MF) approximation for noise inference. Unlike conventional MP algorithms, the proposed formulation directly optimizes the natural parameters of beliefs, establishing a principled connection between MP and gradient estimators for handling non-conjugate terms arising from nonlinear measurement models. Beyond linearization and sampling, we further design a normalizing flow (NF)-based gradient estimator, enabling learnable sampling. End-to-end training tunes NF parameters according to the behavior of MP, improving estimation accuracy while reducing the required number of iterations. To support robot states involving rotations, the method is extended to Lie group spaces. Finally, the method is applied to a multirobot localization task fusing odometry, global navigation satellite system (GNSS) measurements, and inter-robot ultra wideband (UWB) ranging. Extensive simulations and experiments on autonomous surface vehicles (ASVs) demonstrate superior state estimation accuracy, enhanced robustness and adaptability under ranging outliers and varying noise statistics, as well as good generalization across different operating conditions.





## Safety-Critical LiDAR-Inertial Odometry with On-Manifold Deterministic Protection Level
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09383v3
- **Authors**: ['Yueqi Zhu', 'Yan Pan', 'Chufan Rui', 'Jiasheng Luo', 'Bo Zhou']
- **Abstrat**: In safety-critical scenarios, the protection level of the autonomous navigation system is crucial for enabling mobile robots to perform safe tasks. However, existing studies on probabilistic navigation systems for robots usually perform offline accuracy evaluations using limited datasets and assume that the results can be applied to unknown real-world environments. As a result, current autonomous mobile robots often lack protection levels for online safety assessment. To fill this gap, we propose a safety-critical LiDAR-inertial odometry (LIO) that provides deterministic protection levels based on a novel on-manifold set-membership filter. By adopting the unknown but bounded assumption, we derive a neat closed-form relationship between point cloud noise and the uncertainty of the estimation from the iterative closest point algorithm. Using this relationship, we design an on-manifold ellipsoidal set-membership filter (OM-ESMF) and implement it within the LIO system. Leveraging the properties of the set-membership filter, our system offers the feasible sets of the estimated locations as the deterministic protection levels, serving as safety references for the robots' downstream autonomous operations. The experimental results show that our system can provide effective deterministic online safety references for diverse robots in various environments.
