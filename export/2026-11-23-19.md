## NavTTT: Adaptive Parametric Memory via Hierarchical Test-Time Training for Vision-and-Language Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27219v1
- **Authors**: ['Xiaoyu Luo', 'Chengxing Lin', 'Jiaxin Huang', 'Jiale Liu', 'Jingyu Guo', 'Sirui Chen', 'Aolin Li', 'Shaoli Huang', 'Cheng-zhong Xu', 'Hui Kong']
- **Abstrat**: Vision-and-language navigation (VLN) demands that agents accumulate and reason over streaming visual observations throughout long navigation episodes. Existing approaches struggle to maintain long-term history, constrained by the finite context capacity of transformer architectures. To solve the problem, fast-weight test-time training (TTT) provides a mechanism for encoding streaming observations into an evolving parameter state. However, existing TTT does not distinguish informative observations from repetitive visual content, allowing redundant tokens to interfere with the retention of information needed for subsequent decisions. We propose Navigation-Conditioned Test-Time Training (NavTTT), which introduces adaptive memory formation and hierarchical memory integration for vision-and-language navigation. NavTTT leverages global navigation context to prioritize informative visual evidence and integrates language instructions with spatial information to construct spatially grounded memory representations. Episode-local nonlinear fast weights store these representations as Adaptive Parametric Memory (APM), preserving history beyond the visual context for later decisions. To reduce cross-layer coupling in memory formation, NavTTT maintains independent fast-weight memories across multiple decoder depths. This decoupled design prevents errors or biases from spreading across different memory levels. Meanwhile, it enables shared historical information to be independently represented at various levels of abstraction. Experiments demonstrate strong navigation performance on VLN-CE benchmarks and effective instruction following on a real robot.





## Inject, Encode, Navigate: Feature-Wise Time Injection for Rectified Flow with Bidirectional Feature Encoding
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27179v1
- **Authors**: ['Xiaogang Wang', 'Debasish Mishra', 'Jia Hao Toh', 'Wangsheng Liu', 'Yash Shah']
- **Abstrat**: Vision-model-based robotic navigation has gained increasing popularity, yet existing approaches still struggle to deliver effective and efficient navigation performance, \textit{e.g.}, diffusion-based policies that require costly iterative denoising. In this work, we propose three improvements to bridge these gaps. First, we adopt a rectified flow framework to reduce the required inference steps to one while achieving comparable or even superior performance. Second, we leverage a bidirectional Mamba-based visual encoder that employs selective state-space modeling to more effectively capture bidirectional temporal dependencies across observation frames, even with longer input sequences. Most importantly, we find that naïvely combining these two components to balance accuracy with inference speed yields no further performance gains or even produces inferior results. Upon extensive analysis, we realized that the root cause lies in the fact that the Hydra-derived temporal context is shared unchanged across all flow timesteps, and timestep information is introduced only within the velocity network. This weak coupling limits the model's ability to adaptively exploit temporal visual features at different flow states. To address this, we propose a feature-wise timestep conditioning design that explicitly conditions the visual embedding on the flow timestep, enabling noise-level-aware trajectory generation, thereby restoring a meaningful gradient pathway and enabling coherent interaction between the rectified flow and the temporal encoder. Extensive quantitative and qualitative experiments across multiple datasets demonstrate that our approach consistently outperforms both baseline and competitive methods.





## LoopNav: Benchmarking Spatial Consistency in World Models
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22976v4
- **Authors**: ['Kewei Lian', 'Shaofei Cai', 'Yitao Liang', 'Anji Liu']
- **Abstrat**: The ability to simulate the world in a spatially consistent manner is a crucial requirement for effective world models. Such a model enables high-quality visual generation, and also ensures the reliability of world models for downstream tasks such as simulation and planning. It must not only retain long-horizon observational information, but also enables the construction of explicit or implicit internal spatial representations. However, existing datasets do not explicitly enforce spatial consistency constraints, limiting both the ability to systematically evaluate this capability and to learn it through data-driven approaches. Furthermore, most existing benchmarks primarily emphasize visual coherence or generation quality, neglecting the requirement of long-range spatial consistency. To bridge this gap, we propose LoopNav, a dataset and corresponding benchmark centered on loop-based navigation for evaluating spatial consistency. The dataset comprises 200 hours of loop-based navigation videos with actions, collected from diverse locations in the open-world environment of Minecraft. We further introduce a Scene Graph Consistency Score(SGCS) to quantify spatial consistency while remaining invariant to pixel-level variations. Dataset, benchmark, and code are open-sourced to support future research.





## Latent Space Planning with Sequential Monte Carlo
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26706v1
- **Authors**: ['Hrishikesh Viswanath', 'Anindita Ghosh', 'Uttaran Bhattacharya', 'Aniket Bera']
- **Abstrat**: Planning with learned world models amounts to finding action sequences whose predicted rollouts reach a goal. Sampling-based planners often struggle at longer horizons, while gradient-based methods can become trapped in local minima. Meanwhile, model predictive path integral control (MPPI), a complementary approach, alternates between estimating an expectation under a cost-weighted distribution from samples and updating the plan from this estimate. Its samples, however, are drawn afresh at every iteration from a Gaussian proposal and reach the target distribution only through importance weights. Moreover, a target distribution favoring goal-reaching trajectories can be difficult to define directly, motivating a sequence of intermediate distributions. Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) provides a natural mechanism for evolving such a sequence through importance weighting and resampling. Motivated by this, we formulate latent-space planning as optimization guided by SMC, which moves a population of action sequences while a gradient-based optimizer updates the plan. Inspired by classical planning, we further introduce a PDE-derived cost-to-go that guides the evolution of the sample distribution using the structure of the latent state space. On navigation and manipulation tasks with visual and proprioceptive world models, our formulation outperforms gradient-based planners by $29$ and $50$, sampling-based planners by $35$ and MPPI by $7$ percentage points in average success rate, while succeeding in fewer iterations than gradient-based planners and within 4\% of MPPI.

## UDA-Restoration: Bridging Unsupervised Domain Adaptation and Multi-Degradation Denoising for Enhanced AV Semantic Segmentation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27097v1
- **Authors**: ['Zainab Bibi', 'Mohsin Kamal']
- **Abstrat**: Autonomous vehicles rely heavily on vision-based perception systems for safe navigation and decision-making. Adverse weather conditions, sensor noise, moving artifacts and illumination changes, however, often degrade the image quality and hence reduce perception performance. This paper introduces a diffusion-guided unsupervised domain adaptation algorithm for autonomous driving environments, namely UDA-Restoration, which is designed to enhance and denoise images. The proposed method follows the paradigm of Noise-Space Domain Adaptation (Noise-DA), which is based on multi-source degradation learning and diffusion-guided adaptation that spans across synthetic and real-world degradations. The framework has been extensively tested and shown to restore image quality and to be robust to unseen degradation scenarios, such as snow, haze, sensor distortions, and more. The quantitative results indicate that Noise-DA outperforms the evaluated self-supervised and unsupervised baselines while matching the best supervised restoration performance. In addition, semantic segmentation experiments show that the performance of downstream perception tasks is directly improved by better image restoration. The outcomes show the promise of unsupervised domain adaptation based on diffusion as a powerful and scalable method for image restoration tasks in autonomous driving systems under various environments.

## RoboCap-10k: A Metrically Accurate Egocentric Dataset for Robot Learning

- **Abstrat**: LiDAR-Inertial-Visual Odometry (LIVO) is a fundamental technology for autonomous systems; however, deployment on resource-constrained platforms remains challenging due to physical limitations. This paper presents Instant-LIVO, a tightly coupled, high-speed, real-time LIVO system. The system introduces a compact geometric map based on spatial-hashed OctVox and accelerates local-neighborhood retrieval and plane fitting through cache-assisted heuristic staged K-nearest-neighbor searches. The results demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing LIO and LIVO baselines in both accuracy and computational efficiency.The implementation is available at: https://github.com/Faker-0714/Instant-LIVO.git.

## Learning When to Ask: Advisor-Aware Post-Training for SLM Agents
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27443v1
- **Authors**: ['Aasheesh Singh', 'Vishal Vaddina']
- **Abstrat**: Small language models (SLMs) are increasingly attractive for cheap, high-throughput agentic deployment, but they remain weak at multi-step reasoning and tool use that long-horizon tasks demand. We study how to post-train an SLM to seek advice from a stronger model and act on that advice itself, following an advisor-executor pattern. Out of the box, this pattern fails across open- and closed-source small-model families: wiring an SLM to an advisor is a harness-design matter that is not enough on its own. A post-training recipe is needed to teach the model when to ask, in a cost-aware fashion, and how to follow advice across steps. Our method, ADAPT (ADvisor-Aware Post-Training), is a multi-stage recipe that instills this behavior, learning entirely from the policy's own rollouts supervised by the environment's outcome, with no learned reward model or human annotation. Across three domains, compared to an untrained harness baseline, ADAPT improves success rate in embodied text reasoning (TextWorld) from 24.5% to 77.3%, code generation (LiveCodeBench) from 21.8% to 56.0%, and deep web search (GAIA) from 17.5% to 49.3%, while querying the advisor sparingly (~1.0-2.1 calls per task). On the two long-horizon domains it closes 70-85% of the gap to an oracle that perfectly times when to ask for advice, and on code generation it exceeds that oracle. The gains persist when the advisor is replaced by one unseen in training, showing that the learned help-seeking policy is robust to a changing advisor rather than tied to the one it was trained with.





## Mind World Models: Joint Learning of Motor EEG Representations and Action-Conditioned World Dynamics
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27029v1
- **Authors**: ['Weikang Qiu', 'Zheng Huang', 'Tinglin Huang', 'Yujun Yan', 'Rex Ying']
- **Abstrat**: Motor decoding from electroencephalography (EEG) supports non-invasive brain-computer interfaces, yet existing methods either rely on costly cue-based trials and coarse categorical labels or use pretraining objectives that do not directly capture how movement unfolds, limiting their ability to learn transferable motor representations. To this end, we propose the Mind World Model (MWM), a framework that learns motor EEG representations from scalable naturalistic EEG and egocentric video without action annotations. MWM treats EEG observed up to the current frame as the latent action of a world model: a mind model encodes the EEG, and a world model uses it to predict the representation of a future frame, yielding a movement-grounded pretraining objective. We further introduce the Lateralization-Aware Transformer (LAT), which uses frame averaging to separate shared bilateral from side-specific lateral features, encoding hemispheric correspondence to reduce the burden of learning left-right relationships from data. MWM pretraining produces two models: a mind model for downstream motor decoding and a world model that can be adapted to prediction conditioned on real actions, opening a scalable and flexible path to collect egocentric data for embodied AI. Across 9 classification and 7 regression tasks, the mind model improves task-averaged balanced accuracy by 5.9% and Pearson correlation by 16.2% relative to the strongest baseline in each task family. Compared with an inverse-dynamics latent-action baseline, the world model achieves lower prediction error and benefits 3.3 times more from matched real actions within recordings and 4.7 times more on held-out subjects.

## NavRSI: Recursive Harness Self-Improvement for Vision-and-Language Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27499v1
- **Authors**: ['Guangzhao Dai', 'Guoxin Lian', 'Shuo Wang', 'Shengbin Yue', 'Qianru Sun', 'Xiangbo Shu', 'Bin Zhu']
- **Abstrat**: Frontier large language models (LLMs) have enabled vision-and-language navigation (VLN) through direct closed-loop perception and action. However, navigation performance depends strongly on the harness governing LLM-environment interactions, while existing harnesses largely rely on manually designed navigation tools, memory, and agent roles. We introduce NavRSI, a harness-centric recursive self-improvement framework that optimizes the navigation behavior of frozen LLMs through closed-loop experience. In each round, NavRSI iteratively collects navigation trajectories and failure diagnostics, employs a designer agent to rewrite the harness, and retains the revisions that prove effective, while the designer accumulates experience from previous revisions so that the harness and this experience co-evolve. Privileged simulator diagnostics guide only the designer and remain inaccessible to the navigating LLM, enabling deployment with a single RGB camera and primitive actions, without task-specific model training or additional navigation tools. Experiments demonstrate consistent improvements across LLMs from four vendors, with 85.64% success rate on the full R2R-CE val-unseen split with GPT-6-Astra, surpassing the previously reported best by 4.13%. Evaluations on RxR-CE, NavRAG-CE, and a real humanoid robot further demonstrate its generalizability.

- **Abstrat**: Recent embodied agents construct and adapt robot behavior through executable programs, tool use, and interaction feedback. Assisted reference execution and human guidance provide complementary experience. The resulting tool traces and aligned observation-action records support program refinement and policy learning, with common task-goal criteria for subsequent evaluation. We benchmark representative agent systems on complete tasks and demonstrate contact collection and trace-informed program revision. Together, these workflows provide infrastructure for research on embodied recursive self-improvement.





## AceVLN: Asynchronous Cloud Edge Deployment of VLNs via Latent Scheduling and Compensation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27255v1
- **Authors**: ['Zihao Zheng', 'Hangyu Cao', 'Yulong Han', 'Xichang Wu', 'Zhihao Mao', 'Maoliang Li', 'Jiayu Chen', 'Hailong Zou', 'Donggang Cao', 'Xuanzhe Liu', 'Hong Mei', 'Xiang Chen']
- **Abstrat**: Cloud--edge deployment enables asynchronous dual-system VLN on resource-constrained robots, but inference and communication delays cause latent mismatch and navigation errors. Existing adaptation methods reuse historical guidance but cannot provide updated navigation information when it becomes inadequate. We propose AceVLN, a training-free framework for active latent refresh and edge-side compensation. Our analysis reveals that action-relevant mismatch is concentrated in a compact Critical Subspace and can be tracked through fast-system feature drift. Critical Feature Drift Entropy reflects navigation sensitivity and guides adaptive refresh, while Critical Drift Continuity indicates when recent latent updates can support compensation during latency spikes. AceVLN integrates subspace-decoupled mismatch modeling, entropy-guided refresh scheduling, and continuity-guided compensation. Experiments show that \textit{AceVLN} improves success rates by 1.7--2.4 percentage points over the strongest baselines, while reducing amortized computation time by 2.7--10.0\% and FLOPs by 18.4--22.3\%.





## Frequency-Enhanced Token Caching for Efficient Streaming VLN Models
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24391v2
- **Authors**: ['Zihao Zheng', 'Hangyu Cao', 'Qichao Ma', 'Xichang Wu', 'Zhihao Mao', 'Lingyue Zhang', 'Maoliang Li', 'Jiayu Chen', 'Donggang Cao', 'Xuanzhe Liu', 'Hong Mei', 'Xiang Chen']
- **Abstrat**: Streaming vision-and-language navigation (VLN) repeatedly encodes overlapping views. Robot motion can shift a reusable patch to a different position, while small residual shifts near an edge can change its feature. These effects complicate both patch selection and the choice of a reuse budget for each frame. We present FreqCache, a streaming VLN caching framework that integrates frequency-domain characteristics from raw observations with token-level redundancy identification. We analyze their complementarity in cross-frame correspondence, reuse validity, and cache budgeting. Our analysis shows that spectral magnitude and phase cues can recover spatially shifted correspondences, local high-frequency characteristics can reveal structurally sensitive tokens, and single-frame spectral entropy can guide the budget among eligible patches. Based on these insights, FreqCache introduces frequency-enhanced token alignment, selective token refresh, and adaptive cache budgeting to reduce redundant computation while preserving navigation performance. On R2R-CE, FreqCache cuts per-step latency by 6.7--12.0\% relative to VLN-Cache and speeds up inference by up to $1.64\times$ over the original models. Across R2R-CE and RxR-CE, success rate drops by at most 0.7 percentage points.

## BRINA: Bottom-Referenced Inertial Navigation with AKAZE, a Sonar SLAM for Autonomous Marine Vehicles
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27817v1
- **Authors**: ['Adolfo Damiano Cafaro', 'Patrizio Mariani', 'Roberto Galeazzi']
- **Abstrat**: Forward-looking sonar (FLS) provides reliable underwater perception where optical sensing is limited, but its use for navigation is hindered by the unobserved elevation of each return. As a result, motion estimated directly from sonar images suffers from an unknown metric scale that is commonly resolved using a Doppler velocity log (DVL). We present BRINA (Bottom-Referenced Inertial Navigation with AKAZE), a sonar-inertial SLAM system for marine vehicles that uses the FLS as its only exteroceptive sensor. BRINA estimates the sonar altitude from the first seabed return in each beam and uses it to project matched features onto the seabed, providing metric odometry without a DVL. We further derive a bound relating the remaining scale error to seabed tilt and slope variability. The method is evaluated in simulation with an autonomous underwater vehicle, and in field experiments with uncrewed surface vehicles over ten missions, using two FLS systems under different operating conditions.





## Joint Optimization of Fleet Size, Localization Infrastructure, and Trajectories for Multi-AUV Persistent Monitoring
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27539v1
- **Authors**: ['Jan Mikołajczyk', 'Jakub Bałenkowski', 'Maciej Borowiecki', 'Jan Ciecierski', 'Marcel Kolecki', 'Julian Maleta', 'Maksymilian Bernacki']
- **Abstrat**: Persistent monitoring of large-scale subsea infrastructure, such as offshore wind farm foundations, export cables, and inspection corridors, remains a fundamental challenge for autonomous underwater systems. The problem is not solely spatial coverage, but rather temporal coverage: ensuring that all relevant regions of interest are revisited within bounded time intervals under limited sensing, navigation uncertainty, and constrained vehicle resources. We compare three classes of trajectory strategies: (1) deterministic lane-based coverage (lawnmower patterns), (2) stochastic exploration (biased random walk with boundary constraints), and (3) coordinated partition-based strategies (Voronoi tessellation). Rather than seeking a universally superior method, we evaluate how varying levels of localization accuracy impact temporal coverage across two distinct operational profiles: persistent monitoring of the entire operational workspace versus targeted inspection of critical infrastructure. We present a simulation-based, quantitatively grounded study of temporal coverage in multi-AUV persistent monitoring, disentangling three interacting design dimensions: (i) trajectory generation strategy, (ii) fleet size, and (iii) localization accuracy provided by fixed acoustic beacon infrastructure. We introduce a cost-aware evaluation framework combining fleet size, number of beacons, and mission energy expenditure into a unified cost function. Results demonstrate that beyond a certain accuracy threshold, improvements in localization yield diminishing returns compared to coordinated trajectory design, an effect often obscured in integrated navigation-planning approaches. Coordinated-partition trajectories enable a reduced fleet size to achieve superior temporal coverage at a 39-46% lower overall life-cycle cost than rigid deterministic paths unoptimized for the localization infrastructure.

## Latent Space Planning with Sequential Monte Carlo
