## Semantically Adaptive Control Barrier Functions with Vision-Language Models for Real-Time Robot Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21142v2
- **Authors**: ['Jeffrey Chen', 'Rohan Chandra']
- **Abstrat**: Robots operating in dynamic, unstructured environments must maintain safety without becoming unnecessarily conservative. While control barrier functions (CBFs) provide a principled mechanism for collision avoidance, their behavior is typically determined from geometric state alone and therefore cannot distinguish between semantically different situations with similar physical configurations. We present AlphaAdj, a vision-to-control navigation framework that uses egocentric RGB observations and a vision-language model (VLM) to adapt CBF conservativeness according to visual semantic context. The VLM produces a scalar contextual caution signal that dynamically modulates the CBF parameter alpha, while a geometry- and speed-dependent cap bounds admissible aggressiveness. To support real-time operation despite VLM latency, inference runs asynchronously with the control loop, and the semantic signal is extracted directly from token-level output probabilities rather than full autoregressive text generation. We evaluate AlphaAdj across matched-geometry, dynamic-human, and cluttered navigation scenarios, as well as on a physical Clearpath Jackal. When obstacle geometry is held fixed, AlphaAdj allocates 0.405 m more clearance to a human than to a benign object, while geometry-only and non-visual adaptive CBF baselines produce no semantic differentiation. This additional behavioral reserve improves robustness to unexpected human motion, including cluttered conditions in which all evaluated geometric baselines collide while AlphaAdj remains collision-free. The system maintains a 0.0167 s control period with 0.158 s mean VLM round-trip latency, demonstrating that semantic visual reasoning can complement geometric safety control without entering the critical control path.





## CHOP: Counterfactual Human Preference Labels Improve Obstacle Avoidance in Visuomotor Navigation Policies
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02004v2
- **Authors**: ['Gershom Seneviratne', 'Jianyu An', 'Vaibhav Shende', 'Sahire Ellahy', 'Yaxita Amin', 'Kondapi Manasanjani', 'Samarth Chopra', 'Jonathan Deepak Kannan', 'Dinesh Manocha']
- **Abstrat**: Visuomotor navigation policies have shown strong perception-action coupling for embodied agents, yet they often struggle with safe navigation and dynamic obstacle avoidance in complex real-world environments. We introduce CHOP, a novel approach that leverages Counterfactual Human Preference Labels to align visuomotor navigation policies towards human intuition of safety and obstacle avoidance in navigation. In CHOP, for each visual observation, the robot's executed trajectory is included among a set of counterfactual navigation trajectories: alternative trajectories the robot could have followed under identical conditions. Human annotators provide pairwise preference labels over these trajectories based on anticipated outcomes such as collision risk and path efficiency. These aggregated preferences are then used to fine-tune visuomotor navigation policies, aligning their behavior with human preferences in navigation. Experiments on the SCAND dataset show that visuomotor navigation policies fine-tuned with CHOP reduce near-collision events by 49.7%, decrease deviation from human-preferred trajectories by 45.0%, and increase average obstacle clearance by 19.8% on average across multiple state-of-the-art models, compared to their pretrained baselines. These improvements transfer to real-world deployments on a Ghost Robotics Vision60 quadruped, where CHOP-aligned policies improve average goal success rates by 24.4%, increase minimum obstacle clearance by 6.8%, reduce collision and intervention events by 45.7%, and improve normalized path completion by 38.6% on average across navigation scenarios, compared to their pretrained baselines. Our results highlight the value of counterfactual preference supervision in bridging the gap between large-scale visuomotor policies and human-aligned, safety-aware embodied navigation.





## VEGA: Learning Navigation VLAs from In-the-Wild Egocentric Video with Geometric Trajectory Supervision
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18426v3
- **Authors**: ['Gershom Seneviratne', 'Yohan Abeysinghe', 'Jianyu An', 'Vaibhav Shende', 'Rahul Kumar', 'Dinesh Manocha']
- **Abstrat**: We introduce VEGA, an approach for training navigation VisionLanguage-Action (VLA) models from unlabeled egocentric navigation videos. Internet-scale egocentric videos provide a scalable source of navigation-relevant visual observations, capturing cluttered scenes, close-range obstacles, and natural human motion through real-world spaces. However, these videos are not directly usable for policy learning because they do not provide obstacle-aware trajectories conditioned on explicit navigation goals in the robot's coordinate frame. VEGA addresses this gap by reconstructing local scene geometry from monocular video, sampling navigation goals (represented as text, image, or spatial waypoints) and generating obstacle-aware trajectories using the constructed geometry. The resulting trajectory distribution is then used to train a flow-matching VLA navigation policy. By using geometry exclusively during training, VEGA distills obstacle-aware planning directly into a vision-based policy. Furthermore, we introduce VEGA-Bench, a benchmark containing 250k scenes and approximately 5 million navigation goals paired with scene geometry, designed to evaluate goal progress, collision avoidance, and obstacle clearance of VLAs. Our evaluation shows that VEGA achieves competitive goal progress while reducing collisions by 33.0% and improving obstacle clearance by 17.9% over the strongest baseline on VEGABench, while improving success by at least 150.0%, reducing collisions by at least 66.7%, and improving obstacle clearance by at least 60.0% in real-world trials. Ultimately, we demonstrate that video-derived geometric supervision provides a scalable and effective signal for training obstacle-aware navigation VLAs. The code and benchmark will be released at the time of publication.





## When the Past Shapes Action: Understanding Embodied Memory
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41688v1
- **Authors**: ['Siqi Li', 'Ziyi Bai', 'Xinrun Xu', 'Tinglei Huang', 'Börje F. Karlsson']
- **Abstrat**: Recent advances in generative foundation models have expanded the capabilities of embodied AI, enabling increasingly general navigation, manipulation, and long-horizon task execution. However, effective interaction with dynamic environments requires more than only strong perception and reasoning: agents must act with partial knowledge and retain, retrieve, and update information from past observations, actions, and experiences. This survey examines embodied memory as a mechanism for preserving decision-relevant information beyond the current observation. We organize existing approaches along two complementary dimensions: six fundamental memory requirements, spanning world-state persistence, task progress, belief maintenance, and experiential learning; and eight architectural categories, including recurrent states, fast weights, visual and symbolic representations, spatial memory, and experience-based systems. We analyze their capabilities and limitations, and examine how existing benchmarks evaluate memory-dependent behavior, distinguishing genuine use of historical information from general task performance. Finally, we identify open challenges in selecting relevant experiences, maintaining consistent beliefs in changing environments, and consolidating knowledge across episodes. Our analysis provides a framework for understanding memory as an essential component of reliable, adaptive, and lifelong-horizon embodied intelligence.





## WildOS: Open-Vocabulary Object Search in the Wild
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19308v2
- **Authors**: ['Hardik Shah', 'Manthan Patel', 'Erica Tevere', 'Deegan Atha', 'Marcel Kaufmann', 'Shehryar Khattak', 'Marco Hutter', 'Jonas Frey', 'Patrick Spieler']
- **Abstrat**: Autonomous navigation in complex, unstructured outdoor environments requires robots to operate over long ranges without prior maps and limited depth sensing. In such settings, relying solely on geometric frontiers for exploration is often insufficient. In such settings, the ability to reason semantically about where to go and what is safe to traverse is crucial for robust, efficient exploration. This work presents WildOS, a unified system for long-range, open-vocabulary object search that combines safe geometric exploration with semantic visual reasoning. WildOS builds a sparse navigation graph to maintain spatial memory, while utilizing a foundation-model-based vision module, ExploRFM, to score frontier nodes of the graph. ExploRFM simultaneously predicts traversability, visual frontiers, and object similarity in image space, enabling real-time, onboard semantic navigation tasks. The resulting vision-scored graph enables the robot to explore semantically meaningful directions while ensuring geometric safety. Furthermore, we introduce a particle-filter-based method for coarse localization of the open-vocabulary target query, that estimates candidate goal positions beyond the robot's immediate depth horizon, enabling effective planning toward distant goals. Extensive closed-loop field experiments across diverse off-road and urban terrains demonstrate that WildOS enables robust navigation, significantly outperforming purely geometric and purely vision-based baselines in both efficiency and autonomy. Our results highlight the potential of vision foundation models to drive open-world robotic behaviors that are both semantically informed and geometrically grounded. Project Page: https://leggedrobotics.github.io/wildos/





## Beyond Trial-and-Error: A Geospatial-Guided Active Reasoning Framework for Robust Aerial Vision-and-Language Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41446v1
- **Authors**: ['Jiawei Ge', 'Wenkang Gong', 'Jiuxin Cao', 'Bo Liu', 'Lei Cui', 'Haoran Wang', 'Xuelin Zhu', 'Biwei Cao', 'Chang Liu', 'Chen Feng', 'Ioannis Patras']
- **Abstrat**: Aerial Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) requires UAVs to locate language-specified goals in large-scale urban environments, where objects often appear small and visually ambiguous from high-altitude viewpoints. Existing methods largely follow a reactive paradigm common in indoor VLN, repeatedly updating navigation states from local observations while underutilizing the geospatial structure that remains stable during navigation. As a result, visually similar distractors can cause a reactive goal search to repeatedly reinforce locally plausible goal hypotheses based on passively accumulated evidence, leading to unreliable localization. To address this, we propose GeoReasoner, a robust framework that shifts the paradigm of Aerial VLN from reactive trial-and-error to geospatial-guided active reasoning. Specifically, GeoReasoner first performs pre-search geospatial reasoning to derive region-level priors and constrain the goal hypothesis space. Building on these, it further conducts hypothesis-driven exploration to actively acquire discriminative evidence and refine competing goal hypotheses. Finally, consistency-aware goal verification adversarially assesses complementary evidence sources before stopping, hence reducing premature or erroneous goal confirmations. Experiments on the real-world CityNav benchmark demonstrate that GeoReasoner sets a new SOTA among zero-shot Aerial VLN methods while competing favorably with supervised approaches.

## Visual-Inertial RGB-D SLAM with Encoders for a Differential Wheeled Robot
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41768v1
- **Authors**: ['Zhanghao Zhu', 'Yutaka Kaizu', 'Kenichi Furuhashi', 'Kenji Imou']
- **Abstrat**: Recent years have seen multiple impressive results in visual-inertial odometry (VIO) techniques, by which accurate state estimation can be achieved via the extended Kalman filter (EKF) or nonlinear optimization. However, these approaches are rarely open source, and they tend to fail in real experiments due to the temporary lack of feature points and fast motion. Therefore, in this study, we used encoders to overcome the temporary failure of purely vision-based simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM). Here, we propose a generative measurement model for encoders and derive an expression for the maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimate and necessary Jacobians for optimization. We use our theory to present a novel tightly coupled visual-inertial encoder RGB-Depth (RGB-D) SLAM system. Tests on our system on an in-house dataset confirmed that our modeling effort led to accurate (with a root mean squared error (RMSE) of approximately 2-7 cm) and robust state estimation in real time. The source code and our dataset containing the encoder information have been published for verification.

## A Hybrid Path Planning Framework for Assistive Wheelchair Navigation in Dynamic Pedestrian Environments
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42049v1
- **Authors**: ['Damla Leblebicioğlu', 'Rachel Barume', 'Taşkın Padır']
- **Abstrat**: Autonomous wheelchairs can improve the quality of life for individuals with mobility impairments by providing greater independence. However, reliable navigation especially in human environments is a major challenge due to factors such as dynamic obstacles, unpredictable pedestrian behavior, and the users' need to complete multiple stops along the way before reaching their final destination. This paper presents a hybrid path planning framework for multi-goal navigation in dynamic environments. The framework integrates the Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP) for global task ordering with the Potential Field (PF) method and heuristic local planners such as Rapidly-exploring Random Tree (RRT) and A-star (A*) to achieve collision-free navigation. Within this hybrid framework, TSP determines the visiting order of intermediate stations, PF transfers global path information, and local planners avoid obstacles while guiding the robot to the stations. The local planners generate feasible paths that allow the robot to follow the global trajectory and reroute to avoid static and dynamic obstacles. For this purpose, two algorithms have been developed: (i) TSP-PF-RRT and (ii) TSP-PF-A*. The performance of the proposed algorithms are evaluated using Monte Carlo simulations in crowded and extra-crowded scenarios that contain heterogeneous dynamic and static obstacles. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed hybrid framework enables efficient multi-goal navigation while maintaining safe operation in dynamic environments with humans, with TSP-PF-RRT achieving fewer or no collisions and shorter travel distances.

## AC-SMPC: Differentiable Actor--Critic MPC with State Constraints for Safe and Fast Robot Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41477v1
- **Authors**: ['Chaoran Wu', 'Jiayu Fan', 'Qichen Tan', 'Yuyi Liu', 'Jin Wang']
- **Abstrat**: Achieving safe navigation in confined dynamic environments remains a challenging problem, as robots must balance navigation efficiency with safety. Existing actor--critic MPC architectures typically employ differentiable MPC layers, but this way cannot readily accommodate explicit state constraints, making it intractable to involve collision avoidance within learning-based planning. To address this issue, we propose an actor--critic model predictive control framework with state constraints. We first use vertices to represent the robot and obstacles, and half-spaces to represent the workspace. Based on these, the nonlinear robot dynamics is locally linearized and the hyperplane-based collision avoidance constraints are convexified into the differentiable MPC layer, and then the actor exploits closed-loop feedback to adjust the MPC objective parameters that shape local motion behavior, while the critic evaluates long-term task performance. We further relax the convexified constraints with bounded and penalized slack variables to form an elastic quadratic program, accommodating cases where the local constraint approximations cannot be satisfied simultaneously. Simulation tests and experiments, involving dynamic collision avoidance for multi-robot in narrow cases, illustrate the advantages of the proposed framework in adaptively adjusting the learned policy and improving navigation efficiency over existing approaches while maintaining the motion safety.

## A Capability-Aware Backup-Recoverability Filter for Heterogeneous Multi-Robot Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41188v1
- **Authors**: ['Joonkyung Kim', 'Yanze Zhang', 'Wenhao Luo', 'Yiwei Lyu']
- **Abstrat**: Safe navigation in heterogeneous multi-robot teams faces two coupled challenges: differences in robots' braking and turning capabilities limit how each robot can respond to an approaching neighbor, while simultaneous interactions require a single control action to satisfy multiple collision-avoidance constraints. Together, these challenges can make local safety constraints incompatible with a robot's admissible controls, leaving a decentralized safety controller without a feasible action. We propose a Capability-Aware Backup-Recoverability Filter (CA-BRF), based on the insight that navigation actions should preserve access to a certified recovery maneuver. First, we construct a backup-recoverable set in a shared Cartesian operational space by evaluating whether prescribed backup maneuvers maintain pairwise safety until the robots come to rest. Each maneuver respects the corresponding robot's kinematic and actuation constraints, making the recovery certificate capability-dependent. Second, CA-BRF filters candidate controls by selecting admissible interpolations between candidate and backup actions while coordinating the selection across simultaneous interactions. This preserves backup recoverability while retaining as much of the candidate action as possible and ensures that all pairwise recovery conditions are satisfied by a single executable control for each robot. - **Abstrat**: Payroll-fraud screening requires finding hidden employee--payee links while distinguishing suspicious relationships from legitimate ones. We formulate this problem as inductive link prediction and entity ranking on synthetic human-resources and enterprise resource planning (HR/ERP) graphs, with unseen organizations and a matched benign twin for every planted case. An original study on 24 held-out organizations and a prospectively frozen extension compare analyst rules, explicit path features and graph neural networks (GNNs). A tuned gradient-boosted model on chained-join features achieved the highest overall link average precision (AP; 0.823), above the GraIL-style GNN (0.663); its 4-hop AP was 0.646 versus 0.272. Thus, the observed multi-hop gain is not specific to GNNs. On a new synthetic cohort with 2.0% positive entities, chained-join boosting again led (AP 0.798); the relational entity GNN also exceeded record-aware rules (0.707 versus 0.504). At a 2% review budget, their precision was 0.695 versus 0.602. Under 40% failed identifier joins, the entity GNN ranked above chained-join boosting (AP 0.712 versus 0.683, descriptively). Structure-only auditing exposed a shortcut in the initial generator; matched twins reduced the audited twin-pool AUROC to 0.493--0.514. These results separate multi-hop candidate discovery, record-based discrimination and review prioritization within an explicit synthetic evaluation design.
