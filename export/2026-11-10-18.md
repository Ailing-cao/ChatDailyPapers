## LHNav: Long-Horizon Humanoid Navigation via Multirate Coordination and Physics-Grounded Action Data
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12136v1
- **Authors**: ['Xueyang Wang', 'Zehong Shen', 'Dingkun Wei', 'Huaijin Pi', 'Changpeng Wang', 'Xing Zhu', 'Hujun Bao', 'Yujun Shen', 'Xiaowei Zhou']
- **Abstrat**: We study humanoid navigation across multiple destinations under long-horizon language instructions. This task is challenging because robots must maintain progress across successive goals while continuously producing natural, stable whole-body motion through confined spaces. However, task decomposition in existing methods does not by itself ensure progress throughout execution, while waypoint-based navigation does not guarantee natural, route-faithful whole-body motion. Our key idea is to organize navigation into three complementary roles operating at different time scales: a Brain for task reasoning, a Cerebellum for local motion generation, and a Brainstem for whole-body execution. We realize this idea in LHNav, an asynchronous system that uses progress and arrival feedback to coordinate navigation goals, VLA motion-token prediction, and whole-body control. To train the Cerebellum for natural, route-faithful motion, we develop a physics-grounded data engine that improves physical tracking of motion references along collision-aware routes and pairs the resulting demonstrations with rendered visual observations. Together, these designs allow task planning to proceed alongside continuous movement and extend single-goal navigation to successive destinations with natural, route-faithful whole-body motion. In simulation, our navigation VLA outperforms OmniNav and DualVLN in single-goal success and motion smoothness, with fewer collisions and falls. Using a VLA trained only on single-goal demonstrations, LHNav achieves a 75.56% success rate on long-horizon navigation tasks. Deployment on the Unitree G1 further demonstrates single-goal navigation and zero-shot long-horizon task execution in real-world environments, including layouts with repositioned objects. Project website: https://robbyant-research.github.io/LHNav/.





## Compass 1: Towards Open-World and Persistent Embodied Navigation from Monocular RGB Videos
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12087v1
- **Authors**: ['Hai Zhang', 'Siqi Liang', 'Yanjun Guo', 'Shoufeng Zhang', 'Yunhao Wang', 'Hangyu Du', 'Haoguang Mai', 'Hongchen Li', 'Jiazhi Yang', 'Jialong Zeng', 'Yu Hu', 'Chen Chen', 'Gangcheng Jiang', 'Zishun Zhuo', 'Lirui Zhao', 'Zhigang Li', 'Yu Qiao', 'Zhenjie Yang', 'Fu Zhang', 'Hongyang Li']
- **Abstrat**: Embodied navigation requires agents to achieve persistent spatial awareness and long-horizon execution stability in open-world environments. Without altering the underlying architecture, we introduce History Representation, which integrates sparse visual keyframes with an explicit History Representation Map to preserve long-term spatiotemporal context. To effectively leverage this history, we further introduce History Utilization, a structured VQA supervision protocol comprising three categories: task-stage grounding, exploration consistency, and spatiotemporal target retrieval, enabling map-grounded reasoning and stable persistent long-horizon navigation. ## RoboMoCap: A Large-Scale Whole-Body Motion Capture Dataset with Egocentric Vision for Robot Learning

However, existing egocentric datasets often lack high-fidelity optical motion capture or systematic coverage of diverse loco-manipulation behaviours. To address this gap, we introduce RoboMoCap, a large-scale multimodal dataset combining egocentric vision with marker-based optical motion capture, specifically designed for locomotion-centric humanoid learning. By pairing egocentric vision with high-fidelity human motion across diverse locomotion and interaction scenarios, RoboMoCap provides a foundation for egocentric motion understanding and humanoid learning from human demonstrations.





## HSGNav: Hypothesis Scene Graphs for Multi-Scale Lifelong Navigation under Object Relocation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10547v1
- **Authors**: ['Kaiyao Liao', 'Qingfeng Li', 'Chen Chen', 'Qing Sun', 'Ningbo Gu', 'Xiaoze Wu', 'Haochen Zhao', 'Ying Li', 'Jianwei Niu']
- **Abstrat**: In real homes, assistive robots must repeatedly locate targets from large furniture to tiny tabletop items, as people move objects between household surfaces. Uniformly instantiating detections at different scales as 3D objects can be brittle for small on-surface items; without explicit object-state validation, stale entries may persist after scene changes. Our key insight is to separate persistent 3D furniture entities from revisable surface-object hypotheses, retaining their visual evidence without requiring independent object geometry. We formalize this design as the Hypothesis Scene Graph (HSG), which preserves small-object evidence and supports local revision without rebuilding the stable entity topology. Building on HSG, HSGNav uses this separation for multi-scale lifelong navigation: retrieval selects entity or hypothesis evidence according to target scale, exploration acquires additional surface observations, and verification confirms target presence or invalidates contradicted hypothesis records. Because existing lifelong benchmarks such as GOAT-Bench rarely require mid-episode memory invalidation or multi-scale search, we introduce FAROS-Bench (Find And Re-find Objects across Scales) for mid-episode relocation of surface objects and multi-scale find goals. Experiments show that HSGNav performs competitively on GOAT-Bench while substantially outperforming prior methods on FAROS-Bench.





## Tiresias: Towards Language-Conditioned World Models
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10392v1
- **Authors**: ['Fengyi Wu', 'Yifei Dong', 'Yetong Sha', 'Lingdong Kong', 'Qiyu Hu', 'Bohan Xiong', 'Qi Dai', 'Alexander G. Hauptmann', 'Zhi-Qi Cheng']
- **Abstrat**: World models enable planning through internal simulation, while natural language provides intuitive task specification. Yet, language-conditioned world modeling remains fragmented across domains with disparate action spaces, datasets, and evaluation protocols. We introduce LCWM, a shared task that maps an initial agent-view observation and a language instruction to an action sequence that fulfills the instruction. Its accompanying benchmark, LCWM-Bench, integrates 15 datasets across navigation, robotics, 3D gaming, and driving under a shared action interface and evaluation protocol, pairing 142,987 episodes with 428,961 instructions in three styles. We further propose Tiresias, an agent that plans via selective imagination: a language-conditioned diffusion policy generates candidate trajectories, a trajectory ranker shortlists them, and a video-pretrained world model imagines their visual consequences. A visual ranker evaluates these rollouts, complementing trajectory scores to select the final plan. Tiresias achieves competitive planning and imagination performance on LCWM-Bench. Ablations demonstrate the complementary benefits of trajectory ranking and visual verification, while experiments with alternative proposal methods show that the trained rankers remain effective beyond our diffusion policy. Together, LCWM-Bench and Tiresias provide a unified evaluation setting and an effective starting point for studying language-guided planning through world models.

- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12099v1
- **Authors**: ['Lucas Hoefs', 'Hui Xie', 'Senjian An', 'Fangyi Zhang']
- **Abstrat**: Traversability prediction is a core component of autonomous navigation, yet most vision-based methods operate frame-wise and produce temporally inconsistent predictions. ## Particle Filtering with Generative Trajectory Models for State Estimation and Control
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11732v1
- **Authors**: ['Lukas Lao Beyer', 'Samvit Das', 'Sertac Karaman']
- **Abstrat**: Generative models can support both state estimation and control in robotics. For control, a common approach is to use the model as a policy that directly samples actions conditioned on observation histories. However, models that jointly represent states, actions, and observations can also predict how the robot state and its environment evolve. We investigate whether these predictions can improve state estimation and control through a change in inference procedure, without modifying the pretrained model. To this end, we use particle filtering to maintain competing hypotheses about state histories. A single pretrained diffusion model generates these hypotheses along with their predicted observations and associated action proposals. As new measurements arrive, particles are reweighted according to how well their predicted observations match the measurements. While particle filtering conventionally uses these weights only for state estimation, we also use them to guide action selection, favoring proposals associated with hypotheses that better explain the observations. Simulation experiments in partially observed navigation and vision-based manipulation demonstrate improvements in both state estimation and task performance compared to action generation without filtering, using the same pretrained model and no additional test-time information.

## FUSE-PnPL: Flexible, Unified, Statistically Consistent, and Asymptotically Efficient Camera Pose Estimation from Points and Lines
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16151v2
- **Authors**: ['Guangyang Zeng', 'Yuan Shen', 'Yiguo Peng', 'Guodong Shi', 'Biqiang Mu', 'Junfeng Wu']
- **Abstrat**: Camera pose estimation from 3D-2D correspondences is fundamental to robotics. Three practical considerations motivate this work. First, point and line features provide complementary geometric information, motivating a unified estimator. Second, the number and composition of available features vary across scenarios, requiring support for minimal and arbitrary non-minimal configurations. Third, modern visual front ends extract abundant features from individual images, enabling highly accurate pose estimation. We propose FUSE-PnPL, a flexible and unified camera pose estimator that is statistically consistent and asymptotically efficient. Specifically, we represent each line using two distinct points and adopt a unified image-space noise model for point and line measurements. By employing Plücker coordinates for line parameterization, we explicitly characterize the propagation of image endpoint noise into line residuals and formulate a joint maximum likelihood (ML) estimation problem for combined point and line measurements. To efficiently solve this problem, FUSE-PnPL employs a two-stage estimation scheme. First, noise variance estimation and bias elimination yield a consistent initial pose estimate. A single Gauss-Newton iteration then refines this estimate, achieving asymptotic efficiency as its mean squared error asymptotically attains the Cramér-Rao lower bound. Moreover, the proposed estimator has linear computational complexity with respect to the number of correspondences, making it suitable for high-precision and real-time robot pose estimation. Extensive experiments on synthetic data and real-world benchmarks validate the theoretical properties of FUSE-PnPL and demonstrate its advantages in both static global localization and dynamic SLAM systems.





## DPGS-SLAM: Diffusion-Prior and Provenance-Aware Monocular Gaussian Splatting SLAM for Dynamic Visual Sensing
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12038v1
- **Authors**: ['Yifan Luo', 'Takafumi Matsumaru', 'Vibekananda Dutta', 'Xin He']
- **Abstrat**: This paper presents DPGS-SLAM (Diffusion-Prior and Provenance-Aware Monocular Gaussian Splatting SLAM) for dense mapping in environments containing dynamic objects. Dynamic observations can contaminate camera tracking and scene reconstruction in visual SLAM (simultaneous localization and mapping). Although 3DGS (3D Gaussian splatting) enables efficient dense mapping, existing 3DGS-SLAM systems may exhibit instability when dynamic objects violate their underlying static-scene assumption. Excluding dynamic observations can reduce their influence on tracking and mapping, but may leave occluded static backgrounds missing or weakly constrained. Our proposed DPGS-SLAM is a monocular RGB SLAM framework that supports provisional representations of insufficiently observed static backgrounds through asynchronous diffusion-based background completion and provenance-aware Gaussian mapping. Dynamic regions are identified through optical-flow-based motion discovery and temporally consistent mask propagation by SAM2 (segment anything model 2). For selected keyframes, a fine-tuned diffusion inpainting model generates plausible background appearance in occluded regions, while Metric3D v2 predicts metric depth from the completed images to initialize provisional Gaussian primitives. Provenance labels distinguish generated primitives from observation-supported primitives, excluding generated content from camera-pose estimation and observed-map optimization. When previously occluded backgrounds become visible, eligible generated primitives are removed, and observation-supported primitives are independently initialized from real RGB observations and predicted depth. Experimental results demonstrated competitive camera tracking, with average ATE RMSE values of 1.2 cm on TUM RGB-D and 2.7 cm on BONN RGB-D. On StaDy4D, DPGS-SLAM improved average PSNR by 4.60 dB over WildGS-SLAM using only monocular RGB input.

## Bias-Eliminated PnP for Stereo Visual Odometry: Provably Consistent and Large-Scale Localization
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17410v2
- **Authors**: ['Guangyang Zeng', 'Yuan Shen', 'Ziyang Hong', 'Yuze Hong', 'Viorela Ila', 'Guodong Shi', 'Junfeng Wu']
- **Abstrat**: In this paper, we first present a bias-eliminated weighted (Bias-Eli-W) perspective-n-point (PnP) estimator for stereo visual odometry (VO) with provable consistency. Specifically, leveraging statistical theory, we develop an asymptotically unbiased and $\sqrt {n}$-consistent PnP estimator that accounts for varying 3D triangulation uncertainties, ensuring that the relative pose estimate converges to the ground truth as the number of features increases. Next, on the stereo VO pipeline side, we propose a framework that continuously triangulates contemporary features for tracking new frames, effectively decoupling temporal dependencies between pose and 3D point errors. We integrate the Bias-Eli-W PnP estimator into the proposed stereo VO pipeline, creating a synergistic effect that enhances the suppression of pose estimation errors. We validate the performance of our method on the KITTI and Oxford RobotCar datasets. Experimental results demonstrate that our method: 1) achieves significant improvements in both relative pose error and absolute trajectory error in large-scale environments; 2) provides reliable localization under erratic and unpredictable robot motions. The successful implementation of the Bias-Eli-W PnP in stereo VO indicates the importance of information screening in robotic estimation tasks with high-uncertainty measurements, shedding light on diverse applications where PnP is a key ingredient.





## Scale-Consistent Monocular Fisheye Visual Odometry via Spherical Bundle Adjustment-Guided Unsupervised Depth Learning
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11934v1
- **Authors**: ['Wanhui Luo', 'Hongbo Zhao', 'Yi Zhou', 'Qijun Chen', 'Yiming Nie', 'Rui Fan']
- **Abstrat**: The wide field of view of fisheye cameras and the simplicity of a monocular setup make monocular fisheye visual odometry (VO) attractive for robotic localization and mapping. Nevertheless, scale drift remains a major challenge over long trajectories. Unsupervised depth learning can help to mitigate this issue, but the pronounced distortion of fisheye images poses challenges for accurate depth estimation. This article presents S$^2$D-FVO, a monocular fisheye VO framework integrating spherical bundle adjustment (SBA)-guided unsupervised depth learning to improve scale consistency and depth estimation accuracy. Given that pixel reprojection residuals do not uniformly reflect angular deviations between viewing rays and that the first-order linearization of a scalar angular reprojection residual is insensitive to perturbations along one tangent direction, SBA employs tangent-space angular reprojection residuals derived from the spherical logarithmic map. These residuals retain sensitivity along both tangent directions, with their norms equal to the corresponding scalar angular residuals. A median depth-based scale regularization term is further integrated into SBA to constrain the reconstruction scale. Building on these formulations, SBA replaces the pose estimation network during unsupervised depth learning, providing more reliable relative poses for view synthesis. In turn, depth estimates from the well-trained depth estimation network provide scale references for SBA, improving scale consistency while allowing individual 3-D landmark positions to be refined. Extensive experiments conducted on multiple public datasets, as well as two newly collected large-scale datasets, demonstrate that the proposed framework outperforms representative existing approaches in both depth and trajectory estimation accuracy and is applicable to different camera models.

## Prop-WRF: Proposal-Guided Wireless Radiance Field for Scalable Multi-Frequency Outdoor Channel Frequency Response Prediction
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11845v1
- **Authors**: ['Yulin Fu', 'Jiancun Fan', 'Shiyu Zhai', 'Zhibo Duan', 'Jie Luo']
- **Abstrat**: Sparse-directional wireless radiance fields (WRFs) enable complex channel frequency response (CFR) prediction from receiver locations and multipath direction priors. However, in large-scale outdoor environments, long sampling intervals make it difficult to concentrate a limited sampling budget around valid virtual anchors (VAs), degrading prediction accuracy and training stability. This paper proposes Prop-WRF, a proposalnetwork-guided WRF for CFR prediction across multiple subcarriers. A lightweight proposal network learns VA occupancy densities through online distillation from the main network and iteratively guides resampling toward likely VA locations. The main network then predicts complex radiance over the refined intervals and synthesizes CFRs using an analytical propagation model. By introducing frequency encoding only into the radiance branch, a single model jointly learns shared VA geometry from observations across subcarriers while representing frequencydependent complex radiance. A stage-wise hybrid normalized loss and an interval-weight regularizer further strengthen weakchannel supervision and suppress spurious VA peaks. In addition, a multipath simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) front end refines receiver states and path associations to construct geometrically consistent training priors. Simulations show that Prop-WRF improves CFR prediction accuracy and training stability over conventional sparse-directional WRF baselines in larger environments.

## WanderBench: Can World Models Explore Dynamic Worlds from Video?
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12557v1
- **Authors**: ['Yuhang Chen', 'Jiheng Li', 'Jiale Wang', 'Zai Wang', 'Guangyue Liu']
- **Abstrat**: Video world models conditioned on an input video make it possible to explore real, recorded dynamic scenes, enabling re-shooting of footage, free-viewpoint replay, and real-to-sim environments for embodied agents. These evaluations do not directly assess whether exploration preserves subject identity, event progression, and scene consistency upon revisiting a location. We introduce WanderBench, a benchmark for exploring dynamic worlds from video. It comprises 745 single-shot source videos of real and AI-generated content in three difficulty tiers (3-25 s), paired with camera routes given as keyboard actions, and evaluates six dimensions with 24 human-validated sub-metrics. Benchmarking eight models, we find that as clips grow longer, models keep the static scene and visual quality but lose camera control and the source dynamics, and that none keeps revisited places consistent. We further propose WanderLand, a training-free framework that conditions each frame of a frozen model on the same-instant source and, on revisits, on its own earlier static content. WanderLand improves five models on every dimension, by up to 8.3 points in source-dynamics fidelity.

## LawKAN: Group-Law Layers for Composition -- When Imposing a Known Law Pays Off
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12567v1
- **Authors**: ['Anna Mishchenko', 'Nikolai Gavrilin']
- **Abstrat**: Many tasks compose transformations by a known rule: chained rotations in robotics and navigation. Learned models relearn it, compounding error; classical estimators hardcode it and miss hardware deviations. We impose the known structure and learn only the remainder: an exact non-commutative group law sits in the node of a self-contained layer, its edges learn local increments, and a zero-initialized correction psi learns unknown deviations of the law. A pre-training criterion predicts exact representability.
