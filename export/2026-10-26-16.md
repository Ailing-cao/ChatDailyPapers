## SceneBloom: Agentic Indoor Environment Generation with Realistic and Diverse Layouts for Embodied Learning
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32978v1
- **Authors**: ['Yuhan Liu', 'Lianhui Qin', 'Shenji Wan']
- **Abstrat**: Training generalizable embodied agents requires realistic, varied, and simulation-ready indoor environments. Language-guided methods support semantic planning and object-level reasoning, but precise spatial grounding remains difficult. Visual generative models offer rich priors over plausible arrangements, yet their outputs require geometric reasoning to become physically valid 3D scenes. We introduce SceneBloom, an agentic framework that combines visual layout sampling with contract-guided 3D compilation. A hierarchical sampler uses diffusion priors to generate furniture arrangements within functional zones. A compiler then uses relations extracted from each proposal to guide geometric fitting and local pose correction. Across five room types, SceneBloom attains the highest visual quality and physical stability among the compared methods and is the only method with no detected mesh collisions and out-of-bounds objects. Its layouts are also closer to the distribution of real rooms than those of all compared baselines. Object navigation policies trained in these realistic scenes achieve a higher success rate in held-out real scans than those trained on an equal number of real training scans. Code is available at https://github.com/Tinaliu0123/scenebloom.





## SP-Guide: Enhancing Aerial Vision-Language Navigation with Spatial Priors
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32697v1
- **Authors**: ['Haoxiang Tang', 'Dong Zhou', 'Shen Tan', 'Minxuan Jiang', 'Xitong Li']
- **Abstrat**: Aerial vision-language navigation (AVLN) requires a UAV to reach a language-described target from partial egocentric observations while reasoning over large outdoor scenes. Existing approaches typically fuse view-centered RGB observations and globally anchored history maps as generic visual embeddings, leaving cross-modal spatial alignment to be learned from sparse navigation supervision. We present SP-Guide, a transformer- based goal-prediction framework that addresses this weakness of alignment through a Spatial Prior Cross-Modal Encoder, an Instruction Enhanced Decoder, and a Goal Convergence Filter for terminal target refinement. Experiments implemented on the CityNav benchmark show that SP-Guide improves success rate over the representative baselines, with the largest gains on unseen environments. These results indicate that physically grounded spatial priors provide an effective inductive bias for robust AVLN.





## HGWM: Hierarchical Graph-Guided World Model for Zero-Shot Object Navigation via Graph Matching
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32554v1
- **Authors**: ['I-tak Ieong', 'Hao Tang']
- **Abstrat**: Zero-shot object navigation requires an embodied agent to choose where to search while retaining evidence from previous observations. HGWM couples an LLM-generated room-anchor prior with persistent spatial-semantic memory. A coarse stage assigns visual-semantic scores to candidate headings on a 0-10 scale; a fine stage measures symbolic compatibility between observed objects, room context, and the goal prior. Phase-dependent fusion combines these scores with an LLM direction recommendation before local action selection. On HM3D v0.1, the reported HGWM configuration achieves 62.6% success rate (SR) and 31.5% success weighted by path length (SPL), compared with 58.1% and 31.2% for WMNav. On MP3D, the corresponding values are 46.8% and 17.9%, compared with 45.4% and 17.2%. The results indicate higher success point estimates and modest path-efficiency gains. On HM3D v0.2, the full model obtains 74.4% SR and 31.9% SPL. Additional component ablations reduce SR by 0.9-2.3 percentage points and SPL by 0.6-1.5 points. These are author-reported aggregate measurements. The accompanying implementation repairs pass offline tests; episode-level rerun records are not available. Evaluation is confined to simulation.





## From Freehand Demonstrations to Autonomous Robotic Ultrasound: An Anatomy-Anchored Learning Framework
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32525v1
- **Authors**: ['Yuan Bi', 'Fangxu Han', 'Ye Su', 'Yiping Zhou', 'Zhongliang Jiang', 'Nassir Navab']
- **Abstrat**: Ultrasound imaging is highly operator-dependent, motivating robotic systems that reproduce expert scanning skills consistently. These skills involve coordinated interpretation of anatomical landmarks, ultrasound images, and probe-tissue interactions that are difficult to encode in hand-designed control strategies. Imitation learning offers an alternative, but its observation and action representations should reflect the anatomically structured nature of scanning. We propose an anatomy-anchored imitation-learning framework that combines anatomy-guided probe placement with learned ultrasound navigation. The placement module identifies a task-specific anatomical starting point from external visual observations and establishes the initial probe pose as the reference frame for scanning. A flow-based policy then generates scanning motions from anchor-relative probe poses, ultrasound images, probe-mounted camera views, and contact forces, while a termination head predicts task completion. This avoids continuous reliance on third-person vision and reduces dependence on the robot-patient configuration. A handheld acquisition platform enables multimodal demonstrations to be collected during natural freehand scanning without a robot. On 16 unseen participants, task success reaches 96%, 94%, 88%, and 94% for kidney, gallbladder, and liver standard-plane acquisition and radial artery sweeping, respectively. Compared with a fine-tuned pi_0 model, success on the abdominal tasks improves by 10-42 percentage points, with lower time-to-target, peak contact force, and trajectory jerk. Ablations support the complementary roles of anatomical anchoring, egocentric vision, and force feedback. End-to-end trials with automatic placement achieve 90-100% success, and the policies transfer without retraining to a second robot with 83-100% success.





## Probing Collision Grounding in Vision-Language Models for Safe Human-Robot Collaboration
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31196v2
- **Authors**: ['Jun Wang', 'Xiaohao Xu', 'Xiaonan Huang']
- **Abstrat**: Safe human--robot collaboration requires more than visual description: a monitor must determine whether the robot body is safely separated, already colliding with the scene or a person, or about to collide. We call this capability collision grounding: binding visual observations to robot body geometry, camera viewpoint, scene layout, human proximity, and temporal motion in order to infer present and imminent contact. We introduce TouchSafeBench, a physics-grounded benchmark for evaluating collision grounding in vision-language models (VLMs). Built in Habitat~3.0, TouchSafeBench contains 2,940 simulated indoor co-presence episodes across social navigation and social rearrangement, with synchronized multi-view RGB-D observations, top-down trajectory maps, calibrated camera metadata, and simulator-derived contact labels. We study two deployment-facing tasks: classifying the current safety state and warning about imminent collision before contact. Across three frontier or robotics-oriented VLMs and nine visual representations, current models remain far from reliable: the best average Macro-F1 stays below 50\%, explicit depth is not automatically transformed into robot-body collision evidence, and robot--scene contact is consistently harder than human-contact risk. TouchSafeBench reveals a central limitation of embodied VLMs: visual fluency does not imply physical accountability. Reliable robot safety monitors will need representations that explicitly bind viewpoint, robot morphology, metric geometry, and future collision. The benchmark is available at https://jun25336-bit.github.io/TouchSafeBench/ .





## TravSUITE: Traversability via Self-Supervised, Uncertainty-Aware IRL and Terrain Estimation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32130v1
- **Authors**: ['Samuel Triest', 'Amirreza Shaban', 'David D. Fan', 'Wenshan Wang', 'Sebastian Scherer']
- **Abstrat**: Traversability analysis in off-road settings remains a fundamental challenge for mobile robots. Key difficulties include constructing an accurate and expressive local map from multimodal sensor data, and using the map to design traversability rules that yield desirable navigation behavior. Importantly, this system must be resilient to the limited sensing regime brought about by complex environments and high speeds. In this paper, we present TravSUITE, a traversability system suitable for high-speed navigation in off-road environments. TravSUITE consists of two major components: 1) an efficient voxel mapper that leverages visual foundation models (VFMs) to build a rich geometric-semantic local map from streams of on-board sensor data, and 2) a unified neural network that jointly predicts traversability-relevant quantities in bird's eye view (BEV), including geometry, semantics, speed and cost. Our training strategy is entirely annotation-free and self-supervised, leveraging tasks such as map inpainting and inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) to learn both map representations and traversability. We perform a thorough ablation study and comparison to state-of-the-art approaches, and the results indicate that cost learning and auxiliary inpainting each contribute significantly to planning quality, and their combination is critical for achieving state-of-the-art performance in off-road path planning. We also design a simple risk adaptation mechanism to leverage our method's uncertainty estimates at deploy-time, and demonstrate that a combination of inpainting and risk estimation can result in 80% fewer navigation errors and 5% faster autonomous traversal speeds in real-world hardware experiments.





## See What I Need, Dream What I Want: A Navigation Foundation Model on a Self-Constructed Representation Space
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31762v1
- **Authors**: ['Jiawei Chen', 'Yinxi Wang', 'Zihan Yang', 'Meijun Gao', 'Yaowei Liu', 'Shuming Zhang', 'Zecheng Li', 'Xiangju Mi', 'Yuxuan Liu', 'Runzhong Huang', 'Tianlin Li', 'Xun Zhang', 'Zhengyang Wang', 'Xu Miao', 'Tong Yang']
- **Abstrat**: What visual representation should a navigation policy act on? Navigation policies built on vision foundation models largely leave representation design to the encoders' pretraining objectives. We present RepresentNav, a navigation foundation model built on a self-constructed representation space: a visual representation constructed by the policy's own objectives from vision foundation models. RepresentNav builds this space with Representation Residuals, a compact three-layer transformer that attends over features spanning the full depth of multiple complementary vision foundation models, so that the policy learns to see what it needs for navigation. In the same space it dreams what it wants: before every action a latent chain-of-thought predicts the representation of the scene a moment ahead. The dream is trained end-to-end with the policy as a joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA), with a next-representation prediction loss and a regularizer on its targets. RepresentNav sets a new state of the art on VLNVerse, VLN-CE R2R, EVT-Bench, and two point-goal benchmarks, and its gains carry over to a physical robot amid clutter and moving people. On VLNVerse fine-grained, RepresentNav achieves 83.80% success, outperforming the previous state of the art by 20.05 percentage points, and on EVT-Bench distracted tracking it reaches 85.5% with a single camera (+10.1). With the same encoders, the self-constructed space clearly outperforms concatenating their outputs and learns to weight their layers unevenly, and a dream in it cuts collisions by a quarter. A single dreamed latent decodes into the depth, semantics, and 3D occupancy of the scene a moment ahead, more accurately than pretrained encoders applied to the real future frame, although occupancy is never supervised, and the rollout tracks the robot's own motion over the next second: the dream emerges as a compact latent world model.

## Embodied Emissions of 2.5D and 3D Scaling in Chipletized GPUs for Low-Carbon AI
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32830v1
- **Authors**: ['Hannah Watson', 'Arjun Singh', 'Jennifer Stephenson', 'Job Soethoudt', 'Debjyoti Bhattacharjee', 'James Myers']
- **Abstrat**: GPU compute resources are scaling to support growing AI workloads, leading to further rises in the climate change impact of hyperscale data centers that rely on high-performance GPUs. To model how packaging decisions impact the carbon footprint we have estimated the embodied emissions (EE) of a chiplet-based GPU that has been scaled in 2.5D and 3D using the imec.netzero modelling platform. We doubled the number of compute units and high bandwidth memory (HBM) capacity in the 2.5D and 3D scaled systems and found that 3D scaling results in an 89% increase in EE, as compared to an increase of 141% for 2.5D scaling, positioning 3D scaling as an attractive solution to limit the embodied emissions due to GPU scaling.





## 3D Generation for Embodied AI and Robotic Simulation: A Survey
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26509v4
- **Authors**: ['Tianwei Ye', 'Yifan Mao', 'Minwen Liao', 'Jian Liu', 'Chunchao Guo', 'Dazhao Du', 'Quanxin Shou', 'Fangqi Zhu', 'Song Guo']
- **Abstrat**: Embodied AI and robotic systems increasingly depend on scalable, diverse, and physically grounded 3D content for simulation-based training and real-world deployment. While 3D generative modeling has advanced rapidly, embodied applications impose requirements far beyond visual realism: generated objects must carry kinematic structure and material properties, scenes must support interaction and task execution, and the resulting content must bridge the gap between simulation and reality. This survey reviews 3D generation for embodied AI and organizes the literature around three roles that 3D generation plays in embodied systems. In Data Generator, 3D generation produces simulation-ready objects and assets, including articulated, physically grounded, and deformable content for downstream interaction; in Simulation Environments, it constructs interactive and task-oriented worlds, spanning structure-aware, controllable, and agentic scene generation; and in Sim2Real Bridge, it supports digital twin reconstruction, data augmentation, and synthetic demonstrations for downstream robot learning and real-world transfer. We also show that the field is shifting from visual realism toward interaction readiness, and we identify the main bottlenecks, including limited physical annotations, the gap between geometric quality and physical validity, fragmented evaluation, and the persistent sim-to-real divide, that must be addressed for 3D generation to become a dependable foundation for embodied intelligence. Our project page is at https://3dgen4robot.github.io.

## Natural-Language to Nav2: Operational Policies for Costmap-Based Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32633v1
- **Authors**: ['Jiyeong Chae', 'Hyunkyo Seo', 'Heonjae Lee', 'Kyung-Joon Park']
- **Abstrat**: Autonomous mobile robots support material transport in warehouses and industrial facilities, where navigation must adapt to changing operating conditions. Sensor observations reveal physical obstacles, but do not by themselves specify temporary access rules, route preferences, or exceptions known to operators. This paper presents a natural-language policy interface that brings this knowledge into ROS 2 Nav2. A local large language model converts instructions into JSON policies specifying destinations, spatial constraints, and temporal or event conditions. Deterministic checks govern mission acceptance, and three costmap adapters project accepted policies into the existing navigation stack without changing its planner or controller. On 150 researcher-authored English commands, four local models produce 107-142 correct accepted policies, compared with 44 for a rule-based parser, with 3-20 incorrect acceptances per model. Fault injection records rejection of eight endpoint/JSON faults and 29 validator-stage faults. Navigation experiments use correct task policies to evaluate keepouts, zone costs, person-dependent regions, and event-conditioned passage in simulation and on a TurtleBot3. For Tasks 1-3, mean travel times are 18.4-24.5% lower in simulation and 23.1-35.0% lower on the physical testbed than with the baseline lacking those policies. A native KeepoutFilter comparison and two-robot demonstrations examine basic keepout behavior and shared policy projection. The interface connects operator instructions to explicit navigation policies while retaining Nav2's planning and control components.

## VORL-EXPLORE: A Hybrid Learning Planning Approach to Multi-Robot Exploration in Dynamic Environments
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07973v2
- **Authors**: ['Ning Liu', 'Sen Shen', 'Zheng Li', 'Sheng Liu', 'Dongkun Han', 'Shangke Lyu', 'Thomas Bräunl']
- **Abstrat**: Hierarchical multi-robot exploration decouples frontier allocation from local navigation, making systems brittle in dynamic environments. Without awareness of execution difficulty, allocators may send robots toward frontiers whose routes are congested or unsafe, causing bottlenecks, oscillatory replanning, and redundant coverage. We propose VORL-EXPLORE, a hybrid learning and planning framework that makes exploration execution-aware through execution fidelity, an estimate of local navigability and execution reliability. This fidelity score is used to penalize risky or crowded frontiers in a fidelity-coupled Voronoi allocation objective with inter-robot repulsion, and to arbitrate between global $A^*$ guidance and a reactive reinforcement learning policy during navigation. As a result, robots follow efficient global plans in reliable regions and rely more on safer local reactions in crowded or changing areas. The fidelity model is recalibrated using self-supervised pseudo-labels derived from recent progress and safety outcomes. Quantitative experiments on randomized dynamic grids and an ablation under severe traffic show higher success rates, shorter exploration lengths, and lower inter-robot coverage overlap. A Gazebo factory scenario is included as a deployment illustration. The source code is available at https://github.com/21ning/VORL-EXPLORE.





## From Tabletop to Walking Bot: Deploying a VLA Model on a Legged Manipulator Robot
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32271v1
- **Authors**: ['Giovanni Minelli', 'Antonio Langella', 'Giovanni B. Dessy', 'Lorenzo Amatucci', 'Giulio Turrisi', 'Victor Barasuol', 'Claudio Semini']
- **Abstrat**: Vision-language-action (VLA) models are typically pretrained on broad, diverse datasets and subsequently fine-tuned on a comparatively small dataset tailored to a specific target robot. Such adaptation recipe is now widely established, achieving its most mature results on fixed-base arms due to their static base dynamics and consistent reference frame. Mounting the same arm on a quadruped extends the workspace to the entire floor, but fundamentally changes the embodiment: rather than controlling the arm in isolation, the policy must jointly coordinate base navigation, body posture, and end-effector motion. In this work, we study the practical demands of this embodiment change across data collection, model adaptation, and inference design to achieve closed-loop loco-manipulation on a Unitree Go2 with an AgileX Piper arm. We report the choices that made this real-robot deployment feasible, namely model variant, action horizon, and demonstration conventions, together with a study tailored to a pick-and-place task, separating data coverage from model capacity. We show that expanding demonstration coverage reduces held-out end-effector error by 30%, and incorporating secondary skills (such as pushing rather than grasping) introduces new behavioral modes without degrading baseline performance. Finally, closed-loop real-world experiments show how dataset composition shapes success and failure, validating the pipeline for more complex loco-manipulation.

## Deep Learning Warm Starts for Trajectory Optimization on the International Space Station
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05588v4
- **Authors**: ['Somrita Banerjee', 'Abhishek Cauligi', 'Marco Pavone']
- **Abstrat**: Trajectory optimization is a cornerstone of modern robot autonomy, enabling systems to compute trajectories and controls in real-time while respecting safety and physical constraints. However, it has seen limited usage in spaceflight applications due to its heavy computational demands that exceed the capability of most flight computers. In this work, we provide results on the first in-space demonstration of using machine learning-based warm starts for accelerating trajectory optimization for the Astrobee free-flying robot onboard the International Space Station (ISS). We formulate a data-driven optimal control approach that trains a neural network to learn the structure of the trajectory generation problem being solved using sequential convex programming (SCP). This work represents a significant milestone in the use of learning-based control for spaceflight applications and a stepping stone for future advances in the use of machine learning for autonomous guidance, navigation, & control.
