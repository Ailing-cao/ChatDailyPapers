## Adapt for Planning, Not just Prediction: Optimistic Test-Time World Model Adaptation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35636v1
- **Authors**: ['Akshay Mete', 'Dileep Kalathil', 'P. R. Kumar']
- **Abstrat**: Latent world models are typically pre-trained offline and kept frozen at test time, so planning with them can degrade under distribution shift. Test-time adaptation (TTA) can mitigate this degradation by updating the model during deployment. Unlike pre-training on fixed data, TTA for planning is closed-loop: the planner uses model rollouts to select actions, and the resulting transitions drive the next update. This raises the question: which objective should guide TTA for planning? Under a prediction-only objective, the model is fit only to the outcomes of its own actions, so it can remain wrong about the actions that would reach the goal, and low prediction error need not translate into better planning. Inspired by reward-biased maximum likelihood from adaptive control, we introduce Optimistic Test-Time Adaptation (OpTTA). Its objective has two terms, the prediction loss on observed transitions and the planner's goal-reaching cost on its candidate rollouts, both minimized over the model parameters. The planning term biases adaptation toward models that are optimistic about reaching the goal, without any additional environment interaction. Across visual and environment shifts on manipulation, reaching and navigation tasks, OpTTA raises final planning success over the frozen model by 13.5 percentage points on average, compared with 6.9 for the prediction-only objective (AdaJEPA), when only the predictor is adapted. When the encoder is adapted as well, the gains are 13.5 and 7.5 points. These experiments span three JEPA world models with different encoders and training objectives and, for temporal straightening, both CEM and gradient-based planners. Our results suggest that test-time adaptation of world models for planning benefits from an optimistic, task-aware term in addition to the prediction loss.





## Beyond Being Accurate: Implications for Using Natural Language Descriptions for Accessible 3D Modeling with~Blind and Low Vision Makers
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35562v1
- **Authors**: ['Gene S-H Kim', 'Nicholas Oskiper', 'Sean Follmer', 'Megan Hofmann']
- **Abstrat**: People who are blind or have low vision (BLV) are largely excluded from 3D modeling tools that rely on visual representations of geometry. Natural language descriptions offer a potential path to accessibility, but it is unclear whether language alone can support the deep spatial understanding that editing, not just browsing, requires. Advances in generative AI offer the promise of automatic model description, yet descriptions suited to BLV modelers are structurally distinct from alt-text and from existing 3D model captioning datasets, and the domain-specific requirements for generating them are not well understood. We address this through two coordinated studies: an autoethnographic pilot in which the blind first author worked with 12 sighted participants to develop a taxonomy of descriptive strategies, and a Wizard-of-Oz main study that applied those strategies as idealized scripts with 10 BLV participants. We identify a systematic use-to-editing gap: descriptions that support accurate but incomplete mental models are insufficient for specifying modifications, because editing requires knowing the state of features the user did not know to ask about. We show that this gap produces four failure types (accuracy, completeness, expression, and navigation) that accuracy-focused evaluation frameworks cannot detect. We propose a two-phase evaluation framework that diagnoses the failure type a description system exhibits and guides developers toward targeted design interventions, thereby providing a methodological standard for assessing whether description-based systems genuinely support BLV modelers as creators.





## Probing Visual Planning in Image Editing Models
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22868v2
- **Authors**: ['Zhimu Zhou', 'Yanpeng Zhao', 'Qiuyu Liao', 'Bo Zhao', 'Xiaojian Ma']
- **Abstrat**: Visual planning represents a crucial facet of human intelligence, especially in tasks that require complex spatial reasoning and navigation. Yet, in machine learning, this inherently visual problem is often tackled through a verbal-centric lens. While recent research demonstrates the promise of fully visual approaches, they suffer from significant computational inefficiency due to the step-by-step planning-by-generation paradigm. In this work, we present EAR, an editing-as-reasoning paradigm that reformulates visual planning as a single-step image transformation. To isolate intrinsic reasoning from visual recognition, we employ abstract puzzles as probing tasks and introduce AMAZE, a procedurally generated dataset that features the classical Maze and Queen problems, covering distinct, complementary forms of visual planning. The abstract nature of AMAZE also facilitates automatic evaluation of autoregressive and diffusion-based models in terms of both pixel-wise fidelity and logical validity. We assess leading proprietary and open-source editing models. The results show that they all struggle in the zero-shot setting, finetuning on basic scales enables remarkable generalization to larger in-domain scales and out-of-domain scales and geometries. However, our best model that runs on high-end hardware fails to match the zero-shot efficiency of human solvers, highlighting a persistent gap in neural visual reasoning.





## Learning Counterfactual World Models for Embodied Reasoning under Partial Observability
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05834v2
- **Authors**: ['Todd Y. Zhou', 'Daniel Zhang']
- **Abstrat**: World models promise a general route to embodied intelligence: learn predictive dynamics once, then reason, plan, and act with them. Increasingly, the representations beneath such models are pretrained on large-scale video, interaction, and multimodal corpora, which raises a question prediction quality alone cannot answer: when is a learned representation actually actionable? We identify a failure mode we call counterfactual collapse: a model predicts visually plausible futures while failing to distinguish interventions with different behavioral consequences. This arises whenever a representation is optimized for perceptual similarity rather than intervention structure, which is precisely the objective under which most large-scale pretrained encoders are learned. We introduce Counterfactual Latent World Models (CLWM), which combine a recurrent belief-state encoder, action-conditioned latent dynamics, and a contrastive counterfactual objective that separates futures induced by distinct interventions even when their observations look alike. Across occluded manipulation, aliased navigation, and long-horizon manipulation, CLWM improves planning success over the strongest baseline (65.1% $\to$ 74.6% on Occluded Push and 67.3% $\to$ 78.9% on Aliased Maze) and reduces exploitative planning failures (18.4% $\to$ 9.7% on Deferred Kitchen), with ablations attributing the gains to hard counterfactual negatives, especially perceptual-alias negatives. Finally, our counterfactual separability metric, which tracks planning success across the five baseline model classes ($r \ge 0.94$), is representation-agnostic: given intervention-outcome labels, it can audit any encoder, pretrained or trained from scratch, before a planner trusts it. We do not yet measure it on large-scale pretrained encoders. Here we establish the metric and its relationship to planning success for world models trained from scratch.





## IndustryNav: Evaluating Spatial Reasoning and Safety of Embodied Agents in Dynamic Industrial Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17384v3
- **Authors**: ['Yifan Li', 'Lichi Li', 'Anh Dao', 'Xinyu Zhou', 'Wenjun Huang', 'Tianyi Ma', 'Yicheng Qiao', 'Zheda Mai', 'Daeun Lee', 'Zichen Chen', 'Pan Wang', 'Lehan Yang', 'Tianlong Wang', 'Zhen Tan', 'Sheng Li', 'Mohit Bansal', 'Yang Ni', 'Yu Kong']
- **Abstrat**: Visual large language models (VLLMs) are increasingly used as embodied agents, yet industrial warehouses demand more than reaching a goal: agents must navigate safely around moving workers and vehicles. To evaluate embodied agents' goal reaching and safety, we introduce IndustryNav, a simulation environment with 24 warehouse scenes for closed-loop navigation to specified destinations. Alongside task success and efficiency, we introduce safety-aware metrics that capture collisions and near-collision warnings along each trajectory. These experiments show that policies can succeed on new routes in familiar warehouses yet fail to transfer to held-out layouts, making cross-scene generalization an open challenge.





## Behavior Spatial Benchmark: Evaluating Spatial Understanding for Robotics

## Explaining Navigation Decisions: Stage-Aware Visual Attribution for Vision-Language Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34028v1
- **Authors**: ['Yiyuan Yao', 'Xinpeng Ding', 'Minglei Chen', 'Fei Gao', 'Lihuo He', 'Jie Yuan', 'Aijun Li', 'Nannan Wang', 'Xinbo Gao']
- **Abstrat**: As Vision-Language Models (VLMs) increasingly enable robots to follow language instructions, understanding their navigation decisions becomes important for reliable deployment. Existing explainability methods overlook a key challenge in Vision-Language Navigation (VLN): evidence for the current action may be distributed across historical and current observations and associated with different instruction stages. We identify instruction-stage insensitivity, where existing methods produce similar explanations despite changes across different navigation stages. To address this issue, we propose a stage-aware attribution framework for VLN. We first construct action-conditioned temporal instruction--visual attributions across observations. These attributions are projected into instruction space to estimate stage associations, which guide the aggregation of relevant historical evidence for refining the current explanation. An instruction-guided spatial filter further suppresses isolated responses while preserving compact, instruction-relevant regions. Experiments across multiple VLN policies and benchmarks show improved attribution quality over existing methods. We further demonstrate that our method acts as a diagnostic tool for analyzing failure cases, and validate its applicability to real-world navigation on a Unitree G1 robot.





## StepTrigger: Contact-State-Triggered Backdoor Attacks on VLM-Powered Legged Robots
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26131v2
- **Authors**: ['Jiageng Zhang', 'Doniyorkhon Obidov', 'Kaichen Yang']
- **Abstrat**: Large language models and vision-language models are increasingly used as high-level planners in robotic systems, using task goals and sensor summaries to select navigation or manipulation actions. This creates a new backdoor surface: a compromised planner can behave normally in most runs, yet change its target selection when a hidden trigger is present. Prior attacks on LLM-powered or embodied agents mainly rely on triggers that appear in language, camera-visible objects, scene semantics, or specific sequences of past actions. This paper presents StepTrigger, a contact-state-triggered backdoor attack for VLM-powered legged robots. The trigger is not a prompt token or a visible marker. It is produced by pressure and foot-ground contact patterns that arise when a Unitree Go1 quadruped walks across a dense terrain patch.   Unlike conventional visual or textual triggers, contact signals are inherently noisy and may also arise during benign locomotion. To avoid treating every pressure anomaly as a trigger, StepTrigger learns a selective backdoor policy from multimodal robot state, using incidental pressure events as benign examples and dense-patch contacts as poisoned examples. In a stratified offline evaluation, the trained planner achieved 98.75% clean behavior preservation, 92.50% false-trigger rejection, 76.25% true-trigger activation, and 89.17% overall parsed behavior accuracy. These results reveal a backdoor surface in proprioceptive and contact channels that is not captured by defenses focused only on language, vision, or action history.





## MOON: Learning When to Forget and How to Move On in Vision-and-Language Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33546v1
- **Authors**: ['Jingmeng Zhou', 'Seson Sun', 'Binyao Xu', 'Lingming Zhang', 'Hanshi Wang', 'Fudong Ge', 'Weiming Hu', 'Jin Gao', 'Zhipeng Zhang']
- **Abstrat**: Recent vision-and-language navigation (VLN) policies act on their visual history, and an incorrect action can take them off the instructed route. Paired interventions on four VLN policies show that clearing the history doubles pooled success at the onset of a deviation but lowers it on route, while later in a deviation, removing only the off-route frames helps much less. After a deviation, the retained history keeps the policy from turning where a turn is needed, which we call behavioral inertia. We propose MOON, a framework that equips a frozen VLN policy with two lightweight modules, adding less than 1% of its parameters. A deviation judge decides when to forget, raising an alarm that clears the history when it predicts that the agent is off route. A recovery policy, trained with GRPO from alarm states, learns how to move on, turning more of the successes found only by sampling into a single greedy rollout. Experiments on R2R-CE and RxR-CE show that MOON reaches the highest R2R-CE success rate among the compared methods and raises the success rate of its frozen base by 5.2 and 6.0 points, and an ablation with a random trigger indicates that this gain depends on when the history is cleared. Without retraining, MOON also improves zero-shot object-goal navigation on HM3D-OVON. Together, these results suggest that deviation recovery depends on knowing when to forget, not only how to act. Code is available at https://github.com/AutoLab-SAI-SJTU/MOON.





## Resilient Decentralized Radar-LiDAR-Inertial Odometry in Challenging Subterranean Environments
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35363v1
- **Authors**: ['Moumita Mukherjee', 'Avijit Banerjee', 'Anton Koval', 'George Nikolakopoulos']
- **Abstrat**: Accurate localization in GPS-denied and visually degraded environments affected by smoke, dust, or fog remains a critical challenge for autonomous systems. While LiDAR provides high-resolution spatial perception, its performance deteriorates under particulate occlusion. In contrast, frequency-modulated continuous-wave (FMCW) radar offers robustness under such conditions but suffers from sparse measurements and outliers. This article presents a resilient decentralized LiDAR Radar Inertial Odometry (LRIO) framework that fuses the complementary strengths of LiDAR, radar, and inertial sensing to achieve robust pose estimation in challenging subterranean environments. At the core of the framework is an Adaptive Residual Thresholding (ART) mechanism that autonomously detects temporal sensor degradation and selectively adjusts the fusion process to minimize unreliable measurements. Building on this, a Robust Error-Tolerant Optimal Information Filter (RETOIF) is employed within a decentralized fusion architecture to integrate LiDAR-inertial and multiple radar-inertial odometry estimates in a fault-aware manner, enhancing resilience to environmental disturbances and intermittent sensor failures. The proposed framework is evaluated using real-world sensor data collected in GPS-denied subterranean tunnels under smoke-free and smoke-degraded conditions. In the smoke-free experiment, the proposed RETOIF framework achieves a root mean square localization error (RMSE) of 2.1733, compared with 2.9929 for the conventional centralized LRIO approach, corresponding to a 27.4\% reduction in RMSE. Experimental results further demonstrate the framework's ability to maintain pose estimation under smoke-induced sensor degradation, highlighting its potential for resilient autonomous navigation and mapping in challenging subterranean environments.

## VPR-Space: A Benchmark for Visual Place Recognition in Space Analog Domains
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35642v1
- **Authors**: ['Richard Fechner', 'Peer Neubert']
- **Abstrat**: Visual Place Recognition (VPR) is an important means for loop closure detection in visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM). While state-of-the-art VPR and SLAM approaches excel in urban and feature-rich natural environments, the vast and feature-poor environments of extraterrestrial surfaces like on the Moon or Mars pose severe challenges for existing approaches. This paper presents a novel benchmark, VPR-Space, to evaluate VPR capabilities on extraterrestrial surfaces. There are three contributions: (1) We collected sixteen VPR database-query datasets based on existing data from Space Analog Domains (SAD), terrestrial environments that resemble properties of the Moon or Mars. The collection includes a range of challenges regarding environment, viewpoint change, and illumination conditions. (2) To address the ill-posed nature of VPR, we propose a novel approach to generate the ground-truth information in VPR benchmarks. Instead of defining that a query and datbase image show the same place if and only if their camera poses are within fixed position and viewing direction thresholds, we propose to make the decision dependent on whether the match would be valuable in the downstream SLAM task. To avoid the overhead and restrictions of evaluating VPR in-the-loop within SLAM systems, we provide VPR ground-truth (GT) matrices based on the performance of an ensemble of state-of-the-art Relative Pose Estimators (RPE). Under this GT we require a VPR method to identify exactly those image pairs for which a subsequently applied RPE is expected to provide a valuable pose estimate in a SLAM context. (3) We evaluated nine state-of-the-art VPR approaches. The results show considerable room for improvement and indicate directions for future research. We provide a pre-release version of the dataset on github: $\texttt{https://github.com/rfechner/VPR-Space}$





## Bracketing Shows the Way: Adaptive Multi-Exposure Policy as Input for Robust Visual SLAM
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33174v1
- **Authors**: ['Olivier Gamache', 'Anthony Lavertu', 'David-Alexandre Duclos', 'Jean-Michel Fortin', 'François Pomerleau', 'Philippe Giguère']
- **Abstrat**: Rapid illumination transitions can disturb Visual SLAM (VSLAM) by saturating images before the Automatic Exposure (AE) policy adapts, removing the visual correspondences needed to maintain tracking. Our proposed methods mitigate this disturbance at two levels: B-Adapt acquires adaptive exposure brackets to cover the scene dynamic range while promoting photometric overlap, and BVSLAM directly uses the multi-exposure sequence as constraints in a factor graph state estimator without relying on High Dynamic Range (HDR) merging. We evaluate both methods against state-of-the-art approaches in nominal environments and during a repeated tunnel-exit transition at an emulated vehicle speed of 32 m s-1. In the tunnel, combining our B-Adapt with BVSLAM reduces the state estimation error by 78 % compared with AE images. These results suggest that bracketing efficiently improves visual pose estimation robustness to rapid illumination disturbances while retaining low error under nominal conditions. Our code is available at https://github.com/norlab-ulaval/bracketing-shows-the-way.

## Geometry-Aware Epipolar Correspondence Graphs for Monocular Visual Odometry
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04554v3
- **Authors**: ['Prateeth Rao', 'Sachit Rao']
- **Abstrat**: We demonstrate Graph Representation Learning to address challenges that arise in visual odometry. In this context, the change in pose of a camera is typically estimated using matched correspondences in frames that satisfy the epipolar constraint; finding this unique set under different situations is not a simple task. Employing an existing detector-free model to find matching correspondences in a pair of images, a correspondence graph is constructed whose topology is guided by the epipolar constraint. Starting with a graph where every node is connected to $k$ closest neighbours (by distance), this graph is pruned based on an initial estimate of the Essential Matrix; this feature is in stark contrast with traditional graph formulations where graphs from local spatial neighborhoods defined by heuristics are constructed. The graph learning module is then trained to generate an aggregated representative vector that summarises the geometric context of the graph. This vector forms an input to a Multi-layer Perceptron (MLP), whose outputs are standard rotation and translation elements that define the change in pose. To investigate the effect of graph learning on this representation, four variations of Graph Neural Networks (GNNs): GCN, GAT, GIN, and Graph-based Cross Attention are evaluated under a unified learning framework. The application to monocular visual odometry is validated using outdoor benchmark datasets such as KiTTi, ETH3D, King's College, and Tartan Air. Experimental results demonstrate consistent improvements in trajectory and relative pose estimation across diverse metrics when compared with classical consensus-based estimators and learning-guided correspondence methods.





## TinyFlow: Breaking the Warping Bottleneck for Edge Optical Flow
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33338v1
- **Authors**: ["Xiao'ao Song", 'Chahat Deep Singh']
- **Abstrat**: High-performance optical flow networks are difficult to deploy on resource-constrained edge accelerators because their iterative refinement warps features by the current flow, which relies on flow-dependent resampling and data-dependent memory access. We present TinyFlow, a 1.26M-parameter warp-free optical flow network that preserves iterative coarse-to-fine refinement by replacing the warp with a fixed-window correlation over constant offsets, so the whole network compiles as a single static INT8 graph. Quantization-aware fine-tuning, warp-teacher distillation, and model souping reduce the Float32-to-INT8 penalty to below 0.2 end-point error (EPE) on MPI-Sintel. TinyFlow outperforms EdgeFlowNet and NanoFlowNet on Sintel Clean, Sintel Final, and FlyingChairs in both Float32 and INT8, while using 54% fewer parameters than EdgeFlowNet, and our INT8 model achieves 6.29/7.13 EPE on Sintel Clean/Final. In flow-only monocular visual odometry on TartanAir and UZH-FPV, the same INT8 model yields the lowest trajectory error on seven of eleven sequences.

## Labference: Benchmarking the Reasoning-Action Connection in Robotic Laboratories

Together, these findings highlight the need to connect task-appropriate reasoning with reliable, timely action. By making this connection measurable and diagnosable, Labference supports targeted improvements toward autonomous experimentation for Embodied AI for Science.

## EAST: Embodied AI Safety Testing via Systematic Fault Injection in Digital Twins
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34358v1
- **Authors**: ['Yuhao Tian', 'Tao Li', 'Ling Zhou', 'Dave Towey', 'Zhaoxin Fan', 'Wenjun Wu', 'Rubing Huang', 'Tsong Yueh Chen']
- **Abstrat**: Task success is not a sufficient measure of safety for embodied artificial intelligence (AI), because robots may complete objectives while violating safety requirements. Existing evaluations often emphasize performance and robustness, but lack a systematic route from documented safety hazards to executable, verifiable tests. We present EAST (Embodied AI Safety Testing), a digital-twin framework that converts literature-grounded safety evidence into fault-injection tests across perception, instructions, environments, actions, and timing. EAST combines an expert-reviewed safety-fault taxonomy and eight executable operators with applicability and validity constraints. Its three-stage workflow generates test specifications, verifies fault realization during simulated execution, and independently assesses safety challenges, violations, task outcomes, and behavioral signatures using simulator-state evidence. The framework preserves execution traces and replayable records, supporting attribution and reproducibility across experiments.
