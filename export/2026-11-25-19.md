## Lost on Campus: Evaluating Embodied Spatial Reasoning of Vision-Language Models in the Wild
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33145v1
- **Authors**: ['Zehan Zheng', 'Yanyuan Chen', 'Deming Li', 'Yutao Tang', 'Jianwen Xie', 'Alan Yuille', 'Jieneng Chen', 'Cheng Peng']
- **Abstrat**: Embodied Spatial Reasoning (ESR) is central to deploying Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in real-world embodied tasks, yet remains inadequately evaluated. Existing benchmarks primarily assess spatial reasoning in static images or synthetic indoor environments, where semantic shortcuts often suffice, making it difficult to faithfully evaluate spatial reasoning capabilities in the wild. In light of this, we present Lost on Campus, a benchmark for evaluating ESR in large-scale real-world outdoor 3D environments reconstructed by 3D Gaussian Splatting. Our benchmark introduces a unified reasoning-action evaluation framework that seamlessly integrates diagnostic QA for isolated reasoning with closed-loop interactive navigation for active reasoning under multimodal instructions. To enable fine-grained diagnosis, we evaluate six capability aspects of ESR: action grounding, spatial foresight, metric awareness, goal-directed planning, ego-allocentric localization, and spatio-temporal consistency. Extensive experiments reveal that: (i) Compared to indoor settings, real-world outdoor environments impose substantially higher demands on spatial reasoning, where existing models exhibit poor performance; (ii) VLMs still struggle with fine-grained visual alignment, with failures in spatial foresight and ego-allocentric localization emerging as primary bottlenecks; (iii) Improving multimodal interaction capabilities and long-term spatial reasoning is crucial for advancing embodied intelligence. These findings highlight that faithful evaluation of ESR demands benchmarks tightly coupling perception, reasoning and action within realistic and continuous environments.





## An XR-Based System for Enhanced Spatial Awareness in Mobile Robot Teleoperation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32584v1
- **Authors**: ['Subhransu Sourav Priyadarshan', 'Valentina Pericu', 'Federico Rollo', 'Gianni Viardo Vercelli', 'Navvab Kashiri']
- **Abstrat**: Teleoperation of mobile robots represents a critical capability for operations in hazardous environments, yet maintaining adequate situational awareness remains challenging without effective visualization and control interfaces. This work presents a multi-modal extended reality (XR) system for robot teleoperation and scene exploration that integrates immersive visual and haptic feedback modalities. To address motion sickness commonly induced by 360-degree camera feeds in head-mounted displays (HMDs), the system employs a fixed dual-camera configuration: a forward-facing camera for primary navigation and a rear-facing camera for enhanced situational awareness without operator discomfort. For navigation in confined or low-visibility conditions, a 16-channel Velodyne LiDAR generates real-time 2D occupancy grid maps, which are converted into vibrational haptic feedback on handheld controllers to provide intuitive, non-visual obstacle detection. User evaluation with 20 participants demonstrates that this haptic modality significantly reduces the cognitive load associated with obstacle avoidance during teleoperation.





## ROVO: Robust Rotation-Only Visual Odometry for UAVs at Low and Moderate Altitudes
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32475v1
- **Authors**: ['Xiaokai Song', 'Li Zhang', 'Xichao Teng', 'Erting Pan', 'Yibin Ye', 'Kun Wang', 'Leqi Liu', 'Guangcheng Dai']
- **Abstrat**: Image noise can confound rotation and translation estimates in monocular visual navigation for unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) at low and moderate altitudes, where even small perturbations may cause large errors in translation direction. Inspired by angular cues in long-range visual perception, we propose rotation-only visual odometry (ROVO) for monocular aerial imagery. ROVO estimates pairwise rotations from matched images, robustly averages them over overlapping windows, and aligns consecutive windows through shared frames. By retaining only orientation in the trajectory state, it prevents uncertain landmark depth from propagating to later estimates. In trajectory simulation, sliding-window ROVO achieved a mean orientation error just 10.7% as large as that of the conventional SLAM baseline. On real flight data, ROVO achieved rotation accuracy comparable to ORB-SLAM3 and DPVO under the original Bell412 settings. With a narrower FoV or at higher flight altitude, ROVO reduced relative rotation RMSE by 19.7--69.4% compared with the next-best method in each comparison. Code and simulation tools are available at https://github.com/sssxxxkkkk/ROVO.





## CriticJEPA: World Models Are Better Critics Than Optimizers
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32357v1
- **Authors**: ['Xvyuan Liu', 'Jianjie Fang', 'Honglin Zhang', 'Zhaolu Wang', 'Xin Zhang', 'Wei Wu', 'Fengli Xu', 'Chen Gao', 'Yong Li']
- **Abstrat**: Visual world models enable goal-directed planning by predicting action outcomes. Gradient-based planners optimize actions to lower predicted goal cost, but continued optimization can exploit prediction errors after execution stops improving. Comparing plans requires informative relative scores; improving them also requires reliable update directions. Exact-state execution reveals that a frozen hierarchical world model ranks goal-conditioned proposals even when its rankings among outputs of prolonged gradient search are uninformative. Effective planning pairs goal-directed candidates with predictions that distinguish their outcomes. We introduce CriticJEPA, which learns goal-conditioned flow proposals from reward-free trajectories and uses frozen rollouts to select among them at every hierarchy level. Forward selection forms a complete planner; short anchored updates refine candidates before selection. Across visual navigation and manipulation, CriticJEPA increases success at a fraction of the planning compute, transferring shared settings across independently trained models, hierarchy depths, and unseen tasks while keeping every world-model weight fixed.





## Relation Before Action: Explicit Relation Modeling for Latent Action Pretraining
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32226v1
- **Authors**: ['Boning Zhang', 'Junyu Gao', 'Changsheng Xu']
- **Abstrat**: Learning latent actions from unlabeled video sequences offers a promising way to acquire reusable action representations without relying on embodiment-specific control annotations. However, existing latent-action pretraining approaches commonly couple the modeling of visual transitions with action inference, directly mapping paired observations or derived transition cues into latent action codes. This formulation leaves two distinct tasks entangled: characterizing the relational structure of an observed transition and abstracting it into a compact action representation. We propose RELACT, a relation-first framework that explicitly separates transition relation modeling from predictive action abstraction. RELACT first constructs a structured relation representation $R$ that captures how visual elements and their interactions evolve across observations. It then infers a compact, state-conditioned stochastic latent action $U$, whose predictive effects are learned through action-conditioned latent dynamics. The resulting action representation can be grounded in native control spaces for model-based planning or used to supervise downstream embodied policies. Extensive experiments across in-domain and out-of-domain image-goal navigation, goal-conditioned manipulation, and downstream policy adaptation demonstrate improved predictive action representations and consistent gains in planning and control, supporting the effectiveness and generality of relation-first latent action pretraining.





## PoseVLN: Learning Geometric State Evolution for Vision-Language Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32218v1
- **Authors**: ['Pengyu Hou', 'Mingfei Han', 'Haihong Hao', 'Xiaolong Wu', 'Bo Dong', 'Xiaojun Chang', 'Salman Khan', 'Bin Shi']
- **Abstrat**: Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) requires an embodied agent to relate past experience, current spatial state, and future motion while following natural-language instructions. Recent approaches improve these capabilities through long-horizon memory, state-aware reasoning, and future prediction, yet these components are often modeled separately, leaving navigation state evolution insufficiently connected across time. We argue that camera pose provides a natural geometric representation for bridging past, present, and future states. Based on this insight, we propose PoseVLN, a pose-aware VLN framework that jointly learns navigation decisions and geometric state evolution. PoseVLN organizes visual memory and the current observation in an episode-relative coordinate frame, and jointly predicts the next K navigation actions together with a local $Δ$Pose over the same horizon. The predicted $Δ$Pose is composed with the current trajectory-level pose to obtain the future pose state, which is recursively propagated throughout navigation, forming a continuous geometric state evolution from past memory to future motion. Experiments on the R2R-CE and RxR-CE Val-Unseen splits demonstrate the effectiveness of explicitly modeling geometric state evolution, with PoseVLN outperforming the strongest compared baselines by 2.6 and 2.9 percentage points in success rate, respectively, using only single RGB observations.

## AirLeg: A Robust and Accurate Self-Supervised Visual-Inertial-Leg SLAM System
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32523v1
- **Authors**: ['Kuan Xu', 'Tianxin Hu', 'Ruofei Bai', 'Ruimeng Liu', 'Yuefan Hao', 'Zeyu Jiang', 'Zitong Zhan', 'Shenghai Yuan', 'Chen Wang', 'Lihua Xie']
- **Abstrat**: Visual-inertial-leg (VIL) state estimation offers cost-effective localization for legged robots, adding only a camera to the onboard IMU and joint encoders. However, learning-based leg odometry is limited by its supervision: networks trained in simulation suffer from the sim-to-real gap, while real-world labels require costly sensors or inherit the errors of visual estimates. To address this, we present AirLeg, a self-supervised VIL SLAM system in which vision and leg proprioception improve each other from training to deployment. We pre-train the leg odometry network in simulation and adapt it to the robot without ground truth through imperative learning, where multi-sensor bundle adjustment uses the leg odometry to refine the visual map, which in turn supervises the network. During deployment, the fused estimates adapt the network online. To improve both localization and this supervision, a dual-window estimator fuses vision, IMU, and leg odometry, relates loaded feet to the visually reconstructed terrain through a deformation-aware constraint, and weights the constraints by variance component estimation. AirLeg completes all 17 GrandTour sequences with the lowest average error (0.19 m) among 19 methods. On a low-cost quadruped, it achieves the lowest drift (0.6%) with only 0.5 h of unlabeled data, in real time on an embedded computer.

## GOLD-SLAM: Gaussian-splatting Omnidirectional LiDAR-Depth SLAM
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32335v1
- **Authors**: ['Weicong Zhang', 'Yuan Huang', 'Yu Zheng', 'Lin Chenxiao', 'Yingying Li', 'Lilong Fan', 'Liangdong Zhuang', 'Yufan Chen', 'Ruiping Liu', 'Junwei Zheng', 'Jiaming Zhang']

To assess deployment feasibility on resource-constrained platforms such as UAVs, we evaluate an embedded configuration of GOLD-SLAM on a Jetson Orin NX (16 GB), demonstrating online operation at the cost of reconstruction quality.

## Embodied AI Fails the Mirror Test: How a Simple Mirror Breaks Robot Policies
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33010v1
- **Authors**: ['Xuanchen Liu', 'Hejun Zhang', 'Boxuan Dong', 'Ying Wu', 'Heshuai Li', 'Wen Gao', 'Yonghong Tian', 'Weihao Yu']
- **Abstrat**: Can a simple mirror expose the limitations of today's robotic manipulation policies? Inspired by the mirror self-recognition test in animal cognition, we investigate whether robot policies can reliably manipulate physical objects in scenes containing their reflections. We introduce RoboDojo-Mirror, a benchmark built on RoboDojo that preserves task objectives, success criteria, and policy checkpoints while comparing a mirror-free baseline with single-mirror, dual-mirror, and mirrored-tabletop conditions. Across five policies spanning Vision-Language-Action models, World Action Models, and agentic policies, every mirror condition reduces aggregate success rates: averaged over the three conditions, the drop ranges from 10.8 to 56.7 percentage points across policies, and it reaches 62.5 points on the mirrored tabletop. Because a mirror is also a physical obstacle, we compare it against geometrically matched non-reflective panels; 50--88\% of mirror-specific failures occur without recorded robot--panel contact, so direct collisions alone do not explain the degradation. Trajectory and contact analyses instead reveal recurring behaviors consistent with phantom targeting of reflected objects and spatial mislocalization of real ones. Targeted diagnostics further characterize grasping near reflections, policy-dependent pushing progress, and physical-target interaction under main-camera occlusion. Real-robot experiments with $π_{0.5}$ and FastWAM also show lower overall success rates under the upright-mirror and mirrored-tabletop conditions. These findings reveal a shared vulnerability to reflective environments across diverse robot policies and motivate reflection-aware evaluation of embodied intelligence. Code is available at https://github.com/iPPLab/robodojo-mirror.





## DiffAnchor: Anchor-Space Diffusion for Monocular 3D Hand Pose Estimation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32513v1
- **Authors**: ['Changlong Jiang', 'Youci Tang', 'Haohong Kuang', 'Hang Xu', 'Jinghong Zheng', 'Ran Wang', 'Zhenghong Yu', 'Chen Feng', 'Xiaofeng Yue', 'Yang Xiao']
- **Abstrat**: Accurate and efficient monocular 3D hand pose estimation is an important perception capability for embodied human interaction and dexterous manipulation. Recent diffusion-based methods model pose uncertainty by iteratively denoising 3D joint coordinates. However, directly diffusing the final joints requires the generative process to simultaneously explore plausible poses and recover precise coordinates, often relying on additional denoising steps or multiple hypotheses and consequently increasing inference cost. In this work, we propose DiffAnchor, a few-step anchor-space diffusion framework that diffuses anchors rather than joints. Our key insight is that stochastic exploration does not need to operate directly on the final output. Instead, diffusion searches for informative intermediate anchors that serve as local 3D regressors, while anchor-to-joint offset prediction and weighted aggregation complete precise joint localization. Specifically, fixed context anchors provide stable global references, whereas noisy dynamic anchors are iteratively refined toward informative joint regions. An importance-guided anchor selection and replenishment mechanism further retains useful anchors, removes ineffective ones, and explores insufficiently covered regions. This design reduces the precision burden of the diffusion process and enables accurate prediction using only a few denoising steps and a single hypothesis. Extensive experiments on HO-3D V2, NYU, ICVL, and InterHand2.6M demonstrate state-of-the-art performance on the first three benchmarks and competitive performance on challenging interacting-hand estimation, while maintaining inference efficiency comparable to deterministic regression baselines.

- **Abstrat**: Streaming reconstruction from monocular image sequences remains challenging, as existing methods typically favor either high-quality rendering or accurate geometry, but rarely both. ## ObsBridge: Observation-Grounded Conditioning for Ego Trajectory Prediction
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32447v1
- **Authors**: ['Zhenhao Wang', 'Shaofeng Liang', 'Jiemin Wu', 'Sijia Chen', 'Keni Shi', 'Nan Li', 'Wenshuo Chen', 'Bowen Tian', 'Runwei Guan', 'Yutao Yue']
- **Abstrat**: Pedestrian forecasting from ego-view observations is challenging because occlusion, limited visibility, and tracking errors corrupt observed motion histories. Such errors are particularly consequential for autonomous robot navigation. Existing paradigms typically either impute noisy history positions before forecasting or jointly train an auxiliary history reconstruction branch. However, predictors trained on clean histories may be sensitive to residual errors in recovered histories at deployment. Meanwhile, auxiliary reconstruction still computes inter-agent interactions over raw, corrupted coordinates. We investigate conditioning future prediction on both recovered historical states and raw observation evidence, with progressive training to accommodate imperfect recovery. Building on this insight, we propose ObsBridge, a flow-based trajectory forecasting framework with observation-grounded historical bridging. Specifically, ObsBridge first employs a frozen estimator to recover historical motion from agent-relative and ego-relative positions, image-plane locations, and visibility states. During training, we introduce an agent-wise curriculum that gradually replaces clean teacher histories with complete estimated histories before context encoding. This progressively exposes the predictor to estimated histories during training. Furthermore, we design a residual geometry adapter to fuse the encoded motion with original observation evidence. We aggregate the resulting representations through social attention to condition the flow-based trajectory generator. Extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks demonstrate that ObsBridge outperforms state-of-the-art methods. On EgoTraj-TBD, it reduces minADE@20 by 6.5\% and minFDE@20 by 8.7\%. The code will be provided at https://github.com/shawnliang420/ObsBridge.





## Does Research-Paper Context Improve LLM-Generated Quadruped Navigation? An Exploratory Simulation Study
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32381v1
- **Authors**: ['Barnabas Juhasz', 'Rolf Pfister']
- **Abstrat**: Large language models can write competent robot navigation code, but not only does the research on the topic advance, a model may also, due to the volume of findings, fail to recall relevant findings and apply them to the task at hand. In this work, we test whether providing particular research papers as context improves the LLM-written navigation code for the Go2 quadruped, with the LLM (Claude Opus 5) and locomotion policy frozen. Specifically, we compare the navigation controllers the LLM writes when provided with and without particular papers across three settings: behavior trees, navigators edited from a shared base, and controllers written from scratch. When a maze's shortest route is blocked mid-episode, 2 of 8 controllers written with the context of an incremental-replanning paper complete all four trips under Isaac Sim physics, against 7 of 8 without it (two-sided Fisher's exact test, p = 0.041). This difference appears only under physics: in an idealized kinematic simulator, the controllers written with and without the paper are indistinguishable. We hypothesize that an LLM provided research papers as context tends to overweight both their content and their goals -- in this case, the efficiency of incremental replanning -- negatively affecting the fulfillment of the main task and leading to less robust code.





## SkillHarness: Execution-Aware Coordination with Plug-and-Play Skills for Long-Horizon Traversal
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32186v1
- **Authors**: ['Bailin He', 'Xiting Zhao', 'Kang Wang', 'Xinyu Zhou', 'Yongxing Cui', 'Haojie Liu', 'Qiao Sun', 'Hongyan Zhang', 'Wenqiang Zhang']
- **Abstrat**: Traversing complex terrain requires robots to coordinate diverse locomotion skills while adapting to unseen obstacles and changing environments. Although perceptive reinforcement learning enables agile locomotion, conventional multi-terrain locomotion rely on fixed policy, limiting adaptability and skill extensibility. Perceptual disturbances and unreliable skill transitions further challenge robust execution. To address these challenges, we propose SkillHarness, an execution-aware coordination framework that integrates a flexible, plug-and-play locomotion skill library. To enhance traversal robustness, we introduce a perceptive reinforcement learning approach that preserves task-relevant environmental references while continuously updating proprioceptive feedback, reducing sensitivity to perceptual fluctuations. A vision-language-guided Harness coordinates navigation and skill execution through event-triggered reasoning, geometric feasibility verification, and execution-phase constraints.
