## PolyGandr: Steering Generative Models via Spatial Navigation through Latent Space
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37446v1
- **Authors**: ['Mingwei S. G. Li', 'Susie S. Y. Li', 'Isabel Moreira de Oliveira', 'Ryan P. Adams', 'Remco Chang']
- **Abstrat**: Generative models support content creation across text, images, videos, and design disciplines, yet their interfaces rely primarily on natural language. While effective for specifying high-level intent, language can be inefficient and imprecise for fine-grained control, iterative refinement, and open-ended exploration. We present PolyGandr, a visual interface for exploring relationships between latent representations and generated outputs, complementing natural-language model steering. PolyGandr formulates generative control as high-dimensional navigation, enabling users to define starting points, steering directions, and step sizes through a unified map-and-mixing-palette interface. Combining global exploration with local refinement supports both broad design-space navigation and fine-grained output manipulation without repeatedly translating visual intent into prompts. We evaluate PolyGandr across three use cases: StyleGAN2 face generation, Stable Diffusion chair design, and rotational kirigami engineering. A formative study indicates that PolyGandr complements existing workflows by supporting real-time editing while simultaneously providing an overview and means to explore the broader design space.





## SeeNav-Agent: Enhancing Vision-Language Navigation with Visual Prompt and Step-Level Policy Optimization
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02631v2
- **Authors**: ['Zhengcheng Wang', 'Zichuan Lin', 'Yijun Yang', 'Haobo Fu', 'Deheng Ye']
- **Abstrat**: Existing Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) agents based on Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) often suffer from errors of perception, reasoning, and planning, which significantly hinder their navigation performance. To address these limitations, a novel VLN agent framework, named SeeNav-Agent, is proposed. First, to reduce hallucinations of the visual module of the VLN agent, a dual-view Visual Prompt (VP) technique is introduced, which can also improve the agent's understanding of current spatial states. Subsequently, a novel step-level Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) method, Step Reward Group Policy Optimization (SRGPO), is designed for post-training. In SRGPO, we first define verifiable process rewards for navigation, and then perform efficient step-level advantage estimation by randomly grouping navigation steps. SRGPO provides dense reward signals for the RFT of the VLN agent and enhances its planning capability. Experiments on both the EmbodiedBench Navigation (Embench-Nav) and VLN-CE validate the effectiveness of the proposed method. By introducing the zero-shot VP module, the GPT-4.1 achieves a navigation success rate of 86.7%, surpassing the current best LVLM by approximately 20 percentage points (pp) on Embench-Nav and achieves an 11.1 pp improvement on VLN-CE compared to the base model. With VP+SRGPO, the Qwen2.5-VL-3B model reaches a navigation success rate of 72.3%, outperforming the best existing LVLM model by 5.6 pp on Embench-Nav and achieves 8.9 pp improvement on VLN-CE compared to the baseline method. Moreover, compared to GRPO and GiGPO, the proposed SRGPO demonstrates significant improvements in training stability, convergence efficiency, and generalization capability.

## CLEAR-IR: Clarity-Enhanced Active Reconstruction of Infrared Imagery
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04883v3
- **Authors**: ['Nathan Shankar', 'Marton Gonczy', 'Hujun Yin', 'Pawel Ladosz']
- **Abstrat**: This paper presents CLEAR-IR (Clarity Enhanced Active Reconstruction of Infrared Imagery), a novel framework for enabling robust robotic perception in dark environments using active infrared (IR) sensing. IR streams are less susceptible to noise than RGB in low light conditions but are dominated by structured light emitter patterns that hinder high level tasks such as object detection, tracking, and localisation. CLEAR-IR addresses this by reconstructing clean, RGB styled images from emitter populated IR inputs using a dual stream architecture inspired by DeepMAO, enabling standard RGB trained vision pipelines to operate without visible light. Evaluation across object detection, ArUco marker detection, and visual SLAM demonstrates that CLEAR-IR produces reliable perception in conditions ranging from well lit to completely dark, where passive RGB based enhancement methods fail entirely due to the physical absence of sensor signal.

## Zooming without Zooming v2: From Seeing Details to Connecting Evidence for Generalizable Multimodal Perception
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37037v1
- **Authors**: ['Lai Wei', 'Yatai Li', 'Liangbo He', 'Jun Lan', 'Renjie Gu', 'Linhan Cao', 'Junkai Lin', 'Huijia Zhu', 'Weiqiang Wang', 'Linghe Kong', 'Yue Wang', 'Zhuosheng Zhang', 'Weiran Huang']
- **Abstrat**: Multimodal perception forms the foundation of multimodal intelligence. In particular, spatial perception and multimodal long-context understanding are two pivotal tasks that underpin embodied AI and multimodal agents. However, synthesizing targeted, high-quality training data for these tasks remains difficult and costly. Our key insight is that these tasks share an underlying capability, which we call connecting evidence: identifying relevant visual facts, binding them to the correct entities, recovering their relations, and composing them into an answer. Crucially, targeted training data are far easier to synthesize for this capability than for these tasks themselves. To strengthen this capability, we propose EvidenceConnect, an easy-to-implement data synthesis framework that uses zoomed views of related regions within a single image to generate questions requiring comparison, aggregation, or chaining of visual evidence. To ensure quality, a reference answer is retained only when independent VLMs agree on it, and region-ablation tests discard questions whose answer survives the removal of selected regions. We then train base VLMs on the resulting data with RLVR, obtaining Zooming without Zooming v2 (ZwZ2). Although the synthesized data are not tailored to any downstream benchmark, ZwZ2 improves fine-grained and spatial perception, with further gains on embodied spatial reasoning, multimodal long-context understanding, multimodal agent tasks, and general visual reasoning. These results establish EvidenceConnect as an effective framework for supervising evidence connection and suggest that strengthening this shared capability leads to more generalizable multimodal perception.





## Executable Archaeology of Programs That Model Programming: Reanimating the Heuristic Compiler (1961) and KBEmacs (1984)
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36341v1
- **Authors**: ['Jeff Shrager', 'Kent M. Pitman', 'Mia Golec']
- **Abstrat**: Early AI programs were often also theories of how people think, and programs that wrote programs were, in part, theories of how people program. Here we report on the reanimation of two such programs that offer two seemingly different theories of programming and were developed for quite different purposes. The first, Herbert Simon's Heuristic Compiler (1961), was an experimental model of programming as problem-solving, applying the means-ends analysis methods of the General Problem Solver to investigate the nature of the programming task. The second, KBEmacs (1984), was a working prototype of an intelligent programming assistant developed by MIT's Programmer's Apprentice project, built on the view that expert programmers recognize and combine familiar patterns, or "cliches." It let a programmer build up programs by combining cliches through a stylized English command language. We approach both through the methodology of Executable Archaeology, the study of historical computational systems by running their original code, a process we call "reanimation." Our reanimation of the Heuristic Compiler reproduces the compiled code appearing in Simon's 1963 paper, and our reanimation of KBEmacs reproduces the SQRT sequence of its December 1984 self-test. Examined side by side, the two theories turn out to have much in common: the Heuristic Compiler also works by adapting existing routines, and Simon later argued that search alone requires a large body of programming knowledge. To highlight the reflexivity of this project, we deliberately used a modern AI-assisted programming tool, Anthropic's Claude Code (Opus 5.5), to carry out much of the detailed reanimation work, building on a significant base of existing software. The explicit, executable theories of programming that both systems embodied are largely absent from today's far more capable AI coding assistants.





## AmaraSpatial-10K: A Spatially and Semantically Aligned 3D Dataset for Spatial Computing and Embodied AI
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23018v3
- **Authors**: ['Mohammad Sadegh Salehi', 'Alexander J. P. Perkins', 'Igor Pardo Maurell', 'Ashkan Dabbagh', 'Raymond Wong']
- **Abstrat**: Web-scale 3D asset collections are abundant but rarely ready to ingest without cleanup, suffering from arbitrary metric scaling, incorrect pivots, brittle geometry, and incomplete textures, defects that limit their use in embodied AI, robotics, and spatial computing. We present AmaraSpatial-10K, a dataset of 10,071 synthetic 3D assets standardised for direct ingestion. Each asset ships as a metric-scaled, deterministically anchored .glb with separated PBR maps, a convex collision hull, a paired reference image, and multi-sentence text metadata. Alongside the dataset we introduce a reusable evaluation suite for 3D asset banks, a continuous Scale Plausibility Score (SPS), a keyword-based Concept Density metric, anchor-error auditing, and a cross-modal CLIP coherence protocol, and apply it to AmaraSpatial-10K alongside matched subsets of Objaverse, HSSD, ABO, and GSO. We check the annotations independently of the LLM/VLM pipeline that produced them: measured ABO object heights fall inside our scale intervals 88.7-90.7% of the time, and three non-author raters auditing a stratified sample of 314 assets judge 95.5% plausibly scaled and 99.7% correctly anchored. AmaraSpatial-10K improves CLIP Recall@5 by 3.4x over Objaverse (0.612 vs. 0.181, median rank 267 -> 3), achieves a 99.1% settling-stability rate under Habitat-Sim in static placement and collision tests, with ~20x lower per-load wall time, and as a drop-in Holodeck asset bank improves planned-scale matching and is preferred by blinded human raters in 27 of 40 paired scenes. Controlled ablations on the same asset bank attribute the retrieval gain to description richness.





## E0.5: From Reconstructive to Future-Predictive 3D Foundations for Robotic World-Action Modeling
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36011v1
- **Authors**: ['X-Era AI Lab Team', ':', 'Jianhao Lai', 'Zhihao Zhan', 'Zhiquan Liao', 'Qichang Li', 'Shubin Chen', 'Zhiyuan Liu', 'Hongsen Lei', 'Jianman Lin', 'Xiaoxin Lin', 'Ke Gong', 'Baiyue Gu', 'Shiming Chen', 'Junlin He', 'Longqi Yang', 'Junrong Xiao', 'Haolin Luo', 'Xiaojuan Su', 'Jianfeng Li', 'Yu Luo', 'Xu Sun', 'Likui Zhang', 'Jusheng Zhang', 'Qinhan Lyu', 'Tianshui Chen', 'Liang Lin', 'Guangrun Wang']
- **Abstrat**: World-action models (WAMs) offer a promising framework for embodied intelligence, yet mainstream approaches remain centered on video foundation models or vision-language models. Even when geometric features extracted by 3D models such as VGGT are incorporated, they are typically passed into a video model or VLM that serves as the primary backbone, leaving 3D geometry as an auxiliary input or prediction target rather than the native foundation for world modeling. We present E0.5, a geometry-native world-action model built directly on 3D foundation models. E0.5 introduces two key innovations. First, it adopts a 3D foundation model as the primary backbone, making 3D geometry the native representation for both world modeling and action prediction. Second, it explicitly predicts future 3D states together with actions, enabling the model to capture geometric world dynamics beyond action-only modeling or future video prediction. Experiments on RoboTwin 2.0 first demonstrate the effectiveness of E0.5 without embodied-data pretraining, achieving success rates of 89.90\% and 50.09\% under clean and randomized settings, respectively. Building on these results, we further pretrain E0.5 on more than 160k robot trajectories from AgiBotWorld-Beta, jointly learning robot actions and future geometry within the pretrained 3D foundation. This embodied pretraining improves mean real-world manipulation success rates by approximately 15 and 7 percentage points on AgileX Piper and Zerith H1, respectively. Together, these results demonstrate the potential of geometry-native foundations to acquire robot interaction knowledge and transfer it to physical manipulation. E0.5 embodies this geometry-native approach as a member of the VWA family of world-action models.
