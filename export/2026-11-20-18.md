## One Prior, Two Dynamics: Repurposing Video Diffusion Transformers for Self-Supervised Latent Action Learning
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26617v1
- **Authors**: ['Yuxin Jiang', 'Wenqiang Sun', 'Jiaqi Li', 'Yuchao Gu', 'Lvmin Zhang', 'Ivor Tsang', 'Mike Zheng Shou']
- **Abstrat**: Videos reveal how the world changes, but rarely the actions that cause those changes. Latent action learning recovers such supervision from visual transitions, yet often learns the underlying dynamics largely from scratch. We ask whether a pretrained video generator can instead provide the prior for both inverse and forward dynamics. We introduce O$^{2}$LaF, built on a simple principle: one prior, two dynamics. We initialize both models from the same pretrained video DiT and adapt them to their distinct roles: a feed-forward transition encoder with learnable action queries for inverse dynamics, and a causal, action-conditioned video generator for forward dynamics. By controlling information access and optimizing both dynamics end-to-end through a compact bottleneck, O$^{2}$LaF learns transferable actions from unlabeled videos. Across navigation and robotic manipulation, O$^{2}$LaF sets a new state of the art in few-shot action probing and enables stronger zero-shot action transfer across visual contexts and embodiments. Beyond generative video priors, we further extend our framework to predictive representation priors such as V-JEPA2, achieving competitive performance. Our findings suggest a broader paradigm for latent-action learning: repurposing pretrained video models to discover transferable actions and predict their consequences.





## ZeroVLN: Learning to Navigate without Supervised Fine-Tuning of the VLM Brain
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26414v1
- **Authors**: ['Zixuan Huang', 'Yang Zhou', 'Guli Zhang', 'Sunzhu Li', 'Hongyan Xie', 'Zehao Chen', 'Fuzhen Zhuang', 'Kaixuan Wang', 'Xiaozhi Chen', 'Yikun Ban', 'Deqing Wang']
- **Abstrat**: Representative vision-language navigation (VLN) methods typically couple a vision-language model (VLM) brain with a learnable action head and fine-tune the VLM brain on navigation-domain data to predict geometric goals. However, such adaptation can substantially alter base VLM weights and degrade general visual capabilities. We introduce ZeroVLN, a slow-fast navigation framework that achieves effective navigation with zero navigation-domain SFT of the VLM brain. Rather than teaching the brain to predict geometric goals, we equip it with a semantic tool-calling interface for conveying movement decisions to an action expert in natural language. We first adapt the action expert to carry out these movement requests, then jointly improve the brain's movement decisions and the action expert's continuous execution through closed-loop reinforcement learning (RL). Trained on the same navigation episodes, ZeroVLN substantially outperforms representative SFT-based hierarchical VLN methods on standard VLN-CE (continuous environments) benchmarks, while inducing only minor changes to the brain's weights and largely preserving its general capabilities. To our knowledge, ZeroVLN is the first slow-fast VLN framework with zero navigation-domain SFT of the VLM brain, and the first to jointly optimize its slow and fast policies through closed-loop agentic RL in VLN-CE.





## SpecWM: Turning Verification into Refinement for Streaming World Models
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26211v1
- **Authors**: ['Jiaming Li', 'Lingzhen Li', 'Xiaze Yang', 'Chenyu Zhu', 'Kaiqi Liu', 'Xiaotong Zhao', 'Songlian Li', 'Toyota Li', 'Alan Zhao', 'Haobin Zhong', 'Yue Ma', 'Zhiyuan Ma']
- **Abstrat**: Recently, video world models have advanced interactive generation, with large models offering higher quality and smaller models enabling faster inference. Existing speculative methods combine their strengths through fast draft generation and selective target regeneration guided by rewards on decoded frames. However, verification covers only the external scorer's evaluation dimensions and adds decoding and scoring costs. Moreover, these scores provide no correction, requiring costly target regeneration for rejected drafts. To address these limitations, we propose Speculative World Model (SpecWM), a unified, training-free inference framework for streaming world models. It verifies draft chunks using the target's latent velocity field. Verification also yields a reusable correction; two thresholds select Accept, Refine, or Reject. Specifically, one-step refinement reuses it to improve perceptual quality, while three-step refinement strengthens camera following and trajectory consistency. These findings motivate transition-aware refinement. On WBench Navigation, SpecWM improves visual quality at 1.63$\times$ speedup over the target and raises NavScore from the draft's 75.99 to 85.63, achieving the best quality-speed-control trade-off. Additionally, our LongSpecBench shows sustained quality and control over long, multi-turn rollouts, while experiments on Hy-Nomas validate SpecWM across backbones and denoising budgets.





## From Sequence to Space: Explicit Spatial Belief with Vision-Language Distillation for Active Geo-Localization
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25866v1
- **Authors**: ['Ziyang Xu', 'Peng Han', 'Hai Zhou']
- **Abstrat**: Active geo-localization (AGL) asks an agent to navigate a small aerial search grid and reach a target specified by an aerial image, a ground-level photograph, or text. Existing methods align the goal modalities well, but they keep the navigation state inside a large sequential Transformer and train with a dense distance reward that uses the hidden goal location. This paper proposes EAGLE, an Explicit spatial-belief Agent for Geo-Localization and Exploration. Offline training jointly optimizes a teacher-conditioned Swin branch and a context-free Swin student from the same randomly masked partial mosaics. The teacher branch attends to frozen full-scene context extracted by Qwen2.5-VL-7B, both branches receive masked-cell aerial-feature reconstruction and uncertainty supervision, while the student additionally distills stop-gradient feature predictions from the teacher. After distillation, Qwen, its cached context, and the teacher-attention branch are discarded. The retained student predicts cell-level aerial-feature embeddings and predictive uncertainty from partial observations alone. These predictions are combined with the modality-aligned goal embedding and a trajectory-derived visitation mask to construct the spatial belief tensor. The student is then frozen while a compact CNN actor-critic is optimized with PPO. EAGLE achieves state-of-the-art average success rates of 78.1%, 76.4%, and 76.8% on MASA, xBD zero-shot transfer, and the MM-GAG zero-shot aerial-goal split, respectively, without using a VLM or cached visual tokens at inference. Code will be made publicly available.





## Cognitive-Horizon Action Chunking: Memory-Guided Adaptive Reasoning for Embodied Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25587v1
- **Authors**: ['Weile Gong', 'Zijian Lu', 'Jiahao Zheng', 'Bo Xu', 'Yiping Zuo', 'Jiajia Guo', 'Xin He', 'Weijun Wang', 'Weibei Fan']
- **Abstrat**: Multimodal foundation models are advancing embodied navigation, but frequent inference incurs substantial computational cost. We present Cognitive-Horizon Action Chunking (CHAC), a training-free framework for memory-guided adaptive reasoning with a frozen navigation policy. Retained visual context, completion cues, and controller feedback determine the cognitive horizon, or how many cached waypoints the robot executes before refreshing its prediction. At the policy's stop request, local visitation history guides a bounded correction with at most one extra movement and no additional model calls. Across four physical navigation tasks with 40 runs per method, CHAC achieves 18 successes versus 14 for native execution, reducing model calls by 54.9% and GPU inference time by 49.7%.

- **Abstrat**: We propose LCLA (Language-Conditioned Latent Alignment), a framework for vision-language navigation that learns modular perception-action interfaces by aligning sensory observations to a latent representation of an expert policy. ## When and How Much to Imagine: Adaptive Test-Time Scaling with World Models for Visual Spatial Reasoning
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08236v3
- **Authors**: ['Shoubin Yu', 'Yue Zhang', 'Zun Wang', 'Jaehong Yoon', 'Huaxiu Yao', 'Mingyu Ding', 'Mohit Bansal']
- **Abstrat**: Despite rapid progress in MLLMs, visual spatial reasoning remains unreliable when correct answers depend on how a scene would appear under unseen or alternative viewpoints. Recent work addresses this by augmenting reasoning with world models for visual imagination, but questions such as when imagination is actually necessary, how much of it is beneficial, and when it becomes harmful, remain poorly understood. In practice, indiscriminate imagination can increase computation and even degrade performance by introducing misleading evidence. In this work, we present an in-depth analysis of test-time visual imagination as a controllable resource for spatial reasoning. We first study when static visual evidence is sufficient, when imagination improves reasoning, and how excessive or unnecessary imagination affects accuracy and efficiency. To support this analysis, we then introduce AVIC, an adaptive test-time framework with world models that explicitly reasons about the sufficiency of current visual evidence before selectively invoking and scaling visual imagination. Finally, to further learn this gating and planning behavior without any annotation of when and how much to imagine, we introduce AVIC-R, which trains the policy via GRPO from QA-correctness rewards and penalties by imagination cost. Across spatial reasoning benchmarks (SAT, MMSI) and an embodied navigation benchmark (R2R), our results reveal clear scenarios where imagination is critical, marginal, or detrimental, and show that selective control can match or outperform fixed imagination strategies with substantially fewer world-model calls and language tokens. Our AVIC-R surpasses strong proprietary baselines including GPT-4o and GPT-4.1 while invoking the world model less often. Overall, our findings highlight the importance of analyzing and controlling test-time imagination for efficient and reliable spatial reasoning.





## GEMS: Generation and Experience Memory for Subgoal-Guided Long-Horizon World Model Planning
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25134v1
- **Authors**: ['Yangchen Zhao', 'Ali Payani', 'Caiwen Ding']
- **Abstrat**: Visual world models enable effective short-horizon goal-conditioned planning, such as reaching nearby targets and pushing objects over short distances, by predicting candidate action outcomes and comparing their representations with a goal representation. However, extending this approach to long-horizon tasks is challenging: prediction errors accumulate over extended rollouts, while latent-space similarity to distant goals provides limited guidance for ranking candidate outcomes. Existing methods address these limitations by introducing intermediate subgoals, either generated by learned models or selected from experience memory. Generated subgoals can contain hallucinations or be inaccurate in aspects such as object positions and timing. Memory-based approaches are constrained by experience coverage and may lack precise guidance for selecting appropriate intermediate states from stored observations. We introduce GEMS, which generates temporally structured subgoals to guide action search and state selection from Latent Experience Memory (LEM). A distance gate determines whether predicted outcomes are evaluated against the generated subgoal or the selected memory state. Experiments across six tasks and task suites spanning manipulation, navigation, and reaching demonstrate the effectiveness of GEMS for long-horizon planning. GEMS outperforms state-of-the-art world-model planning baselines across different manipulation settings, achieving success rates of 92.8% on Cube, 68.4% on Push-T, and 54.1% on Meta-World. It improves over the strongest evaluated baseline on these tasks by 24.0, 7.3, and 22.2 percentage points, respectively. These results demonstrate that generated subgoals and experience memory can jointly strengthen long-horizon planning with a frozen world model.

## Accord: Universal Online Metric Localization and Dense Mapping from one Camera and an IMU
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25279v1
- **Authors**: ['Jaafar Mahmoud', 'Arthur Movsesyan', 'Cuong Huynh', 'Mikhail Iumanov', 'Sergey Zagoruyko', 'Sergey Kolyubin']
- **Abstrat**: Dense metric mapping from a single camera and IMU remains challenging: visual-inertial odometry (VIO) provides metric motion but sparse geometry, whereas feed-forward depth models predict dense structure without ensuring metric scale. We present Accord, which constructs an accurate online dense metric map from any camera with an IMU. A VIO filter publishes metric poses, a depth foundation model conditioned on them predicts depth for short keyframe windows, and a factor graph fuses the resulting submaps. Recent work has explored several approaches to this problem. What is essential is the multi-view agreement (Accord), which is considered in SLAM as a source of geometric self-consistency. Accord anchors each keyframe window to metric 3D points triangulated from the filter poses, then exploits bidirectional dense depth agreement between co-visible images to estimate and correct per-image scale errors while preserving the metric scale established by the anchors. Context keyframes further improve depth prediction, and the resulting submaps are fused in a factor graph. We evaluate a single mapping configuration across five public camera-IMU setups and a new Phone-rig/GoPro dataset. With nothing tuned per dataset/setup and every map aligned only by Accord's own trajectory, Accord achieves consistently best/second-best mean F-score at 5 cm on every platform. Accord is open-source along with the phone-rig dataset for the benefit of the research community

## AnyVIO: Feed-Forward Streaming Visual-Inertial Odometry Across Platforms
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26625v1
- **Authors**: ['Omoruyi Atekha', 'Marcus Greiff', 'Ray Zhang', 'Luca Carlone']
- **Abstrat**: Geometric foundation models (GFMs) estimate camera mo- tion and scene structure from images without requiring ex- pert parameter tuning, but either lack metric scale or rely on learned priors to infer scale, which is unobservable from monocular images alone. Inertial sensing offers a way to observe metric scale, but existing methods add inertial in- formation through a filter or factor graph, which reintro- duces bespoke parameter tuning and optimization costs that can grow with the trajectory. To bridge this gap, we present AnyVIO, a real-time, feed-forward streaming model for monocular visual-inertial odometry (VIO), which combines a streaming GFM with an inertial foundation model (IFM). Both the GFM and IFM predict the metric motion between consecutive frames, and a lightweight learned fusion head quantifies the reliability of each estimate and blends them in a single forward pass. Across nine datasets recorded from cars, legged and ground robots, and handheld and head- mounted devices, AnyVIO reduces the metric position error of its visual model by 36% while publishing each pose esti- mate 70 ms after an image is received. We further deploy AnyVIO onboard two real vehicles at up to 140 km/h, where it remains accurate in poor lighting and heavy dust.





## Stereo Visual Odometry with In-the-Loop Geometric Feed-Forward Models Towards Dense Metric Mapping
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25445v1
- **Authors**: ['Fahira Afzal Maken', 'Sagun Man Singh Shrestha', 'Edward Vanderfeen', 'Lucas Carvalho de Lima', 'Rongxin Li', 'Changming Sun', 'Milad Ramezani']
- **Abstrat**: We present a stereo visual odometry (SVO) framework that integrates geometric feed-forward models (GFMs) for dense depth prediction with factor-graph-based visual odometry to achieve accurate localisation and dense metric mapping. Our key idea is to exploit sparse but reliable metric geometry estimated from stereo and temporal multi-view observations to calibrate dense GFM predictions, while retaining the robustness and efficiency of model-based SVO. Rather than directly conditioning the GFM on potentially noisy stereo depth, optimised 3D landmarks are used to jointly estimate a scale-shift transformation over all keyframes within each local submap, yielding metrically consistent dense depth predictions. To balance accuracy and computational efficiency, we introduce a hierarchical factor-graph architecture. At the local level, sliding-window optimisation estimates high-frequency camera poses and multi-view-refined 3D landmarks. Dense predictions are generated only for selected keyframes and registered within each submap using SE(3) Generalised ICP. Consecutive submaps are further aligned in Sim(3) using their shared geometry, allowing residual scale inconsistencies to be corrected. These intra- and inter-submap constraints are incorporated into a global factor graph to produce a consistent trajectory and dense metric reconstruction. Extensive experiments on public and in-house datasets demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves competitive odometry accuracy against geometric and learning-based baselines while providing accurate dense metric reconstruction and improved robustness to failures in learned depth prediction.

## JARVIS: An Agent That Designs, Builds, and Tests Circuit Boards on Its Own
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26503v1
- **Authors**: ['Shibo Zhou', 'Tianqi Hong', 'Zheng Yan', 'He Yang']
- **Abstrat**: Power electronics is central to the energy transition, but developing new hardware still relies on human-led design, fabrication, testing, and iteration. JARVIS maintains machine-specific capabilities and constraints, scans its workspace using a robot-mounted 3D scanner, and constructs an executable digital twin in which it rehearses robot motions, fabrication steps, and circuit behavior before consuming materials. A single text instruction initiates the physical experiment. Its digital-twin rehearsal identified five issues before fabrication, including a test criterion that would have incorrectly rejected a functioning board. During execution, the agent also resolved encountered faults autonomously. These results demonstrate an integrated agent-driven workflow for physical design, fabrication, measurement, and iterative improvement of power-electronic circuitry. To our knowledge, JARVIS is the first system in power electronics in which an agent closes the physical design-build-test-learn loop for circuit boards.

This paper presents a lightweight physics-informed probabilistic framework for efficient GSL under spatially varying indoor airflow. As the robot navigates, it continuously collects wind and gas measurements and reconstructs the spatially varying airflow field online. The estimated wind field drives a forward dispersion model that predicts the gas pattern for each candidate source location. These predictions are compared with the measured gas pattern to update a probabilistic belief over the source location. As the wind field is reconstructed incrementally, the framework also accounts for the spatially non-uniform reliability of wind field estimation and its influence on the source estimation. The resulting belief map is then used for informative path planning to guide the robot toward informative regions. High-fidelity simulations provide a comprehensive evaluation of the individual components and their integrations within the complete system, while physical indoor experiments assess the system performance under realistic sensing, motion, and airflow conditions. The results demonstrate that incorporating lightweight physical knowledge significantly improves localization robustness under spatially complex airflow and enables more efficient exploration.
