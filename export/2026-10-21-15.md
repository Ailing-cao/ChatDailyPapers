## EchoNav: Evidential BEV Completion from Historical Observations for Visual Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27616v1
- **Authors**: ['Diwen Liu', 'Yiyang Shao', 'Borui Wang', 'Chi Zhang', 'Yunhui Liu']
- **Abstrat**: Visual navigation in cluttered environments requires robots to recover previously observed structure and infer obstacles and free space that remain occluded. We present a framework that combines temporal aggregation and occupancy completion in an evidential bird's-eye-view (BEV) representation predicted from short RGB sequences. A decoder adapts frozen VGGT-Omega features and combines historical residual proposals using cellwise weights and a learned no-update option. The BEV predictor produces a local map in the latest ego frame without depth or camera-pose inputs or explicit geometric warping. The evidential model treats observed free space separately and predicts occupancy with associated confidence across the remaining supported region. We train the predictor on simulated RGB sequences paired with complete and visibility-masked occupancy annotations. Our experiments evaluate navigation in simulation and on a wheeled robot, with additional demonstrations on quadruped and humanoid robots. In simulated sequential two-goal navigation, our system achieves 89.6% success, outperforming the strongest evaluated baseline by 32.5 percentage points. Across 60 wheeled-robot trials in 20 scenes, it achieves 73.3% success, compared with 33.3% for the strongest evaluated baseline.





## MatchCityLoc: Aerial Localization in LoD City Models via Surface Normal Matching
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27110v1
- **Authors**: ['Oussema Dhaouadi', 'Zuria Bauer', 'Johannes Michael Meier', 'Marc Pollefeys', 'Daniel Cremers', 'Olaf Wysocki']
- **Abstrat**: Autonomous Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) navigation in urban environments requires precise pose estimation. Because Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) signals are often unreliable in cities, visual localization serves as a crucial complement. Within this domain, textureless Level-of-Detail (LoD) city models provide a lightweight and privacy-preserving substitute for heavy 3D textured meshes. However, localizing against abstract geometry is challenging. Existing pipelines typically score 2D silhouette overlaps, which can suffer from severe geometric ambiguities. We present MatchCityLoc, a framework that shifts this paradigm to direct feature matching from a noisy 6-DoF pose prior. By extracting surface normals from the query image using a zero-shot foundation model and matching them against rendered LoD normals, we establish explicit 3D-to-2D correspondences. This enables a Perspective-n-Point (PnP) pose solve, which we iteratively refine through a render-and-match loop. Experiments demonstrate strong pose recall and accurate translation and rotation among the evaluated off-the-shelf LoD-based methods with very few renderings. By bridging the modality gap between aerial imagery and texture-free geometry, MatchCityLoc enables accurate localization using lightweight, privacy-preserving city models.

## World2HOSI: A Multi-Expert Harness for Long-Horizon Human-Object-Scene Interaction Generation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27725v1
- **Authors**: ['Jiushu Li', 'Haoran Wang', 'Yumeng Liu', 'Zhiyang Dou', 'Yuan Liu', 'Yuexin Ma', 'Cheng Lin']
- **Abstrat**: Long-horizon human-object-scene interaction (HOSI) is central to embodied AI, animation, and game development. Existing human-object interaction (HOI) and human-scene interaction (HSI) models excel in their respective domains, but none alone covers tasks requiring multiple interaction skills. Training unified HOSI models is hindered by scarce and costly data. We introduce World2HOSI, a multi-expert orchestration harness that generates continuous HOSI data from 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) scenes and natural language instructions. Our key insight is that long-horizon HOSI hinges not on a stronger single model but on orchestration. Given a shared, structured scene state and an orchestration harness grounded in it, existing experts can jointly accomplish tasks beyond the reach of any of them alone. World2HOSI realizes this insight as a closed loop around a persistent scene state built from decoupled background and object assets. A harness plans action stages over this state, invokes experts via their native conditioning interfaces, and writes each action's effects back, so that every invocation is grounded in the evolving world. To reduce physical artifacts, bounded residual optimization and contact-driven physics simulation further ensure the physical plausibility of human motion and object manipulation, respectively, while preserving interaction intent. Experiments and a perceptual user study show that World2HOSI achieves the best task completion and interaction quality on the evaluated HOSI tasks, while generating more diverse data.





## NeoWorld-Pro: Programming Interactive Scenes from Monocular Images for Embodied Simulation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24212v2
- **Authors**: ['Yumeng He', 'Eachen Soong', 'Xiaotian Yang', 'Weijia Zhang', 'Zanwei Zhou', 'Junru Gong', 'Xiaokang Yang', 'Yunbo Wang']
- **Abstrat**: The advancement of Embodied AI necessitates high-quality simulation assets that faithfully mirror the real world. However, transforming raw visual observations into simulation-ready scenes remains challenging due to the lack of physical grounding and scene-level interactivity in current image-to-URDF methods. ## SPAR: Search-trained Push Action Ranker for Efficient Navigation Among Movable Obstacles
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28362v1
- **Authors**: ['Dhruv Metha Ramesh', 'Tri-An Nguyen', 'Junshan Huang', 'Jingjin Yu', 'Kostas E. Bekris', 'Abdeslam Boularias']
- **Abstrat**: Navigation Among Movable Obstacles (NAMO) requires a robot to rearrange objects that block access to its goal. For a pushing robot, progress may depend on a setup push that enables a later opening without itself clearing a passage. The number of candidate sequences can grow exponentially with sequence length, making physics-based search costly. We introduce the Search-trained Push Action Ranker (SPAR), which learns to prioritize push primitives from simulated search outcomes. Searches over sequences of up to two pushes per object provide supervision that distinguishes immediate openings from setup pushes. A transformer trained with regression and ranking losses scores primitives using contact tokens conditioned on scene geometry and reachability regions. The same model guides simulator evaluations in SPAR Search and selects actions for direct execution in SPAR Policy. Both modes solve successive keyhole problems that open access to neighboring free-space regions within a NAMO planner. Across successful runs on $300$ simulated two-push keyhole problems, SPAR Search reduces median simulator calls by $7.0\times$, leading to a $3.8\times$ reduction in planning time relative to Random Search. Without retraining, the same ranker guides search on $600$ keyhole problems which have no solution within two pushes. At a depth limit of five pushes, SPAR Search achieves $50.7\%$ success within $100$ simulator calls, compared with $20.2\%$ for Random Search. These gains carry over to NAMO planning: on hard simulated scenes, SPAR Search achieves $47\%$ success within $100$ simulator calls, compared with $21\%$ for Random Search. In real-robot experiments, SPAR Policy succeeds in $37/40$ trials without a physics-based digital twin, compared with $33/40$ for SPAR Search and $27/40$ for Random Search.

## CovNav: Conformal Prediction-Guided Adaptive Inference for Object Goal Navigation
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27677v1
- **Authors**: ['Pengying Wu', 'Zhejia Wen', 'Kang Ding', 'Kangjie Zhou', 'Chunxuan Jiao', 'Wei Pan', 'Xiangli Nie', 'Chang Liu']
- **Abstrat**: In object goal navigation (ObjectNav), an embodied agent must find a target object (e.g., "find a bed") in a previously unseen environment, relying on spatial commonsense such as object co-occurrence. Large Language Models (LLMs) encode this commonsense, but deploying them on robots exposes a trade-off between reasoning quality and efficiency: cloud-based models incur latency and privacy risks, whereas small on-device LLMs are fast but prone to hallucinated spatial judgments. Hybrid reasoning LLMs offer both a fast non-thinking mode and a slow thinking mode, which raises a key question for embodied agents: when is slow thinking worth its cost? We propose CovNav, a training-free framework that answers this question with Conformal Prediction (CP), turning dual-process (System 1 / System 2) inference into an uncertainty-aware decision. Much like recent decision models such as Jev, CovNav casts each spatial query as a structured binary decision rather than free-form generation: a local LLM in fast mode judges whether the target is likely near an observed object, CP converts its token probabilities into a prediction set, and only ambiguous queries are escalated to slow chain-of-thought reasoning. The object-pair formulation keeps calibration tractable and supports open-set objects through off-the-shelf detectors. Experiments on Gibson and HM3D show that CovNav surpasses the strongest zero-shot baseline by 0.8 and 1.7 percentage points in success rate on HM3D v1 and v2, while invoking slow reasoning for only 12.4% of queries and retaining most of the gain of always-on thinking at a fraction of its latency. Real-robot experiments on a TurtleBot 4 further validate the approach. CovNav shows how calibrated uncertainty can decide when an embodied agent should think slowly, offering a principled form of adaptive test-time reasoning for LLM-based robots.

## Towards Autonomous Bricklaying Using Boston Dynamics Spot

## Interruption-Aware Recurrent Reinforcement Learning for UAS Navigation Under Temporary Overrides
- **Url**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28121v1
- **Authors**: ['Christopher Conrad', 'Deepak Panda', 'Weisi Guo', 'Antonios Tsourdos']
- **Abstrat**: Safe autonomous navigation of UASs requires tactical deconfliction to address conflicts that strategic coordination cannot prevent. This task is challenging under partial observability, when sensor or traffic data are missing, delayed, or degraded. Geometry-based methods can offer separation assurances but face limitations in dense encounters. Recurrent reinforcement learning has therefore been proposed to learn navigation policies that integrate observation-action histories to optimise long-term safety-efficiency trade-offs. However, operational safeguards may require a remote pilot, onboard detect-and-avoid system, or runtime assurance mechanism to temporarily override the learned policy. A research gap remains in understanding how these interruptions affect recurrent policies when the agent regains authority. Benchmark evidence shows that interruptions make task variables less decodable from the recurrent hidden state and increase post-interruption collision risk. The findings suggest that altered recurrent context prolongs post-handback degradation through an action-observation feedback loop, in which changed actions alter the flight path and subsequent recurrent updates. Across simulations, all three architectures preserve more decodable task information than a standard recurrent policy. Moreover, the strongest architecture reduces post-interruption collisions by 74% and interruption-induced mission failures by 69% relative to the recurrent baseline, without degrading interruption-free performance. These results show that safe interruption does not guarantee safe handback, and support interruption-aware recurrent design for resilient UAS autonomy.

- **Authors**: ['Michael Chen', 'Hong Chul Nam', 'Xi Deng', 'Anima Anandkumar']

- **Authors**: ['Emanuele Mezzi', 'Gertjan Burghouts', 'Fabio Massacci', 'Mengyuan Zhang']
- **Abstrat**: Multimodal question answering (MMQA) is based on integrating heterogeneous data sources, selectively leveraging relevant modalities, and ignoring distractors.
